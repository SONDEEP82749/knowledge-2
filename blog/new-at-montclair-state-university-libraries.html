New at Montclair State University Libraries: A Complete Guide to Maximizing Your Research & Study Success

10 Actionable Strategies to Succeed at Montclair State University Libraries

Strategy 1: Master the Library Catalog (Montclair OneSearch)

What it is: The Montclair OneSearch is your first stop for finding books, e-books, journal articles, and more across all university libraries.

How to use it effectively:

  • Use advanced search filters – Instead of a simple keyword search, refine your results by publication date, subject, format (e.g., e-book, article), and availability.
  • Check for full-text availability – If a book isn’t in stock, request it via Interlibrary Loan (ILL)—most articles arrive within 24-48 hours.
  • Save and organize your searches – Create personal folders in OneSearch to track research progress.

Real-World Example: A psychology major researching sleep disorders starts with a broad search for "sleep disorders" but quickly narrows it down using filters:

  • Publication Date (2018-Present)
  • Format (Peer-Reviewed Articles Only)
  • Subject (Neuroscience & Sleep Medicine)

This reduces irrelevant results and ensures they find recent, credible sources.

Common Mistake & Fix: ❌ Only searching for books without checking journals or databases. ✅ Always expand your search to include articles, dissertations, and multimedia for a comprehensive view of your topic.

Strategy 2: Leverage Database-Specific Tools (Beyond OneSearch)

While OneSearch is powerful, specialized databases provide deeper, more targeted research.

Key Databases at Montclair State:

Database Best For How to Access
JSTOR Humanities, Social Sciences, Arts Available via Montclair OneSearch
ScienceDirect STEM Research Linked from the library website
ProQuest Business, Education, Health Searchable via Montclair’s A-Z Database List
Google Scholar Cross-disciplinary research Set up Montclair State as your library link in Scholar Preferences

How to Optimize Your Searches:

  • Use Boolean operators (AND, OR, NOT) to narrow or broaden results.
    • Example: "climate change" AND "policy" (finds articles on both topics)
    • Example: "AI" NOT "fiction" (excludes non-academic AI references)
  • Check for PDF availability – Some databases offer full-text downloads, while others require interlibrary loan requests.

Real-World Example: A business student analyzing supply chain disruptions uses ProQuest to search for:

  • "supply chain resilience" AND "COVID-19"
  • Filters by "Case Studies" to find real-world case analyses.

Common Mistake & Fix: ❌ Assuming all databases work the same way. ✅ Each database has unique features—take time to explore help guides (available on the library’s Research Guides page).

Strategy 3: Utilize Research Guides for Subject-Specific Help

Montclair State Libraries provides subject-specific research guides created by librarians to simplify complex topics.

How to Find Them:

  1. Go to Montclair LibGuides.
  2. Browse by subject (e.g., Nursing, Computer Science, History).
  3. Each guide includes:
    • Recommended databases
    • Key journals & books
    • Citation style guides (APA, MLA, Chicago)
    • Tutorials on advanced search techniques

Real-World Example: A nursing student researching diabetes management visits the Nursing Research Guide and finds:

  • Top 5 journals in diabetes care (e.g., Diabetes Care, Journal of Diabetes Research).
  • A step-by-step guide on finding evidence-based practice (EBP) articles.
  • Citation examples in APA format.

Common Mistake & Fix: ❌ Wasting time searching randomly without knowing where to start. ✅ Use research guides as your roadmap—they save hours of trial and error.

Strategy 4: Request Materials via Interlibrary Loan (ILL)

Not every book or article is available at Montclair State. Interlibrary Loan (ILL) lets you borrow materials from other institutions for free.

How to Request:

  1. Search Montclair OneSearch or a database.
  2. If the item says "Not Available," click "Request via Interlibrary Loan."
  3. Fill out the form with your student ID and contact info.
  4. Most articles arrive in 24-48 hours; books take 3-5 business days.

Pro Tips:

  • Check processing times—some institutions take longer than others.
  • Set up email alerts for ILL updates.
  • Return borrowed items on time to avoid fines.

Real-World Example: A literature major needs a rare 19th-century novel not in Montclair’s collection. They request it via ILL and receive a digital scan within 48 hours, allowing them to complete their analysis without delays.

Common Mistake & Fix: ❌ Assuming ILL is slow and unreliable. ✅ Most requests arrive quickly—it’s a game-changer for niche research topics.

Strategy 5: Attend Library Workshops & Research Help Sessions

Montclair State Libraries offers free workshops on:

  • Citation management (Zotero, EndNote)
  • Academic writing & avoiding plagiarism
  • Data analysis with SPSS or R

How to Find Workshops:

  • Check the Library Events Calendar.
  • Some are in-person, while others are virtual (great for remote students).

Real-World Example: *A graduate student struggling with SPSS for statistics attends a Data Analysis Workshop and learns:

  • How to clean datasets efficiently.
  • How to generate accurate visualizations for their thesis.
  • How to interpret p-values correctly.

Common Mistake & Fix: ❌ Skipping workshops because they seem "too basic." ✅ Even advanced students benefit—workshops fill knowledge gaps you might not realize you have.

Strategy 6: Use Zotero or EndNote for Citation Management

Managing citations manually is error-prone and time-consuming. Zotero (free) and EndNote (paid, but available via Montclair) automate the process.

How to Set It Up:

  1. Download Zotero (https://www.zotero.org/).
  2. Create a library and add references from databases or PDFs.
  3. Generate citations & bibliographies in APA, MLA, Chicago, etc.
  4. Sync across devices (Zotero Web, mobile apps).

Real-World Example: *A history student researching the American Revolution uses Zotero to:

  • Collect 50+ sources from JSTOR and Google Scholar.
  • Auto-generate an APA bibliography in seconds.
  • Insert citations directly into Word with one click.

Common Mistake & Fix: ❌ Waiting until the last minute to organize citations. ✅ Start early—Zotero saves hours of stress during final revisions.

Strategy 7: Explore Open Access & Free Resources

Not all research requires paid subscriptions. Montclair State supports open-access (OA) resources, including:

  • DOAJ (Directory of Open Access Journals) – Free peer-reviewed articles.
  • PubMed Central – Biomedical research.
  • Project MUSE – Humanities & social sciences.
  • arXiv – Preprint server for physics, math, and computer science.

How to Find OA Resources:

  • Use Google Scholar and filter by "Free PDF."
  • Check the Montclair Open Access Guide.

Real-World Example: *A computer science student researching machine learning algorithms finds a free preprint on arXiv that becomes the foundation of their research paper.

Common Mistake & Fix: ❌ Assuming everything requires a subscription. ✅ Open-access resources are growing—always check before paying for access.

Strategy 9: Ask for Research Help from Librarians

Montclair’s librarians are experts in research strategies and can:

  • Help refine your search terms.
  • Teach you how to evaluate sources.
  • Guide you through complex databases.
  • Review your bibliography for accuracy.

How to Get Help:

  • Chat with a Librarian (available via Ask a Librarian).
  • Schedule a Research Appointment (https://montclair.libcal.com/appointments).
  • Visit the Research Help Desk (located in the Library & Learning Commons).

Real-World Example: A political science student struggles to find primary sources on the Vietnam War. A librarian helps them:

  • Locate declassified documents in the National Archives database.
  • Use keyword variations like "Vietnam War correspondence" instead of just "Vietnam War."
  • Find oral histories from veterans.

Common Mistake & Fix: ❌ Assuming you can figure everything out alone. ✅ Librarians are there to help—don’t hesitate to ask!
