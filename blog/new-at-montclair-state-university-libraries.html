Chapter 1: Getting Oriented – Understanding Montclair State University Libraries

Before diving into strategies, it’s essential to know the layout and key features of the libraries. Montclair State has three main campus libraries, each with unique strengths:

1. The Library at the Center for Academic Success (CAS Library)

  • Location: Inside the Center for Academic Success (CAS) building
  • Specialty: First-year student support, study groups, and one-on-one research help
  • Key Features:
    • 24/7 study rooms (reservable via the Library Portal)
    • Tech kiosks for printing, scanning, and basic editing
    • Peer research assistants (PRA) available for guidance

2. The Library at the School of Business (SB Library)

  • Location: Within the School of Business building
  • Specialty: Business, economics, and entrepreneurship research
  • Key Features:
    • Bloomberg Terminal (for finance and business data)
    • Specialized databases like IBISWorld and Statista
    • Quiet study zones ideal for case study work

3. The Library at the School of Education (SE Library)

  • Location: Inside the School of Education building
  • Specialty: Education, psychology, and social sciences
  • Key Features:
    • ERIC database (Education Resources Information Center)
    • Curriculum materials for teaching practice
    • Collaboration spaces for group projects

4. The Digital Commons & Open Access Resources

  • What it is: A repository of student research, faculty publications, and open-access materials
  • Why it matters: You can publish your work here for free and access peer-reviewed papers without paywalls.

Pro Tip: Bookmark library.montclair.edu—this is your one-stop portal for all library services, including database access, interlibrary loan, and research guides.

Chapter 2: 10 Actionable Strategies to Master Montclair State University Libraries

Now that you know the physical and digital landscape, let’s explore practical strategies to make the most of your library experience.

Strategy 1: Use the Library’s Advanced Search Tools Like a Pro

Montclair State’s library catalog (Montclair OneSearch) is powerful, but most students only scratch the surface. Here’s how to maximize it:

A. Boolean Operators for Precision Searching

Instead of typing vague terms like "climate change," use Boolean operators to refine results:

  • AND → "climate change AND policy" (finds sources with both terms)
  • OR → "global warming OR climate crisis" (finds sources with either term)
  • NOT → "climate change NOT politics" (excludes irrelevant results)

Example: ❌ "How does social media affect mental health?" (too broad) ✅ "social media AND mental health AND adolescents NOT gaming" (narrower, more relevant)

B. Filter by Source Type

After searching, use the left-hand filters to narrow down:

  • Peer-reviewed journals (for academic papers)
  • Books & e-books (for in-depth analysis)
  • News articles (for current events)
  • Datasets (for research projects)

C. Save & Organize Your Searches

  • Create an account in Montclair OneSearch to save searches for later.
  • Use folders in your library account to organize research by class or topic.

Real-World Example: A business student researching "sustainable packaging trends" might:

  1. Search "sustainable packaging AND consumer behavior"
  2. Filter for peer-reviewed articles (2020–2024)
  3. Save the top 5 results in a "Marketing Research" folder

Strategy 3: Access Digital Resources Without Paywalls

One of the biggest frustrations for students is paywalls. Montclair State provides free access to many premium resources. Here’s how to bypass them:

A. Use the "Check for Full Text" Button

  • After searching in Montclair OneSearch, click "Check for Full Text"—if available, you’ll get a direct link.
  • If not, try:
    • Interlibrary Loan (ILL) (request a PDF in 24–72 hours)
    • Google Scholar’s "All Versions" (sometimes finds open-access copies)

B. Explore Open Access Repositories

  • DOAJ (Directory of Open Access Journals) – https://doaj.org
  • PubMed Central (for health sciences) – https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/pmc
  • Montclair’s Digital Commons – https://digitalcommons.montclair.edu

Real-World Example: *A nursing student needs a 2023 study on telehealth in rural areas but hits a paywall in PubMed. ✅ Solution:

  1. Search PubMed → Find the study.
  2. Click "Montclair State University" in the "Check for Full Text" section.
  3. If unavailable, submit an interlibrary loan request—the library will get it for free.

Strategy 4: Master the Art of Citation & Avoid Plagiarism

Citations are non-negotiable, but formatting can be confusing. Montclair State supports multiple styles (APA, MLA, Chicago, etc.), and here’s how to do it right:

A. Use Zotero or RefWorks for Automatic Citations

  • Zotero (free, browser extension) – https://www.zotero.org
  • RefWorks (Montclair’s licensed tool) – https://refworks.proquest.com

How to Set Up Zotero:

  1. Install the Zotero browser connector.
  2. Save PDFs and citations from Montclair databases.
  3. Auto-generate bibliographies in your preferred style.

B. Check for Plagiarism with Turnitin

  • Montclair’s Turnitin integration (via Blackboard) helps catch accidental plagiarism.
  • Tip: Always quote and paraphrase properly—don’t just copy-paste.

Common Mistake: Assuming "paraphrasing" means changing a few words. ❌ Problem: "The study found that climate change is increasing temperatures" (original) vs. "Research shows global warming is rising" (still plagiarized if too similar). ✅ Solution: Rewrite in your own words and cite the source.
