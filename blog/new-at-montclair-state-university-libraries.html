Chapter 1: Getting Started with Montclair State University Libraries

1.1 How to Access Your Library Account & Borrowing Privileges

Before diving into research, you need to set up your library account—this is your gateway to borrowing books, accessing digital resources, and requesting interlibrary loans.

Step-by-Step Guide:

  1. Log in via the MSU Libraries Portal

    • Go to https://www.montclair.edu/library and click "Log In" (top-right corner).
    • Use your MSU username and password (same as for Blackboard or email).

  2. Activate Your Borrowing Privileges

    • Once logged in, navigate to "My Account" (usually under your name or profile icon).
    • If you see a message like "Your account is pending activation," contact the Circulation Desk (via email at library@montclair.edu or phone at 973-655-7070).

  3. Understand Loan Periods & Fines

    • Undergraduate students: Most books check out for 28 days (renewable once if no holds).
    • Graduate students & faculty: Longer loan periods (up to 90 days).
    • Fines: $0.25 per day for overdue books (but late fees are waived for first-time offenders if you return the item within 7 days).

⚠️ Common Mistake: Assuming you can borrow unlimited books without checking your account status. Solution: Always check your "Loans" and "Holds" sections in your account to avoid fines.

1.2 Exploring the Physical & Digital Library Spaces

MSU Libraries consist of three main locations, each serving different academic needs:

A. The Library at the Center for Academic Success (CAS)

  • Location: 1 Normal Avenue, Montclair, NJ
  • Best for: First-year students, general research, study groups, and tech support
  • Key Features:
    • 24/7 computer labs (with software like SPSS, MATLAB, and Adobe Creative Suite).
    • Group study rooms (reservable via the "Study Rooms" tab on the library website).
    • The "MakerSpace"—a 3D printing, scanning, and prototyping hub for STEM and design projects.

B. The Library at the School of Business (SBL)

  • Location: 1 Normal Avenue (inside the Business School building)
  • Best for: Business, economics, and finance research
  • Key Features:
    • Bloomberg Terminals (for real-time financial data—highly valuable for MBA students).
    • Specialized business databases (e.g., IBISWorld, Statista, and Mergent Online).
    • Quiet study zones ideal for case study analysis.

C. The Library at the School of Education (SEL)

  • Location: 1 Normal Avenue (inside the Education Building)
  • Best for: Education majors, K-12 research, and curriculum development
  • Key Features:
    • ERIC Database (for education research).
    • Special collections on special education and teaching methodologies.
    • Collaboration spaces for lesson planning.

💡 Pro Tip: If you're unsure which library to visit, use the "Library Locator" tool on the MSU Libraries homepage—it maps resources by subject.

1.3 Navigating the MSU Libraries Website: A Tour

The MSU Libraries website is packed with tools, but many students don’t know how to use them effectively. Here’s a breakdown:

Key Sections to Bookmark:

  1. 🔍 "Find" Tab

    • Search Bar: The main search function (like Google for academic sources).
    • Advanced Search: Filter by date, subject, format (books, articles, videos), and peer-reviewed status.
    • Subject Guides: Pre-made research paths for every major (e.g., "Psychology Research Guide" or "English Literature Guide").

  2. 📚 "Databases" Tab

    • Alphabetical List: Over 500 databases (e.g., JSTOR for humanities, PubMed for health sciences).
    • Subject-Based List: Easier navigation if you know your field (e.g., "Business Databases" or "STEM Databases").

  3. 📖 "Research Help" Tab

    • Live Chat (24/7): Ask librarians instantly (even after hours).
    • Workshops & Tutorials: Free sessions on citation, APA/MLA, and database searches.
    • Citation Generators: Tools like ZoteroBib (free) and EasyBib (integrated with MSU Libraries).

  4. 📦 "Request & Borrow" Tab

    • Interlibrary Loan (ILL): Request books/articles not available at MSU (takes 3–5 business days).
    • Scan & Deliver: Get PDFs of book chapters (if MSU owns the book).
    • Course Reserves: Professors often place required readings here—check under "My Course Reserves."

  5. 🎓 "Student Resources" Tab

    • Writing Center: Free one-on-one tutoring for essays and research papers.
    • Data Services: Help with statistical analysis (SPSS, R, Python).
    • Open Access Resources: Free e-books and journals (e.g., Project MUSE, Directory of Open Access Journals).

🔍 Real-World Example: A business student named Jamal needed data on small business trends in New Jersey for his capstone project. Instead of Googling, he used the "Business Databases" section under the "Find" tab and found IBISWorld reports—exactly what his professor required. Without this, he might have wasted hours on unreliable sources.

Chapter 2: 10 Actionable Strategies to Master MSU Libraries

2.1 Strategy 1: Use the "Advanced Search" Instead of Basic Search

Problem: Basic search returns too many irrelevant results (e.g., searching "climate change" might pull up news articles, Wikipedia, and unrelated journals).

Solution: Master Advanced Search to refine your queries.

How to Do It:

  1. Click "Advanced Search" (next to the main search bar).
  2. Add multiple keywords (e.g., "climate change AND New Jersey AND 2020-2023").
  3. Filter by:
    • Date range (e.g., last 5 years).
    • Source type (peer-reviewed journals, books, videos).
    • Subject (e.g., "Environmental Science").

📌 Example: Instead of searching "psychology of learning," try:

  • "cognitive psychology" AND "memory retention" AND "college students"
  • Filter by "Peer-Reviewed Journals" only.

2.2 Strategy 2: Leverage Librarian Workshops & One-on-One Help

Problem: Many students don’t know librarians can help with narrowing topics, finding sources, and avoiding plagiarism.

Solution: Attend workshops or schedule a consultation.

How to Access Help:

  • Workshops: Check the "Research Help" tab for upcoming sessions (e.g., "APA Citation Masterclass" or "Finding Primary Sources").
  • Individual Appointments: Book a 30-minute session via the "Ask a Librarian" page.

💡 Real-World Example: *A nursing student, Priya, struggled to find peer-reviewed articles on diabetes management. She scheduled a 15-minute chat with a health sciences librarian, who:

  1. Showed her how to use CINAHL (a nursing database).
  2. Taught her how to evaluate sources for credibility.
  3. Provided a pre-made search string for her topic. Result: Priya found 12 relevant articles in 20 minutes instead of hours.*

2.3 Strategy 3: Use Zotero for Free & Organized Research

Problem: Keeping track of sources, citations, and PDFs manually is time-consuming and error-prone.

Solution: Use Zotero, a free citation manager integrated with MSU Libraries.

How to Set It Up:

  1. Download Zotero (https://www.zotero.org/).
  2. Install the Browser Extension (for Chrome/Firefox).
  3. Connect to MSU Libraries:
    • Go to Zotero Preferences > Advanced > Library of Congress Control Number (LCCN) Lookup.
    • Enter your MSU email to sync with MSU’s institutional access.
  4. Save & Organize Sources:
    • Drag articles from JSTOR, Google Scholar, or MSU databases into Zotero.
    • Create folders by topic (e.g., "Thesis Chapter 2: Literature Review").
    • Auto-generate citations in APA, MLA, or Chicago style.

📌 Pro Tip: Zotero can detect duplicates and fill in missing metadata (author, title, DOI) automatically.

2.4 Strategy 4: Master Google Scholar & Link It to MSU Libraries

Problem: Google Scholar is great for finding papers, but paywalls block access to many articles.

Solution: Set up Google Scholar to recognize MSU’s institutional access.

How to Do It:

  1. Go to https://scholar.google.com.
  2. Click the three-line menu (☰) > Settings > Library Links.
  3. Search for "Montclair State University" and check the box.
  4. Now, when you find a paywalled article, click "Montclair State University"—it will redirect you to MSU’s licensed version.

🔍 Example: *A computer science student found a 2022 paper on AI ethics on Google Scholar. Instead of paying $35, she clicked "Montclair State University" and got a free PDF through MSU’s ScienceDirect database.

2.5 Strategy 5: Use Interlibrary Loan (ILL) for Books Not at MSU

Problem: Your required textbook isn’t available at MSU, and you can’t buy it.

Solution: Request it via Interlibrary Loan (ILL)—it’s free and fast for MSU students.

How to Request:

  1. Search the MSU Libraries catalog for the book.
  2. If it says "Not Available," click "Request via Interlibrary Loan."
  3. Fill out the form (usually takes 3–5 business days).
  4. Check your email for the digital PDF (or pick it up at the Circulation Desk if physical).

⚠️ Common Mistake: Assuming ILL only works for books. Solution: You can also request articles, dissertations, and conference papers.

2.6 Strategy 6: Find Primary Sources for History, Literature & Social Sciences

Problem: Many students confuse primary sources with secondary sources (e.g., using a textbook summary instead of an original document).

Solution: Use MSU’s specialized databases for primary sources.

Best Databases by Subject:

Subject Database What It Contains
History JSTOR Archives Original letters, speeches, and historical documents.
Literature Literary Reference Center First editions, author biographies, and critical essays.
Political Science Congressional Research Reports Government documents, bills, and hearings.
Sociology Sociological Abstracts Survey data and field research studies.

📌 Example: *A history student researching Women’s Suffrage found original newspaper clippings from 1920 in JSTOR Archives—far more credible than a modern textbook summary.

2.7 Strategy 7: Use the "Citation Tracker" to Avoid Plagiarism

Problem: Accidentally paraphrasing poorly or forgetting to cite leads to plagiarism warnings.

Solution: Use MSU’s Citation Tracker (built into Zotero and some databases).

How to Use It:

  1. Copy-paste your paper into Turnitin (via Blackboard or MSU’s Writing Center).
  2. Check for "matches"—Turnitin will highlight unintentional plagiarism.
  3. Use Zotero to auto-generate citations as you write.

💡 Pro Tip: Always keep track of your sources as you research—don’t wait until the last minute!

2.8 Strategy 8: Attend Study Sessions in the Library’s Group Rooms

Problem: Studying alone leads to distractions and less retention.

Solution: Book a study room in the library for focused group work.

How to Reserve:

  1. Go to the "Study Rooms" section on the MSU Libraries website.
  2. Filter by availability (some rooms require MSU ID for booking).
  3. Select a time slot (some are first-come, first-served).

🔍 Real-World Example: *A group of biology majors used a library study room to practice dissections and discuss exam questions. They completed their study guide in half the time compared to studying individually.
