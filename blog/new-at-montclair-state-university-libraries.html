New at Montclair State University Libraries: A Complete Guide to Maximizing Your Research & Study Experience

Chapter 1: Getting Started – Your First Steps at MSU Libraries

Before diving into advanced strategies, let’s cover the basics to ensure you’re set up for success.

1.1 How to Access the Library Catalog & Databases

The MSU Libraries’ online catalog is your first stop for books, journals, and digital resources. Here’s how to use it effectively:

  • Start at MSU Libraries’ Homepage – Bookmark this for quick access.
  • Use the search bar (top center) to find books, articles, and multimedia.
  • Filter by format (e.g., "Books," "Journal Articles," "Streaming Videos").
  • Check availability – If a book is checked out, request it via Interlibrary Loan (ILL) (more on this later).

Pro Tip: Use advanced search filters (like subject, date, or peer-reviewed status) to refine results.

1.2 Your MSU Library Card: What You Need to Know

Every student gets a free MSU Library Card—here’s how to use it:

  • Physical & Digital Access: Your card grants access to all physical branches (including the Science & Math Library and Library Annex) and online databases.
  • Renewals & Fines: Most books can be renewed online via your account (no late fees if returned on time).
  • Off-Campus Access: Use your MSU username and password to access databases remotely.

Real-World Example: A nursing student named Priya needed a rare textbook for her pharmacology class. Since the book was checked out, she used Interlibrary Loan (ILL) to borrow it from another NJ college in just 48 hours—saving her from last-minute stress.

Chapter 2: 10 Actionable Strategies to Master MSU Libraries

Now that you’re familiar with the basics, let’s dive into proven strategies to make the most of your library experience.

2.1 Strategy 1: Use the "Find It" Button for Full-Text Articles

Many databases (like JSTOR, ProQuest, or Google Scholar) only provide abstracts unless you have full access. The "Find It" button (usually a blue icon) automatically checks if MSU has the full article—saving you from paywalls.

How to Use It:

  1. Search for an article in Google Scholar.
  2. Click the "Find It @ MSU" link (if available).
  3. If MSU doesn’t have it, request it via Interlibrary Loan (ILL).

2.2 Strategy 2: Leverage Subject Guides for Research Help

The MSU Libraries create subject-specific guides (like Business Research, Psychology, or Engineering) with pre-selected databases, journals, and resources tailored to your major.

Where to Find Them:

  • Go to Library Guides.
  • Click your department (e.g., "Business," "Education").
  • Bookmark the guide for quick research access.

Real-World Example: *A computer science major, Raj, was struggling to find peer-reviewed papers on AI ethics. He checked the Computer Science Research Guide, which listed exactly the databases he needed—saving him hours of trial and error.

2.3 Strategy 3: Master Zotero for Citation Management

Citing sources manually is error-prone and time-consuming. Zotero (a free citation manager) lets you:

  • Save and organize articles, books, and websites.
  • Auto-generate citations in APA, MLA, Chicago, and more.
  • Insert citations directly into Word or Google Docs.

How to Set It Up:

  1. Download Zotero from zotero.org.
  2. Install the Zotero Connector for Chrome/Firefox.
  3. Drag-and-drop sources into your library.

Pro Tip: Use Zotero’s "Related Items" feature to find additional relevant sources automatically.

2.4 Strategy 4: Use Interlibrary Loan (ILL) for Borrowing from Other Libraries

If MSU doesn’t have a book or article, Interlibrary Loan (ILL) lets you borrow it from other NJ/NY libraries—for free.

How to Request:

  1. Go to ILL Request Form.
  2. Fill in the details (title, author, your MSU email).
  3. Most requests arrive in 3–5 business days.

Common Mistake: Waiting until the last minute—request early to avoid delays!

2.5 Strategy 5: Attend Library Workshops & Research Help Sessions

The MSU Libraries offer free workshops on:

  • Academic research
  • Citation management
  • Data analysis
  • Open Access publishing

Where to Find Workshops:

  • Check Library Events Calendar.
  • Sign up via MS Teams or Zoom.

Real-World Example: *A history student, Maria, attended a "Primary Source Research" workshop and learned how to analyze archival documents—a skill that boosted her thesis research by 30%.

2.6 Strategy 6: Explore Digital & Streaming Resources

The MSU Libraries provide thousands of e-books, videos, and datasets—no need to visit the physical library!

Key Digital Resources:

  • Ebrary & EBSCOhost (e-books)
  • Kanopy & Swank (streaming films)
  • Statista & ICPSR (data & statistics)

Pro Tip: Use Google Scholar’s "Library Links" to auto-link to MSU’s full-text articles.

2.7 Strategy 7: Use the "Ask a Librarian" Chat for Instant Help

Stuck on a research question? The MSU Libraries’ "Ask a Librarian" chat is available 24/7 via:

  • Live Chat
  • Email (library@montclair.edu)
  • Phone (973-655-7915)

Best For:

  • Quick citation checks
  • Database troubleshooting
  • Finding hard-to-locate sources

2.8 Strategy 8: Take Advantage of Study Spaces & Group Work Areas

The MSU Libraries offer multiple study environments, from quiet carrels to collaboration pods. Some top spots:

  • Main Library (3rd Floor) – Great for individual study.
  • Science & Math Library – Ideal for STEM students.
  • Library Annex – Less crowded, perfect for deep work.

Pro Tip: Reserve a group study room via Library Study Rooms if you need a private space.

2.9 Strategy 9: Use the "Library of Things" for Borrowing Tech

Need a laptop, camera, or even a gaming console? The Library of Things lets you borrow tech gear for free (with your MSU ID).

What’s Available:

  • Laptops & tablets
  • Calculators & graphing tools
  • Audio equipment (microphones, headphones)
  • Gaming consoles (Nintendo Switch, PlayStation)

How to Reserve:

  1. Check Library of Things.
  2. Browse available items.
  3. Pick up at the Main Library Circulation Desk.

2.10 Strategy 10: Explore Open Access & Free Resources

Not all research needs to come from paid databases. The MSU Libraries provide free, open-access alternatives, such as:

  • Google Scholar (filter by "Linked to MSU")
  • Directory of Open Access Journals (DOAJ)
  • Project MUSE (free articles via MSU login)

Pro Tip: Use Google Scholar’s "Scholar Alerts" to get email updates on new research in your field.

Chapter 3: Common Mistakes at MSU Libraries (And How to Avoid Them)

Even the best students make avoidable mistakes at the library. Here’s how to steer clear of them:

3.1 Mistake 1: Not Using Subject Guides

Why It’s Bad: Wasting time searching random databases instead of pre-vetted resources.

Solution: Always check your department’s research guide before diving into Google Scholar.

3.2 Mistake 2: Ignoring Interlibrary Loan (ILL) Deadlines

Why It’s Bad: Last-minute requests can take weeks, not days.

Solution: Request 2–3 weeks before your deadline to avoid stress.

3.3 Mistake 3: Not Checking for Full-Text Access

Why It’s Bad: Paying for articles when MSU already has them.

Solution: Always click "Find It" before paying for PDFs.

3.4 Mistake 4: Overlooking Digital Resources

Why It’s Bad: Missing e-books, videos, and datasets that could save time.

Solution: Explore MSU’s Digital Collections regularly.

3.5 Mistake 5: Not Asking for Help When Stuck

Why It’s Bad: Struggling alone when librarians are there to assist.

Solution: Use "Ask a Librarian" or attend workshops for expert guidance.

Chapter 4: FAQs About Montclair State University Libraries

❓ How do I find books in the MSU Libraries?

Answer: Use the MSU Library Catalog to search by title, author, or subject. Once you find a book, note its call number (e.g., HA115 .S65 2020) and locate it on the shelves. If you’re unsure where it is, ask a librarian or use the "Find It" button for help.

❓ Can I borrow books from other universities?

Answer: Yes! Use Interlibrary Loan (ILL) to borrow books from other NJ/NY libraries. Requests typically arrive in 3–5 business days. Check the ILL Request Form for details.

❓ How do I access databases off-campus?

Answer: Use your MSU username and password to log in to any MSU database from off-campus. If prompted, select "Montclair State University" as your institution. If you encounter issues, try clearing your browser cache or using VPN (if required).

❓ Are there quiet study areas in the MSU Libraries?

Answer: Yes! The Main Library (3rd Floor) and Science & Math Library have quiet study zones. For absolute silence, check out the Library Annex or carrel seating. You can also reserve a study room via Library Study Rooms.

❓ How do I cite sources using Zotero?

Answer:

  1. Install Zotero and add your sources (drag-and-drop or manually enter details).
  2. Right-click on a citation and select "Copy Item as → Word RTF".
  3. Paste into Microsoft Word—Zotero will auto-format citations in your chosen style (APA, MLA, etc.).
  4. Insert footnotes by right-clicking and selecting "Add to Word".
