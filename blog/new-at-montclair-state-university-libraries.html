New at Montclair State University Libraries: A Complete Guide to Maximizing Your Research & Study Success

Introduction: Why Montclair State University Libraries Are Your Academic Powerhouse

Welcome to Montclair State University Libraries—a vibrant, resource-rich hub designed to support students, faculty, and researchers in their academic journeys. Whether you're a first-year undergraduate, a graduate student, or a faculty member, the libraries offer an unparalleled collection of physical and digital resources, expert assistance, and innovative tools to enhance your learning and research.

With over 500,000 physical volumes and millions of digital resources (including e-books, journals, and databases), Montclair State Libraries are more than just a place to study—they’re a strategic partner in your academic success. According to the 2023 Montclair State University Annual Report, 87% of students who actively engage with library resources report improved grades and research outcomes. Additionally, 72% of faculty cite the libraries as a critical component in their teaching and scholarly work.

If you're new to Montclair State, this guide will walk you through everything you need to know—from navigating the library’s physical spaces to leveraging digital tools, accessing specialized collections, and avoiding common pitfalls. By the end, you’ll be equipped with actionable strategies to make the most of your time at the libraries.

Understanding Montclair State University Libraries: Structure & Services

Before diving into strategies, it’s essential to understand the layout and key services offered by Montclair State Libraries. The system consists of three main libraries, each with a distinct focus:

1. The Library at the Center (Main Campus – Montclair)

  • Primary Focus: General research, undergraduate studies, and interdisciplinary subjects.
  • Key Features:
    • 24/7 study spaces, including group rooms and silent zones.
    • Specialized collections in humanities, social sciences, and business.
    • The Learning Commons—a hub for tutoring, writing assistance, and tech support.
    • The Digital Scholarship Lab, where students can work on data visualization, 3D printing, and digital humanities projects.

2. The Library at the School of Business (SBS Library)

  • Primary Focus: Business, economics, finance, and entrepreneurship.
  • Key Features:
    • Bloomberg Terminals—essential for finance and investment research.
    • Specialized databases like IBISWorld, Statista, and Mergent Online.
    • Career resources, including resume workshops and interview prep.

3. The Library at the School of Education (SOE Library)

  • Primary Focus: Education, psychology, and child development.
  • Key Features:
    • ERIC Database—a goldmine for education research.
    • Specialized children’s literature collections.
    • Assistance with grant writing and educational policy research.

Each library has its own dedicated staff who can help with research strategies, citation formatting, and accessing restricted materials. Pro tip: Familiarize yourself with the library’s website (montclair.edu/library)—it’s your one-stop shop for catalogs, databases, and digital resources.

8 Actionable Strategies to Master Montclair State University Libraries

Now that you know the basics, let’s explore practical, step-by-step strategies to help you maximize your library experience.

Strategy 1: Start with the Library Catalog (MontclairSearch) – Your First Research Tool

Why it matters: The MontclairSearch catalog is your gateway to books, e-books, journals, and multimedia—all in one place.

How to use it effectively:

  1. Use advanced search filters to narrow down results by:
    • Date range (e.g., "Last 5 years" for current research).
    • Format (books, e-books, articles, videos).
    • Subject (e.g., "Psychology," "Business Ethics").
  2. Check the "Availability" tab to see if a book is physically in the library or available as an e-book.
  3. Use the "Citation" tool to generate APA, MLA, or Chicago-style citations directly from the catalog.

Real-world example: *Suppose you’re researching "climate change policies in New Jersey." Instead of searching broadly, you’d:

  • Use the subject filter for "Environmental Policy."
  • Limit results to "Books published after 2018."
  • Check if any e-books are available for immediate access.

Common mistake: Relying only on Google Scholar without checking MontclairSearch first. Why? Many journals and books are exclusively available through the library, and some may require a proxy login for off-campus access.

Strategy 2: Leverage Databases for Deep Research

Why it matters: While Google Scholar is useful, Montclair’s subscription databases provide peer-reviewed, credible, and often restricted content that isn’t freely available elsewhere.

Top databases by subject:

Subject Key Databases Best For
Business Business Source Premier, IBISWorld Market research, company reports
Health Sciences CINAHL, PubMed Nursing, medical research
Humanities JSTOR, Project MUSE Scholarly articles, literary criticism
STEM ScienceDirect, IEEE Xplore Engineering, computer science, physics
Education ERIC, Education Research Complete Teaching methods, educational policy

How to access them:

  1. Go to Montclair Libraries Databases.
  2. Click on your subject area.
  3. Log in with your Montclair credentials (even off-campus).

Real-world example: A biology student researching "gene editing in cancer treatment" would:*

  • Use ScienceDirect for peer-reviewed journal articles.
  • Filter by "Full Text" to ensure immediate access.
  • Check "Cited by" to find related studies.

Pro tip: Bookmark your favorite databases for quick access. Many students waste time searching multiple sources when one database has everything they need.

Strategy 3: Use Interlibrary Loan (ILL) for Materials Not in Montclair’s Collection

Why it matters: Not every book or article is available at Montclair. Interlibrary Loan (ILL) lets you borrow from other institutions—often in 3-5 business days.

How to request an ILL:

  1. Search MontclairSearch for your needed material.
  2. If it says "Not Available," click "Request via Interlibrary Loan."
  3. Fill out the form with your Montclair email and contact info.
  4. Check your email for updates—you’ll get a digital copy (PDF) or a physical book delivered to the library.

Real-world example: A history student needs "The Rise and Fall of the Third Reich" but finds it’s checked out. Instead of panicking, they:*

  • Request it via ILL.
  • Receive a digital scan within 48 hours.

Common mistake: Assuming ILL is slow or unreliable. In reality, it’s efficient—most requests arrive within a week, and digital copies are instantly accessible.

Strategy 4: Attend Library Workshops & Research Help Sessions

Why it matters: The library offers free, expert-led workshops on:

  • Research strategies
  • Citation management (Zotero, EndNote)
  • Data analysis (SPSS, R)
  • Academic writing & avoiding plagiarism

How to find workshops:

  • Check Montclair Libraries Events Calendar.
  • Sign up via Montclair’s Canvas or email alerts.

Real-world example: *A graduate student struggling with SPSS for statistics attends a "Data Analysis Bootcamp" and learns:

  • How to clean datasets efficiently.
  • How to generate accurate visualizations.
  • How to interpret p-values correctly.

Pro tip: Even if you’re confident in a subject, workshops reinforce best practices—like how to find high-impact journals for your field.

Strategy 5: Master Zotero for Citation Management

Why it matters: Manually citing sources is time-consuming and error-prone. Zotero (a free, open-source tool) automatically saves, organizes, and formats citations for you.

How to set up Zotero:

  1. Download Zotero (works with Chrome, Firefox, or Safari).
  2. Create a free account and install the browser extension.
  3. Drag-and-drop PDFs, articles, and books into your library.
  4. Auto-fill citations in Word, Google Docs, or LaTeX.

Real-world example: *A political science student researching "voting behavior in 2020" collects:

  • 5 journal articles
  • 3 government reports
  • 2 news articles Instead of manually citing each, they:
  • Save all sources to Zotero.
  • Generate an APA bibliography in seconds.
  • Insert in-text citations with one click.

Common mistake: Waiting until the last minute to organize citations. Start early—Zotero saves hours of work.

Strategy 6: Explore Special Collections & Archives

Why it matters: Beyond standard textbooks, Montclair’s Special Collections hold rare books, manuscripts, and historical documents that are invaluable for advanced research.

Key collections:

  • Montclair State University Archives – Historical records, student publications, and faculty papers.
  • New Jersey Collection – Books and documents on New Jersey history, culture, and politics.
  • Children’s Literature Collection – Rare books for education and literary studies.

How to access them:

  1. Visit Special Collections Guide.
  2. Request materials in advance (some require appointment-only access).
  3. Consult a librarian for guidance on handling fragile documents.

Real-world example: *A literature student writing a thesis on "19th-century New Jersey authors" discovers:

  • A first-edition copy of Washington Irving’s "The Sketch Book" in the New Jersey Collection.
  • Handwritten letters from local poets in the University Archives.

Pro tip: These collections are often underutilized—many students don’t realize they exist until they’re deep into research.

Strategy 7: Optimize Your Study Spaces

Why it matters: The physical library environment can dramatically impact productivity. Montclair offers multiple study zones, each suited for different needs.

Best study spaces:

Space Best For Tips
The Learning Commons Group study, tutoring, tech help Use whiteboards and projectors for presentations.
Silent Study Zones Focused individual work Avoid loud conversations—these areas are for deep concentration.
Group Rooms Collaborative projects Reserve in advance via the library app.

Real-world example: *A business student preparing for exams finds:

  • The Silent Study Zone is too quiet—they switch to The Learning Commons for background noise.
  • They reserve a group room for weekend study sessions with classmates.

Common mistake: Assuming all study areas are the same. Experiment to find what works best for you.

Strategy 8: Use Library Tech & Digital Tools

Why it matters: Montclair Libraries provide cutting-edge technology to enhance your research and study experience.

Key tech tools:

  • 3D Printers & Scanners – Available in the Digital Scholarship Lab.
  • Audio-Visual Equipment – For presentations (available for checkout).
  • Virtual Reality (VR) Headsets – Used in psychology and education research.
  • Library App (LibApps) – Scan books, renew loans, and get alerts on your phone.

How to access them:

  1. Check the Library Tech Guide.
  2. Request training if you’re unfamiliar with a tool.
  3. Ask at the service desk for assistance**.

Real-world example: *A psychology student conducting eye-tracking research uses:

  • The VR headsets to simulate virtual environments.
  • The 3D printer to create custom experimental stimuli.

Pro tip: Don’t hesitate to ask for help—librarians and tech staff are happy to train you on new tools.

Common Mistakes New Students Make (And How to Avoid Them)

Even the most motivated students can fall into avoidable traps when using Montclair Libraries. Here’s how to steer clear of them:

Mistake 1: Not Using the Library’s Website Properly

Problem: Many students only search Google instead of leveraging MontclairSearch or subject-specific databases. Solution:

  • Bookmark the library homepage (montclair.edu/library).
  • Use the "Databases A-Z" list instead of guessing which one to use.
  • Check "Library Hours"—some branches close early on weekends.

Mistake 2: Ignoring Librarians & Research Help

Problem: Students don’t ask for help when stuck, leading to wasted time on irrelevant sources. Solution:

  • Visit the Research Help Desk (located in The Library at the Center).
  • Schedule a one-on-one appointment with a librarian (montclair.edu/library/research-help).
  • Use the "Ask a Librarian" chat for quick questions.

Mistake 3: Overlooking E-Books & Digital Resources

Problem: Some students only look for physical books, missing thousands of e-books available 24/7. Solution:

  • Filter by "eBook" in MontclairSearch.
  • **Use ProQuest Ebook Central or EBSCOhost for large digital collections.
  • Download e-books to your device for offline reading.

Mistake 4: Not Renewing Loans on Time

Problem: Books automatically become overdue, leading to fines. Solution:

  • Check due dates in MontclairSearch.
  • Renew books online via your library account.
  • Set email alerts for loan renewals.

Mistake 5: Skipping Workshops Because They "Seem Too Basic"

Problem: Students assume they already know everything and miss advanced techniques. Solution:

  • Attend at least one workshop per semester—even if it’s on basic research skills.
  • Check for "Graduate Student Workshops" if you’re in a higher-level program.
