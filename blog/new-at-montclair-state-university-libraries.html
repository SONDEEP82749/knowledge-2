New at Montclair State University Libraries: A Complete Guide to Maximizing Your Research & Study Success

Introduction: Why Montclair State University Libraries Are Your Academic Powerhouse

Welcome to Montclair State University Libraries—a vibrant hub of knowledge, innovation, and academic excellence. As a new student, faculty member, or researcher, navigating a university library system can feel overwhelming at first. But fear not! With the right strategies, you’ll unlock a world of resources designed to support your learning, research, and professional growth.

Whether you're working on a capstone project, thesis, or simply need help with coursework, the libraries offer over 1.2 million physical and digital items, including rare collections, specialized databases, and expert librarian guidance.

Chapter 1: Getting Started – Your First Steps at Montclair State University Libraries

Before diving into research, it’s essential to understand the library’s structure, services, and tools. Here’s how to set yourself up for success from day one.

1.1 How to Access the Library’s Website & Portal

The Montclair State University Libraries website (libraries.montclair.edu) is your one-stop shop for all things research-related. Here’s how to make the most of it:

  • Bookmark the Portal: Save the link to your browser’s homepage for quick access.
  • Use the Search Bar: The unified search allows you to find books, journal articles, videos, and more in a single query.
  • Explore the A-Z Database List: Not everything is searchable via the main portal. Some specialized databases (like JSTOR, ProQuest, or EBSCO) require direct access.

Real-World Example: A graduate student in education was struggling to find peer-reviewed articles on "inclusive classroom strategies." Instead of searching broadly, they used the A-Z Database List to filter for Education Source, a specialized database tailored to their field. This saved them hours of irrelevant results and led them directly to high-quality research.

1.2 Understanding Your Library Card & Borrowing Privileges

Every Montclair State student, faculty, and staff member receives a library card (usually your student ID). Here’s what you need to know:

  • Borrowing Limits:
    • Undergraduates: Up to 50 items at a time (with some exceptions for rare materials).
    • Graduate Students & Faculty: Higher limits, often unlimited for digital resources.
  • Loan Periods:
    • Books: Typically 28 days, renewable if no one else is waiting.
    • Reserves: Short-term loans (often 2 hours to 7 days) for course materials.
  • Fines & Overdue Policies: Late fees apply, but the library offers grace periods and renewal options to avoid penalties.

Common Mistake & Fix: Many students assume they can keep books indefinitely. Don’t wait until the last day! Set a calendar reminder for renewal deadlines to avoid fines.

Chapter 2: 10 Actionable Strategies to Master Montclair State University Libraries

Now that you’re familiar with the basics, let’s dive into practical strategies to efficiently find, evaluate, and use library resources.

2.1 Strategy 1: Use Advanced Search Filters to Narrow Down Results

The default search can be overwhelming. Advanced search filters help you refine your queries like a pro.

How to Apply Filters:

  1. Click "Advanced Search" (or use the "+" filters in the main search bar).
  2. Limit by:
    • Date Range (e.g., "2018–Present" for the latest research).
    • Resource Type (books, journal articles, videos, etc.).
    • Subject (e.g., "Psychology," "Business Management").
    • Full Text Availability (ensure you can read the article online).

Real-World Example: A business student researching "sustainable supply chain strategies" used the subject filter to exclude unrelated fields. They also limited to peer-reviewed journals, ensuring only credible sources appeared in their results.

Common Mistake: Students often ignore date filters, leading to outdated or irrelevant information. Always check the publication year—especially in fast-evolving fields like technology or healthcare.

2.2 Strategy 2: Leverage Librarian Research Guides

Montclair State librarians have created specialized research guides for every major, minor, and even specific courses. These guides include:

  • Recommended databases for your field.
  • Citation styles (APA, MLA, Chicago, etc.).
  • Study tips and writing resources.

How to Find Them:

  • Visit Research Guides.
  • Search by subject (e.g., "Computer Science," "Nursing").
  • Look for course-specific guides (e.g., "ENG 101: College Writing").

Real-World Example: *A nursing student struggling with evidence-based practice found the Nursing Research Guide, which included:

  • A pre-selected list of nursing journals (e.g., Journal of Nursing Scholarship).
  • A step-by-step guide on evaluating clinical studies. This saved her weeks of trial and error.*

2.3 Strategy 3: Use Zotero or RefWorks for Citation Management

Manually tracking citations is time-consuming and error-prone. Citation managers like Zotero (free) and RefWorks (paid, but available through Montclair) help you:

  • Save and organize sources.
  • Generate citations in seconds.
  • Create bibliographies automatically.

How to Get Started:

  1. Download Zotero (zotero.org) and install the browser extension.
  2. Save articles directly from library databases into your Zotero library.
  3. Format citations as you write (APA, MLA, etc.).

Real-World Example: *A history student working on a 15-page thesis used Zotero to:

  • Auto-fill citations while writing.
  • Avoid duplicate sources by checking for duplicates.
  • Share a private library with their advisor for feedback. This reduced their plagiarism risk and streamlined their workflow.*

Common Mistake: Students often wait until the last minute to organize citations. Start early—even with just 5 sources, you’ll save hours later.

2.4 Strategy 4: Access Full-Text Articles Without Paywalls

Not all articles are freely available online. Here’s how to get full-text access through Montclair State:

  1. Check for the PDF Icon – If you see a green "PDF" or "Full Text" link, click it.
  2. Use "Get It" or "Check Availability" – If the article isn’t directly accessible:
    • Click "Check Availability" (in EBSCOhost, ProQuest, etc.).
    • The system will search other databases or request the article via Interlibrary Loan (ILL).
  3. Interlibrary Loan (ILL) Service – If Montclair doesn’t have the article:
    • Submit a request through ILL Portal.
    • Most articles arrive within 24–48 hours.

Real-World Example: *A political science student found a highly cited article in a paywalled journal. Instead of giving up, they:

  1. Clicked "Check Availability" in the database.
  2. The system automatically requested it via ILL.
  3. Received the PDF within 3 days—no cost to them. This saved them from wasting time on less relevant sources.*

Common Mistake: Students ignore the "Get It" button and assume the article is unavailable. Always check before concluding.

2.5 Strategy 5: Utilize Study Spaces & Tech Resources

Montclair State Libraries offer more than just books—they provide study environments, tech tools, and quiet zones.

Popular Study Spaces:

  • Group Study Rooms (bookable via Room Scheduling).
  • Silent Study Zones (for focused work).
  • Tech Labs (with scanners, 3D printers, and recording equipment).

How to Book a Study Room:

  1. Go to Room Scheduling.
  2. Select your preferred date/time.
  3. Choose a room (some have whiteboards, projectors, or large tables).

Real-World Example: *A group of business students preparing for a case competition booked a group study room for 4 hours. They used:

  • The projector to present their analysis.
  • The whiteboard to brainstorm strategies.
  • The quiet environment to avoid distractions. This boosted their productivity compared to studying in the dorms.*

2.6 Strategy 6: Attend Library Workshops & Research Help Sessions

The library offers free workshops on:

  • Research methods
  • Citation management
  • Data analysis
  • Thesis writing

How to Find Workshops:

  • Check Library Events.
  • Sign up via email or the portal.

Real-World Example: *A biology major attended a "How to Find Peer-Reviewed Articles" workshop. They learned:

  • How to spot predatory journals (and avoid them).
  • How to use PubMed effectively for medical research.
  • How to contact librarians for one-on-one help. This saved them from wasting time on low-quality sources.*

Common Mistake: Students assume they don’t need help and skip workshops. Even experts benefit from these sessions!

2.7 Strategy 7: Use the Library’s Digital Collections & Special Archives

Montclair State has unique digital collections that most students overlook:

  • Montclair State University Archives (historical documents, yearbooks, alumni records).
  • Digital Commons @ Montclair (open-access student research, faculty publications).
  • Newspaper Archives (local and national historical newspapers).

How to Access:

  • Visit Digital Collections.
  • Search by keyword, date, or collection name.

Real-World Example: *A history student researching Montclair’s role in the Civil Rights Movement found:

  • Primary sources (letters, speeches, newspaper clippings).
  • Rare photographs of local protests.
  • Oral histories from activists. This enriched their thesis with firsthand accounts instead of just secondary sources.*

2.8 Strategy 8: Ask a Librarian – They’re Your Secret Weapon

Librarians at Montclair State are highly trained researchers who can:

  • Help you refine your search strategy.
  • Find obscure sources you couldn’t locate alone.
  • Teach you how to evaluate sources for credibility.

How to Get Help:

  • Chat with a Librarian (live help via Ask a Librarian).
  • Schedule a Research Consultation (link).
  • Email a Subject Specialist (each department has a dedicated librarian).

Real-World Example: *A philosophy student was struggling to find ethical theories in ancient Greek texts. They:

  1. Chatted with a Librarian via the live help widget.
  2. The librarian recommended specific databases (e.g., JSTOR’s "Classical Studies" collection).
  3. They guided the student on how to translate Latin/Greek terms in the search. This cut their research time in half.*

Common Mistake: Students only ask for help at the last minute. Reach out early—librarians can save you weeks of work.

2.9 Strategy 9: Use the Library’s Open Access & Free Resources

Not everything requires a subscription. Montclair State provides free, open-access resources, including:

  • Google Scholar (with Montclair State’s full-text links).
  • Open Access Journals (e.g., PLOS, Directory of Open Access Books).
  • Creative Commons-licensed images, videos, and music.

How to Find Them:

  • Use Montclair’s Google Scholar Setup to link your search results to the library’s full-text access.
  • Browse Open Access Resources.

Real-World Example: *A graphic design student needed royalty-free images for a presentation. Instead of using unspecified stock photos, they:

  1. Searched Creative Commons.
  2. Found high-quality, legally free images from Flickr Commons.
  3. Cited the sources properly using CC licenses. This avoided copyright issues and impressed their professor.*

2.10 Strategy 10: Plan Ahead for Thesis & Capstone Research

If you’re working on a thesis, dissertation, or capstone, the library offers specialized support:

  • Thesis Submission Guidelines (link).
  • Data Management Plans (for research projects).
  • Copyright & Permissions help.

How to Prepare:

  1. Start Early – Thesis research can take months.
  2. Use the Thesis Guide – Follow Montclair’s Thesis Template.
  3. Request a Thesis Consultation – Librarians can review your bibliography before submission.
