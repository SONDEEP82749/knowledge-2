Chapter 1: Understanding Montclair State University Libraries – A Deep Dive

Before diving into strategies, it’s essential to understand the structure, services, and unique offerings of Montclair State’s libraries.

1.1 The Library System: What You Need to Know

Montclair State University operates three main library locations, each with distinct strengths:

  • Library West (Main Campus – Upper Montclair)

    • Largest collection (books, journals, multimedia)
    • 24/7 study spaces (including group rooms and silent zones)
    • Special Collections (rare books, historical documents)
    • Technology Hub (scanners, 3D printers, audio-visual equipment)

  • Library East (Lower Montclair Campus)

    • Focus on STEM & Engineering (specialized databases, lab resources)
    • Collaboration spaces for group projects
    • Science & Math Tutoring Center (free drop-in help)

  • Library South (South Campus – Global Studies & Education)

    • Humanities & Social Sciences emphasis
    • Digital Humanities Lab (for multimedia projects)
    • Accessibility services (screen readers, Braille materials)

Each library has unique databases, subject specialists, and research guides tailored to different disciplines. For example:

  • Business students should explore Library West’s Bloomberg Terminal (financial data).
  • Engineering students will find Library East’s IEEE Xplore (technical papers) invaluable.

1.2 Key Services You Might Not Know About

Beyond books and databases, Montclair State Libraries offer hidden gems that can boost your grades and research efficiency:

✅ Interlibrary Loan (ILL) Service – Borrow books from other NJ universities (Rutgers, Princeton) in 3-5 business days. ✅ Research Consultations – One-on-one help from librarians to refine search strategies (schedule via Montclair State LibCal). ✅ Zotero & EndNote Training – Learn how to organize citations and generate bibliographies automatically. ✅ Open Access Initiatives – Publish your work for free via Montclair State’s Institutional Repository. ✅ Study Abroad Resources – Access global databases (e.g., JSTOR Global Plants) for international research.

Pro Tip: Bookmark the Montclair State Libraries’ Research Guides—they’re subject-specific roadmaps (e.g., "Psychology Research Guide," "Business Data Sources").

Chapter 2: 10 Actionable Strategies to Master Montclair State University Libraries

Now that you know what’s available, let’s dive into practical steps to become a library power user.

Strategy 1: Start with the Library’s Homepage & Search Effectively

Problem: Many students Google their research topics instead of using the library’s advanced search tools.

Solution: The Montclair State Library homepage (lib.montclair.edu) is your one-stop shop for:

  • OneSearch (combines books, articles, and databases)
  • Subject Guides (curated by librarians)
  • New Arrivals (latest books in your field)

How to Search Like a Pro:

  1. Use Boolean Operators (AND, OR, NOT) to narrow results.
    • Example: Instead of searching "climate change," try:
      • "climate change AND policy" (fewer, more relevant results)
      • "climate change OR global warming" (broader search)
  2. Filter by Source Type (books, peer-reviewed journals, news).
  3. Check "Full Text" Only to avoid paywalls.
  4. Save & Organize using folder tags in OneSearch.

Real-World Example: A sociology student researching "youth mental health" could:

  • Start with a broad search → "youth mental health"
  • Refine with Boolean logic → "youth mental health AND pandemic"
  • Filter by "Peer-Reviewed Journals" to find credible sources.

Strategy 2: Leverage Database Specialists for Your Major

Problem: Students often don’t know which databases are best for their field.

Solution: Montclair State librarians specialize by subject. For example:

  • Business: Use Business Source Premier, IBISWorld, and Mergent Online.
  • STEM: ScienceDirect, IEEE Xplore, and Web of Science.
  • Humanities: JSTOR, Project MUSE, and Gale Literary Sources.

How to Find Your Database Expert:

  1. Visit Montclair State’s Research Guides.
  2. Click "Ask a Librarian" in the bottom-right corner.
  3. Request a subject-specific consultation.

Real-World Example: An engineering student working on a sustainable energy project should:

  • Use IEEE Xplore for technical papers.
  • Check ScienceDirect for peer-reviewed studies.
  • Ask the STEM Librarian for recommendations on emerging trends.

Strategy 3: Use Zotero or EndNote to Manage Citations

Problem: Students lose track of sources or struggle with citations, leading to plagiarism risks.

Solution: Zotero (free) and EndNote (paid, but available via Montclair State) help you:

  • Save PDFs & metadata in one place.
  • Auto-generate citations in APA, MLA, Chicago, etc.
  • Create bibliographies instantly.

How to Set It Up:

  1. Download Zotero (zotero.org).
  2. Install the browser extension to auto-save citations from databases.
  3. Connect to Montclair State’s library via Zotero’s "Find Full Text" plugin.

Real-World Example: A history student writing a 10-page paper could:

  • Save 20+ sources in Zotero.
  • Use the "Cite While You Write" plugin in Word to insert citations automatically.
  • Export a perfect bibliography in MLA format in seconds.

Strategy 4: Explore Special Collections & Rare Books

Problem: Most students only use general collections, missing unique primary sources.

Solution: Montclair State’s Special Collections (in Library West) holds:

  • Historical NJ documents (18th–20th century).
  • Rare books (first editions, signed copies).
  • Archival materials (student newspapers, faculty papers).

How to Access Them:

  1. Check the Special Collections Guide.
  2. Request materials via email (specialcollections@montclair.edu).
  3. Visit in person (appointment recommended).

Real-World Example: A literature student researching NJ authors could:

  • Find first editions of Edith Wharton’s works.
  • Access original manuscripts from Montclair’s literary alumni.
  • Use digitized archives for remote research.

Strategy 5: Master the 24/7 Study Spaces & Tech Hubs

Problem: Students don’t know where to study efficiently or lack access to tech tools.

Solution: Montclair State Libraries offer:

  • Silent Study Zones (Library West, 2nd floor).
  • Group Study Rooms (reservable via LibCal).
  • Tech Hub (scanners, 3D printers, audio recording booths).

How to Use Them:

  1. Check availability on LibCal.
  2. Book a study room for group projects.
  3. Use the Tech Hub for scanning papers, printing 3D models, or recording podcasts.

Real-World Example: A business student working on a case study presentation could:

  • Reserve a group study room for brainstorming.
  • Use the Tech Hub’s scanner to digitize handwritten notes.
  • Print professional-looking slides at the library’s high-quality printers.

Strategy 6: Attend Library Workshops & Research Training

Problem: Students don’t know about free training that could improve their research skills.

Solution: Montclair State Libraries host weekly workshops, including:

  • "How to Find Peer-Reviewed Articles"
  • "Advanced Google Scholar Search"
  • "Data Management for Thesis Research"

How to Join:

  1. Check Library Events Calendar.
  2. Sign up via LibCal (some require advance registration).
  3. Ask questions during Q&A sessions.

Real-World Example: A grad student preparing for a dissertation could:

  • Attend "Data Management Workshop" to learn how to organize large datasets.
  • Take "Advanced Database Search" to find niche research.
  • Get one-on-one help from a data librarian.

Strategy 7: Use Interlibrary Loan (ILL) for Books Not in Montclair’s Collection

Problem: Students get frustrated when their required book isn’t available.

Solution: Interlibrary Loan (ILL) lets you borrow books from other NJ universities (Rutgers, Princeton, NJIT) in 3-5 business days.

How to Request:

  1. Go to ILL Page.
  2. Search for the book in OneSearch.
  3. Click "Request via ILL" and fill out the form.
  4. Check your email for updates (usually delivered in 3-7 days).

Real-World Example: A philosophy student needing "Nietzsche’s Complete Works" (not in Montclair’s collection) could:

  • Request it via ILL.
  • Receive it within a week for free.
  • Return it without late fees (unlike Montclair’s 2-week loan period).

Strategy 8: Access Open Educational Resources (OER) & Free E-Books

Problem: Students pay for textbooks that could be free online.

Solution: Montclair State Libraries provide thousands of free e-books via:

  • Project MUSE (humanities & social sciences).
  • JSTOR (academic journals).
  • Google Scholar (free PDFs for many papers).

How to Find Them:

  1. Use Google Scholar (scholar.google.com) and filter by "Free PDF".
  2. Check Montclair’s E-Book Collection.
  3. Use LibGen (Library Genesis) responsibly (some content may be gray-area legal).

Real-World Example: A psychology student needing "Theories of Personality" could:

  • Find a free PDF on Project MUSE.
  • Use Google Scholar to locate open-access chapters.
  • Avoid buying a $150 textbook by using library-provided resources.

Strategy 9: Use Library Apps for On-the-Go Access

Problem: Students forget to check due dates or lose track of loans.

Solution: Montclair State Libraries offer mobile apps for:

  • Renewing books (no late fees if renewed on time).
  • Checking due dates.
  • Scanning QR codes for reserving study rooms.

How to Use:

  1. Download Montclair State Libraries App (available on iOS & Android).
  2. Log in with your Montclair credentials.
  3. Set up push notifications for due dates.

Real-World Example: A busy student could:

  • Renew books via the app before the due date.
  • Check if a book is available before visiting the library.
  • Reserve a study room while waiting in line.

Strategy 10: Ask for Help – Librarians Are Your Secret Weapon

Problem: Students don’t know how to ask for help, leading to wasted time.

Solution: Montclair State librarians love helping students—they’re experts in research strategies.

How to Get Help:

  1. Chat with a Librarian (live chat on the library website).
  2. Email (askalibrarian@montclair.edu).
  3. Schedule a Research Consultation (LibCal).
  4. Visit the Research Desk (Library West, 1st floor).

Real-World Example: A grad student struggling with quantitative analysis could:

  • Email a Data Librarian for SPSS/Excel help.
  • Attend a workshop on "Statistical Analysis in R".
  • Get a custom research guide tailored to their thesis topic.
