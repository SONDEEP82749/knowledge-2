Chapter 1: Setting Up Your Library Account & Accessing Resources

Before diving into research, you must activate your library account and understand how to navigate the digital and physical collections. Here’s how to get started.

1.1 How to Create & Activate Your Library Account

Every Montclair State student, faculty member, and staff member receives automatic access to the library system upon enrollment. However, you must activate your account to borrow materials, request interlibrary loans, and access digital resources.

Steps to Activate Your Account:

  1. Visit the Montclair State University Libraries Website.
  2. Click on "Log In" (top-right corner).
  3. Select "Montclair State University" as your institution.
  4. Enter your Montclair State username and password (the same credentials used for MyMSU Portal).
  5. If prompted, complete multi-factor authentication (MFA) if enabled.
  6. Once logged in, your account will be active—you can now check out books, renew items, and track due dates.

⚠️ Common Mistake: Many students forget to log in before accessing restricted resources. Always ensure you’re signed in when using e-books, databases, or online journals.

One of the biggest advantages of Montclair State Libraries is off-campus access to thousands of e-books, journals, and databases. Here’s how to ensure seamless remote access:

  • Use the VPN (Virtual Private Network) if you encounter proxy errors. Download it from the Montclair State IT Services page.

  • Enable "Persistent Links" when citing sources—these ensure uninterrupted access to articles and books.

Chapter 2: Navigating the Library Catalog & Finding What You Need

The Montclair State University Libraries use WorldCat Discovery, a powerful search tool that scans millions of books, articles, and multimedia across 100+ databases. Mastering this system will save you hours of fruitless searching.

2.1 Advanced Search Techniques

Basic keyword searches often return too many irrelevant results. Instead, use these proven strategies:

Strategy 1: Use Boolean Operators (AND, OR, NOT)

  • "Climate Change AND Policy" → Finds articles only about climate change policies.
  • "Renewable Energy OR Solar Power" → Expands results to include both terms.
  • "Global Warming NOT Fiction" → Excludes non-academic sources.

Strategy 2: Apply Filters for Precision

After a search, refine results using:

  • Date Range (e.g., "Last 5 Years" for cutting-edge research).
  • Resource Type (e.g., "Peer-Reviewed Journals" for scholarly articles).
  • Subject Terms (e.g., "Economic Impact" to narrow down topics).

Strategy 3: Use Truncation & Wildcards

  • "Climat"* → Finds "Climate," "Climatic," "Climatology."
  • "Psycholog"* → Covers "Psychology," "Psychological," "Psychotherapist."

📌 Example Search: A business student needs market analysis reports on e-commerce in Latin America. Instead of searching broadly, they use:

  • "E-commerce" AND "Latin America" AND "market analysis"
  • Filter by "Reports" under Resource Type.
  • Limit to "2020–Present" for the latest data.

2.2 Finding Books Not Available at Montclair

If a book isn’t in the Montclair State Libraries, you can request it via Interlibrary Loan (ILL)—a free service that borrows materials from other academic libraries nationwide.

How to Request an ILL:

  1. Search the catalog for the book.
  2. Click "Request Item" (if unavailable).
  3. Fill out the ILL form with your Montclair ID and contact info.
  4. Processing time: 3–10 business days (faster for electronic articles).
  5. Loan period: 28 days (renewable once if needed).

⚠️ Common Mistake: Some students forget to check their email for ILL notifications. Set up library account alerts in your Montclair Portal to avoid missing requests.

2.3 Using Subject Guides for Research

Montclair State Libraries provides subject-specific research guides created by librarians to help you find the best resources for your field.

How to Access Subject Guides:

  1. Go to Montclair State Library Guides.
  2. Browse by subject (e.g., Business, Education, STEM, Humanities).
  3. Each guide includes:
    • Recommended databases
    • Key journals & books
    • Citation guides
    • Research tips

💡 Pro Tip: For STEM students, the Science & Engineering Research Guide links to IEEE Xplore, ScienceDirect, and PubMed—essential for technical research.

3.2 How to Find the Best Articles for Your Paper

Not all articles are created equal. Follow this step-by-step process to locate high-quality sources:

  1. Start with a Subject Guide (as mentioned in Section 2.3).
  2. Use Google Scholar (scholar.google.com) to find citations and then check if Montclair has full-text access.
  3. Check the "Peer-Reviewed" filter in databases like JSTOR or ScienceDirect.
  4. Look for "Impact Factor" (a measure of journal prestige) in Web of Science.
  5. Avoid "predatory journals"—use the Beall’s List (though outdated, still useful) or Think. Check. Submit. to verify legitimacy.

📌 Real-World Example: *A political science student was writing a paper on voting behavior in New Jersey. Instead of just searching "voting patterns," they:

  • Used Political Science Complete (via Montclair’s databases).
  • Applied filters for "New Jersey" and "2010–2024."
  • Found 3 key articles from Harvard’s Election Data Service and Pew Research Center.
  • Cited them properly in APA format for full credit.*

3.3 Accessing Full-Text Articles When the PDF Isn’t Available

Sometimes, you’ll find a citation but no direct PDF link. Here’s how to get the full article:

✅ Option 1: Check "Get It" or "Full Text Finder"

  • Many databases (like JSTOR or ProQuest) have a "Get It" button.
  • Clicking it may redirect you to Montclair’s subscription or trigger an ILL request.

✅ Option 2: Use the "Citation Linker" in WorldCat

  • Paste the DOI or title into the Montclair Citation Linker.
  • If available, the system will provide a direct link.

✅ Option 3: Request via Interlibrary Loan (ILL)

  • If the article isn’t in Montclair’s collection, submit an ILL request (takes 1–3 days).

⚠️ Common Mistake: Students sometimes ignore paywalls and assume an article is free. Always check Montclair’s databases first before paying for access.
