Chapter 1: An Overview of Montclair State University Libraries

Montclair State University’s libraries system is designed to support undergraduate, graduate, and professional students across 10+ academic disciplines, including business, education, health sciences, and the arts. The primary campus library, The Library at Montclair State, is a modern, tech-driven space with:

  • Over 500,000 physical and digital books
  • 100+ research databases (including JSTOR, ProQuest, and EBSCOhost)
  • 24/7 access to online resources via the library portal
  • Specialized collections (e.g., archival materials, rare books, and multimedia resources)

Key Locations & Services

Montclair State’s libraries are spread across three main locations:

  1. The Library at Montclair State (Main Campus) – The hub for general research, featuring:
    • Study carrels (reservable for group work)
    • Printing, scanning, and 3D printing services

  2. The Education Library (School of Education) – Specializes in K-12 teaching resources, including:
    • Children’s and young adult literature collections
    • Curriculum materials for classroom use
  3. The Health Sciences Library (School of Health & Human Services) – Focuses on nursing, public health, and exercise science, with:
    • Anatomical models and simulation software
    • Access to medical journals and clinical databases

Chapter 2: 8 Actionable Strategies to Master Montclair State University Libraries

Now that you know what’s available, let’s dive into how to use it effectively. These strategies are backed by real student success stories and library staff recommendations.

Strategy 1: Start with the Library’s Homepage & Search Tools

Problem: Many new students go straight to Google instead of using the library’s dedicated search tools, which can lead to irrelevant or paywalled results.

Solution: Use Montclair State’s library portal (library.montclair.edu) as your first stop for research.

How to Do It Right:

  1. Use the "Everything" Search Bar – This searches books, articles, videos, and more in one go.
  2. Refine with Advanced Filters – After searching, use filters like:
    • Date range (e.g., "Last 5 Years" for current research)
    • Source type (e.g., "Peer-Reviewed Journals" for academic credibility)
    • Full-text availability (to avoid paywalls)
  3. Bookmark Key Databases – Some subjects require specific databases:
    • Business students? Try Business Source Premier.
    • Education majors? Use ERIC (Education Resources Information Center).
    • Health sciences? CINAHL is a must.

Real-World Example: *A senior business major, Jamal, was struggling to find reliable market analysis reports for his capstone project. After switching from Google to the library’s Business Source Premier, he found three peer-reviewed articles in under 10 minutes—saving him weeks of manual searching.

Strategy 2: Leverage Librarians as Your Personal Research Assistants

Problem: Many students don’t realize that librarians are trained research experts—not just people who check out books.

Solution: Schedule a research consultation or chat with a librarian via the library’s Ask a Librarian service.

How to Do It Right:

  • For quick help: Use the live chat (available 24/7 via the library website).
  • For deep dives: Book a one-on-one appointment (takes 5-10 minutes).
  • For group projects: Request a workshop on citation styles, database searches, or primary sources.

Real-World Example: *A graduate student in public health, Priya, was researching vaccine hesitancy but kept getting biased or outdated sources. After a 30-minute consultation, her librarian taught her how to:

  • Use Boolean operators ("AND," "OR," "NOT") for precise searches.
  • Identify high-impact journals in her field.
  • Access government health reports (e.g., CDC, WHO). Result? She completed her literature review in half the time and submitted a stronger thesis proposal.

Strategy 3: Use Interlibrary Loan (ILL) for Books & Articles You Can’t Find Locally

Problem: Montclair State’s collection is vast but not infinite. If a book or article isn’t available, you might think you’re stuck—but you’re not.

Solution: Interlibrary Loan (ILL) lets you borrow materials from other NJ libraries (and sometimes nationally) for free.

How to Do It Right:

  1. Search the library catalog first.
  2. If the item is not available, click "Request via Interlibrary Loan."
  3. Turnaround time: Usually 3-7 business days (sometimes faster for articles).
  4. Renewals: Most ILL items can be extended if needed.

Real-World Example: *A history major, Carlos, needed a rare 19th-century newspaper for his thesis on Industrial Revolution labor. Since Montclair didn’t have it, he used ILL to request it from Rutgers University. The library scanned and emailed it to him in 48 hours—no shipping delays.

Strategy 4: Explore Digital & Open-Access Resources (No Paywalls!)

Problem: Some students assume they can’t access premium content because they don’t have a paid subscription.

Solution: Montclair State provides free access to thousands of digital resources, including:

  • JSTOR (humanities & social sciences)
  • Project MUSE (literature & philosophy)
  • Kanopy (documentaries & educational films)
  • Open Access Repositories (e.g., arXiv for STEM, SSRN for business)

How to Do It Right:

  • Check the library’s "Databases A-Z" list for subject-specific recommendations.
  • Use Google Scholar (but link it to Montclair’s library to access full-text PDFs via "Get It @ Montclair").
  • Look for "Open Access" labels—these are free for everyone.

Real-World Example: *A film student, Aisha, was researching post-colonial cinema but kept hitting paywalls on Film & History journals. By switching to Kanopy and the library’s Kanopy Plus subscription, she found 10+ documentaries for free—including rare interviews with directors.

Strategy 5: Use the Library’s Citation & Writing Tools to Avoid Plagiarism

Problem: 56% of college students (2023 survey) don’t cite sources correctly, leading to plagiarism warnings or failing grades.

Solution: Montclair State’s library offers free citation generators and writing help:

  • Zotero (reference manager)
  • NoodleTools (for annotated bibliographies)
  • Writing Center (free tutoring on APA, MLA, Chicago styles)

How to Do It Right:

  1. Install Zotero (browser extension) to automatically save and cite sources.
  2. Use NoodleTools for group projects (tracks everyone’s contributions).
  3. Visit the Writing Center (in-person or virtual) for peer reviews.

Real-World Example: *A biology major, Taylor, was panicking about her APA-style lab report. She used Zotero to organize her sources, then had a Writing Center tutor review her citations. Result? Her professor praised her "exceptional formatting"—and she got an A.

Problem: Students forget about library resources until they need them last-minute, leading to stress and incomplete work.

Solution: Use library apps and alerts to stay ahead:

  • Email alerts (for new journal articles in your topic)

How to Do It Right:

  1. Download LibCal to reserve spaces in advance.
  2. Set up Google Scholar alerts (via the library’s Scholar link).
  3. Check the library’s blog for subject-specific updates.

Real-World Example: *A psychology major, Mia, set up email alerts for new studies on "mental health in Gen Z." When a breakthrough paper was published, she downloaded it immediately—giving her a head start on her research paper.

Chapter 3: Common Mistakes New Students Make (And How to Avoid Them)

Even with great resources, some students waste time or miss opportunities due to simple mistakes. Here’s how to avoid them:

Mistake 1: Not Using the Library’s "Get It" Button

What happens? You find a great article in Google Scholar but can’t access the full text because it’s paywalled. Fix: Always check for the "Get It @ Montclair" button—it links to the library’s full-text version.

Mistake 2: Ignoring the Library’s "Ask a Librarian" Service

What happens? You struggle with a complex search but don’t ask for help, leading to wasted hours. Fix: Librarians are there to help—use chat, email, or appointments.

Mistake 3: Not Checking for Digital Versions of Books

What happens? You only look for physical books, missing e-books that are available 24/7. Fix: Filter by "Available Online" in the library catalog.

Mistake 4: Overlooking Open Access & Free Resources

What happens? You pay for articles when the library already provides free access. Fix: Always check the library’s databases first before buying.

Mistake 5: Not Using Interlibrary Loan (ILL) Early

What happens? You wait until the last minute to request a book, leading to delays. Fix: Request ILL items as soon as you need them—some take days to arrive.

Mistake 6: Not Attending Workshops or Events

What happens? You miss out on free training that could save you hours of trial and error. Fix: Check the library’s calendar and attend at least one workshop per semester.
