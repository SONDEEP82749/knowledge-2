8 Actionable Strategies to Succeed at Montclair State University Libraries

Now that you understand the why, let’s explore how to make the most of your library experience. These strategies are backed by real-world examples and designed to save you time, reduce stress, and boost your academic performance.

1. Master the Library Catalog: Beyond Google Searches

Problem: Many students rely on Google Scholar or Wikipedia for research, missing out on peer-reviewed journals, e-books, and exclusive Montclair resources.

Solution: Use the Montclair State University Libraries’ online catalog (via Library Portal) to:

  • Search by keyword, title, author, or subject (e.g., "climate change policies in New Jersey").
  • Filter results by format (books, articles, videos), date, and accessibility (e.g., "full text online").
  • Set up alerts for new publications in your field.

Real-World Example: A political science major researching local governance initially struggled to find New Jersey-specific case studies. By using the advanced search filters (limiting to "New Jersey" + "local government"), they uncovered three unpublished theses from Montclair alumni—goldmine material for their thesis.

Common Mistake: Skipping the "Full Text Online" filter, leading to wasted time chasing paywalled articles that could have been accessed via the library’s subscriptions.

2. Leverage Librarian Subject Guides for Instant Expertise

Problem: You don’t know where to start when researching a complex topic.

Solution: Each department at Montclair State has a dedicated Librarian Subject Guide, a curated collection of the best resources for your major. For example:

  • Business students can access guides on market research databases (e.g., IBISWorld, Mergent Online).
  • Education majors find ERIC (Education Resources Information Center) and teacher resource databases.
  • Art students discover VADS (Visual Arts Database) and museum archives.

Real-World Example: A nursing student* needed evidence-based practice articles for a capstone project. Instead of searching blindly, they visited the Nursing & Health Sciences LibGuide, which pre-selected 15 high-impact journals—saving them weeks of research time.

Pro Tip: Bookmark these guides early! Many include step-by-step research tutorials and citation help.

3. Use Interlibrary Loan (ILL) to Get Books & Articles You Can’t Find Locally

Problem: Your required textbook isn’t in the Montclair collection, or a key article is paywalled.

Solution: Interlibrary Loan (ILL) allows you to borrow materials from other institutions for free. Here’s how:

  1. Search the catalog—if the item isn’t available, click "Request via Interlibrary Loan."
  2. Submit your request (usually takes 3–7 business days for books, 1–2 days for articles).
  3. Receive digital copies (PDFs) or physical books mailed to you.

Real-World Example: A philosophy major needed a rare 19th-century text not held by Montclair. Via ILL, they received a scanned PDF within 48 hours—no need to visit another university.

Common Mistake: Assuming ILL is slow and unnecessary. In reality, 90% of requests are fulfilled within a week (Montclair’s 2023 ILL report).

4. Explore Digital Archives & Primary Sources for Unique Research

Problem: You want original documents (e.g., historical letters, government records) but don’t know where to look.

Solution: Montclair’s Digital Collections house thousands of primary sources, including:

  • New Jersey Historical Society archives (e.g., letters from the Revolutionary War era).
  • Montclair State University’s institutional repository (e.g., student publications from the 1950s).
  • Government documents (e.g., FBI files, census data).

Real-World Example: A history student was researching Montclair’s role in the Civil Rights Movement. By exploring the Digital Collections, they found unpublished interviews with local activists—material not available in any textbook.

Pro Tip: Check the "Special Collections" section for rare books, manuscripts, and oral histories.

5. Attend Library Workshops & Research Help Sessions

Problem: You’re unsure how to cite sources properly or evaluate scholarly articles.

Solution: The libraries offer free workshops on:

  • Avoiding plagiarism (using Turnitin & SafeAssign).
  • Advanced database searching (e.g., Google Scholar vs. Montclair’s databases).
  • Data visualization (using Tableau & SPSS).

Real-World Example: A business student struggled with APA citations until attending a "Citation Bootcamp" workshop. They left with a cheat sheet and confidence to format their entire paper correctly.

How to Sign Up:

  • Check the Library Events Calendar.
  • Request a one-on-one research consultation with a librarian.

7. Use Library Tech Tools to Boost Your Workflow

Problem: You’re manually organizing notes, citations, and sources—wasting time and risking errors.

Solution: The libraries provide free access to productivity tools:

  • Zotero (for organizing citations & PDFs).
  • RefWorks (for automated bibliographies).
  • OverDrive & Hoopla (for e-books & audiobooks).
  • 3D Printers & Scanners (for physical research projects).

Real-World Example: A psychology major used Zotero to auto-cite 50 sources in APA format, reducing their citation time from 6 hours to 30 minutes.

Common Mistake: Not backing up your Zotero library to the cloud—always sync with Zotero Cloud or Dropbox.

Problem: You want real-world research opportunities but don’t know where to start.

Solution: Montclair Libraries partner with:

  • New Jersey State Archives (for government records).
  • New York Public Library (NYPL) (via passport program).

FAQ: Your Burning Questions About Montclair State University Libraries

To help you quickly find answers, here are five frequently asked questions with schema markup for better search visibility.

1. How do I access e-books and digital resources from off-campus?

Answer: To access e-books, journal articles, and databases when not on campus, you must:

  1. Log in via the Library Portal using your Montclair username and password.
  2. Bookmark the "Off-Campus Access" page for quick links.
  3. Use the "Find It" button in Google Scholar to redirect to Montclair’s paid subscriptions.

Pro Tip: Install the Montclair Libraries app (available on iOS/Android) for off-campus logins without entering credentials repeatedly.

2. Can I request physical books from other libraries through Interlibrary Loan (ILL)?

Answer: Yes! The Interlibrary Loan (ILL) service allows you to:

  • Borrow books from other NJ universities (e.g., Rutgers, Princeton).
  • Receive them in 3–7 business days (faster for articles).
  • Renew books if needed (with approval).

How to Request:

  1. Search the Montclair catalog.
  2. If unavailable, click "Request via Interlibrary Loan."
  3. Fill out the form with your student ID and contact info.

Important: Books are typically loaned for 4 weeks (renewable if not requested by another user).

3. Are there any special collections or rare books I can access?

Answer: Absolutely! Montclair’s Special Collections include:

  • New Jersey Historical Society archives (e.g., Revolutionary War letters).
  • Montclair State University’s institutional repository (e.g., student newspapers from the 1920s).
  • Rare books (e.g., first editions of Hemingway, Fitzgerald).

How to Access:

  • Visit the Special Collections desk in Library South (Basement).
  • Request appointments for delicate or fragile items.
  • Check the Digital Collections for online access.

Fun Fact: One rare book is a 17th-century alchemy manuscript—perfect for history of science research!

4. How can I get help with citations and avoiding plagiarism?

Answer: Montclair Libraries offer multiple citation tools to ensure academic integrity:

  • Zotero (free, auto-cites in APA, MLA, Chicago).
  • RefWorks (for large bibliographies).
  • Turnitin & SafeAssign (for plagiarism checks before submitting papers).

Workshops & Resources:

  • Attend a "Citation Bootcamp" (check the Library Events Calendar).
  • Use the Montclair Citation Guide for step-by-step examples.

Warning: Self-plagiarism (reusing old work) is still plagiarism—always paraphrase and cite properly.
