Chapter 1: Getting Started – Your First Steps at MSU Libraries

1.1 How to Access Montclair State University Libraries: Physical & Digital Entry Points

Montclair State University Libraries consist of three main locations:

  • Library North (home to the main collection, including books, journals, and study spaces)
  • Library South (specializing in digital media, archives, and specialized research)
  • The Learning Commons (a hub for tutoring, tech support, and group study)

First-Time Users: Your MSU NetID is your key to everything. Here’s how to access resources:

✅ Physical Access:

  • Student ID Card: Required for entry. If you don’t have one, visit the MSU Office of Student Affairs to get a replacement.
  • Hours: Libraries are open 24/7 during peak semesters (check the official hours page for updates).
  • Reservations: Book a study room via the LibCal system (more on this later).

✅ Digital Access:

  • Library Website: https://www.montclair.edu/library – Your gateway to e-books, databases, and research guides.
  • OneSearch: The unified search bar on the homepage lets you find books, articles, and multimedia in one place.
  • Proxy Login: Off-campus? Use your MSU NetID and password to access restricted resources.

Pro Tip: Bookmark the Library’s Research Guides—they’re curated by librarians to help you find subject-specific resources without wasting time.

1.2 Your First Visit: What to Expect (And How to Stand Out)

Your first trip to the library can feel overwhelming, but MSU Libraries are designed for new users. Here’s what to look for:

📌 The Help Desk (Library North, 2nd Floor):

  • Staffed by librarians and student assistants who can help with:
    • Finding books
    • Navigating databases
    • Citing sources (APA, MLA, Chicago)
    • Printing & scanning (more on this in Section 3)

📌 The Learning Commons (1st Floor):

  • Free tutoring services (math, writing, STEM)
  • Tech support for laptops, software, and printing issues
  • Collaboration spaces with whiteboards and video conferencing setups

📌 The Archives & Special Collections (Library South):

  • Rare books, historical documents, and primary sources for advanced research.
  • Appointment required—email archives@montclair.edu to schedule.

Real-World Example: A first-year biology major, Priya, struggled to find peer-reviewed articles for her paper on climate change. Instead of guessing, she visited the Help Desk, where a librarian guided her to JSTOR and ScienceDirect, two databases with high-impact research papers. Within 30 minutes, she had a fully cited bibliography—something she couldn’t have done alone.

Chapter 2: Mastering Montclair State University’s Digital Resources

2.1 The Power of OneSearch: Your All-in-One Research Tool

OneSearch is Montclair State’s unified discovery tool, allowing you to search books, journal articles, videos, and more simultaneously. However, many students don’t use it effectively.

✅ How to Use OneSearch Like a Pro:

  1. Use Advanced Search Filters:

    • Limit by date, source type (peer-reviewed only), or full text availability.
    • Example: If researching "AI ethics," filter for "peer-reviewed articles published in the last 5 years."

  2. Check the "Availability" Tab:

    • If a book is not at MSU, request it via Interlibrary Loan (ILL) (free for students!).

  3. Save & Organize Results:

    • Click "Add to Folder" to save citations for later.
    • Export to Zotero, EndNote, or Google Scholar for seamless referencing.

Common Mistake: Students often assume OneSearch only finds books. It also indexes thousands of journal articles—a goldmine for research papers.

2.3 E-Books & Audiobooks: Study Anywhere, Anytime

Montclair State Libraries offer thousands of e-books through Ebrary, EBSCO eBooks, and OverDrive. Here’s how to make the most of them:

📚 How to Download E-Books:

  1. Search for your book in OneSearch.
  2. Click "Available Online" and select "Download PDF" or "Read Online."
  3. For OverDrive, use the Libby app (free) to borrow audiobooks.

🎧 Audiobooks for Active Learning:

  • Great for commuting, studying while working out, or multitasking.
  • Try "Libby" (free with MSU login) or "Audible" (limited free trials).

Pro Tip: Use Zotero to automatically save e-book citations—no manual copying needed.

Chapter 3: Printing, Scanning, and Tech Support at MSU Libraries

3.1 Printing & Scanning Made Simple

Montclair State Libraries offer free printing and scanning for students, but many don’t know how to use the system efficiently.

🖨 Printing:

  • Cost: $0.10 per black & white page, $0.25 per color page.
  • How to Pay:
    • Add funds via MyMSU Portal > Student Account > Library Printing.
    • Alternative: Use Google Cloud Print (if your laptop is set up).

✏ Scanning:

  • Self-Service Scanners (Library North, 1st Floor) allow PDF or email scans.
  • Mobile Scanning: Use the "Scanbot" app (free) to scan documents and email them to yourself.

Common Mistake: Students forget to check their print balance before printing. Always verify funds in MyMSU to avoid surprises.

3.2 Tech Support & Software Access

Need help with software, laptops, or software installations? The Learning Commons has you covered.

💻 Available Services:

  • Microsoft Office (Word, Excel, PowerPoint) – Pre-installed on all MSU computers.
  • Adobe Creative Cloud – Free for students (request via IT Help Desk).
  • Software Requests – Need SAS, SPSS, or MATLAB? Submit a request at IT Help Desk.

Real-World Example: A computer science major, Priya, needed MATLAB for her algorithms project. She didn’t know it was available for free through MSU. After contacting the IT Help Desk, she received instant access—saving her from paying $100+ for a personal license.

Chapter 4: Study Spaces & Group Work at MSU Libraries

4.1 Finding the Perfect Study Spot

Montclair State Libraries offer multiple study environments, each suited for different needs:

📍 Quiet Zones (Library North, 3rd Floor):

  • Carrels & individual booths for deep focus.
  • Best for: Writing papers, studying for exams.

📍 Collaboration Spaces (Learning Commons, 1st Floor):

  • Whiteboard tables, video conferencing setups.

📍 24/7 Study Lounges (Library North Basement):

4.2 Group Study Tips for Maximum Productivity

Working in a group? Here’s how to make the most of MSU’s study spaces:

✅ Set Clear Goals:

  • Example: "We’ll spend 45 minutes discussing Chapter 3, then take a 10-minute break."

✅ Use the Whiteboard:

  • Sketch mind maps, flowcharts, or study schedules to stay organized.

✅ Take Breaks Strategically:

  • Follow the Pomodoro Technique (25 min work, 5 min break).

Common Mistake: Groups often overstay in reserved rooms. Always check the time and clear the space when done.

Chapter 5: Research & Citation Help at MSU Libraries

5.1 How to Get Research Help from Librarians

Montclair State Librarians are experts in research methodology, but many students don’t know how to ask for help.

🔍 How to Request a Research Consultation:

  1. Email: library@montclair.edu (response within 24 hours).
  2. Chat: Use the "Ask a Librarian" widget on the library website.
  3. In-Person: Visit the Help Desk during business hours.

What to Ask For:

  • "How do I find peer-reviewed articles on [topic]?"
  • "Can you help me narrow my research question?"
  • "What are the best databases for my field?"

Real-World Example: A history graduate student, Daniel, was stuck on primary source analysis. He scheduled a 30-minute consultation with a librarian, who taught him how to use the Digital Public Library of America (DPLA) to find rare historical documents. His thesis gained depth because of this guidance.

5.2 Citation Tools & Avoiding Plagiarism

Citing sources correctly is critical—but many students struggle with formatting.

📌 Best Citation Tools at MSU:

  • Zotero (Free, browser-based)
  • EndNote (Paid, but available via MSU Software Center)
  • EasyBib (Web-based, simple for beginners)

How to Use Zotero (Step-by-Step):

  1. Download Zotero from https://www.zotero.org.
  2. Add browser extensions for Google Scholar & OneSearch.
  3. Drag & drop citations into your word processor.
  4. Auto-generate bibliographies in APA, MLA, or Chicago style.

Common Mistake: Students copy-paste citations without checking for errors. Always double-check with Purdue OWL’s citation guide (https://owl.purdue.edu).
