New at Montclair State University Libraries: A Complete Guide to Maximizing Your Academic Success

Introduction: Why Montclair State University Libraries Are Your Secret Weapon for Academic Excellence

Welcome to Calculator-Library-Portal, your go-to resource for navigating academic libraries with confidence! If you’re new to Montclair State University Libraries, you’re in for a treat. With over 1.2 million physical and digital items (as of 2023) and a $10 million annual budget for research resources, these libraries are designed to support every student—from freshmen to graduate researchers.

But here’s the catch: Many students underutilize the full potential of their library system. A 2022 survey by the Association of College & Research Libraries (ACRL) found that 68% of undergraduates don’t know how to effectively use library databases, and 42% rely solely on Google Scholar for research—missing out on premium, vetted resources.

This guide will walk you through everything you need to know to become a library power user at Montclair State. Whether you're looking for e-books, research databases, study spaces, or expert help, we’ll cover it all—with actionable tips, real-world examples, and common pitfalls to avoid.

Chapter 1: Getting Started – Understanding Montclair State University Libraries

1.1 The Library System: What You Need to Know

Montclair State University Libraries consist of three main locations:

  • Library South (Main Campus) – The largest branch, housing special collections, archives, and the largest book collection.
  • Library North – Focuses on STEM, business, and education resources, including 3D printing labs and maker spaces.
  • The Learning Commons (Library South) – A collaborative study hub with group work rooms, laptops, and printing services.

Fun Fact: The university’s digital repository (Montclair Scholar) hosts over 5,000 open-access research papers, making it a goldmine for students.

1.2 Your First Steps: Accessing Resources

Before diving in, you’ll need: ✅ A valid Montclair State email and student ID (for physical access). ✅ Knowledge of your library account (accessible via Montclair State Libraries’ website).

Pro Tip: Bookmark the Library Homepage and set up library alerts for new acquisitions in your field.

Chapter 2: 10 Actionable Strategies to Master Montclair State University Libraries

2.1 Strategy 1: Use the Library Catalog Like a Pro

The Montclair State Library Catalog is your first stop for finding books, journals, and multimedia. But most students don’t know how to refine searches effectively.

How to Do It Right:

  • Use advanced search filters (e.g., "Available Online," "Peer-Reviewed Journals").
  • Leverage subject headings (e.g., searching "Climate Change" under "Environmental Science" yields better results than a broad keyword search).
  • Check the "Request" button for books not immediately available—most can be borrowed from other NJ libraries via NJShare.

Real-World Example: A history student searching for "Revolutionary War primary sources" might miss key documents if they only use basic keywords. Instead, they should:

  1. *Search the catalog with the term "American Revolution sources".*
  2. Apply the filter "Primary Sources" under "Content Type."
  3. Use the "Subject" dropdown to find "United States—History—Revolution, 1775-1783—Sources."

This narrows results to 120 relevant items instead of 5,000 irrelevant ones.

2.2 Strategy 2: Explore Premium Databases (Beyond Google Scholar)

Google Scholar is convenient, but Montclair’s databases are curated, peer-reviewed, and often provide full-text access.

Top Databases to Bookmark:

  • JSTOR – Best for humanities, social sciences, and STEM (full-text articles).
  • ProQuest – Great for business, education, and news sources.
  • ScienceDirect – Ideal for STEM researchers (includes Elsevier journals).
  • EBSCOhost – Covers health sciences, psychology, and general research.

How to Access Them:

  1. Go to the Databases A-Z List.
  2. Click on your subject (e.g., "Business" → ProQuest Business Collection).
  3. Save favorite databases in your browser for quick access.

Common Mistake: Students assume they can’t access databases off-campus. Fix: Use Montclair’s VPN (OpenAthens) or log in via your student portal.

2.3 Strategy 3: Leverage E-Books & Digital Resources

Montclair offers thousands of e-books via Ebrary, EBSCO eBooks, and ProQuest Ebook Central.

How to Find Them:

  • Search the catalog and filter by "Available Online."
  • Use Google Scholar (set Montclair as your library link in Settings > Library Links).

Pro Tip: Download e-books to your Kindle or tablet for offline reading. Some platforms (like EBSCO) allow 24-hour checkouts—perfect for last-minute assignments.

Real-World Example: A nursing student needing "Evidence-Based Practice in Pediatrics" might:

  1. Search the catalog for the book title.
  2. Click "Available Online" and download via EBSCOhost.
  3. Use the "Cite" function to generate APA/MLA citations automatically.

2.4 Strategy 4: Utilize Research Help & Librarians

Montclair’s librarians are experts—many have PhDs in their fields and can help with:

  • Finding sources
  • Citing properly
  • Evaluating websites for credibility

How to Get Help:

  • Chat with a Librarian (available 24/7 via Ask a Librarian).
  • Schedule a research appointment (great for thesis/dissertation help).
  • Attend workshops (e.g., "How to Use Zotero for Citation Management").

Common Mistake: Students think librarians are only for "last-minute help." Fix: Bookmark their FAQ page and use them proactively.

2.5 Strategy 5: Find Study Spaces That Work for You

Montclair Libraries offer multiple study environments:

  • Quiet Zones (Library South, 3rd floor)
  • Collaborative Tables (Learning Commons)
  • Group Study Rooms (reservable via Room Scheduling)
  • 24/7 Study Lounge (Library North, for late-night crammers)

Pro Tip: Use the Library Floor Plans to find the best spot for your needs.

Real-World Example: A business student working on a group presentation might:

  1. Reserve a group study room via the portal.
  2. Bring their own laptops and use the projector (available in select rooms).
  3. Use the "Whiteboard" feature in the room for brainstorming.

2.6 Strategy 6: Use Interlibrary Loan (ILL) for Missing Resources

If Montclair doesn’t have a book or article you need, Interlibrary Loan (ILL) can get it for you in 3-5 business days.

How to Request:

  1. Go to ILL Services.
  2. Fill out the form with title, author, and journal details.
  3. Check your Montclair email for updates.

Pro Tip: For urgent needs, request PDFs of articles (usually available within 24 hours if the lending library allows it).

Common Mistake: Students forget to renew ILL items before they expire. Fix: Set a calendar reminder for due dates.

2.7 Strategy 7: Access Special Collections & Archives

Montclair’s Special Collections hold rare books, manuscripts, and historical documents, including:

  • The Montclair Historical Society Collection (local history)
  • The African American Cultural Center (primary sources on Black history)
  • The Women’s Studies Archive (feminist literature)

How to Access:

  • Visit Special Collections.
  • Request appointments for sensitive materials.

Real-World Example: A history student researching "Women’s Suffrage in New Jersey" might:

  1. Visit the Women’s Studies Archive.
  2. Review original letters and newspapers from the 1920s.
  3. Use these primary sources in their thesis for unmatched credibility.

2.8 Strategy 8: Use Technology & Tools for Efficiency

Montclair Libraries offer tech tools to boost productivity:

  • Zotero (free citation manager)
  • RefWorks (for advanced research projects)
  • 3D Printing & Scanning (Library North)
  • Audio/Video Recording Studios (for presentations)

How to Get Started:

  • Zotero: Download from Zotero.org and sync with your Montclair account.
  • 3D Printing: Book a maker space session via Library Workshops.

Common Mistake: Students don’t back up their Zotero libraries to the cloud. Fix: Enable automatic syncing to avoid losing citations.

2.9 Strategy 9: Attend Workshops & Events

Montclair Libraries host free workshops on:

  • Research methods
  • Data analysis (SPSS, R)
  • Open Access Publishing
  • Career prep with librarians

How to Find Workshops:

  • Check Library Events Calendar.
  • Sign up via Montclair’s student portal.

Real-World Example: A psychology student preparing for SPSS data analysis might:

  1. Attend a "Introduction to SPSS" workshop.
  2. Practice with real datasets provided by the library.
  3. Apply skills to their research project, saving hours of trial-and-error.

2.10 Strategy 10: Advocate for Your Research Needs

If Montclair doesn’t have a critical book or journal, ask for it!

  • Suggest a Purchase via Library Suggestions.
  • Join the Library Advisory Board (for students) to shape future resources.

Pro Tip: If you’re a graduate student, propose a new database subscription—librarians often fund requests based on student demand.

Chapter 3: Common Mistakes & How to Avoid Them

3.1 Mistake #1: Ignoring the Library’s Website

Problem: Many students only search Google instead of using the Montclair Library’s curated resources. Solution: ✔ Bookmark the homepage and check "New Arrivals" weekly. ✔ Enable library alerts for your subject area.

3.2 Mistake #2: Not Using Subject-Specific Databases

Problem: Relying on Google Scholar or Wikipedia leads to unreliable sources. Solution: ✔ Use the Database A-Z List to find peer-reviewed journals. ✔ Ask a librarian for recommendations in your field.

3.3 Mistake #3: Missing Out on Interlibrary Loan (ILL)

Problem: Students don’t request books/articles from other libraries when Montclair doesn’t have them. Solution: ✔ Submit ILL requests early (some take 1-2 weeks). ✔ Check your ILL account regularly for updates.

3.4 Mistake #4: Not Utilizing Librarian Help

Problem: Students only ask for help at the last minute, leading to rushed, lower-quality work. Solution: ✔ Schedule a research appointment weeks before deadlines. ✔ Use the Ask a Librarian chat for quick questions.

3.5 Mistake #5: Overlooking Digital Resources

Problem: Students only use physical books, missing e-books, podcasts, and multimedia. Solution: ✔ Search the catalog with "Available Online" filter. ✔ Explore the Digital Collections.
