New at Montclair State University Libraries: A Comprehensive Guide to Maximizing Your Research & Study Experience

Introduction: Why Montclair State University Libraries Are Your Academic Powerhouse

Welcome to Montclair State University Libraries—a hidden gem for students, researchers, and lifelong learners! With a rich history spanning over a century, the libraries at Montclair State have evolved into a modern, tech-driven hub that supports everything from undergraduate research to cutting-edge faculty scholarship. In the 2022-2023 academic year alone, the university reported that over 1.2 million physical and digital resources were accessed by students, faculty, and staff, making it one of the most utilized academic resources on campus.

But what makes Montclair State’s libraries different? Unlike traditional libraries that simply house books, these facilities offer: ✅ Cutting-edge digital archives with rare manuscripts and historical documents ✅ Specialized research databases tailored to fields like education, business, and the arts ✅ Collaborative study spaces designed for group work and quiet focus ✅ Expert librarians who provide one-on-one research assistance ✅ 24/7 access to online resources through the Library Portal

If you're new to Montclair State—or even if you're a returning student looking to level up your research skills—this guide will walk you through everything you need to know to make the most of your library experience.

Chapter 1: Navigating the Montclair State University Libraries – A Beginner’s Guide

Before diving into strategies, it’s essential to understand the structure of the libraries. Montclair State has three main campus libraries, each serving different academic needs:

1. The Library at the Center for Academic & Student Excellence (CASE)

📍 Location: 1 Normal Avenue, Montclair, NJ 🔹 Primary Focus: Undergraduate research, study spaces, and general academic support 🔹 Key Features:

  • The Learning Commons – A hub for tutoring, writing workshops, and tech support
  • Group Study Rooms – Bookable for collaborative projects
  • Tech Lending – Laptops, tablets, and audio-visual equipment available for checkout

2. The Library at the School of Business

📍 Location: 1 Normal Avenue (within the Business School building) 🔹 Primary Focus: Business, economics, and management research 🔹 Key Features:

  • Specialized databases like IBISWorld, Mergent Online, and Statista
  • Case study collections for business students
  • Career resources including LinkedIn Learning and industry reports

3. The Library at the School of Education

📍 Location: 1 Normal Avenue (within the School of Education building) 🔹 Primary Focus: Education research, teaching methodologies, and K-12 resources 🔹 Key Features:

  • ERIC (Education Resources Information Center) database
  • Curriculum materials for student teachers
  • Special collections on educational history and policy

How to Access the Library Portal

All three libraries are connected through a single online portal, accessible via: 🔗 Montclair State University Libraries Website 🔹 Log in with your Montclair State credentials to unlock:

  • Full-text journal articles
  • E-books and audiobooks
  • Interlibrary loan requests
  • Personalized research guides

Pro Tip: Bookmark the Library Portal—it’s your one-stop shop for all academic resources!

Chapter 2: 10 Actionable Strategies to Master Montclair State University Libraries

Now that you know the basics, let’s dive into practical strategies to help you research smarter, save time, and excel in your courses.

Strategy 1: Use the Library’s Advanced Search Features

Many students make the mistake of only searching Google for academic papers. Montclair State’s Library Portal offers powerful search tools that go beyond basic keyword matching.

How to Do It:

  1. Go to the Library Portal
  2. Use the advanced search (usually a dropdown or "Advanced" link)
  3. Apply filters like:
    • Date range (e.g., "Last 5 years" for current research)
    • Source type (peer-reviewed journals, books, dissertations)
    • Subject keywords (e.g., "climate change" + "New Jersey")

Real-World Example: A political science student researching gun control laws could:

  • Search "Second Amendment" AND "New Jersey"
  • Filter by "Legislation" under Source Type
  • Limit results to "2020–Present" for the most relevant case studies

Common Mistake: Only using the basic search bar without refining results. Fix: Always apply filters to narrow down irrelevant sources.

Strategy 2: Leverage Librarian Research Assistance

Did you know Montclair State librarians are trained research experts? Many students don’t take advantage of this free service.

How to Access Help: 🔹 Chat with a Librarian (available 24/7 via the portal) 🔹 Schedule a Research Appointment (for in-depth help) 🔹 Visit the Research Help Desk (during library hours)

Real-World Example: A biology major struggling with a lab report on genetic mutations could:

  1. Email a librarian with their topic
  2. Receive a customized list of databases (e.g., PubMed, ScienceDirect)
  3. Get tips on evaluating sources for credibility

Common Mistake: Assuming librarians are only for "last-minute help." Fix: Book a session early—librarians can save hours of wasted searching.

Strategy 4: Master the Art of Citation & Avoid Plagiarism

Plagiarism is one of the most common academic offenses, but proper citation can be confusing. Montclair State’s libraries offer free citation tools to help.

How to Cite Correctly:

  1. Use Zotero or RefWorks (both available via the portal)
  2. Choose your citation style (APA, MLA, Chicago, etc.)
  3. Automatically generate citations and bibliographies

Real-World Example: A history student writing on the Civil Rights Movement could:

  • Import sources from JSTOR into Zotero
  • Auto-generate APA citations in seconds
  • Export a full bibliography for their paper

Common Mistake: Manually copying citations and getting them wrong. Fix: Use citation managers—they eliminate errors.

Montclair State’s digital archives contain rare historical documents that most students don’t even know exist.

What’s Available?

  • Rare books on literature and philosophy

How to Access:

  1. Visit the Digital Collections Page
  2. Search by keyword, date, or topic

Real-World Example: A graduate student researching Montclair’s role in the 1960s civil rights protests could:

  • Use firsthand accounts in their thesis
  • Cite rare documents to stand out in their field

Common Mistake: Ignoring digital archives in favor of general Google searches. Fix: Check the archives first—they often have unique, unpublished material.

Strategy 7: Take Advantage of Interlibrary Loan (ILL) for Hard-to-Find Books

Not every book you need is in Montclair’s collection. Interlibrary Loan (ILL) lets you borrow from other universities—for free.

How to Request:

  1. Search the Library Portal for your book
  2. If it’s not available, click "Request via Interlibrary Loan"
  3. Fill out the form (usually takes 3–5 business days)

Real-World Example: A philosophy student needing a rare 19th-century text on existentialism could:

  • Search the portal → Book not found
  • Submit an ILL request → Borrowed from Princeton University
  • Receive a digital scan within a week

Common Mistake: Giving up if a book isn’t in Montclair’s collection. Fix: Use ILL—it’s fast and free!

Even the most motivated students can fall into avoidable traps when using the library. Here’s what to watch out for:

Mistake #1: Not Using the Library Portal’s Full Potential

Problem: Many students only search Google and don’t explore the portal’s databases. Solution: ✔ Bookmark the portal and use it as your first search tool ✔ Enable alerts for new research in your field

Mistake #2: Ignoring Librarian Help

Problem: Students don’t ask for help until the last minute. Solution: ✔ Schedule a research appointment early (don’t wait until Week 10) ✔ Use the chat function for quick questions

Mistake #3: Overlooking Digital Archives

Problem: Students only look for books and miss unique digital collections. Solution: ✔ Check the digital archives for primary sources ✔ Use them in papers to stand out from peers

Mistake #4: Poor Time Management with Study Spaces

Problem: Students pick the wrong study space (e.g., noisy zones for exams). Solution: ✔ Match your task to the space (silent = exams, group rooms = projects) ✔ Book study rooms in advance
