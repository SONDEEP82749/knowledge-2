Chapter 1: Getting Started – Navigating Montclair State University Libraries

Before diving into research, it’s essential to understand the layout, services, and unique offerings of Montclair State’s libraries. The system consists of three main branches, each serving different academic needs:

1. The Library at the Center for Academic Success (CAS Library)

  • Location: Inside the Center for Academic Success (CAS) building.
  • Best for: Undergraduate students, first-year students, and those needing one-on-one research assistance.
  • Key Features:
    • 24/7 study spaces (including group rooms and silent zones).
    • Reserve a research consultant for personalized help with papers and projects.
    • High-tech computer labs with software like SPSS, MATLAB, and Adobe Creative Suite.

2. The Library at the School of Business (SB Library)

  • Location: Within the School of Business building.
  • Best for: Business, economics, and finance students.
  • Key Features:
    • Specialized business databases (Bloomberg Terminal, IBISWorld, Mergent).
    • Case study collections for real-world business scenarios.
    • Career resources, including resume workshops and LinkedIn optimization guides.

3. The Library at the School of Arts & Sciences (SAS Library)

  • Location: Main campus, near the Science & Math Center.
  • Best for: Humanities, social sciences, STEM, and interdisciplinary research.
  • Key Features:
    • Rare book collections, including first editions of Shakespeare and historical manuscripts.
    • Digital humanities labs with tools for text analysis and data visualization.
    • Interdisciplinary research guides tailored to specific majors.

How to Find What You Need: The Montclair State Library Portal

The Montclair State University Libraries website (libraries.montclair.edu) is your one-stop shop for all things research. Here’s how to use it effectively:

Actionable Tip #1: Master the Search Bar (Beyond Google)

Many students default to Google for research, but Montclair’s library search engine (LibSearch) is far more powerful because:

  • It aggregates books, journal articles, e-books, and digital archives in one place.
  • It includes peer-reviewed sources automatically, saving you time filtering results.
  • It links directly to full-text articles (no paywalls!).

How to Use It Like a Pro:

  1. Use advanced search filters (e.g., date range, source type, subject).
  2. Combine keywords with Boolean operators (AND, OR, NOT) for precise results.
    • Example: "climate change AND policy OR legislation NOT opinion"
  3. Save searches for future reference.

Real-World Example: A history student researching the American Civil Rights Movement could search:

  • "Civil Rights Movement" AND "New Jersey" AND "1960-1970" This narrows results to localized, primary-source documents from Montclair’s archives.

Chapter 4: Avoiding Common Pitfalls – How Not to Waste Time

Even the best resources can become time-sinks if you don’t use them wisely. Here’s how to avoid the biggest mistakes new users make.

Common Mistake #1: Relying Only on Google Scholar

Problem: Google Scholar is incomplete—it doesn’t always link to Montclair’s full-text articles (you’ll hit paywalls). Solution:

  • Use LibSearch first, then Google Scholar for supplementary sources.
  • Check the "Get It @ Montclair" button in databases to access full texts.

Example: A student searching for "climate change policy" in Google Scholar might find a great article, but when they click the PDF, they’re asked to pay $35. Instead, they should:

  1. Search the same terms in LibSearch.
  2. Click "Full Text Online" (if available via Montclair).
  3. If not, use "Interlibrary Loan" (free document delivery in 3-5 days).

Common Mistake #2: Ignoring Interlibrary Loan (ILL)

Problem: You find a perfect book or article, but Montclair doesn’t have it. Solution: Use Interlibrary Loan (ILL)—it’s free and fast!

How to Use ILL:

  1. Find the item in LibSearch or a database.
  2. Click "Request via Interlibrary Loan".
  3. Fill out the form (usually takes 1-3 business days).
  4. Check your Montclair email for the digital copy.

Pro Tip: Set up ILL notifications so you don’t forget to check for deliveries.

Common Mistake #3: Not Using Zotero or RefWorks for Citations

Problem: Manually citing sources is error-prone and time-consuming. Solution: Use free citation managers like Zotero or RefWorks.

How to Set Up Zotero:

  1. Download Zotero (works with Chrome/Firefox).
  2. Save citations as you research (drag-and-drop from databases).
  3. Generate bibliographies automatically in APA, MLA, etc.
  4. Insert citations into Word/Google Docs with one click.

Real-World Example: A history student researching Prohibition in New Jersey could:*

  • Save 50+ sources in Zotero.
  • Generate a 10-page bibliography in 5 minutes instead of hours.
  • Avoid plagiarism by properly citing every source.

Common Mistake #4: Skipping the "Ask a Librarian" Service

Problem: You’re stuck on a research question but don’t know who to ask. Solution: Montclair’s librarians are experts—use them!

How to Get Help:

  • Chat with a Librarian (live chat on the Montclair Libraries homepage).
  • Email a Research Consultant (libref@montclair.edu).
  • Schedule a 1-on-1 appointment (Calendly Link).

Example: A grad student working on a thesis about urban planning could:*

  • Email a librarian with their topic and key questions.
  • Get recommended databases, books, and even primary sources they wouldn’t have found alone.
