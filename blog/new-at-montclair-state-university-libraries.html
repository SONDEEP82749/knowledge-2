8 Essential Strategies to Succeed at Montclair State University Libraries

Now that you know why these libraries are exceptional, let’s explore actionable strategies to help you maximize your experience.

Strategy 1: Master the Library Catalog (Montclair OneSearch)

What it is: The Montclair OneSearch is your first stop for finding books, journals, articles, and digital resources.

How to use it effectively:

  • Use advanced search filters (e.g., "Peer-Reviewed," "Full Text," "Date Range") to refine results.
  • Try keyword variations—if you search for "climate change in New Jersey," also try "NJ environmental policy" for broader results.
  • Save searches & set alerts for new publications in your field.

Real-World Example: A political science student researching New Jersey’s voting laws could search:

  • "New Jersey election reform 2020-2024"
  • "NJ voting rights legislation"
  • "Montclair State political science databases"

By combining keywords with database-specific filters, they can narrow down to the most relevant sources in minutes.

Strategy 2: Leverage the AI Research Assistant (Montclair’s Smart Search Tool)

What it is: Montclair State offers an AI-powered research assistant that helps students find sources, summarize articles, and even generate citations.

How to use it:

  1. Ask specific questions (e.g., "Find me 5 peer-reviewed articles on renewable energy in NJ").
  2. Use it for citation generation—paste a source, and the AI will format it in APA, MLA, or Chicago style.
  3. Get summaries of long articles to save time.

Common Mistake: Only using the AI for basic searches without refining prompts. Fix: Be precise—instead of "Show me articles on psychology," try "List 3 studies on cognitive behavior therapy for anxiety in college students, published in the last 5 years."

Strategy 3: Explore Special Collections & Archives

Montclair State’s Special Collections hold rare books, historical documents, and unique research materials that aren’t available elsewhere.

What you can find:

  • First editions of famous novels
  • Historical newspapers from NJ
  • Manuscripts from local authors
  • Archival records of Montclair State’s history

How to access them:

  • Check the Special Collections website for digital exhibits.
  • Request in-person visits for physical items (some require appointments).
  • Ask librarians for guidance—they can help you uncover hidden gems in your research.

Real-World Example: A history student studying Prohibition in New Jersey could explore:

  • Original newspaper clippings from the 1920s.
  • Speeches by local politicians opposing alcohol sales.
  • Personal letters from Montclair residents during the era.

Without these archives, their research would lack primary sources—making their work far more credible.

Strategy 4: Use Database-Specific Tools for Deep Research

While Montclair OneSearch is great for broad searches, individual databases (like JSTOR, ProQuest, or ScienceDirect) offer specialized tools for advanced research.

Key Databases at Montclair State:

Database Best For Pro Tip
JSTOR Humanities & Social Sciences Use "Advanced Search" to filter by article type (e.g., reviews, research articles).
ProQuest Business, Health, & Education Try "Thesaurus Search" to find synonyms for your keywords.
ScienceDirect STEM & Medical Research Use "Citation Tracking" to find later studies that cite key papers.
EBSCOhost Multidisciplinary Research Enable "Email Alerts" for new publications in your field.

Common Mistake: Assuming all databases work the same way. Fix: Learn the unique features of each—some allow full-text downloads, while others require interlibrary loan requests.

Strategy 5: Take Advantage of Interlibrary Loan (ILL) for Missing Resources

Not every book or article is available at Montclair State. Interlibrary Loan (ILL) lets you borrow from other institutions for free.

How to request ILL:

  1. Search Montclair OneSearch—if an item says "Not Available," note the title.
  2. Go to the ILL portal (available via the library website).
  3. Fill out the request form (usually takes 3-7 business days).
  4. Check your email for delivery notifications (digital articles arrive instantly; physical books take longer).

Real-World Example: A literature student needing a rare 19th-century novel finds it’s only available at Rutgers University.

  • They submit an ILL request and receive a digital scan within 48 hours—no need to travel!

Strategy 6: Attend Library Workshops & Research Help Sessions

Montclair State Libraries offer free workshops on:

  • Citation management (Zotero, EndNote)
  • Advanced database searching
  • Writing a literature review
  • Using AI tools ethically in research

How to find them:

  • Check the Library Events Calendar on the website.
  • Sign up via email—workshops fill up fast!
  • Ask librarians for 1-on-1 help if you need personalized guidance.

Common Mistake: Waiting until the last minute to seek help. Fix: Attend early—librarians can save you weeks of frustration by teaching you efficient research techniques.

Strategy 7: Optimize Your Study Space with Library Resources

Montclair State Libraries provide more than just books—they offer study tools to boost productivity.

What’s available: ✅ Silent study rooms (for focused work) ✅ Group collaboration spaces (with whiteboards & tech) ✅ Printing, scanning, and 3D printing services ✅ Tech loan program (laptops, cameras, audio recorders) ✅ Quiet zones (for exams or deep work)

Pro Tip:

  • Ask about "Focus Hours"—some libraries have quiet periods to reduce distractions.

Strategy 8: Use the Library’s Digital Archives & Open Access Resources

Montclair State’s Digital Archives contain thousands of free, downloadable resources, including:

  • Historical NJ documents
  • Montclair State theses & dissertations
  • Open-access journals (no paywalls!)

How to access them:

  1. Visit the Digital Archives page on the library website.
  2. Use keywords (e.g., "Montclair State student life 1950s").
  3. Download PDFs for offline reading.

Real-World Example: A sociology student researching Montclair’s demographic shifts can find:

  • Old city directories listing residents by decade.
  • Newspaper archives from the 1970s-2000s.
  • Student newspapers showing cultural trends over time.

This eliminates the need for expensive commercial databases—saving both time and money.

Strategy 9: Learn How to Evaluate Sources Like a Pro

Not all sources are created equal. Montclair State Libraries teach students the "CRAAP Test" to evaluate sources critically.

What to check:

Criterion What to Look For
Currency Is the source up-to-date? (Check the publication date.)
Relevance Does it directly answer your research question?
Authority Who wrote it? (Look for academic credentials or institutional affiliation.)
Accuracy Are the facts verifiable? (Cross-check with other sources.)
Purpose Is it biased or objective? (Watch for agenda-driven content.)

Common Mistake: Relying only on Google Scholar without verifying credibility. Fix: Use Montclair’s LibGuides for discipline-specific evaluation tips.

Strategy 10: Take Advantage of Faculty & Librarian Collaborations

Many professors partner with librarians to create custom research guides for their courses.

How to use them:

  1. Check your course syllabus—some professors require using the library’s research guides.
  2. Ask your professor if they have a pre-made guide for your topic.
  3. Contact the subject librarian for your major—they specialize in your field!

Real-World Example: *A business student in Marketing 301 finds a custom research guide from their professor that includes:

  • Recommended databases (e.g., IBISWorld, Statista).
  • Key journals like Journal of Marketing Research.
  • Tips on analyzing case studies.

This cuts their research time in half compared to starting from scratch.
