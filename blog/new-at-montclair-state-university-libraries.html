Chapter 1: Understanding Montclair State University Libraries – Structure & Services

Montclair State University Libraries consist of three main campus libraries, each with unique strengths:

1. The Library at the Center for Academic & Student Excellence (CASE)

  • Location: 1 Normal Avenue, Montclair, NJ
  • Specialties:
    • General academic research (humanities, social sciences, STEM)
    • Study spaces (group rooms, silent study zones, tech-equipped pods)
    • Circulation services (books, laptops, calculators, and more)
    • 24/7 digital access via the Montclair State University Library Portal

2. The Science & Engineering Library (SEL)

  • Location: 1 Normal Avenue, Montclair, NJ (within the Science Building)
  • Specialties:
    • STEM-focused resources (biology, chemistry, physics, engineering)
    • Specialized databases (ScienceDirect, IEEE Xplore, PubMed)
    • Research assistance for lab-based projects
    • 3D printing & maker space access

3. The Music & Media Library

  • Location: 1 Normal Avenue, Montclair, NJ (within the Music Building)
  • Specialties:
    • Music scores, recordings, and sheet music
    • Film & media archives (documentaries, indie films, streaming resources)
    • Audio-visual equipment checkout (cameras, microphones, editing software)

Key Services Available Across All Libraries

  • Research Consultations (one-on-one help from librarians)
  • Interlibrary Loan (ILL) – Borrow books from other institutions
  • Digital Preservation & Open Access Support
  • Workshops & Training (research methods, citation tools, data management)
  • Virtual & Remote Access (for online students and off-campus users)

Pro Tip: *Bookmark the Montclair State University Library Portal—your gateway to all digital resources!*

Chapter 2: 10 Actionable Strategies to Master Montclair State University Libraries

Now that you know the layout and services, let’s explore practical strategies to make the most of your library experience.

Strategy 1: Start with the Library Portal – Your Digital Command Center

The Montclair State University Library Portal is your first stop for research. It consolidates:

  • All databases (JSTOR, ProQuest, EBSCOhost, and more)
  • E-books & journals (searchable by title, author, or keyword)
  • Your account dashboard (checked-out items, renewals, fines)
  • Research guides (subject-specific tutorials)

How to Use It Effectively:

  1. Log in with your Montclair State credentials for full access.
  2. Use advanced search filters (peer-reviewed, full-text, date range).
  3. Bookmark frequently used databases (e.g., Academic Search Complete for interdisciplinary research).
  4. Set up email alerts for new publications in your field.

Real-World Example: A graduate student in Environmental Science was struggling to find recent studies on climate policy in New Jersey. Instead of searching Google, they used the portal’s Advanced Search in GreenFILE and ScienceDirect, filtering for 2020–2024 publications. Within 30 minutes, they found five relevant peer-reviewed articles—something they couldn’t easily find on Google Scholar.

Strategy 2: Leverage Research Guides for Subject-Specific Help

Montclair State Libraries provides pre-made research guides for every major and discipline. These guides include:

  • Recommended databases for your field
  • Citation style guides (APA, MLA, Chicago, IEEE)
  • Tutorials on research methods
  • Key journals & books in your area

How to Find Them:

  • Go to the Library Portal → "Research Guides" tab.
  • Select your department or subject (e.g., "Business," "Psychology," "Computer Science").

Example: A Business major researching supply chain management found a guide listing ProQuest ABI/INFORM and Business Source Premier as the best databases. They also discovered a case study database with real-world corporate examples—something not available in general search engines.

Strategy 3: Use Interlibrary Loan (ILL) for Books Not in Montclair’s Collection

Not every book you need will be available at Montclair. Interlibrary Loan (ILL) lets you borrow from other universities in 3–10 business days.

How to Request an ILL:

  1. Search the Library Catalog for your book.
  2. If it’s not available, click "Request via Interlibrary Loan."
  3. Fill out the form with your Montclair email and preferred pickup location.
  4. Track your request in your ILL account.

Pro Tip: For urgent needs, check if the book is available at Rutgers, NJIT, or Princeton—they often have faster ILL turnaround times.

Common Mistake & Fix: ❌ Waiting too long to request an ILL because you "might find it later." ✅ Request early—some books take 2+ weeks to arrive.

Strategy 4: Attend Library Workshops & Research Help Sessions

Montclair State Libraries offers free workshops on:

  • Citation management (Zotero, EndNote, Mendeley)
  • Advanced database searching
  • Data analysis & visualization
  • Open Access publishing

How to Register:

  • Check the "Workshops & Events" section on the Library Portal.
  • Some are in-person, while others are virtual (great for online students).

Real-World Example: A PhD candidate in Education attended a "Advanced Google Scholar Search" workshop. They learned how to filter by date, relevance, and citation count, which helped them find 100+ high-impact studies in two hours—something they couldn’t achieve through random browsing.

Strategy 6: Access Digital Study Spaces & Tech Resources

Montclair Libraries offer more than just books—they provide:

  • Silent study rooms (for focused work)
  • Group study pods (with whiteboards & tech)
  • Laptop & calculator checkout (free for students)
  • 3D printers & scanning services (for STEM projects)

How to Reserve a Study Room:

  1. Go to the Library Portal → "Study Rooms."
  2. Select availability and book online.
  3. Some rooms require a Montclair ID for entry.

Example: *A Computer Science student needed a quiet space to debug code. They reserved a silent study room for 3 hours, which helped them complete a complex algorithm without distractions.

Strategy 7: Use Zotero or EndNote for Citation Management

Manually formatting citations is time-consuming and error-prone. Zotero (free) and EndNote (paid, but available via Montclair) automate citations and generate bibliographies in APA, MLA, Chicago, and more.

How to Set Up Zotero:

  1. Download Zotero from zotero.org.
  2. Install the Zotero Connector (browser extension).
  3. Save articles & books directly to your Zotero library.
  4. Drag & drop citations into your Word/Google Docs paper.

Real-World Example: *A History major was writing a 50-page thesis. Instead of manually citing 200+ sources, they used Zotero, which auto-filled citations and updated references in seconds. This saved them 10+ hours of work.

Strategy 8: Take Advantage of 24/7 Digital Access

Montclair Libraries provide round-the-clock access to:

  • E-books (search via Montclair’s catalog)
  • Digital journals (via JSTOR, ScienceDirect)
  • Online databases (no need to visit campus)

How to Access Off-Campus:

  1. Go to the Library Portal.
  2. Log in with your Montclair credentials.
  3. Search for e-books or articles—most are instantly available.

Pro Tip: Bookmark Montclair’s E-Resource Guide for quick access to all digital collections.

Strategy 9: Ask a Librarian – They’re Your Secret Weapon

Librarians at Montclair are highly trained research experts. Don’t hesitate to ask for help—they can: ✔ Teach you how to search databases efficiently ✔ Help you find primary sources (archives, government documents) ✔ Guide you through complex research methods ✔ Review your bibliography for accuracy

How to Get Help:

  • Chat with a Librarian (live help via the Library Portal)
  • Schedule a Research Appointment (15–60 minutes)
  • Visit the Research Help Desk (in-person at CASE Library)

Example: *A Political Science student was struggling to find primary election data from 2020. They emailed a librarian, who directed them to ICPSR (Inter-University Consortium for Political and Social Research), a goldmine of election datasets. Within 24 hours, they had the exact data they needed.*

Strategy 10: Explore Open Access & Digital Preservation

Montclair Libraries support Open Access (OA) research, meaning:

  • Free, public access to scholarly work
  • No paywalls for students & researchers
  • Increased visibility for your own work

How to Publish Open Access:

  1. Check if your journal is OA (use DOAJ – Directory of Open Access Journals).
  2. Use Montclair’s Institutional Repository (MARC) to share your research.
  3. Consider preprint servers like arXiv (for STEM) or SSRN (for social sciences).

Pro Tip: If you’re writing a thesis, ask your advisor if Montclair’s library can host it—this makes your work permanently accessible.

Chapter 3: Common Mistakes New Users Make (And How to Avoid Them)

Even experienced researchers make mistakes. Here are five pitfalls and how to steer clear of them.

Mistake 1: Relying Only on Google Scholar

Why It’s Bad:

  • No peer-review filter (you get preprints, blogs, and low-quality sources).
  • No Montclair-specific resources (you miss paid databases).
  • Poor search relevance (Google Scholar ranks by citation count, not always quality).

How to Fix It: ✅ Use Montclair’s portal first (better filters, full-text access). ✅ Combine Google Scholar with library databases (e.g., JSTOR + Google Scholar). ✅ Check "Peer-Reviewed" in database filters.

Mistake 2: Ignoring Interlibrary Loan (ILL) Deadlines

Why It’s Bad:

  • Books take 1–3 weeks to arrive.
  • Last-minute requests may not arrive in time for assignments.

How to Fix It: ✅ Request ILL books at least 2 weeks before due dates. ✅ Check ILL status weekly (some requests get delayed). ✅ Have a backup plan (e.g., find an e-book alternative).

Mistake 3: Not Using Subject-Specific Databases

Why It’s Bad:

  • General databases (Google, Wikipedia) lack depth.
  • You miss niche research in your field.

How to Fix It: ✅ Use the "Research Guides" to find best databases for your major. ✅ Ask a librarian which databases are most relevant. ✅ Try multiple databases (e.g., ScienceDirect + Google Scholar).

Mistake 4: Skipping Workshops & Tutorials

Why It’s Bad:

  • You reinvent the wheel (wasting time on inefficient search strategies).
  • You miss out on free expert training.

How to Fix It: ✅ Attend at least one workshop per semester (even if unrelated). ✅ **Watch library tutorials on YouTube or the Library Portal. ✅ **Follow Montclair’s Library Newsletter for upcoming events.
