New at Montclair State University Libraries: A Complete Guide to Maximizing Your Academic Success

Introduction: Why Montclair State University Libraries Are Your Secret Weapon for Success

Welcome to Calculator-Library-Portal, your go-to resource for navigating academic libraries with confidence! If you’re new to Montclair State University Libraries, you’re in for a treat. With over 1.2 million physical and digital items (as of 2023) and a $10+ million annual budget for resources, these libraries are designed to support every student—from freshmen to graduate researchers.

But here’s the catch: many students underutilize the libraries’ full potential. A 2022 survey by the Montclair State University Office of Institutional Research found that only 45% of undergraduates regularly use library databases, while 68% of graduate students rely on them for research. If you’re new, you might be missing out on exclusive databases, expert librarians, and study spaces that could boost your GPA, streamline your research, and even land you a better internship.

This guide will walk you through everything you need to know—from how to find the right books to how to leverage library workshops—so you can start using Montclair State University Libraries like a pro.

Chapter 1: Getting Started – Your First Steps at the Libraries

1.1 Locate the Libraries: Where Are They and How Do You Access Them?

Montclair State University has three main libraries, each serving different academic needs:

  • John J. Cali School of Music Library – Specializes in music scores, audio recordings, and sheet music.
  • University Library (Main Campus) – The largest, housing general academic resources, journals, and digital collections.
  • School of Business Library – Focuses on business, economics, and finance materials.

Pro Tip: Use the Library Locator Map to find the nearest library to your classes. If you’re on campus, the University Library is your best starting point.

1.2 Get Your Library Card & Access Online Resources

Before you can borrow books or access e-books and databases, you need a Montclair State University Library Card.

  • How to Get It:
    • Physical Card: Visit any library with your student ID (or Montclair OneCard).
    • Online Access: Your student email and password grant you 24/7 access to e-books, journals, and databases from anywhere.

Real-World Example: Sarah, a first-year biology major, struggled to find research papers on her topic. After registering for her library card, she discovered ScienceDirect and JSTOR—two databases she didn’t know existed. Within a week, she found five relevant studies that improved her paper’s quality.

1.3 Understand Library Hours & Special Access

Libraries have different hours depending on the day and semester:

  • Monday–Thursday: 8:00 AM – 12:00 AM
  • Friday: 8:00 AM – 6:00 PM
  • Saturday: 10:00 AM – 6:00 PM
  • Sunday: 12:00 PM – 12:00 AM

Late-Night Study Spaces:

  • The University Library’s 2nd Floor has quiet study rooms until 2:00 AM on weekdays.
  • Group Study Rooms (reservable online) are perfect for project collaborations.

Common Mistake: Assuming the library closes at 5 PM. Many students miss out on late-night study sessions because they don’t check the updated hours on the Library Website.

Chapter 2: Finding Books & Resources Like a Pro

2.1 How to Search the Library Catalog Effectively

The Montclair State University Libraries’ catalog is your first stop for finding books, e-books, and other materials. Here’s how to search like an expert:

Step 1: Use Advanced Search Filters

Instead of typing vague terms like "psychology," try:

  • "Cognitive Behavioral Therapy AND depression"
  • "Machine learning" NOT "deep learning" (to narrow results)

Step 2: Check Availability & Formats

  • Physical Books: Look for "Available" status.
  • E-Books: Filter by "Online Access" to find instant downloads.
  • Audiobooks & Videos: Use the "Format" dropdown to find streaming options.

Real-World Example: *James, a computer science student, needed a book on "cybersecurity ethics." He tried searching "cybersecurity" first, but got 1,200 results. After refining his search to "cybersecurity ethics", he found three relevant books, including an e-book available immediately.

Step 3: Use Subject Headings for Precision

If a book appears in search results, check its subject tags (e.g., "Business Ethics," "Cybersecurity"). Clicking these tags brings up more related books.

2.2 Borrowing Books & Managing Your Account

  • Loan Periods:

    • Undergraduates: 2 weeks (renewable for 2 more weeks if no one else requests it).
    • Graduate Students & Faculty: 4 weeks.
    • E-Books: No due dates—read anytime!

  • Fines & Overdue Policies:

    • $0.25 per day for late returns.
    • Holds: If a book is checked out, place a hold and pick it up at the circulation desk.

Pro Tip: Use the My Library Account to renew books online and check due dates.

Common Mistake: Ignoring hold notifications. Many students forget to pick up their books, leading to late fees. Set a phone reminder for when your hold is ready.

Chapter 3: Leveraging Digital Databases for Research

3.1 The Best Databases for Different Subjects

Montclair State provides hundreds of databases, but these are the most useful for students:

Subject Best Database Why It’s Useful
Business Business Source Premier Full-text journals, company reports, and market research.
Health Sciences CINAHL Nursing, medicine, and allied health research.
STEM ScienceDirect Peer-reviewed papers on engineering, physics, and computer science.
Humanities JSTOR Historical documents, literary criticism, and philosophy.
Law Westlaw Campus Legal cases, statutes, and law reviews (great for pre-law students).

Real-World Example: *Ana, a nursing student, needed clinical trial data for her capstone project. She searched PubMed (a free database) but found outdated studies. After her professor recommended CINAHL, she discovered five recent, peer-reviewed articles that directly supported her research.

3.2 How to Cite Sources Properly (Avoid Plagiarism!)

Montclair State requires proper citations in APA, MLA, or Chicago style. The library offers:

  • RefWorks – A free citation manager that auto-generates citations and formats bibliographies.
  • Zotero – Another open-source tool for organizing sources.

Step-by-Step Citation Guide:

  1. Find a source in a database (e.g., JSTOR).
  2. Click "Cite" (usually under the article).
  3. Copy the citation and paste it into RefWorks or Zotero.
  4. Export your bibliography in the required format.

Common Mistake: Manually copying citations from Google Scholar. This often leads to incorrect formatting and plagiarism risks. Always use official citation tools from the library.

Chapter 4: Study Spaces & Workshops – How to Get the Most Out of Them

4.1 Finding the Perfect Study Spot

Montclair State Libraries offer multiple study environments:

  • Quiet Zones (2nd Floor, University Library) – No talking allowed (great for exams).
  • Collaboration Spaces (Group Study Rooms) – Reservable online for group projects.
  • Tech Labs – Computers with specialized software (e.g., SPSS for statistics, Adobe Creative Suite).

Pro Tip: Use the Library Study Space Finder to book a spot in advance, especially during exam weeks.

4.2 Attending Library Workshops & Research Help

The library offers free workshops on:

  • Research methods
  • Citation management (RefWorks/Zotero)
  • Data analysis (SPSS, R, Python)

How to Sign Up:

  1. Check the Library Events Calendar.
  2. Register online (some workshops fill up fast!).

Real-World Example: *Michael, a sociology major, struggled with SPSS for his statistics project. After attending a library workshop on data analysis, he learned how to run regressions and interpret results. His final project earned an A, and he later used the same skills in his internship.

Common Mistake: Assuming workshops are only for grad students. Many undergrad workshops are just as valuable—don’t skip them!

Chapter 5: Special Collections & Unique Resources

5.1 Exploring Rare Books & Archives

Montclair State’s Special Collections include:

  • Historical newspapers (e.g., Montclair Times archives)
  • First editions of famous novels
  • Manuscripts from local authors

How to Access Them:

  • Visit the Special Collections Page.
  • Request materials in advance (some require appointment scheduling).

Real-World Example: *Emily, a history student, needed primary sources on the Civil Rights Movement. She visited Special Collections and found a 1964 letter from a local activist—exactly what her professor was looking for in her thesis.

5.2 Accessing Open Educational Resources (OER)

Montclair State supports Open Access (OER) materials, which are free, legally shareable textbooks and courses. Check out:

  • Montclair OER Portal
  • Khan Academy, MIT OpenCourseWare, and OpenStax

Why Use OER?

  • No copyright issues (unlike some online sources).
  • Often updated by experts in the field.

Common Mistake: Using Wikipedia as a primary source. While Wikipedia is useful for background info, it’s not a credible academic source. Always cross-check with peer-reviewed journals.

Chapter 6: Troubleshooting Common Library Issues

**6.1 Can’t Find a Book? Here’s What to Do

Problem: The book you need is checked out or missing. Solution:

  1. Place a hold on the book.
  2. Check if it’s available at another campus library (via Montclair State’s Interlibrary Loan).
  3. Request a scan of a chapter (if the book is not available digitally).

**6.2 Access Issues with Databases

Problem: You’re getting "Access Denied" errors. Solution:

  • Clear your browser cache (sometimes cookies block access).
  • Try a different browser (Chrome or Firefox work best).
  • Contact IT Support if the issue persists.

**6.3 Printing & Scanning Problems

Problem: Your print job didn’t go through. Solution:

  • Check your account balance (you can add funds online).
  • Verify the printer location (some are only in specific libraries).
  • Use the self-service kiosks if the printer is jammed.

Common Mistake: Assuming all printers accept the same payment method. Some require Montclair OneCard, while others take cash or credit.
