Chapter 1: Getting Started – Your First Steps at Montclair State Libraries

1.1 How to Access Your Library Account & Borrowing Privileges

Before you can check out books, you need to activate your library account. Here’s how:

  • Step 1: Log in to Montclair State’s Library Portal (libraries.montclair.edu) using your MSU username and password.
  • Step 2: Navigate to "My Account" (usually found in the top-right corner).
  • Step 3: Verify your student ID number and borrowing limits (undergraduates typically get 25 items at a time with a 28-day loan period for most books).
  • Step 4: Set up two-factor authentication (2FA) for extra security—this prevents unauthorized access to your account.

Pro Tip: Enable email alerts for due dates to avoid late fees. Montclair State Libraries charge $0.50 per day for overdue books, which can add up quickly.

1.2 Locating the Libraries on Campus

Montclair State has three main library locations, each serving different academic needs:

Library Name Location Best For
John J. Cali School of Music Library Music Building (Room 101) Music scores, audio recordings, and sheet music research.
Library & Learning Commons (LLC) Student Center (Main Campus) General research, study spaces, and tech support (printers, scanners).
University Library University Hall (Upper Level) Academic journals, databases, and specialized research collections.

Real-World Example: *A music major named Alex struggled to find sheet music for their senior recital. After visiting the Cali Music Library, they discovered a rare 19th-century collection of Chopin etudes that wasn’t listed in the online catalog. This led to a standout performance piece and a positive recommendation from their professor.

Chapter 2: The Hidden Gems – Advanced Tools & Services You Didn’t Know Existed

2.1 Interlibrary Loan (ILL) – Borrow Books from Other Universities

Montclair State Libraries don’t have everything—but they can get it for you through Interlibrary Loan (ILL).

  • How it works:
    • If a book isn’t available at MSU, request it via the ILL portal (libraries.montclair.edu/ill).
    • Most requests arrive in 3-5 business days (some take longer for rare items).
    • You can borrow books, journal articles, and even DVDs from other institutions.
  • Cost: Free for students (unlike some private universities that charge fees).

Common Mistake: Students assume ILL is slow and avoid using it. In reality, it’s often faster than waiting for a book to arrive via Amazon.

Pro Tip: For urgent requests, check the "Priority Request" option—some libraries process these within 24 hours.

2.2 Research Databases Beyond Google Scholar

Google Scholar is convenient, but Montclair State Libraries subscribes to premium databases that provide peer-reviewed, credible sources.

Database Name Best For How to Access
JSTOR Humanities, social sciences, and archival journals. Via libraries.montclair.edu/jstor
ProQuest Business, psychology, and education research. libraries.montclair.edu/proquest
ScienceDirect STEM research papers and preprints. libraries.montclair.edu/sciencedirect
EBSCOhost Nursing, education, and multidisciplinary articles. libraries.montclair.edu/ebsco

Real-World Example: *A nursing student named Priya needed clinical trial data for her capstone project. She searched Google Scholar but found mostly paywalled or outdated studies. After switching to EBSCOhost, she discovered a 2023 peer-reviewed study on diabetes management—exactly what her professor requested.

2.3 The "Ask a Librarian" Service – Free Expert Help

Montclair State Libraries offer one-on-one research consultations with librarians who specialize in different fields.

  • How to book an appointment:
    • Visit libraries.montclair.edu/ask.
    • Select your subject (e.g., "Business," "Psychology," "STEM").
    • Schedule a 30-minute virtual or in-person meeting.
  • What they can help with:
    • Narrowing down a thesis topic
    • Finding primary sources
    • Citing sources correctly (APA, MLA, Chicago)
    • Evaluating online sources for credibility

Pro Tip: Even if you’re confident in your research, a 10-minute chat can save you hours of frustration later.

Chapter 3: Study Smarter – Library Resources for Academic Success

3.1 The Learning Commons: More Than Just a Study Space

The Library & Learning Commons (LLC) isn’t just a place to sit—it’s a hub for academic support.

  • Services available:
    • Writing Center – Free editing help for essays and papers.
    • Math & Science Tutoring – One-on-one sessions with grad students.
    • Career Development Workshops – Resume reviews and interview prep.
    • Tech Support – Help with Zotero, EndNote, and other citation tools.

Common Mistake: *Students assume the LLC is only for freshmen. In reality, grad students and professors also use it for collaborative research sessions.

3.2 Zotero & EndNote: Free Citation Managers

Manually citing sources is time-consuming and error-prone. Montclair State Libraries recommend Zotero (free) and EndNote (paid but available for students).

  • How Zotero works:
    • Automatically saves PDFs from databases.
    • Generates citations in APA, MLA, Chicago, and more.
    • Syncs across devices (great for remote research).
  • How to get started:
    • Download Zotero from zotero.org.
    • Install the browser connector to drag-and-drop citations from JSTOR or Google Scholar.
    • Use the Word/Google Docs plugin to insert citations in seconds.

Real-World Example: *A history student named Jamie was one week away from submitting a 50-page thesis when they realized they had no organized citations. After setting up Zotero, they saved 10 hours and submitted a polished final draft with perfect formatting.

3.3 Open Access & Free Resources

Not all research needs to come from paid databases. Montclair State Libraries provide free, open-access alternatives:

Resource What It Offers How to Access
Google Scholar (with MSU filter) Free peer-reviewed articles (filter by "Montclair State University" for full-text). scholar.google.com
Directory of Open Access Journals (DOAJ) 15,000+ free, high-quality journals. doaj.org
Project MUSE Humanities & social sciences articles (some free, some require MSU login). muse.jhu.edu

Pro Tip: Use Google Scholar’s "All Versions" feature to find free PDFs of paywalled articles.

Chapter 4: Avoiding Common Pitfalls – Mistakes That Waste Time & Lower Grades

4.1 Mistake #1: Ignoring the Library’s Website for Updates

Problem: Many students only check the library homepage and miss new databases, workshops, or emergency closures.

Solution:

  • Bookmark the "Library News" section (libraries.montclair.edu/news).
  • Follow @MSULibraries on Twitter/X for real-time updates.
  • Check the "Events" calendar for research workshops (e.g., "How to Use JSTOR Like a Pro").

Real-World Example: *A business student named Carlos missed a free workshop on "Advanced Excel for Data Analysis" because he didn’t check the library’s social media. He later had to pay for an online course to learn the same skills.

4.2 Mistake #2: Overlooking Special Collections

Montclair State Libraries have rare books, archives, and historical documents that most students don’t know exist.

  • What’s available?
    • Newspaper archives (e.g., 19th-century NJ newspapers).
    • Manuscripts (e.g., letters from famous NJ authors).
    • Government documents (e.g., historical census records).
  • How to access them:
    • Visit the Special Collections & University Archives (libraries.montclair.edu/special).
    • Request appointments for delicate or fragile items.

Pro Tip: If you’re writing a history or literature paper, these collections can give you exclusive sources that no one else has used.

4.3 Mistake #3: Not Using the "Find It" Button

When you find a citation in a book or article, you might think you’re done—but not all links work.

  • What the "Find It" button does:
    • Checks if Montclair State has full access to the article.
    • Redirects you to the PDF (if available).
    • Links to interlibrary loan if the article isn’t available.
  • Where to find it:
    • In Google Scholar, click "Find It @ MSU" (if enabled).
    • In JSTOR or ProQuest, look for the "Full Text" or "PDF" button.

Common Mistake: Students copy-paste URLs from Google and assume they’ll work—they often don’t due to paywalls.

Chapter 5: FAQs About Montclair State University Libraries

Here are five of the most common questions students have, along with detailed answers optimized for SEO and schema markup.

❓ FAQ 1: How do I renew books online before they’re due?

Answer: You can renew books up to 24 hours before the due date via your library account.

Steps:

  1. Log in to libraries.montclair.edu.
  2. Go to "My Account" > "Checkouts."
  3. Select the book(s) you want to renew and click "Renew."
  4. If a book can’t be renewed, it may be on hold for another student—check the "Holds" tab.

⚠️ Warning: Some books (e.g., reference materials, DVDs) cannot be renewed.

❓ FAQ 2: Can I use Montclair State Libraries if I’m not a current student?

Answer: Yes! Alumni, faculty, and community members can access some resources with a library card.

Options:

  • Alumni: Apply for an alumni library card (libraries.montclair.edu/alumni) for limited borrowing privileges.
  • Faculty/Staff: Use your MSU email to access all databases and e-books.
  • Community Members: Visit the Public Access Desk for reference help (though borrowing is restricted).

Pro Tip: If you’re a local resident, check if your town library has a reciprocal agreement with Montclair State.

❓ FAQ 3: How do I find primary sources for my history paper?

Answer: Primary sources are original documents (letters, speeches, photographs) that provide firsthand evidence. Montclair State Libraries has multiple ways to find them:

Resource What It Contains How to Access
America’s News Historical newspapers (e.g., The New York Times from 1980s–present). libraries.montclair.edu/americasnews
NewspaperARCHIVE 1700s–2000s newspapers (including NJ local papers). libraries.montclair.edu/newspaperarchive
Special Collections Manuscripts, letters, and rare books (e.g., Mark Twain’s NJ writings). libraries.montclair.edu/special

Example Search:

  • If writing about Prohibition in NJ, search "Newark Evening News" + "1920s alcohol laws" in NewspaperARCHIVE.

❓ FAQ 4: What if I lose or damage a library book?

Answer: If a book is lost or damaged, you’ll need to pay a replacement fee.

Fees (as of 2024):

  • Lost book: $100–$300 (depends on value).
  • Damaged book: Repair cost (e.g., $20 for a torn cover).
