8 Essential Strategies to Succeed at Montclair State University Libraries

1. Master the Library Catalog: Beyond Google Search

Google is great, but Montclair State’s library catalog (Primo) is your one-stop shop for books, e-books, articles, and more.

How to Use It Effectively:

  • Use advanced search filters (e.g., "Available Online," "Peer-Reviewed," "Full Text").
  • Check "Get It" links—if an article isn’t available, the system will automatically request it via interlibrary loan.
  • Save searches & create alerts for new publications in your field.

Real-World Example: A psychology major researching sleep disorders could search "sleep disorders AND cognitive behavior therapy" in Primo, then filter by "Peer-Reviewed Journals" to find reliable, academic sources instead of mixing in general articles.

Common Mistake: Only searching by title or author—this misses relevant books and articles on related topics.

Fix: Use subject headings (e.g., "Sleep Disorders—Therapy") and keyword combinations for broader results.

2. Leverage Subject Guides for Research Success

Montclair State’s libraries offer subject-specific research guides—essentially curated toolkits for your major.

How to Find Them:

  • Visit the Research Guides page and browse by department (e.g., Business, Education, Computer Science).
  • Each guide includes:
    • Recommended databases (e.g., Business Source Premier for MBA students).
    • Citation styles (APA, MLA, Chicago).
    • Tutorials on finding primary sources (e.g., government documents for political science).

Real-World Example: A computer science student working on AI ethics could use the Computer Science Research Guide, which directs them to:

  • ACM Digital Library (for peer-reviewed papers).
  • Ethics in Technology databases (e.g., ScienceDirect).
  • Step-by-step guides on evaluating sources.

Common Mistake: Ignoring guides because they seem "too basic"—these are designed by librarians to save you time!

Fix: Bookmark your department’s guide and revisit it before major assignments.

3. Use Interlibrary Loan (ILL) for Articles & Books Not in the Collection

Not every resource is available at Montclair State—but Interlibrary Loan (ILL) makes it easy to borrow from other institutions.

How to Request:

  1. Search Primo for your needed item.
  2. Click "Get It" → "Request via Interlibrary Loan."
  3. Fill out the form (usually takes 3-5 business days).

Real-World Example: A graduate student researching 19th-century literature finds a rare book in a Princeton University catalog. Instead of traveling, they submit an ILL request, and the book arrives digitally or physically within a week.

Common Mistake: Assuming ILL is slow—while it takes time, most requests arrive within a week, and emergency requests can be expedited.

Fix: Start ILL requests early—don’t wait until the night before your paper is due!

4. Explore Digital & Open-Access Resources

Montclair State provides free access to thousands of e-books, journals, and datasets—no library card needed!

Key Digital Resources:

  • JSTOR (humanities & social sciences).
  • ScienceDirect (STEM & medical research).
  • Project MUSE (literature & philosophy).
  • Montclair State Digital Repository (open-access theses & research).

Real-World Example: A sociology student analyzing urban poverty could access:

  • Full-text reports from the U.S. Census Bureau (via Statista).
  • Peer-reviewed journal articles on gentrification in JSTOR.
  • Datasets from the Inter-University Consortium for Political and Social Research (ICPSR).

Common Mistake: Not checking for open-access versions—some articles are free online (e.g., via Google Scholar), but the library may have paid subscriptions to more recent issues.

Fix: Use Google Scholar’s "All Versions" link to find free PDFs before paying for access.

5. Attend Library Workshops & Get Research Help

Montclair State’s libraries offer free workshops on:

  • Citation management (Zotero, EndNote).
  • Data analysis (SPSS, R).
  • Academic integrity & avoiding plagiarism.

How to Access Them:

  • Check the Library Events Calendar.
  • Request one-on-one research consultations with librarians.

Real-World Example: A business student struggling with APA citations attends a Zotero workshop, learns how to automate references, and submits a perfectly formatted paper without manual formatting errors.

Common Mistake: Thinking workshops are only for freshmen—grad students and faculty also benefit from advanced research techniques.

Fix: Sign up for at least one workshop per semester—even if you’re an expert, you might learn a new database or tool.

6. Utilize Study & Collaboration Spaces

Montclair State’s libraries offer more than just books—they provide study environments tailored to different needs.

Types of Spaces:

  • Silent Study Rooms (for focused work).
  • Group Study Pods (with whiteboards & tech).
  • 3D Printing & Maker Labs (for STEM projects).
  • Accessible Study Areas (for students with disabilities).

Real-World Example: A group of engineering students needs to 3D print prototypes for a robotics project. They reserve a maker lab session, print their designs, and test-fit components in the same visit.

Common Mistake: Assuming all study spaces are the same—some are noisy, while others are strictly silent.

Fix: Check the Library Floor Plans and reserve spaces in advance (especially for group work).

7. Use Library Databases for Primary & Secondary Sources

Depending on your field, you’ll need different types of sources:

  • Primary sources (original documents, interviews, experiments).
  • Secondary sources (analyses, reviews, textbooks).

Best Databases by Discipline:

Field Key Databases Example Use Case
Business Business Source Premier, IBISWorld Market analysis reports
STEM ScienceDirect, IEEE Xplore Peer-reviewed research papers
Humanities JSTOR, Project MUSE Literary criticism
Social Sciences PsycINFO, Sociological Abstracts Psychological studies

Real-World Example: A history student researching the American Revolution could find:

  • Primary sources in America’s Historical Newspapers.
  • Secondary analysis in JSTOR’s "Revolutionary War" collection.

Common Mistake: Mixing primary and secondary sources without context—always cite them differently (e.g., "Primary Source: Declaration of Independence, 1776" vs. "Secondary Analysis: Smith, 2020").

Fix: Use the Citation Guide to format sources correctly.

8. Protect Your Work & Avoid Plagiarism

Montclair State’s libraries provide tools to ensure academic integrity:

  • Turnitin (for checking originality).
  • Plagiarism workshops (hosted by librarians).
  • Citation generators (Zotero, EasyBib).

Real-World Example: A student accidentally paraphrases a source poorly and gets flagged for unintentional plagiarism. They use Turnitin’s feedback to rewrite their paper properly and submit a revised version.

Common Mistake: Assuming "paraphrasing" means changing a few words—true paraphrasing requires rewriting in your own words while keeping the original meaning.

Fix: Use the Academic Integrity Guide and cite every source—even if it’s a common fact.

FAQ: Your Montclair State Library Questions Answered

Here are five of the most common questions new students have about Montclair State University Libraries, answered with schema markup for better SEO visibility.

1. How do I access e-books and digital resources from off-campus?

Answer: Montclair State provides off-campus access to all digital resources via your Montclair State username and password.

  • Step 1: Go to the Library Website.
  • Step 2: Click "Databases" or "E-Books."
  • Step 3: Log in with your Montclair State credentials (same as for Canvas or email).
  • Step 4: Browse or search for your needed resource.

Pro Tip: Bookmark the Off-Campus Access Guide for quick reference.

2. Can I request books from other libraries through Interlibrary Loan (ILL)?

Answer: Yes! Montclair State’s Interlibrary Loan (ILL) service allows you to borrow books and articles from other academic libraries nationwide.

  • Turnaround Time: 3-5 business days (emergency requests may take 24-48 hours).
  • How to Request:
    1. Search Primo for your needed item.
    2. Click "Get It" → "Request via Interlibrary Loan."
    3. Fill out the form and submit.
  • Cost: Free for students, faculty, and staff.

Important: Check your ILL account regularly—some requests may require additional information from you.

3. Are there any special study spaces for group projects?

Answer: Absolutely! Montclair State Libraries offer multiple group study spaces, including:

  • Group Study Pods (with whiteboards, tech, and seating for 4-6 people).
  • Collaboration Tables (in high-traffic areas like the Library & Information Center).
  • Reservable Rooms (for longer group sessions—book via the Library Calendar).

Best For: Business case studies, science experiments, or humanities discussions.

4. How do I find primary sources for my history or social science paper?

Answer: Primary sources are original documents (letters, speeches, laws, interviews, etc.). Montclair State provides multiple databases for them:

  • America’s Historical Newspapers (for 18th–20th century news).
  • Archives Unbound (for diaries, government records).
  • U.S. Congressional Documents (for laws and debates).
  • Montclair State Digital Repository (for local historical documents).

Tip: Use the History Research Guide for a step-by-step search strategy.

5. What if I can’t find a book or article in the library?

Answer: If a book isn’t available physically or digitally, try these steps:

  1. Check Primo → "Get It" → "Request via Interlibrary Loan."
  2. Search Google Scholar → Look for "All Versions" to find free PDFs.
  3. Ask a Librarian—they can help refine your search or suggest alternatives.
  4. Visit another NJ library (Montclair State has partnerships with Rutgers, NJIT, and others
