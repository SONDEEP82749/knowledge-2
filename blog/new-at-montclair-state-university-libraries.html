New at Montclair State University Libraries: A Complete Guide to Maximizing Your Research & Study Experience

Introduction: Why Montclair State University Libraries Are Your Academic Powerhouse

Welcome to Calculator-Library-Portal, your go-to resource for mastering academic research, study strategies, and library navigation—especially for new students at Montclair State University (MSU). With over 15,000 students enrolled annually (as of the 2023-2024 academic year) and a $12 million annual budget dedicated to library resources (per MSU’s 2023-2024 financial report), the Earl & Louise Delaney Library and School of Business Library are more than just book repositories—they’re gateways to success.

Whether you're a freshman navigating your first research paper, a graduate student diving into specialized databases, or a transfer student adjusting to a new academic ecosystem, this guide will help you leverage Montclair State’s library resources like a pro. From hidden study spots to advanced research techniques, we’ll cover everything you need to know to save time, boost your grades, and stay ahead in your coursework.

Why Montclair State University Libraries Stand Out

Before diving into strategies, let’s explore what makes MSU’s libraries unique and essential for your academic journey:

1. A Massive Digital & Physical Collection

Montclair State’s libraries house:

  • Over 500,000 physical volumes (books, journals, and archives)
  • Millions of e-books and e-journals (accessible 24/7 via OneSearch)
  • Special collections, including rare manuscripts and historical documents
  • Multimedia resources, from streaming films to audio recordings

Fun fact: The Delaney Library’s Special Collections includes original works by Langston Hughes, making it a treasure trove for literature and history students.

2. Cutting-Edge Technology & Study Spaces

  • Silent study floors with individual carrels (perfect for deep focus)
  • Group study rooms with whiteboards, projectors, and high-speed Wi-Fi
  • 3D printing labs (for engineering, design, and STEM students)
  • VR (Virtual Reality) stations (available for research and creative projects)

3. Expert Librarians Who Are Your Research Partners

Unlike traditional libraries, MSU’s librarians actively assist students in:

  • Crafting search strategies for complex topics
  • Evaluating sources for credibility
  • Navigating databases like JSTOR, ProQuest, and EBSCOhost
  • Teaching citation styles (APA, MLA, Chicago, and more)

Pro tip: Many librarians offer one-on-one research consultations—book one today if you’re struggling with a paper!

4. 24/7 Access & Remote Learning Support

With off-campus access to most digital resources, you can:

  • Download e-books for offline reading
  • Access databases from anywhere via MSU’s VPN
  • Get research help via libchat (instant messaging with librarians)

8 Actionable Strategies to Master Montclair State University Libraries

Now that you know why the libraries are invaluable, let’s explore how to use them effectively. These strategies are backed by academic research and student success stories from MSU.

Strategy 1: Start with OneSearch – Your Gateway to All Resources

What it is: OneSearch is MSU’s unified search tool that scans books, articles, videos, and more in one place.

How to use it effectively: ✅ Use advanced search filters (e.g., date range, source type, peer-reviewed only) ✅ Combine keywords with Boolean operators (AND, OR, NOT) for better results ✅ Check the "Full Text" filter to ensure you’re accessing free, readable content

Real-world example: A sociology student needed sources on "youth mental health in urban schools." Instead of searching broadly, they:

  1. Used quotes for exact phrases: "youth mental health" AND "urban schools"
  2. Filtered for "peer-reviewed journals" (2015-2024)
  3. Found 12 high-quality articles in JSTOR and PsycINFO

Common mistake: Only searching for books—many research papers require journal articles, which OneSearch also indexes.

Strategy 2: Leverage LibGuides for Subject-Specific Help

What it is: LibGuides are curated research guides created by MSU librarians for specific disciplines (e.g., Business, Education, Nursing).

How to use them: ✅ Check the "Best Databases" section for your major ✅ Look for "Citation Guides" (e.g., APA for psychology, MLA for literature) ✅ Use the "Recommended Websites" for credible online sources

Real-world example: An education major researching "differentiated instruction" found:

  • A LibGuide with top databases like ERIC and Education Source
  • A video tutorial on how to evaluate educational research
  • Recommended books from the Delaney Library’s education section

Common mistake: Ignoring LibGuides because they seem "too basic"—they’re actually the fastest way to find high-quality sources.

Strategy 3: Master Database Navigation (Beyond Google Scholar)

While Google Scholar is useful, MSU provides specialized databases that are more reliable and comprehensive.

Top databases for different fields:

Field Best Database Why It’s Useful
Business Business Source Premier Full-text journals, company reports
Health/Nursing CINAHL Peer-reviewed nursing & medical research
Psychology PsycINFO Largest psychology database
Literature JSTOR Historical & academic journals
STEM ScienceDirect Research papers & preprints

How to search effectively:

  • Use subject headings (found in database descriptions)
  • Limit by date (e.g., last 5 years for current research)
  • Check for PDF availability (not just abstracts)

Real-world example: A nursing student needed evidence-based practices for diabetes management. Instead of searching Google, they:

  1. Went to CINAHL (via OneSearch)
  2. Used the advanced search with:
    • "diabetes management" [MeSH term]
    • "evidence-based practice" [Text Word]
    • Date range: 2020-2024
  3. Found 15 relevant studies, including clinical trial results

Common mistake: Assuming all databases are the same—each has unique strengths based on your field.

Strategy 4: Use Zotero for Free & Powerful Citation Management

What it is: Zotero (free, open-source) helps you collect, organize, and cite sources automatically.

How to set it up:

  1. Download Zotero from zotero.org
  2. Create an account (syncs across devices)
  3. Install the browser extension (for easy source capture)
  4. Connect to MSU’s databases (via Zotero Connector)

Real-world example: A history student was writing a 15-page paper on the Cold War. Instead of manually citing:

  • They saved 40 sources from JSTOR and Google Scholar into Zotero
  • Used Zotero’s citation generator to format APA citations
  • Exported a bibliography in one click

Common mistake: Not backing up Zotero libraries—always sync with the cloud or export to PDF.

Strategy 5: Find Hidden Study Spaces & Quiet Zones

Montclair State’s libraries offer more than just books—they have strategic study environments to boost productivity.

Best study spots:

  • The "Quiet Zone" (Delaney Library, 2nd Floor) – No talking allowed, ideal for exams
  • The "Collaboration Lounge" (Business Library) – Whiteboards & comfy seating for group work
  • The "Silent Study Carrels" (Delaney, 3rd Floor) – Individual booths for deep focus
  • The "Tech Lab" (Delaney, 1st Floor) – Mac & PC stations with scanners & printers

Pro tip: Check the library’s floor maps (available online) to plan your study sessions based on your needs.

Common mistake: Assuming all study areas are the same—some are noisy, while others are strictly silent.

Strategy 6: Attend Library Workshops & Research Help Sessions

Montclair State’s librarians regularly host workshops on:

  • Advanced research techniques
  • Citation management (Zotero, EndNote)
  • Data analysis (SPSS, R)
  • Academic integrity & avoiding plagiarism

How to find them:

  • Check MSU’s Events Calendar (montclair.edu/events)
  • Look for Librarian-led sessions in Blackboard or Canvas
  • Email your subject librarian for personalized help

Real-world example: A biology major struggled with statistical analysis for their research paper. They:

  1. Attended a "SPSS Workshop" hosted by the library
  2. Learned how to run t-tests and ANOVA
  3. Got one-on-one help from a librarian to interpret results

Common mistake: Not attending workshops because they seem "too basic"—many cover advanced skills you won’t learn in class.

Strategy 7: Utilize Interlibrary Loan (ILL) for Books & Articles You Can’t Find

What it is: If MSU doesn’t have a book or article you need, Interlibrary Loan (ILL) lets you borrow it from another library.

How to request:

  1. Go to OneSearch and find the item
  2. Click "Request via Interlibrary Loan"
  3. Fill out the form (usually takes 3-7 business days)

Real-world example: A philosophy student needed a rare book on existentialism not in MSU’s collection. They:

  1. Requested it via ILL
  2. Received a digital scan within 5 days
  3. Used it for their seminar paper

Common mistake: Not checking ILL availability—sometimes, waiting is faster than struggling to find an alternative.

Strategy 8: Explore Special Collections for Unique Research Opportunities

Montclair State’s Special Collections hold rare manuscripts, archives, and historical documents that can elevate your research.

Notable collections:

  • Langston Hughes Papers (for literature & African American studies)
  • New Jersey Political Papers (for history & government students)
  • Women’s Rights Archives (for gender studies & sociology)

How to access them:

  1. Email the Special Collections Librarian (specoll@montclair.edu)
  2. Schedule an appointment (some materials require in-person viewing)
  3. Request digital scans for remote research

Real-world example: An English major was writing a thesis on Harlem Renaissance poetry. They:

  1. Contacted Special Collections about Langston Hughes’ papers
  2. Reviewed original drafts of his poems
  3. Used these primary sources to support their argument

Common mistake: Assuming Special Collections are only for "serious scholars"—even undergraduates can use them for unique research projects.

Strategy 10: Build Relationships with Your Subject Librarian

Every major at MSU has a dedicated subject librarian who can:

  • Help you find niche sources
  • Teach you advanced search techniques
  • Guide you through complex research

How to find yours:

  1. Go to MSU’s Librarian Directory
  2. Email them with your research topic
  3. Schedule a meeting (in-person or virtual)

Real-world example: A political science student was researching "climate policy in New Jersey". Their subject librarian:

  1. Recommended databases like PAIS Index
  2. Taught them how to find state government reports
  3. Helped them locate primary sources from NJ legislative archives

Common mistake: Not reaching out to librarians—they’re paid to help, and many students don’t take advantage.

Common Mistakes New Students Make (And How to Avoid Them)

Even the most motivated students can fall into pitfalls when using the library. Here’s how to steer clear of them:

❌ Mistake 1: Only Using Google for Research

Problem: Google doesn’t filter for credibility—you might end up with biased, outdated, or low-quality sources. Solution: ✔ Use OneSearch or subject databases first ✔ Check the "Peer-Reviewed" filter in databases ✔ Verify sources with librarian help

❌ Mistake 2: Ignoring the "Full Text" Option

Problem: Some database results only show abstracts, not the full article. Solution: ✔ Look for the "PDF" or "Full Text" link in search results ✔ Use Interlibrary Loan (ILL) if MSU doesn’t have the article ✔ Check if the journal is open-access (Google Scholar can help)

❌ Mistake 3: Not Taking Advantage of Librarian Help

Problem: Many students don’t know that librarians offer free research consultations. Solution: ✔ Email your subject librarian with your topic ✔ Attend research workshops ✔ **Visit the "Ask a Librarian"
