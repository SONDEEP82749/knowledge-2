Chapter 1: Getting Started – Your First Steps at Montclair State University Libraries

1.1 Understanding the Library’s Structure: Where to Find What You Need

Montclair State University Libraries consist of three main branches, each with a unique focus:

  1. The Library at the Center for Academic & Student Excellence (CASE)

    • Location: 1 Normal Avenue, Montclair, NJ
    • Specialty: General research, study spaces, and student services
    • Key Features:
      • 24/7 access (via keycard) to the Main Floor (research materials, computers, and group study rooms)
      • The Learning Commons – A hub for tutoring, writing workshops, and academic coaching
      • The Innovation Studio – For 3D printing, virtual reality, and digital fabrication projects

  2. The Library at the School of Business

    • Location: 1 Normal Avenue (Business Building)
    • Specialty: Business, economics, and entrepreneurship resources
    • Key Features:
      • Bloomberg Terminals – For real-time financial data (a must for business majors)
      • Specialized databases like IBISWorld, Mergent Online, and Statista
      • Career services integration – Resume reviews, mock interviews, and networking events

  3. The Library at the School of Education

    • Location: 1 Normal Avenue (Education Building)
    • Specialty: Education research, teaching resources, and K-12 materials
    • Key Features:
      • ERIC Database – The go-to for education research
      • Teacher resource collections – Books, lesson plans, and educational software
      • Collaboration with the School of Education for research grants and publishing support

Pro Tip: Use the Montclair State University Libraries’ website map to visualize each branch’s layout before your first visit. This saves time when you’re on campus!

1.2 Your First Visit: What to Bring and How to Navigate

Your first trip to the library should be organized and purposeful. Here’s what you’ll need:

✔ Your Montclair State University ID (for checkouts, printing, and accessing restricted areas) ✔ A charged laptop/tablet (or use library computers if needed) ✔ A list of your research topics (to avoid aimless browsing) ✔ Your library account credentials (username and password for OneSearch and Interlibrary Loan)

Actionable Tip: Before visiting, bookmark these essential links:

  • OneSearch – The library’s unified search tool
  • Library Catalog – For physical books and media
  • Interlibrary Loan (ILL) – Borrow books from other institutions

Real-World Example: Imagine you’re a freshman in an English Literature class and need primary sources for a paper on 19th-century American poetry. Instead of wandering the stacks, you:

  1. Search "19th century American poetry" in OneSearch
  2. Filter by "Full Text Online" to find digitized editions
  3. Use the "Citation Linker" to access PDFs from JSTOR or Project Gutenberg
  4. Visit the Special Collections (on the 3rd floor) to explore rare manuscripts

This structured approach cuts research time by 50% compared to random browsing.

Chapter 2: Mastering Digital Tools – How to Use Montclair State University Libraries Online

2.1 OneSearch: The Swiss Army Knife of Academic Research

OneSearch is the single most powerful tool at your disposal. It’s not just a search engine—it’s a meta-search platform that combines:

  • Books & eBooks (from the library catalog)
  • Journal articles (from databases like JSTOR and EBSCO)
  • Newspaper archives (ProQuest, Factiva)
  • Audio/visual materials (films, podcasts, datasets)

How to Use OneSearch Like a Pro:

✅ Use Advanced Search Filters

  • Instead of typing vague terms like "climate change," try:
    • "climate change AND policy" (for policy-focused results)
    • File type: "PDF" (to ensure full-text access)
    • Date range: "2010-2024" (for recent research)

✅ Leverage the "Citation Linker"

  • If you find a citation in a book or article but no full text, use the "Citation Linker" to check if Montclair has access.
  • Example: You find a great source in a textbook but can’t download it? The Citation Linker will tell you if it’s available via JSTOR, Google Scholar, or Interlibrary Loan.

✅ Save & Organize Your Results

  • Click "Add to Folder" to create a personal research folder.
  • Export citations to Zotero, EndNote, or MLA/APA formats for your bibliography.

Common Mistake & Fix: ❌ Mistake: Searching only in Google Scholar and missing Montclair’s premium databases. ✅ Fix: Always start with OneSearch, then supplement with Google Scholar for open-access papers.

2.2 Interlibrary Loan (ILL): Borrowing Books from Other Libraries

Not every book you need is at Montclair. Interlibrary Loan (ILL) lets you request items from thousands of institutions nationwide.

How to Request a Book via ILL:

  1. Search OneSearch for your book.
  2. If it says "Not Available at Montclair," click "Request via Interlibrary Loan."
  3. Fill out the form with:
    • Your Montclair username & password
    • Your contact email
    • The exact title & author
  4. Wait 3-10 business days (some books arrive faster via digital delivery).

Pro Tip: For urgent needs, check:

  • WorldCat – A global library catalog
  • HathiTrust – For digitized rare books

Real-World Example: A graduate student in History needs a first-edition copy of The Diary of Anne Frank for their thesis. Since Montclair doesn’t own it, they:

  1. Searches WorldCat and finds it at *Princeton University Library.
  2. Requests it via ILL and receives a digital scan within 5 days.
  3. Uses the text in their paper without leaving campus.

Chapter 3: Physical Resources – How to Use Books, Media, and Special Collections

3.1 Navigating the Library Stacks: Call Numbers & Dewey Decimal System

Montclair uses a modified Dewey Decimal System (for general collections) and Library of Congress (LC) Classification (for humanities/social sciences).

How to Find a Book:

  1. Search OneSearch for the title.
  2. Note the call number (e.g., 306.4 for sociology).
  3. Locate the section (e.g., 300s = Social Sciences).
  4. Find the exact shelf (e.g., 306.4 – 306.9).

Pro Tip: If a book is checked out, use:

  • "Hold Shelf" (if available)
  • Interlibrary Loan (if not at Montclair)

Common Mistake & Fix: ❌ Mistake: Assuming all books are in alphabetical order (they’re not—it’s by call number). ✅ Fix: Memorize the Dewey/LC sections for your major (e.g., 500s = Science, 600s = Technology).

3.2 EBooks & Audiobooks: Accessing Digital Collections

Montclair offers thousands of eBooks via:

  • Ebrary (now ProQuest Ebook Central)
  • Gale EBooks
  • OverDrive (for audiobooks)

How to Access:

  1. Go to Montclair’s EBook Collection.
  2. Search by title, author, or keyword.
  3. Download via OverDrive (for audiobooks) or read online (for eBooks).

Actionable Tip: 🔹 Use Zotero or Mendeley to save eBook citations automatically. 🔹 Check for PDF downloads in OneSearch before requesting ILL.

3.3 Special Collections & Archives: Uncovering Rare Materials

Montclair’s Special Collections (located on the 3rd floor) holds:

  • Historical documents (e.g., Civil Rights Movement papers)
  • Rare books (e.g., first editions of Hemingway)
  • Manuscripts & oral histories

How to Access:

  1. Request an appointment via Special Collections Contact.
  2. Search the catalog for specific items.
  3. Use digitized collections (available via Montclair Digital Collections).

Real-World Example: A History major researching New Jersey’s role in the Civil Rights Movement would:

  1. Search the Special Collections catalog for primary sources
  2. Find letters from NAACP leaders in the 1960s
  3. Use these in their thesis to provide unique, firsthand evidence
