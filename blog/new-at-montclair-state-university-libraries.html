Chapter 1: Understanding the Montclair State University Libraries System

Before you can use the library effectively, you need to understand its structure, services, and key features.

1.1 The Three Main Library Locations

Montclair State University has three primary library branches, each serving different academic needs:

  • John J. Cali School of Music Library – Specializes in musicology, sheet music, and audio recordings, ideal for music majors.
  • Library South (Main Campus) – The largest branch, housing general academic collections, journals, and research databases.
  • Library North (Science & Engineering) – Focuses on STEM subjects, with specialized science journals, lab equipment, and 3D printing resources.

Pro Tip: If you're a science or engineering student, start with Library North for your research needs. For humanities or social sciences, Library South is your best bet.

1.2 Key Services You Didn’t Know You Needed

Montclair State Libraries offer far more than just books. Here are some underutilized but game-changing services:

✅ Interlibrary Loan (ILL) – Borrow books and articles from other universities (usually within 3-5 business days). ✅ Research Consultations – One-on-one help from librarians to refine your search strategy. ✅ Study Rooms & Group Spaces – Book a quiet study room or a collaboration space for group projects. ✅ Printing, Scanning & Copying – Free black-and-white printing (color costs extra) and 24/7 scanning at Library South. ✅ Tech Lending – Borrow laptops, cameras, and audio recorders for free (with a valid ID). ✅ Workshops & Tutorials – Free sessions on research methods, citation tools, and data analysis.

Real-World Example: A graduate student in psychology needed a rare journal article not available at Montclair. Instead of panicking, they used Interlibrary Loan (ILL) and received the PDF within 48 hours—saving them from a last-minute research crisis.

Chapter 2: 10 Actionable Strategies to Master Montclair State Libraries

Now that you know the basics, let’s get practical. These 10 strategies will help you save time, find better sources, and avoid common mistakes.

Strategy 1: Use the Library Catalog Effectively (Beyond Google Search)

Many students default to Google when searching for books, but Montclair’s library catalog (OneSearch) is far more efficient.

How to Search Like a Pro:

  • Use Boolean operators (AND, OR, NOT) to narrow or broaden your search.
    • Example: "climate change AND policy" (finds only sources with both terms).
    • Example: "machine learning OR AI" (finds sources with either term).
  • Filter by format (books, e-books, articles, videos).
  • Check "Available Now" to find physical books on the shelf (no waitlist).

Common Mistake: Searching too broadly (e.g., just "psychology") leads to irrelevant results. Instead, try: "cognitive behavioral therapy AND anxiety disorders"

Strategy 3: Master Interlibrary Loan (ILL) to Avoid Research Bottlenecks

If Montclair doesn’t have the book or article you need, Interlibrary Loan (ILL) is your lifesaver.

How to Use ILL Like a Pro:

  1. Search OneSearch for your needed item.
  2. If it says "Not Available," click "Request via Interlibrary Loan."
  3. Fill out the form (usually takes 24-48 hours for articles, 3-5 days for books).
  4. Check your email for the digital copy (or wait for the physical book to arrive).

Common Mistake: Not requesting early enough. If you need a book for a paper due in 3 days, submit the ILL at least 5 days before to avoid delays.

Pro Tip: For urgent needs, contact the ILL department directly at ill@mail.montclair.edu—they may expedite your request.

Strategy 4: Use Zotero or RefWorks for Citation Management

Manually tracking citations is time-consuming and error-prone. Zotero (free) and RefWorks (paid, but available via Montclair) automate this process.

How to Set It Up:

  1. Download Zotero (browser extension) or sign up for RefWorks.
  2. Save citations as you find them in databases or books.
  3. Generate citations in APA, MLA, Chicago, or IEEE format with one click.
  4. Insert citations into Word/Google Docs automatically.

Real-World Example: *A history student was writing a 50-page thesis and manually citing 200+ sources. They switched to Zotero, which saved them 10+ hours of work and eliminated citation errors.

Strategy 5: Attend Library Workshops to Boost Your Research Skills

Montclair offers free workshops on:

  • Advanced Google Search Techniques
  • Evaluating Sources for Credibility
  • Using EndNote for Citations
  • Data Analysis with SPSS

How to Find Workshops:

  • Check the Library Events Calendar.
  • Sign up early—some fill up fast!

Common Mistake: Assuming you know everything—even experienced students benefit from refresher courses.

Strategy 6: Utilize Study Rooms & Group Spaces for Collaboration

Need a quiet place to study or a collaborative space for group projects? Montclair’s libraries offer:

  • Individual study carrels (first-come, first-served).
  • Group study rooms (bookable via Room Scheduling).
  • Silent study zones (Library South’s 3rd floor).

Pro Tip: If you’re working on a group project, book a study room in advance—they fill up quickly!

Strategy 7: Access E-Books & Digital Resources Anytime, Anywhere

Montclair provides thousands of e-books through Ebrary, JSTOR, and ProQuest. Here’s how to use them:

✅ Search OneSearch → Filter by "eBook." ✅ Download chapters (some allow 24-hour loans, others are permanent access). ✅ Use offline access (if available) for airplane or no-internet zones.

Common Mistake: Not checking e-book availability—some require Montclair login, while others are publicly accessible.

Strategy 8: Ask a Librarian—They’re Your Secret Weapon

Librarians at Montclair are highly trained research experts. Don’t hesitate to ask for help!

How to Get Help:

  • Chat with a Librarian (live chat on the library website).
  • Schedule a Research Consultation (link).
  • Visit the Reference Desk (Library South, 1st floor).

Real-World Example: *A computer science student was struggling to find peer-reviewed papers on quantum computing. They met with a librarian, who guided them to IEEE Xplore and ACM Digital Library—saving them weeks of wasted searching.

Strategy 9: Use the Library’s Tech Lending Program

Montclair’s Tech Lending program lets you borrow:

  • Laptops (for students without one).
  • Cameras & audio recorders (for projects).
  • Calculators & graphing tools (for STEM students).

How to Borrow:

  1. Show your student ID at the Circulation Desk.
  2. Borrow for 2 weeks (renewals available).
  3. Return on time to avoid late fees.

Pro Tip: If you’re working on a research project that requires special equipment, check if the library has it before buying your own.

Strategy 10: Explore Special Collections for Unique Research

Montclair’s Special Collections hold rare books, archives, and historical documents, perfect for:

  • Independent research projects.
  • Thesis work in history, literature, or art.
  • Creative writing inspiration.

How to Access Special Collections:

  • Visit Special Collections & University Archives.
  • Request materials in advance (some require appointment-only access).

Real-World Example: *A literature student was researching 19th-century American poetry. They found first-edition copies of Emily Dickinson’s work in Special Collections—unavailable anywhere else—which elevated their paper significantly.

Chapter 3: Common Mistakes New Students Make (And How to Avoid Them)

Even the most tech-savvy students make avoidable mistakes when using university libraries. Here are the biggest pitfalls and how to fix them.

Mistake #1: Relying Only on Google Scholar

Problem: Google Scholar doesn’t filter for Montclair’s subscriptions, so you may waste time on paywalled articles or low-quality sources.

Solution:

  • Use Montclair’s database links (e.g., JSTOR, ScienceDirect) first.
  • Check OneSearch for full-text availability.
  • If an article is paywalled, use Interlibrary Loan (ILL).

Mistake #2: Not Using Keywords Effectively

Problem: Searching "history" or "science" gives thousands of irrelevant results.

Solution:

  • Be specific (e.g., "Cold War diplomacy AND Nixon").
  • Use synonyms (e.g., "climate change OR global warming").
  • Exclude irrelevant terms (e.g., "psychology NOT sports").

Mistake #3: Ignoring the Library’s Website for Updates

Problem: Many students don’t check the library’s homepage for new databases, events, or policy changes.

Solution:

  • Bookmark the library website (www.montclair.edu/library).
  • Set up email alerts for new acquisitions.
  • Follow @MontclairLib on social media for quick updates.

Mistake #4: Not Taking Advantage of Study Spaces

Problem: Students overcrowd group rooms or don’t book study carrels in advance.

Solution:

  • Book study rooms at least 24 hours in advance.
  • Use silent floors (Library South’s 3rd floor) for focused study.
  • Avoid peak hours (1-3 PM) if you need quiet.

Mistake #5: Failing to Use Citation Tools Early

Problem: Students wait until the last minute to organize citations, leading to disorganized papers and plagiarism risks.

Solution:

  • Start citing from Day 1 of research.
  • Use Zotero or RefWorks to auto-format citations.
  • Double-check citations before submitting.
