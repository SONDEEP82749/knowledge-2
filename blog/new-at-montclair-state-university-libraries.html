Chapter 1: Getting Started – Your First Steps at Montclair State University Libraries

1.1 How to Access the Library System

Montclair State University Libraries consist of four main locations:

  • Library & Information Center (LIB) – The primary campus hub with extensive collections.
  • Science & Engineering Library (SEL) – Specialized resources for STEM fields.
  • Music Library – Dedicated to music research and performance materials.
  • Special Collections & University Archives – Rare books, historical documents, and institutional records.

Actionable Tip: Bookmark the Montclair State University Libraries website and download the LibApps mobile app for 24/7 access to your account, checkouts, and alerts.

1.2 Your Library Card & Account Setup

Every enrolled student receives a free library card upon registration. To activate it:

  1. Visit the LIB front desk with your Montclair State ID.
  2. Set up your account via LibApps to track checkouts, renewals, and fines.
  3. Enable two-factor authentication (2FA) for secure access to digital resources.

Real-World Example: Sophia, a first-year biology major, struggled to locate her reserved textbooks. After setting up her LibApps account, she discovered she could request holds on books and receive text alerts when they were ready for pickup—saving her from last-minute library runs.

1.3 Understanding Library Hours & Special Access

Library hours vary by location:

  • LIB & SEL: Open 8 AM–12 AM (Mon–Thurs), 8 AM–10 PM (Fri), 10 AM–10 PM (Sat), 12 PM–12 AM (Sun) during the semester.
  • Music Library: Open 9 AM–9 PM (Mon–Fri), 10 AM–5 PM (Sat–Sun).
  • Special Collections: Requires advance appointment for rare materials.

Pro Tip: Use the Library Hours Calendar to plan your study sessions efficiently.

Chapter 2: Finding & Accessing Books & Journals

2.1 Searching the Catalog: Beyond Google

Montclair State’s online catalog (LibCat) is your gateway to millions of books, e-books, and multimedia. To maximize your search:

  • Use advanced filters (e.g., "Available Online," "Peer-Reviewed Journals").
  • Leverage subject headings (e.g., instead of searching "climate change," try "climate change mitigation").
  • Check the "Request" option for books not available at your preferred location.

Actionable Strategy: If a book isn’t at Montclair, use Interlibrary Loan (ILL) to borrow it from another institution within 3–5 business days.

2.2 Navigating E-Books & Digital Collections

Montclair offers thousands of e-books via platforms like:

  • Ebrary (now ProQuest Ebook Central)
  • JSTOR
  • Project MUSE

Example: James, a history PhD candidate, needed a rare 19th-century novel for his dissertation. Instead of searching physical shelves, he used JSTOR’s full-text search to find a digitized version in minutes.

2.3 Accessing Journals & Articles

For academic research, databases are your best friend. Key resources include:

  • JSTOR (humanities & social sciences)
  • ScienceDirect (STEM fields)
  • Google Scholar (cross-disciplinary searches)

Common Mistake: Assuming all articles are free—many require Montclair State’s VPN or library login for full access.

How to Avoid It:

  • Enable the Montclair State proxy in Google Scholar (Instructions).
  • Use the "Find It @ Montclair" button in databases to check for full-text availability.

Chapter 3: Specialized Research Tools & Workshops

3.1 Data & Statistics Resources

For research requiring quantitative analysis, Montclair provides:

  • Statista (market research & statistics)
  • ICPSR (social science data)
  • RStudio & SPSS (statistical software access)

Tip: Attend the Library’s Data Literacy Workshop (offered biweekly) to learn how to clean, analyze, and visualize data effectively.

3.2 Citation & Writing Support

Citing sources correctly is critical to academic integrity. Montclair’s Writing Center and Citation Guides help with:

  • APA, MLA, Chicago, and IEEE formats.
  • Zotero & EndNote (reference managers).

Real-World Example: *Ana, a nursing student, struggled with APA citations in her capstone project. She used Montclair’s APA Guide and Zotero’s auto-citation tool, reducing her formatting errors by 90%.*

3.3 Research Consultations & Expert Help

Librarians are not just bookkeepers—they’re research partners. Schedule a one-on-one consultation via:

  • Library Research Help
  • Chat with a Librarian (available 24/7 via LibApps).

Pro Tip: Ask for help with narrowing topics, evaluating sources, or finding primary research—librarians specialize in these skills.

Chapter 4: Technology & Digital Tools at Your Fingertips

4.1 Printing, Scanning, & Copying

Montclair libraries offer:

  • Photocopying (15¢ per page)

4.2 Study Spaces & Group Work Areas

Need a quiet corner or a collaborative table?

  • SEL Group Rooms – Bookable for presentations.
  • 24/7 Study Lounge (LIB Basement) – Open late for night owls.

Common Mistake: Assuming all spaces are first-come, first-served—some require advance booking via LibApps.

How to Avoid It:

  • Check the Study Space Guide for availability.
  • Reserve a room if you need a private space for group work.

4.3 Virtual & Remote Access

Montclair students can access resources anytime, anywhere:

  • Off-campus VPN for secure database access.
  • LibGuides (subject-specific research guides).
  • Digital Exhibits (virtual archives & special collections).

Example: During the pandemic, remote access saved students like Priya from missing critical research. She used Montclair’s VPN and LibGuides to complete her thesis from home.

Chapter 5: Avoiding Common Library Pitfalls

5.1 Overlooking Renewal Policies

  • Books can be renewed (unless another user requests them).
  • Fines accrue at $0.25/day for late returns. Solution: Set automatic renewal reminders in LibApps.

5.2 Not Using Interlibrary Loan (ILL) Early

ILL requests take 3–5 days. If you need a book now, check:

  • Montclair’s e-book collections first.
  • Nearby university libraries (e.g., Rutgers, NJIT) for faster access.

5.3 Ignoring Librarian Workshops

Many students skip research workshops—a huge mistake. These sessions cover:

  • Advanced database searches
  • Evaluating sources for credibility
  • Using citation managers

Tip: Mark your calendar for the Fall Research Bootcamp (early September) to start strong.

5.4 Forgetting About Digital Preservation

Some journals remove free access after a set period. To save your work:

  • Download PDFs of key articles.
  • **Use Zotero or Mendeley to store full-text copies.

Chapter 6: FAQs About Montclair State University Libraries

Here are five frequently asked questions with detailed answers to help you navigate the library system smoothly.

❓ How do I find a book if Montclair doesn’t have it?

Answer: If a book isn’t available at Montclair, use Interlibrary Loan (ILL). Here’s how:

  1. Search the LibCat and click "Request via Interlibrary Loan."
  2. Fill out the form with your Montclair email and preferred pickup location.
  3. Most requests arrive within 3–5 business days (some may take longer for rare items).
  4. Check your email for updates—you’ll receive a notification when it’s ready.

Pro Tip: Request early—especially for final papers or presentations!

❓ Can I use Montclair’s databases off-campus?

Answer: Yes! To access databases from home:

  1. Enable the Montclair State VPN (Download Here).
  2. Log in with your Montclair credentials (same as MyMontclair).
  3. Use the "Off-Campus Access" link in any database (e.g., JSTOR, ScienceDirect).
  4. If prompted, select "Montclair State University" as your institution.

Alternative: Some databases (like Google Scholar) can be set to automatically redirect to Montclair’s full-text versions via the "Find It @ Montclair" button.

❓ How do I cite a source I found in a library database?

Answer: Citation formats vary by discipline. Montclair provides subject-specific guides:

  • APA (Psychology, Education, Social Sciences) → APA Guide
  • MLA (Humanities, Literature) → MLA Guide
  • Chicago (History, Business) → Chicago Guide

Using Citation Managers:

  • Zotero (free, browser-based) → Automatically formats citations.
  • EndNote (paid, but available via Montclair) → Best for large bibliographies.

Example Citation (APA): Smith, J. (2022). The impact of climate change on coastal ecosystems. Journal of Environmental Science, 45(3), 123-145. https://doi.org/xxxx

❓ What if I lose my Montclair State ID card?

Answer: If your Montclair ID is lost or stolen:

  1. Report it immediately to the Montclair State Police (Contact Info).
  2. Replace it online via MyMontclair (under "Student Services").
  3. Cost: ~$10 for replacement.
  4. Temporary access: You can still use LibApps and VPN with your credentials, but you’ll need the physical card for printing, scanning, or checking out books.

Tip: Enable two-factor authentication (2FA) on your account to prevent unauthorized access.*

❓ Are there any quiet study areas for exams?

Answer: Absolutely! Montclair offers designated silent study zones:

  • LIB 2nd Floor (North Wing) – No talking allowed (great for finals).
  • SEL (Science & Engineering Library) – Whisper zones for focused work.
  • 24/7 Study Lounge (LIB Basement) – Open late nights for last-minute cramming.

Pro Tip:

  • Arrive early during peak exam periods (Dec & May).
  • Bring noise-canceling headphones if you’re sensitive to distractions.
