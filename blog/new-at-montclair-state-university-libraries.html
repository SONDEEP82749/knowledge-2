Chapter 1: Understanding Montclair State University Libraries – A Quick Overview

Montclair State University Libraries consist of three main branches, each serving different academic needs:

1. The Library at the Center for Academic Success (CAS)

  • Location: Center for Academic Success (CAS) Building
  • Specialty: General research, undergraduate support, and study spaces
  • Key Features:
    • 24/7 study rooms (reservable via the library portal)
    • Computer labs with specialized software (e.g., SPSS, MATLAB, Adobe Creative Suite)
    • Tutoring & writing centers (often integrated with library services)
    • High-demand textbooks & reserves (check out for short-term use)

2. The Library at the School of Business (SBL)

  • Location: School of Business Building
  • Specialty: Business, economics, and finance research
  • Key Features:
    • Bloomberg Terminal access (for real-time financial data—highly valuable for business majors)
    • Specialized databases like IBISWorld, Mergent, and Statista
    • Case study collections for business simulations

3. The Library at the School of Education (SEL)

  • Location: School of Education Building
  • Specialty: Education research, teaching resources, and K-12 materials
  • Key Features:
    • ERIC (Education Resources Information Center) database (essential for education papers)
    • Children’s & young adult literature collections
    • Curriculum development tools (e.g., lesson plan templates)

4. The Digital & Special Collections (Online & Physical)

  • Location: Accessible via the library website
  • Specialty: Rare books, archives, and digital humanities
  • Key Features:
    • Montclair State Digital Repository (open-access theses, dissertations, and faculty research)
    • Newspaper archives (e.g., The Montclair Times historical issues)
    • Primary source collections (e.g., Civil Rights Movement documents)

Chapter 2: 8 Actionable Strategies to Master Montclair State University Libraries

Now that you know the layout, let’s dive into practical strategies to make the most of these resources—starting today.

Strategy 1: Start with the Library’s Search Portal (Not Google!)

Why? Google is not optimized for academic research—it mixes unreliable sources with credible ones. Montclair State’s library search portal (accessible via montclair.edu/library) filters for peer-reviewed journals, books, and credible databases.

How to Use It:

  1. Go to the library homepage and click "Search" (or use the Google-like search bar).
  2. Refine your search using:
    • Advanced Search (for complex queries)
    • Filters (e.g., "Peer-Reviewed," "Books," "Articles," "Date Range") . Example Search:
    • Instead of searching "climate change effects" (too broad), try:
      • "climate change AND agricultural productivity AND New Jersey"
      • Then apply filters: "Last 5 Years" + "Peer-Reviewed"

Real-World Example: *A graduate student in Environmental Science was struggling to find recent studies on local air pollution in Essex County. Instead of Googling, they used the library’s portal with the keywords "Essex County air quality 2023-2024" and filtered for "New Jersey"—resulting in 12 relevant peer-reviewed articles in minutes.*

Pro Tip:

  • Use Boolean operators (AND, OR, NOT) to narrow or expand your search.
    • "Renewable energy AND solar panels NOT wind turbines" (focuses only on solar)

Strategy 2: Leverage Subject Guides for Your Major

Montclair State Libraries provides subject-specific research guides—essentially curated roadmaps for your field of study.

How to Find Them:

  1. Go to the library homepage.
  2. Click "Research Guides" (usually under "Help" or "Tools").
  3. Select your major or discipline (e.g., "Business," "Psychology," "Computer Science").

What You’ll Find:

  • Recommended databases (e.g., PsycINFO for psychology, IEEE Xplore for engineering)
  • Key journals & books in your field
  • Citation guides & APA/MLA templates
  • Tutorials on using library tools

Real-World Example: A Computer Science major was assigned a project on machine learning algorithms. Instead of guessing which databases to use, they checked the Computer Science Research Guide, which listed ACM Digital Library and IEEE Xplore as top resources. They found three relevant papers in the first 10 minutes.

Common Mistake & Fix: ❌ Mistake: Ignoring subject guides and relying on random Google searches. ✅ Fix: Bookmark your major’s guide and revisit it before every research project.

3. Use Interlibrary Loan (ILL) for Books & Articles Not in Montclair’s Collection

Montclair State Libraries doesn’t have every book or article—but you don’t have to miss out.

How It Works:

  1. Search the library catalog for your needed item.
  2. If it says "Not Available," click "Request via Interlibrary Loan."
  3. Fill out a simple form (usually takes 3-7 business days).
  4. Receive a PDF (articles) or physical book via email or mail.

Real-World Example: *A History student needed a rare 19th-century newspaper article on the Transcontinental Railroad. Montclair didn’t have it, but through Interlibrary Loan (ILL), they received a digital scan within 5 days from a nearby university’s archives.*

Pro Tip:

  • Check the ILL status regularly—some requests get delayed.
  • For urgent needs, contact the Reference Desk (via chat or email) to expedite.

Best Practices:

Real-World Example: *A group of Business majors needed a private space to work on a presentation. They reserved a 4-person study room in the School of Business Library—saving them from noisy café distractions and ensuring undisturbed collaboration.

Chapter 3: Common Mistakes New Students Make (And How to Avoid Them)

Even the most motivated students make these avoidable errors—here’s how to skip the learning curve.

Mistake #1: Not Using the "Full-Text" Filter

Problem: You find a great article, but when you click "PDF," it says "Not Available." Why? Some databases only show abstracts unless you filter for "Full Text."

Fix:

  • Always check the "Full Text" or "PDF" option before assuming an article is unavailable.
  • If it’s not available, use Interlibrary Loan (ILL).

Mistake #2: Ignoring the "Citation" Button

Problem: You find a perfect source, but when you copy-paste the URL, it’s not in APA/MLA format. Why? Library databases auto-generate citations, but students don’t use them.

Fix:

  • Always click "Cite" (usually in the article record) to get a properly formatted citation.
  • Double-check against Purdue OWL (owl.purdue.edu) if needed.

Mistake #3: Overlooking the "Reserves" Section

Problem: You need a textbook for an exam, but it’s not in stock. Why? Professors often place required books on reserve—meaning you can check them out for 2-4 hours.

Fix:

  • Search the library catalog for "Course Reserves."
  • Bring your syllabus—librarians can help you find exactly what your professor assigned.

Mistake #4: Not Using "Save" or "Favorites" in Databases

Problem: You spend hours finding great sources, but when you close your browser, they’re gone. Why? Students don’t bookmark or save their research.

Fix:

  • Most databases allow you to "Save" or "Add to Favorites."
  • Create a folder in Google Drive or OneNote for your research.
