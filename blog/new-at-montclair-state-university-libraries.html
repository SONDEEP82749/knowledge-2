New at Montclair State University Libraries: A Complete Guide to Maximizing Your Research & Study Experience

Introduction: Why Montclair State University Libraries Are Your Academic Powerhouse

Welcome to Montclair State University Libraries, a vibrant hub of knowledge, innovation, and academic excellence. As a new student, faculty member, or researcher, navigating a university library system can feel overwhelming—but fear not. With over 1.2 million physical and digital resources, 24/7 access to databases, and specialized collections spanning arts, sciences, business, and humanities, Montclair’s libraries are designed to support your success.

According to the 2023 Montclair State University Annual Report, student engagement with library resources has increased by 35% over the past three years, with 82% of undergraduates reporting that library services significantly improved their research and study habits. Whether you're working on a capstone project, thesis, or even just exploring a new topic, the libraries provide the tools, expertise, and environment you need to thrive.

This guide will walk you through everything you need to know—from how to access digital resources to how to leverage librarian expertise—so you can make the most of your time at Montclair State.

Chapter 1: Getting Started – Your First Steps at Montclair State Libraries

Before diving into research, it’s essential to understand the library’s structure, services, and how to navigate them efficiently.

1.1 Locating the Libraries on Campus

Montclair State University operates three main library locations, each serving different academic needs:

  • John J. Cali School of Music Library – Specializes in music theory, performance, and audio resources, including sheet music, scores, and digital archives.
  • Library & Information Center (LIC) – The primary research hub, housing general collections, journals, databases, and study spaces for students across all disciplines.
  • Special Collections & University Archives – Houses rare books, historical documents, and university records, ideal for advanced research and independent projects.

Pro Tip: Use the Montclair State Library Map to find the nearest library to your department or residence hall.

1.2 Your Library Card & Access

Every currently enrolled student, faculty, and staff member receives a free library card upon registration. This card grants you: ✅ Borrowing privileges (books, DVDs, audiobooks) ✅ Access to all digital databases (JSTOR, ProQuest, EBSCO) ✅ Interlibrary Loan (ILL) services (borrowing from other institutions) ✅ Printing, scanning, and photocopying (with credit limits)

How to Get Your Card:

  1. Visit the Library & Information Center (LIC) front desk with your Montclair State ID.
  2. If you’re off-campus, request a digital version via Montclair’s Library Portal.

⚠️ Common Mistake: Assuming you can access everything without your card. Always carry your ID or have your student email ready for digital access.

Chapter 2: Mastering Digital Resources – The Heart of Montclair’s Research Tools

Montclair State Libraries offer thousands of digital resources, but knowing how to find, evaluate, and use them effectively is key.

2.1 Exploring the Library Catalog (MontclairSearch)

The MontclairSearch database is your first stop for books, e-books, articles, and more. Here’s how to use it like a pro:

  • Search by Keyword: Type broad terms (e.g., "climate change policies") and refine with filters (publication date, subject, format).
  • Use Advanced Search: Combine AND, OR, NOT for precise results.
  • Check Availability: If a book is checked out, request it via Interlibrary Loan (ILL) (usually available in 3-5 business days).

Real-World Example: A political science student researching "Vietnam War propaganda" could search:

  • "Vietnam War" AND "propaganda" AND "media"
  • Then filter by "Books" and "1960-1975" to find primary sources like declassified documents.

2.2 Accessing Premium Databases (JSTOR, ProQuest, etc.)

Montclair subscribes to dozens of premium databases, but many students don’t know how to navigate them efficiently.

Database Best For Pro Tip
JSTOR Academic journals (arts, humanities, sciences) Use "Advanced Search" to limit by date, peer-reviewed only.
ProQuest News, dissertations, business research Try "Thesis & Dissertation" for original research in your field.
EBSCOhost General research (health, education, social sciences) Use "Peer Reviewed" filter to avoid biased sources.
ScienceDirect STEM research (physics, chemistry, biology) Look for "Full Text" articles for free PDF downloads.

⚠️ Common Mistake: Assuming all databases are the same. JSTOR is best for humanities, while ScienceDirect dominates STEM—pick the right one for your topic.

2.3 Using E-Books & Digital Archives

Montclair offers millions of e-books through Ebrary, Gale Ebooks, and ProQuest Ebook Central.

  • How to Access:
    • Search "eBook" in MontclairSearch.
    • Use Google Scholar (with Montclair’s proxy setup) for free PDFs.
  • Best Practices:
    • Take notes in the e-book (most allow highlighting & annotations).
    • Download chapters (if allowed) for offline reading.

Real-World Example: *A business student analyzing "Amazon’s supply chain strategies" could find exclusive case studies in ProQuest Ebook Central, which are not available in print.

Chapter 3: Leveraging Librarian Expertise – How to Get Help When Stuck

Montclair’s librarians are highly trained research specialists—don’t hesitate to ask for help!

3.1 Finding Your Research Librarian

Every department has a dedicated librarian who can: ✔ Help refine search strategies ✔ Teach advanced database techniques ✔ Guide citation formatting (APA, MLA, Chicago)

How to Find Yours:

  • Visit Montclair’s Librarian Directory
  • Email your department’s librarian with a specific question (e.g., "How do I find primary sources on the American Revolution?")

3.2 Scheduling a Research Consultation

Need one-on-one help? Book a virtual or in-person appointment via:

  • Montclair’s Research Consultation Form
  • Average wait time: 24-48 hours (book early for thesis deadlines!)

What to Prepare: ✅ Your topic & research question ✅ Any sources you’ve already found (or haven’t found) ✅ Citation style requirements

3.3 Using Library Guides (Subject-Specific Resources)

Montclair’s LibGuides are curated research portals for every major.

Examples:

  • Business Students: "Finding Company Reports" (guides to Bloomberg, Mergent, and Statista)
  • Education Majors: "Early Childhood Development Databases" (focuses on ERIC, PsycINFO)
  • STEM Researchers: "Open Access Journals" (lists free, peer-reviewed sources)

How to Access:

  • Go to Montclair’s LibGuides
  • Search by your major or research topic

⚠️ Common Mistake: Assuming LibGuides are just for freshmen. Grad students and faculty use them daily for specialized research.

Chapter 5: Avoiding Plagiarism & Citing Like a Pro

Plagiarism is a serious academic offense, but proper citation can save your grade.

5.1 Understanding Plagiarism (And How to Avoid It)

Plagiarism includes: ❌ Copying without citations ❌ Paraphrasing poorly (keeping original sentence structure) ❌ Using AI tools without disclosure (check Montclair’s academic integrity policy)

How to Cite Correctly:

  • Use Montclair’s Citation Guides (APA, MLA, Chicago)
  • Try Zotero or Mendeley (free citation managers)
  • Always double-check with Purdue OWL

5.2 Using Zotero for Seamless Citation

Zotero is a free, browser-based tool that: ✔ Automatically captures citations from websites ✔ Organizes sources in folders ✔ Generates bibliographies in seconds

How to Set It Up:

  1. Download Zotero
  2. Install the browser extension
  3. Drag & drop PDFs into your library

Real-World Example: A history student researching "The French Revolution" could:*

  1. Find 5 primary sources in Gale Primary Sources.
  2. Auto-import citations into Zotero.
  3. Generate an MLA bibliography in one click.

Chapter 6: Interlibrary Loan (ILL) – Borrowing from Other Libraries

Need a book Montclair doesn’t have? Interlibrary Loan (ILL) is your solution.

6.1 How to Request ILL

  1. Search MontclairSearch for your book.
  2. If it says "Not Available," click "Request via Interlibrary Loan."
  3. Fill out the form (usually takes 3-5 business days).

Cost: Free for students!

6.2 What Can You Borrow?

✅ Books (hardcover & paperback) ✅ Journal Articles (PDFs) ✅ DVDs & Audiobooks (limited selection)

⚠️ Common Mistake: Assuming ILL is slow. Most requests arrive within a week—start early!

Chapter 7: Special Collections – For Advanced Researchers

Montclair’s Special Collections hold rare books, manuscripts, and historical documents—perfect for thesis projects and independent research.

7.1 What’s in Special Collections?

  • First Editions (e.g., The Great Gatsby by F. Scott Fitzgerald)
  • Autographed Letters (from famous authors & activists)
  • University Archives (Montclair’s history, student newspapers)
  • Local NJ History (documents on Newark, Jersey City, etc.)

How to Access:

  • Request an appointment via Special Collections Contact
  • No walking-ins allowed (due to preservation needs)

Real-World Example: *A literature student analyzing "Hemingway’s writing style" could examine first editions of The Sun Also Rises in Special Collections for handwritten annotations.
