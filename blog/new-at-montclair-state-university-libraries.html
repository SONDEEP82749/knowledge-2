New at Montclair State University Libraries: A Complete Guide to Maximizing Your Research & Study Experience

Introduction: Why Montclair State University Libraries Are Your Academic Powerhouse

Welcome to Montclair State University Libraries—a vibrant hub of knowledge, innovation, and academic excellence. As a new student, faculty member, or researcher, navigating a university library system can feel overwhelming at first. But fear not! With the right strategies, you’ll unlock a world of resources that can dramatically enhance your learning, research, and career prospects.

According to the Montclair State University Libraries Annual Report (2023), over 50,000 physical and digital resources are available to students, with 12,000+ e-books alone. This guide will walk you through everything you need to know—from how to access digital databases to how to find hidden research gems—so you can start using the library like a pro. Let’s dive in!

Chapter 1: Getting Started – Your First Steps at Montclair State University Libraries

1.1 How to Access Your Library Account & Borrowing Privileges

Before you can start using the library’s physical and digital resources, you need to activate your library account.

  • Step 1: Log in via the Montclair State University Libraries Portal
  • Step 2: Use your Montclair State username and password (the same one for your email and Canvas).
  • Step 3: Check your borrowing limits—undergraduate students typically get 25 physical items at a time, while graduate students and faculty may have higher limits.
  • Step 4: Set up library notifications (optional but helpful) to receive alerts about overdue items or new e-resources.

Pro Tip: If you’re a remote student, you can still access all digital resources without visiting campus. The library offers 24/7 online access to journals, e-books, and databases.

1.2 Understanding the Library’s Physical & Digital Layout

Montclair State has three main library locations:

  1. The Library at the Center for Academic & Creative Excellence (CACE) – The main campus library, housing reference collections, study spaces, and the Writing Center.
  2. The Library at the School of Business – Specializes in business, economics, and finance resources.
  3. The Library at the School of Education – Focuses on education, psychology, and social sciences.

Digital Resources:

  • Montclair State’s Online Catalog (MSCAT) – Search for books, journals, and multimedia.
  • Databases (EBSCOhost, JSTOR, ProQuest, etc.) – Access peer-reviewed articles, dissertations, and industry reports.
  • Open Access Repositories – Free, legally available research papers.

Real-World Example: A marketing student researching consumer behavior would start in MSCAT to find relevant textbooks, then move to EBSCOhost for peer-reviewed journal articles on trends. Meanwhile, a business graduate student working on a thesis might use ProQuest’s ABI/INFORM for industry case studies.

Chapter 2: 10 Actionable Strategies to Master Montclair State University Libraries

2.1 Strategy 1: Use the Library’s Advanced Search Filters

Not all search results are created equal. The library’s advanced search filters help you narrow down to the most relevant sources.

How to Apply It:

  • In MSCAT, use filters like:
    • Date range (e.g., "Last 5 years" for current research)
    • Format (e.g., "Books," "Journal Articles," "Dissertations")
    • Subject (e.g., "Psychology," "Computer Science")
  • In databases (JSTOR, ProQuest), use Boolean operators (AND, OR, NOT) to refine searches.

Example: Instead of searching "climate change," try: "climate change AND policy AND United States NOT 2010"

2.2 Strategy 2: Leverage the Library’s Research Guides

Montclair State Libraries provides subject-specific research guides created by librarians to help you find high-quality sources.

Where to Find Them:

  • Go to Montclair State Research Guides
  • Browse by subject (e.g., "Business," "Education," "STEM") or course (e.g., "ENG 101," "PSY 302")

Example: A political science student working on a paper about voting patterns would use the Political Science Research Guide, which includes:

  • Recommended databases (e.g., Political Science Complete)
  • Key journals (e.g., American Political Science Review)
  • Data sources (e.g., ICPSR for social science datasets)

2.3 Strategy 3: Take Advantage of Librarian Research Consultations

Librarians are your secret weapon—they can help you find sources, refine your topic, and avoid plagiarism.

How to Book a Session:

  1. Visit Montclair State Library Research Help
  2. Click "Schedule a Research Appointment"
  3. Choose a librarian specializing in your field

Example: A graduate student struggling to find sources on AI ethics could meet with a STEM librarian, who might suggest:

  • Key databases (e.g., IEEE Xplore for tech papers)
  • Ethics frameworks (e.g., Journal of Applied Philosophy)
  • Alternative search terms (e.g., "AI governance" instead of just "AI")

2.4 Strategy 4: Use Zotero or RefWorks for Citation Management

Manually formatting citations is time-consuming and error-prone. Zotero (free) and RefWorks (paid, but available via Montclair State) automate citations.

How to Set It Up:

  1. Download Zotero (https://www.zotero.org/) or use Montclair State’s RefWorks.
  2. Save PDFs and metadata from the library’s databases.
  3. Generate citations in APA, MLA, Chicago, or IEEE formats.

Example: A history student downloading 10 articles from JSTOR can drag-and-drop them into Zotero, then auto-generate a bibliography in MLA format with one click.

2.5 Strategy 5: Explore Open Access & Free Resources

Not all great research is behind paywalls. Montclair State provides free access to:

  • Google Scholar (filter by "Montclair State University" for full-text access)
  • DOAJ (Directory of Open Access Journals)
  • PubMed Central (for medical research)

Example: A biology student researching gene editing could use PubMed Central to find free, peer-reviewed papers on CRISPR without paying for access.

2.6 Strategy 6: Use Interlibrary Loan (ILL) for Missing Resources

If Montclair State doesn’t have a book or article you need, Interlibrary Loan (ILL) gets it for you for free.

How to Request:

  1. Go to Montclair State ILL Service
  2. Fill out the form with title, author, and source details.
  3. Most requests arrive in 3-5 business days.

Example: A philosophy student needing a rare 19th-century text not in MSCAT could request it via ILL and receive it digitally or physically within a week.

2.7 Strategy 7: Attend Library Workshops & Webinars

The library offers free workshops on:

  • Research skills
  • Citation management
  • Data analysis
  • Open Access publishing

Where to Find Them:

  • Montclair State Library Events Calendar

Example: A data science major could attend a "Introduction to SPSS for Social Sciences" workshop to learn statistical analysis tools directly from a librarian.

2.8 Strategy 8: Use the Library’s Study Spaces & Tech Resources

Montclair State Libraries provide quiet study rooms, group workspaces, and tech stations, including:

  • Laptops & tablets (first-come, first-served)
  • 3D printers & scanners (for certain projects)
  • Silent study floors & collaborative zones

Example: A group of engineering students working on a project proposal could reserve a group study room with a whiteboard and projector for brainstorming sessions.

2.9 Strategy 9: Check Out the Library’s Special Collections

Beyond standard textbooks, Montclair State has unique archives, including:

  • The Montclair Historical Society Collection (local history)
  • The African American Cultural Center (primary sources on Black history)
  • The Women’s & Gender Studies Archive

Example: A history student writing about Montclair’s civil rights movement could access original newspaper clippings and oral histories from the Montclair Historical Society Collection.

2.10 Strategy 10: Use the Library’s AI & Chatbot Assistance

Montclair State’s library website features an AI chatbot (powered by AskMontclair) that can:

  • Answer quick reference questions
  • Help with database searches
  • Provide citation examples

How to Access:

  • Visit Montclair State Library FAQ
  • Click "Chat with a Librarian" for instant help.

Example: A student searching for "best databases for psychology research" could ask the chatbot, and it would immediately suggest:

  • PsycINFO
  • Psychology & Behavioral Sciences Collection
  • Google Scholar (with Montclair filter)

Chapter 3: Common Mistakes & How to Avoid Them

3.1 Mistake #1: Not Using Subject-Specific Databases

Problem: Many students only search Google Scholar, missing high-quality, peer-reviewed sources.

Solution:

  • Always start with Montclair State’s recommended databases (e.g., JSTOR for humanities, IEEE for engineering).
  • Avoid relying solely on Wikipedia—use it for background info only, not citations.

3.2 Mistake #2: Ignoring the Library’s Research Guides

Problem: Students reinvent the wheel by searching randomly instead of using pre-made research guides.

Solution:

  • Bookmark your subject’s research guide (e.g., Business Research Guide, Education Research Guide).
  • Follow the "Recommended Sources" section—it’s curated by experts.

3.3 Mistake #3: Not Checking for Full-Text Availability

Problem: Finding a great article but not being able to access it due to paywalls.

Solution:

  • Use the "Get It @ Montclair" button (found in databases like JSTOR).
  • If unavailable, request via Interlibrary Loan (ILL).

3.4 Mistake #4: Overlooking Open Access & Free Resources

Problem: Students pay for articles when they could be free via Montclair State.

Solution:

  • Use Google Scholar with the Montclair filter (set in Scholar Preferences).
  • Check DOAJ for open-access journals.

3.5 Mistake #5: Not Using Citation Management Tools

Problem: Manual citations lead to errors and last-minute formatting stress.

Solution:

  • Use Zotero or RefWorks to auto-generate citations.
  • Export references directly from databases (most have a "Cite" button).

3.6 Mistake #6: Not Asking for Help When Stuck

Problem: Students struggle alone instead of seeking librarian assistance.

Solution:

  • Book a research consultation (15-30 minutes).
  • Use the chatbot for quick questions.

3.7 Mistake #7: Not Exploring Special Collections

Problem: Students only use general textbooks and miss unique primary sources.

Solution:

  • Check the library’s archives (e.g., Montclair Historical Society, Women’s Studies Collection).
  • Ask librarians about rare materials—they often know hidden gems.

  3. Use the "Databases" or "E-Resources" tab to find peer-reviewed articles, e-books, and more.
  4. For off-campus access to Google Scholar, enable "Montclair State University" in your Scholar Preferences.

❓ FAQ 2: Can I renew my overdue library books online?

Answer: Yes! You can renew most physical and digital items online:

  1. Log in to your library account via MSCAT
  2. Go to "My Account"
  3. Select "Renew Items"
  4. Check the boxes for items you want to renew (most can be renewed up to 3 times).
  5. If an item is already checked out to another user, you’ll need to request it via Interlibrary Loan (ILL).

Note: Some high-demand or reference books cannot be renewed.
