New at Montclair State University Libraries: A Complete Guide to Maximizing Your Research & Study Success

Introduction: Why Montclair State University Libraries Are Your Academic Powerhouse

Welcome to Calculator-Library-Portal, your go-to resource for mastering academic research, study strategies, and library navigation—especially for new students at Montclair State University (MSU). If you’re just starting your journey at MSU, you might be wondering: How do I find the best resources? How can I avoid common research pitfalls? And where do I even begin?

Montclair State University Libraries—comprising John J. Cali School of Music Library, Library West, and the University Library—are more than just bookshelves. They are digital hubs, research partners, and study sanctuaries designed to support your academic and creative growth. With over 1.2 million physical and digital items (as of 2023) and partnerships with JSTOR, ProQuest, and EBSCO, these libraries provide access to peer-reviewed journals, e-books, streaming media, and exclusive databases that can elevate your research from good to groundbreaking.

But here’s the catch: Many students underutilize these resources due to lack of awareness or confusion about how to navigate them effectively. According to a 2022 MSU Library User Survey, only 47% of first-year students reported feeling "very confident" in using library resources, while 63% of graduate students admitted they struggled with locating specialized databases.

This guide will demystify Montclair State University Libraries, offering actionable strategies, real-world examples, and common pitfalls to avoid—so you can save time, improve your grades, and unlock academic success from day one.

Chapter 1: Getting Acquainted with Montclair State University Libraries

Before diving into research, it’s essential to understand the structure, services, and unique offerings of MSU Libraries.

1.1 The Three Libraries: Where to Find What You Need

Montclair State’s library system is divided into three specialized branches, each catering to different academic needs:

A. University Library (Main Campus – 1900 University Ave.)

  • Primary Focus: General research, undergraduate studies, and interdisciplinary subjects.
  • Key Features:
    • 24/7 Study Spaces (including group study rooms and silent zones).
    • Digital Scholarship Lab – For 3D printing, data visualization, and multimedia projects.
    • Special Collections – Rare books, archives, and historical documents (e.g., the New Jersey Jazz Collection).
    • Access to 100+ Databases (e.g., Academic Search Premier, ScienceDirect, and Statista).
  • Best For: Students in business, education, social sciences, and humanities.

B. Library West (1 Normal Ave.)

  • Primary Focus: STEM (Science, Technology, Engineering, Math) and health sciences.
  • Key Features:
    • Engineering & Computer Science Resources – Access to IEEE Xplore, MathSciNet, and IEEE Standards.
    • Health Sciences & Nursing Databases – CINAHL, PubMed, and Cochrane Library.
    • High-Tech Workstations – For coding, CAD software, and data analysis.
    • Science & Engineering Research Help Desk – Dedicated librarians for STEM queries.
  • Best For: Engineering, computer science, biology, chemistry, and nursing students.

C. John J. Cali School of Music Library (1 Normal Ave.)

  • Primary Focus: Music theory, performance, composition, and audio engineering.
  • Key Features:
    • Exclusive Music Databases – RISM (Répertoire International des Sources Musicales), Naxos Music Library, and Oxford Music Online.
    • Sheet Music & Scores – Over 50,000+ titles in print and digital formats.
    • Audio & Recording Studios – For students to practice and record.
    • Music Technology Lab – Access to Pro Tools, Logic Pro, and MIDI equipment.
  • Best For: Music majors, composers, and audio production students.

💡 Pro Tip: Use the MSU Libraries Website to explore each library’s floor plans, hours, and specialized collections. Bookmark the Library Guides for subject-specific resources.

Chapter 2: 10 Actionable Strategies to Master Montclair State University Libraries

Now that you know where to go, let’s explore how to use these resources effectively.

Strategy 1: Start with the Library Homepage & Subject Guides

Problem: Many students waste time searching Google or Wikipedia before checking library databases.

Solution: The MSU Libraries homepage is your first stop for research. Instead of guessing where to look, use:

  • A-Z Database List – Browse by subject (e.g., Business, Psychology, Engineering).
  • Library Guides – Pre-made research paths for specific courses (e.g., "ENG 101: Finding Articles" or "PSY 305: Statistical Databases").

Real-World Example: A business student researching "sustainable supply chains" might:*

  1. Go to the Business Library Guide.
  2. Click on "Recommended Databases" and select Business Source Premier.
  3. Use advanced search filters (e.g., "peer-reviewed," "2020–2024") to find 10 high-quality articles in under 15 minutes.

⚠️ Common Mistake: Assuming all databases are the same. Business Source Premier is better for management studies, while ScienceDirect is superior for technical research.

Strategy 2: Use the Library Catalog (OneSearch) Like a Pro

Problem: Students struggle with narrowing down search results in OneSearch.

Solution: OneSearch (the MSU Libraries catalog) is powerful but overwhelming. Master these techniques:

✅ Boolean Operators – Combine keywords:

  • "climate change" AND "policy" (finds articles with both terms).
  • "artificial intelligence" NOT "history" (excludes irrelevant results).

✅ Filters for Precision:

  • Date Range (e.g., "Last 5 Years" for current research).
  • Source Type (e.g., "Peer-Reviewed Journals," "Books," "Theses").
  • Format (e.g., "Full Text Online," "Audio/Video").

✅ Save & Organize:

  • Use folders in your Library Account to save articles, books, and PDFs for later.
  • Set up email alerts for new books on your topic.

Real-World Example: A psychology major studying "anxiety disorders in adolescents" might:

  1. Search "anxiety disorders" AND "adolescents" in OneSearch.
  2. Apply filters: "Peer-Reviewed," "2018–2024," "Full Text."
  3. Find 5 relevant studies in under 10 minutes—far faster than Google Scholar.

💡 Pro Tip: Use truncation symbols (*) to find variations:

  • climat* → Finds "climate," "climatic," "climatology."

Strategy 3: Leverage Databases for Your Major

Problem: Students don’t know which databases are best for their field.

Solution: Each major has specialized databases. Here’s a quick cheat sheet:

Major Best Databases Why?
Business Business Source Premier, IBISWorld Industry reports, case studies
Computer Science IEEE Xplore, ACM Digital Library Peer-reviewed tech papers
Nursing CINAHL, PubMed Evidence-based nursing research
Psychology PsycINFO, PsycARTICLES Clinical & experimental studies
History JSTOR, America: History & Life Primary sources & scholarly articles
Engineering ScienceDirect, IEEE Xplore Technical standards & research

Real-World Example: A nursing student writing a paper on "diabetic foot ulcers" should:

  1. Go to CINAHL (the gold standard for nursing).
  2. Search "diabetic foot ulcers" AND "wound care."
  3. Use CINAHL’s "Full Text" filter to access 15+ relevant studies instantly.

⚠️ Common Mistake: Assuming Google Scholar is enough. While it’s useful, **library databases provide full-text access, citations, and expert-curated content—Google Scholar does not.

Strategy 4: Access E-Books & Audio/Video Resources

Problem: Students don’t realize MSU Libraries has millions of e-books and multimedia.

Solution: E-books are instantly accessible and often more up-to-date than physical copies.

How to Find E-Books:

  1. Search OneSearch → Filter by "Books" → "Available Online."
  2. Use E-Book Platforms:
    • Ebrary (general reference)
    • JSTOR (academic books)
    • Project MUSE (humanities & social sciences)

Real-World Example: A philosophy student researching "existentialism" might:

  1. Search "existentialism" in JSTOR E-Books.
  2. Find "Being and Time" by Martin Heidegger (full-text available).
  3. Highlight, annotate, and cite directly in JSTOR.

🎵 For Music & Media Students:

  • Naxos Music Library (stream 500,000+ classical, jazz, and world music tracks).
  • Alexander Street Press (documentaries, films, and archival footage).

Strategy 5: Use Research Help & Librarians

Problem: Students hesitate to ask for help, thinking librarians are "too busy."

Solution: MSU Librarians are your research partners. They can:

  • Help refine search strategies.
  • Teach you how to use databases.
  • **Guide you on citation styles (APA, MLA, Chicago).

How to Get Help:

  • Chat with a Librarian (live help via MSU Library Website).
  • Schedule a Research Appointment (1-on-1 guidance).
  • Attend Workshops (e.g., "Citing Sources Like a Pro").

Real-World Example: A grad student struggling with quantitative research in Sociology might:

  1. Email the Social Sciences Librarian for help.
  2. Receive a customized database list and step-by-step search guide.
  3. Get tips on analyzing data in SPSS or R.

💡 Pro Tip: Librarians can also help you find gray literature (government reports, theses, conference papers) that aren’t in traditional databases.

Strategy 6: Utilize Interlibrary Loan (ILL) for Books & Articles

Problem: A required book isn’t available at MSU? No problem—Interlibrary Loan (ILL) gets it for you.

Solution:

  1. Search OneSearch → If a book/article is "Not Available," click "Request via Interlibrary Loan."
  2. Fill out the form (takes 24–72 hours).
  3. Receive a digital copy (PDF) or borrow a physical book from another library.

Real-World Example: A student needs "The Psychology of Money" by Morgan Housel but MSU doesn’t have it.

  1. They request it via ILL.
  2. Within 3 days, they receive a PDF scan of the book.

⚠️ Common Mistake: Assuming ILL is slow. While it takes time, it’s free and essential for rare or out-of-print books.

Strategy 7: Master Citation & Avoid Plagiarism

Problem: Students struggle with correct citations and paraphrasing.

Solution: Use MSU’s Citation Guides and tools:

A. Citation Styles:

  • APA (Psychology, Education, Social Sciences)
  • MLA (Humanities, Literature)
  • Chicago (History, Business)
  • AMA (Nursing, Medicine)

🔗 Free Tools:

  • Zotero (organizes sources & auto-generates citations).
  • EasyBib (simple citation builder).
  • MSU’s Citation Guide

Real-World Example: A student quotes "The Great Gatsby" by F. Scott Fitzgerald in an MLA paper. ❌ Incorrect: (Fitzgerald 123) ✅ Correct (MLA):

Fitzgerald, F. Scott. The Great Gatsby. Scribner, 1925, p. 123.

💡 Pro Tip: Always check your professor’s syllabus for preferred style.

Strategy 8: Use Study Spaces & Tech Tools Effectively

Problem: Students don’t know how to maximize library study spaces or tech resources.

Solution: MSU Libraries offer more than just books—they provide study environments and tools to boost productivity.

A. Study Spaces:

  • Silent Zones (for focused work).
  • Group Study Rooms (bookable via MSU Study Rooms).
  • Collaboration Tables (for group projects).

B. Tech Tools:

  • 3D Printing & Scanning (Digital Scholarship Lab).
  • Audio/Video Editing Stations (for music & film students).
  • High-Speed Computers (with Microsoft Office, SPSS, and MATLAB).

Real-World Example: A computer science student working on a machine learning project might:

  1. Use a group study room to collaborate with teammates.
  2. Access Jupyter Notebooks on library computers.
  3. Print final code reports at the copy center.

Strategy 9: Explore Digital Scholarship & Open Access

Problem: Students don’t know about open-access resources or digital scholarship projects.

Solution: MSU Libraries support open-access publishing and digital projects, including:

  • Montclair State University Digital Commons (institutional repository).
  • Open Educational Resources (OER) (free textbooks & course materials).
  • Data Management Plans (for research projects).

Real-World Example: A grad student in Environmental Science could:

  1. Upload their thesis to Digital Commons.
  2. Use open-access databases like NASA’s Earthdata for research.
  3. Get help from librarians on data visualization (using Tableau or R).
