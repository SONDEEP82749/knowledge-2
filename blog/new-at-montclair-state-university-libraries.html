New at Montclair State University Libraries: A Complete Guide to Maximizing Your Research & Study Success

Welcome to Montclair State University Libraries—a vibrant hub of knowledge, innovation, and academic excellence. 10 Essential Strategies to Succeed at Montclair State University Libraries

1. Get Your Library Card & Access Your Account Immediately

Before diving into research, activate your library card—it’s your key to borrowing books, accessing databases, and requesting interlibrary loans.

How to do it:

  • Visit the Library’s website (Montclair State University Libraries) and log in with your Montclair State username and password.
  • Check your account status to ensure no holds or fines are blocking access.
  • Pro Tip: Bookmark the Library Portal on your browser for quick access.

Real-World Example: Sarah, a first-year psychology major, struggled to find her required textbook on the shelf. After checking her library account, she realized she hadn’t activated her card yet. Once she did, she discovered the book was already checked out—but she could request it via Interlibrary Loan (ILL) within 24 hours.

2. Master the Library Search Bar (Beyond Google)

Montclair State’s library search bar (located on the homepage) is not just a basic Google search—it’s a powerful academic tool that indexes books, journal articles, videos, and more from multiple databases.

How to optimize your search:

  • Use Boolean operators (AND, OR, NOT) to refine results.
    • Example: "climate change AND policy"
  • Try truncation (climat will pull "climate," "climatic," etc.).
  • Use quotes for exact phrases ("social media impact").
  • Filter by date, format (e-book, journal), or subject for precision.

Common Mistake: Searching too broadly (e.g., just "history") leads to thousands of irrelevant results. Instead, narrow your topic early.

3. Explore Subject-Specific Databases (Not Just Google Scholar)

While Google Scholar is useful, Montclair’s subject databases (like JSTOR, EBSCOhost, and ProQuest) provide peer-reviewed, credible sources that Google often misses.

Top Databases by Major:

Major Best Databases
Business Business Source Premier, ABI/INFORM
Psychology PsycINFO, Psychology & Behavioral Sciences
Engineering IEEE Xplore, ScienceDirect
Education ERIC, Education Research Complete
Literature MLA International Bibliography, JSTOR

Real-World Example: James, an engineering student, needed a peer-reviewed article on renewable energy. Instead of searching Google Scholar, he used IEEE Xplore—where he found a 2023 study on solar panel efficiency that directly supported his thesis.

4. Use the "Find It" Button for Full-Text Access

When searching the library catalog, you’ll often see "Find It" links next to articles. Clicking this connects you to:

  • The full PDF (if available online).
  • A link to request the article via Interlibrary Loan (ILL).
  • Alternate sources if the article isn’t available.

How to avoid frustration:

  • If an article isn’t accessible, request it via ILL (usually delivered in 24-72 hours).
  • Check if the library has the e-book version instead of a print copy.

Common Mistake: Giving up if an article isn’t immediately available. Most journals are just a few clicks away!

5. Leverage the Library’s Research Guides

Montclair State Libraries offers subject-specific research guides created by librarians to help you find the best sources for your field.

How to use them:

  • Visit the Research Guides page (Montclair State Research Guides).
  • Select your major or topic (e.g., "Business Research," "Psychology Databases").
  • Follow the step-by-step recommendations for databases, books, and citation styles.

Real-World Example: A biology student struggling with citation formatting found the APA Research Guide on the library website. It included examples, templates, and direct links to Zotero—saving her hours of guesswork.

6. Attend Library Workshops & One-on-One Help

Montclair State Libraries offers free workshops on:

  • Research strategies
  • Citation management (Zotero, EndNote)
  • Data analysis
  • Thesis writing

How to access them:

  • Check the Library Events Calendar (Montclair State Events).
  • Request a personal research consultation with a librarian.

Real-World Example: Emily, a grad student, attended a Zotero workshop and learned how to automatically cite sources in her paper. By the end of the semester, she saved 10+ hours of manual formatting.

7. Use E-Books & Digital Resources for Convenience

With thousands of e-books, Montclair’s digital library is available 24/7 from anywhere.

How to access e-books:

  • Search the library catalog and filter by "eBook."
  • Use OverDrive or EBSCOhost for popular titles.
  • Download apps like Libby (for e-books) or Zotero (for citations).

Pro Tip: Many e-books allow annotating and bookmarking—great for study groups and group projects.

Common Mistake: Assuming all books are in print. Check the "eBook" filter first!

8. Request Interlibrary Loan (ILL) for Unavailable Materials

If Montclair doesn’t have a book or article you need, Interlibrary Loan (ILL) lets you borrow it from other universities.

How to request ILL:

  1. Search the library catalog.
  2. Click "Request via Interlibrary Loan" if unavailable.
  3. Fill out the form with your Montclair ID and contact info.
  4. Articles arrive in 24-48 hours; books take 5-10 business days.

Real-World Example: A history student needed a rare 19th-century journal not held by Montclair. Via ILL, she received a digital scan within 3 days—perfect for her paper.

9. Organize Your Sources with Zotero or EndNote

Manually citing sources is time-consuming and error-prone. Zotero (free) and EndNote (paid) automatically store, organize, and cite your research.

How to set it up:

  1. Download Zotero (zotero.org).
  2. Install the browser extension to auto-save citations.
  3. Use the Word plugin to insert citations in APA, MLA, or Chicago style.

Real-World Example: A political science student used Zotero to collect 50+ sources for his thesis. When he needed to update citations, he simply right-clicked and reformatted—saving days of work.

Montclair State Libraries have multiple locations with different hours and study environments.

Pro Tip: Use the "Library Hours" widget on the homepage to check real-time availability.

Common Mistakes New Students Make (And How to Avoid Them)

❌ Mistake 1: Not Using the Library’s Website Properly

Problem: Many students only search Google instead of the Montclair Library Portal. Solution: Bookmark the Library Homepage and use the advanced search filters.

❌ Mistake 2: Ignoring Subject Databases

Problem: Relying on Google Scholar or Wikipedia leads to uncredible sources. Solution: Always start with Montclair’s subject databases for peer-reviewed articles.

❌ Mistake 3: Not Requesting ILL Early

Problem: Waiting until the last minute to request a book via ILL can delay your work. Solution: Check availability early and request ILL at least 1 week before deadlines.

❌ Mistake 4: Overlooking E-Books

Problem: Assuming all books are in print wastes time searching shelves. Solution: Filter by "eBook" first—many are instantly accessible.

❌ Mistake 5: Not Attending Workshops

Problem: Missing free research help means reinventing the wheel. Solution: Sign up for workshops on citation, databases, and thesis writing.

Answer: To find books, use the library search bar on the homepage. Enter your title, author, or keywords, then:

  1. Filter by "Books" to see physical copies.
  2. Check the "Location" (e.g., "Library North") to find the shelf.
  3. Use the "Request" button if the book is checked out.

❓ Can I access library resources from off-campus?

Answer: Yes! All digital resources (e-books, journal articles, databases) are accessible from anywhere using your Montclair State login credentials. Just ensure you’re logged into the VPN if needed.

❓ How long does Interlibrary Loan (ILL) take?

Answer:

  • Articles: 24-48 hours
  • Books: 5-10 business days Check your ILL account for updates.

❓ Does Montclair State Libraries offer study rooms?

Answer: Yes! Reserve a group study room via the Library’s Study Room Booking System (Montclair Study Rooms). Some are first-come, first-served, while others require advance booking.

❓ How do I cite sources using Zotero?

Answer:

  1. Install Zotero and its Word plugin.
  2. Save citations as you research (right-click → "Save to Zotero").
  3. Insert citations in your paper (click the Zotero toolbar).
  4. Format automatically (APA, MLA, Chicago, etc.).
