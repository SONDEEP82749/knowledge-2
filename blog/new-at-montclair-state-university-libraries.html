New at Montclair State University Libraries: A Comprehensive Guide to Maximizing Your Research & Study Experience

But with so many tools, databases, and services available, how do you make the most of them?

Chapter 1: Understanding the Montclair State University Libraries System

Before diving into strategies, it’s essential to understand the structure and offerings of the libraries. Montclair State University operates three main library locations, each with unique strengths:

1. The Library at the Center for Academic Success (CAS Library)

  • Location: Center for Academic Success (CAS), 1 Normal Avenue, Montclair, NJ
  • Specialization: General academic research, undergraduate studies, and study spaces
  • Key Features:
    • 24/7 study lounges (including silent study zones)
    • Computer labs with high-speed internet
    • Group study rooms (reservable via the Library Calendar)
    • Access to Summit (a regional library sharing system for borrowing books from other NJ institutions)

2. The Library at the School of Business (SB Library)

  • Location: School of Business, 1 Normal Avenue, Montclair, NJ
  • Specialization: Business, economics, finance, and entrepreneurship research
  • Key Features:
    • Bloomberg Terminals (for real-time financial data and market analysis)
    • Specialized business databases (IBISWorld, Mergent, Statista)
    • Career resources (resume workshops, LinkedIn training)

3. The Library at the School of Education (SE Library)

  • Location: School of Education, 1 Normal Avenue, Montclair, NJ
  • Specialization: Education research, teaching methodologies, and K-12 resources
  • Key Features:
    • ERIC (Education Resources Information Center) database (the largest education database in the world)
    • **Access to ProQuest Education Journals (peer-reviewed research)
    • Special collections (historical education documents, teacher resource guides)

4. The Digital & Special Collections (Online & Physical)

  • Location: Accessible via Montclair State’s Library Website
  • Specialization: Rare books, archives, and digital humanities
  • Key Features:
    • Montclair State University Archives (historical documents, university records)
    • Digital Commons @ Montclair State (open-access student research, faculty publications)
    • Rare Book Collection (first editions, historical manuscripts)

Why This Matters: Knowing which library serves your discipline best saves you time and frustration. For example, a business student should prioritize the SB Library’s Bloomberg Terminals, while an education major will find the SE Library’s ERIC database indispensable.

Chapter 2: 10 Actionable Strategies to Master Montclair State University Libraries

Now that you understand the layout and specializations, let’s dive into practical, step-by-step strategies to maximize your library experience.

Strategy 1: Get Your Montclair State University ID & Library Card

Why It’s Critical: Without a valid Montclair State ID, you cannot borrow books, access restricted databases, or use printing services.

How to Do It:

  1. Visit the CAS Library’s Circulation Desk (or the SB/SE Library if applicable).
  2. Show your student ID (or government-issued photo ID if you’re a faculty member).
  3. Request a library card—most students get one automatically upon enrollment.
  4. Set up your account in Montclair State’s Library Catalog (LibCat).

Pro Tip:

  • Activate your card immediately—some databases require two-factor authentication (2FA) via your Montclair State email.
  • Keep your card safe—if lost, report it online to avoid fines.

Real-World Example: A first-year psychology student tried to check out a book but was denied because she didn’t have her library card. After visiting the Circulation Desk, she realized she needed to activate her account online first—a simple step she missed.

Strategy 2: Use the Library Catalog (LibCat) Like a Pro

Why It’s Critical: The Montclair State Library Catalog (LibCat) is your gateway to books, journals, and digital resources. Without mastering it, you’ll waste time searching manually.

How to Do It:

  1. Go to LibCat and log in with your Montclair credentials.
  2. Use advanced search filters:
    • Title, Author, Subject, Keyword
    • Date range (e.g., "2015–2023" for recent research)
    • Format (books, e-books, articles, videos)
  3. Leverage Boolean operators:
    • "AND" (e.g., "climate change AND policy")
    • "OR" (e.g., "global warming OR climate crisis")
    • "NOT" (e.g., "AI NOT ethics")
  4. Check "Available Online" to find e-books and PDFs instantly.

Pro Tip:

  • Save your favorite searches as alerts (e.g., "new books on renewable energy").
  • Use "Get It" buttons to request books from other NJ libraries via Summit.

Real-World Example: *A graduate student in environmental science was struggling to find peer-reviewed articles on solar energy in urban areas. By using LibCat’s advanced search with "AND" and "NOT", she narrowed down results to only the most relevant studies—saving hours of manual searching.*

Strategy 3: Explore Databases by Subject (Not Just Randomly Browsing)

Why It’s Critical: Montclair State provides hundreds of databases, but not all are equally useful for your field. Wasting time in irrelevant databases slows down research.

How to Do It:

  1. Go to the Databases A-Z List.
  2. Click on your subject (e.g., Business, Education, Psychology, Engineering).
  3. Bookmark your top 3–5 databases for quick access.

Top Databases by Major:

Major Best Databases Why?
Business Bloomberg, IBISWorld, Mergent, Statista Real-time financial data, market analysis
Education ERIC, ProQuest Education Journals Peer-reviewed education research
Psychology PsycINFO, Psychology & Behavioral Sciences Comprehensive mental health studies
Engineering IEEE Xplore, ScienceDirect Cutting-edge tech and research papers
History JSTOR, Project MUSE Academic journals and primary sources
Nursing CINAHL, PubMed Healthcare and medical research

Pro Tip:

  • Check if a database has "full-text" availability—some require interlibrary loan (ILL).
  • Use "Cited By" links in databases like Google Scholar to find related research.

Real-World Example: A nursing student needed evidence-based practices for diabetic patient care. Instead of searching random databases, she used CINAHL, which specializes in nursing and healthcare, and found exactly the studies she needed in minutes.

Strategy 4: Request Books & Articles via Interlibrary Loan (ILL)

Why It’s Critical: Montclair State’s collection is extensive but not exhaustive. If a book or article isn’t available, ILL is your lifeline.

How to Do It:

  1. Search LibCat for the item.
  2. Click "Request via Interlibrary Loan" (if available).
  3. Fill out the form (takes 24–72 hours for delivery).
  4. Pick up at the Circulation Desk (or request digital delivery for articles).

Pro Tip:

  • Check the "Availability" status—if it says "Not at Montclair", ILL is your best option.
  • For urgent needs, email ill@montclair.edu for priority processing.

Real-World Example: A PhD candidate in literature needed a rare 19th-century novel not held by Montclair. By submitting an ILL request, the book arrived within 48 hours, allowing her to complete her analysis on time.

Why It’s Critical: Montclair State Libraries offer multiple study environments, but choosing the wrong one can distract you or waste time.

How to Do It:

  • Reserve group rooms in advance via the Library Calendar.
  • Use headphones in shared spaces to reduce noise.

Real-World Example: A business student was struggling to analyze financial data in a noisy café. By moving to the SB Library’s silent zone, she completed her project in half the time with zero distractions.

Strategy 6: Attend Library Workshops & Research Help Sessions

Why It’s Critical: Librarians at Montclair State are experts in research strategies, but many students don’t know they exist.

How to Do It:

  1. Check the Library Events Calendar.
  2. Attend workshops like:
    • "Finding Scholarly Articles"
    • "Citing Sources in APA/MLA"
    • "Using Bloomberg Terminals"
  3. **Schedule a one-on-one research appointment with a librarian.

Pro Tip:

  • Workshops are free and open to all students—no registration needed for most.
  • Ask specific questions (e.g., "How do I find primary sources for my history paper?").

Real-World Example: *A sociology major was overwhelmed by her literature review. After attending a "Research Strategies" workshop, she learned *how to use Google Scholar effectively and found 10 relevant sources in 30 minutes.

Strategy 7: Leverage Digital Resources (E-Books, Databases, & Open Access)

Why It’s Critical: Print books are convenient but limited. Digital resources offer instant access, annotations, and searchability.

How to Do It:

  1. **Use LibCat’s "Available Online" filter to find e-books.
  2. **Explore Montclair State’s Digital Commons for open-access student/faculty research.
  3. Bookmark key databases (e.g., JSTOR, ScienceDirect, ProQuest).
  4. **Use Zotero or Mendeley to organize and cite digital sources.

Pro Tip:

  • **Check for PDF downloads—some databases only provide abstracts.
  • Use Google Scholar’s "Related Articles" feature to find similar research.

Real-World Example: A computer science student needed access to a 2022 research paper not in Montclair’s print collection. By searching Google Scholar and using "Get It" links, she downloaded the full PDF instantly.
