Chapter 1: Getting Started – Your First Steps at Montclair State University Libraries

1.1 How to Access the Libraries: Physical & Digital Entry Points

Montclair State University operates three main library locations, each with unique strengths:

  • John J. Cali School of Music Library – Specializes in musicology, audio recordings, and sheet music.
  • Library & Information Center (LIB 100-104) – The main hub for general research, study spaces, and 24/7 computer labs.
  • Special Collections & University Archives (LIB 200) – Houses rare books, historical documents, and university archives.

How to Get Your Library Card (Physical & Digital Access)

  1. Physical Access:

    • Students: Your Montclair State ID (student ID card) grants you entry to all libraries.
    • Faculty/Staff: Use your employee ID or contact the Circulation Desk for access.
    • Visitors/Alumni: Some restricted areas require advance permission—check the library website for details.

  2. Digital Access (Off-Campus):

    • Use your Montclair State username and password to access e-books, databases, and online journals from anywhere.
    • Trouble logging in? Contact the IT Help Desk or the Library’s Digital Access Team.

⚠️ Pro Tip: Bookmark the Montclair State Libraries Homepage—it’s your one-stop portal for all resources.

Montclair State Libraries offer far more than book checkouts. Here’s what you might be missing:

Chapter 2: Mastering Montclair State’s Digital Resources

2.1 The Ultimate Guide to Montclair State’s Databases

With over 200+ databases, Montclair State Libraries provide academic goldmines. But not all databases are created equal—here’s how to find the best ones for your needs.

How to Search Databases Effectively

  1. Start with the Library Database A-Z List

    • Broad subjects? Try Academic Search Premier or JSTOR.
    • Narrow topics? Use ScienceDirect (STEM), PsycINFO (Psychology), or Business Source Premier (Business).

  2. Use Advanced Search Filters

    • Peer-reviewed only? Check the "Scholarly (Peer-Reviewed) Journals" box.
    • Full-text available? Look for the "PDF Full Text" or "HTML Full Text" option.
    • Date range? Limit to last 5 years for cutting-edge research.

  3. Boolean Operators for Precision

    • AND → Narrows results (e.g., "climate change AND policy").
    • OR → Broadens results (e.g., "global warming OR climate crisis").
    • NOT → Excludes terms (e.g., "AI NOT ethics" to avoid bias studies).

⚠️ Common Mistake: Overly broad searches (e.g., just typing "machine learning") lead to thousands of irrelevant results. Always refine with keywords.

🔍 Real-World Example: *A business student, Priya, needed recent studies on remote work productivity. Instead of searching "remote work", she used:

  • "remote work" AND "productivity metrics" AND "2020-2024" This gave her 50 highly relevant articles instead of 2,000.

2.2 E-Books & Digital Collections: How to Find & Use Them

Montclair State offers 100,000+ e-books through platforms like:

  • Ebrary (now ProQuest Ebook Central)
  • JSTOR
  • Gale Ebooks
  • OverDrive (for fiction/non-fiction)

How to Access & Download E-Books

  1. Search the Library Catalog and filter by "eBook."
  2. Click "Check Out" (most allow 28-day loans).
  3. Download via:
    • Browser (read online)
    • OverDrive app (for mobile)
    • PDF/EPUB format (for offline reading)

💡 Pro Tip: Bookmark your favorite e-books in Zotero or Mendeley (citation managers we’ll cover later).

⚠️ Common Mistake: Assuming all e-books are free to download—some require campus login or have limited concurrent users. Always check the loan status.

Not all research is behind paywalls. Montclair State supports:

Chapter 3: Research Strategies – How to Find What You Need (Fast)

3.1 The 5-Step Research Process (With Montclair State Tools)

  1. Define Your Topic

    • Too broad? Narrow it down (e.g., "climate change" → "climate change in New Jersey, 2020-2024").
    • Use Montclair’s Research Guides for topic ideas.

  2. Find Background Information

    • Start with Credo Reference or Gale Virtual Reference Library for overviews.
    • Example: If researching Renissance art, start with Britannica Online.

  3. Locate Scholarly Articles

    • Use Google Scholar (set Montclair State as your library link in settings).
    • Check JSTOR for humanities & social sciences.

  4. Evaluate Sources (CRAAP Test)

    • Currency (Is it recent?)
    • Relevance (Does it answer your question?)
    • Authority (Who wrote it? Is it peer-reviewed?)
    • Accuracy (Are claims verifiable?)
    • Purpose (Is it biased or objective?)

  5. Synthesize & Cite

    • Use Zotero or EndNote (free for students) to organize and cite sources.

💡 Pro Tip: Librarians can help you refine your search—schedule a 15-minute consultation via Library Consultations.

3.2 How to Use Google Scholar Like a Pro

Many students only use Google Scholar but don’t know how to maximize its power:

✅ Do This:

  • Set Montclair State as your library link (under Settings > Library Links).
  • Use advanced search operators:
    • site:montclair.edu (finds Montclair’s own research).
    • filetype:pdf (limits to PDFs).
    • intitle:"climate policy" (finds titles with exact keywords).

❌ Avoid This:

  • Assuming all Google Scholar results are free—some require paywalls. Use "Get it at Montclair" to check for full-text access.

🔍 Real-World Example: *A history student, Sophia, needed primary sources on the Civil Rights Movement. Instead of just searching "Civil Rights," she used:

  • site:montclair.edu "Civil Rights" filetype:pdf This gave her direct access to Montclair’s digital archives without paywalls.

Chapter 4: Citation & Plagiarism – Avoiding Academic Pitfalls

4.1 Why Citation Matters (And How to Do It Right)

Plagiarism is not just copying—it includes:

  • Paraphrasing without credit
  • Using ideas without citing
  • Buying papers online

Montclair State strictly enforces academic integrity, and librarians can help you cite correctly.

Common Citation Styles & How to Use Them

Style When to Use Montclair’s Best Tool
APA (Psychology, Education, Social Sciences) Most common in humanities & sciences. APA Guide
MLA (Literature, Arts, Humanities) Used in English, Philosophy, Languages. MLA Guide
Chicago/Turabian (History, Business) Common in history & business research. Chicago Guide
AMA (Health Sciences) Used in medicine & nursing. AMA Guide

💡 Pro Tip: Use Zotero (free) to auto-generate citations and build bibliographies.

4.2 How to Use Zotero (Step-by-Step Guide)

Zotero is a free citation manager that saves time and reduces errors.

Step 1: Download & Install

  • Get it from Zotero.org (works with Chrome, Firefox, Edge).

Step 2: Add References

  • Drag & drop PDFs from databases into Zotero.
  • Auto-detects citations from Google Scholar, JSTOR, etc.

Step 3: Organize Your Library

  • Create folders (e.g., "Thesis Research," "Coursework").
  • Tag sources (e.g., #peerreviewed, #primarysource).

Step 4: Generate Citations

  • Right-click > Copy > Citation (chooses your style).
  • Auto-fill bibliographies in Word/Google Docs.

🔍 Real-World Example: *A grad student, Michael, was writing a 50-page thesis. Instead of manually citing 150 sources, he used Zotero to auto-generate APA citations, saving him 10+ hours of work.

4.3 Common Plagiarism Mistakes & How to Avoid Them

Mistake Why It’s Wrong How to Fix It
Copy-pasting without quotes Even with quotes, not citing the source is plagiarism. Always cite, even for direct quotes.
Paraphrasing poorly Changing a few words but keeping the same structure is still plagiarism. Rewrite in your own words and cite.
Using Wikipedia as a primary source Wikipedia is not scholarly—it’s a starting point, not a citation. Use Wikipedia to find topics, then find peer-reviewed sources.
Not checking for accidental plagiarism Similarity tools (like Turnitin) catch unintentional plagiarism. Use Montclair’s Turnitin Guide.
Ignoring fair use rules Even short quotes may violate copyright if not cited properly. Check Montclair’s Copyright Policy.

💡 Pro Tip: Use Turnitin (available via Blackboard) to check your work before submitting.
