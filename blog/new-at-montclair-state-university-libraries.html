Chapter 2: 10 Actionable Strategies to Master Montclair State University Libraries

Now that you know what’s available, let’s explore practical strategies to make the most of your library experience.

Strategy 1: Use the Library’s Search Tool Like a Pro (Beyond Google)

Many students default to Google Scholar or their major’s department page—but Montclair State’s library search tool (powered by WorldCat Discovery) is far more powerful.

How to Optimize Your Searches: ✅ Use Boolean operators (AND, OR, NOT) to refine results.

  • Example: "climate change AND policy NOT fiction" ✅ Leverage filters (publication date, subject, format) to narrow down results. ✅ Try subject-specific databases (e.g., JSTOR for humanities, IEEE Xplore for engineering). ✅ Use the "Cited By" feature to find related research.

Real-World Example: A business student researching sustainable packaging could:

  1. Search "circular economy" AND "plastic waste" in the library database.
  2. Filter by "peer-reviewed journals" and "2020–2024" to get the most relevant studies.
  3. Use "Cited By" to find newer articles that reference the top results.

Common Mistake: Assuming Google is better than the library search tool. Fix: The library’s database includes exclusive access to paid journals that Google can’t provide.

Strategy 2: Take Advantage of Subject Librarians (Your Free Research Coaches)

Montclair State assigns subject librarians to each major—these are experts who can:

  • Help you find high-quality sources for your papers.
  • Teach you advanced search techniques.
  • Guide you on ethical research practices.

How to Connect:

  • Visit Montclair State’s Librarian Directory.
  • Schedule a 15-minute appointment via email or Zoom.
  • Attend workshops (e.g., "How to Find Primary Sources").

Real-World Example: A psychology major struggling to find studies on neuroplasticity in aging could:

  1. Email their psychology subject librarian for a list of key databases.
  2. Receive a customized search guide with pre-set filters.
  3. Get recommendations for empirical journals like The Journals of Gerontology.

Common Mistake: Not realizing subject librarians exist. Fix: Book a meeting—it’s free and saves hours of wasted searching.

Strategy 3: Use Zotero or RefWorks to Organize Your Research (No More Lost PDFs)

Manually organizing citations is time-consuming and error-prone. Zotero (free) and RefWorks (paid, but available via Montclair) are bibliography managers that:

  • Auto-save citations from databases.
  • Generate citations in any format (APA, MLA, Chicago).
  • Sync across devices (so you’re never without your references).

How to Set It Up:

  1. Download Zotero (zotero.org) or use RefWorks via Montclair’s portal.
  2. Create a folder system (e.g., "Research Papers," "Course Notes").
  3. Drag-and-drop PDFs into your library.
  4. Use the browser extension to save citations directly from library databases.

Real-World Example: A history student writing a paper on the American Revolution could:

  1. Find 5 key sources in the library database.
  2. Drag each citation into Zotero while browsing.
  3. Generate an APA bibliography in one click.

Common Mistake: Waiting until the last minute to organize citations. Fix: Start early—Zotero saves you from all-nighter citation panic.

Common Mistake: Assuming everything is free on Google Scholar. Fix: Always check Montclair’s paid databases first—they’re legal and high-quality.

Strategy 7: Leverage Interlibrary Loan (ILL) for Books You Can’t Find

If Montclair doesn’t have a book you need, Interlibrary Loan (ILL) lets you borrow it from other universities.

How to Request ILL:

  1. Search the book in WorldCat.
  2. If unavailable at Montclair, submit an ILL request via Montclair’s ILL Portal.
  3. Wait 3–7 days for delivery (digital or physical).

Real-World Example: A philosophy student* needing Heidegger’s original German texts could:

  1. Search in Montclair’s catalog—not available.
  2. Request via ILL from Princeton or NYU.
  3. Receive a digital scan within 5 days.

Common Mistake: Giving up if a book isn’t at Montclair. Fix: ILL is free and fast—use it!
