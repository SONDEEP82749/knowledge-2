New at Montclair State University Libraries: A Complete Guide to Maximizing Your Research & Study Success

If you're new to Montclair State, you might be wondering:

  • How do I navigate the library’s vast resources?

Chapter 1: Getting Started with Montclair State University Libraries

Before diving into research, you need to set up your library access properly. Here’s how to get started efficiently.

1.1 Register for a Library Card (The First Step)

Every Montclair State student automatically receives a library card upon enrollment. However, you must activate it to access physical and digital resources.

How to Register:

  1. Online via MyMontclair Portal – Log in to your student account and navigate to the Library Services tab.
  2. In-Person at the Circulation Desk – Visit Feliciano School of Business Library (for business students) or University Library (for general use) with your student ID.
  3. Mobile App (LibApp) – Download the Montclair State Libraries app (available on iOS and Android) to check holds, renew books, and access digital resources.

Pro Tip: Always keep your student ID handy—it’s your key to borrowing books, using printers, and accessing restricted databases.

1.2 Understanding Library Locations & Services

Montclair State has three main library branches, each serving different academic needs:

Library Name Location Specialization Key Features
University Library 1 Normal Ave, Montclair General Research Largest collection, study spaces, 24/7 access (via keycard)
Feliciano School of Business Library 1 Normal Ave, Montclair Business & Economics Bloomberg Terminals, financial databases, case studies
School of Education Library 1 Normal Ave, Montclair Education Research Early childhood, special education, teaching resources

Real-World Example: A business student struggling with financial modeling found the Feliciano Library’s Bloomberg Terminals invaluable for real-time market data. Without this resource, they would have spent hours searching free online sources—leading to outdated or incomplete information.

1.3 Exploring Digital vs. Physical Resources

Montclair State Libraries offer both physical and digital materials. Here’s how to decide which to use:

Resource Type Best For How to Access
Physical Books In-depth reading, textbooks, reference materials Search MontclairCat, request holds, pick up at circulation
E-Books 24/7 access, mobile reading Use Ebscohost, ProQuest, or OverDrive (via library website)
Journals & Articles Academic research, peer-reviewed sources Search JSTOR, ScienceDirect, or Google Scholar (with library login)
Databases Specialized research (e.g., PsycINFO for psychology) Access via A-Z Database List on the library website

Common Mistake: Assuming all articles are free online. Solution: Always check the Montclair State Libraries website for full-text access—many journals require a library login.

Chapter 2: 10 Actionable Strategies to Master Montclair State Libraries

Now that you’re set up, let’s dive into practical strategies to make the most of your library experience.

2.1 Strategy 1: Use the Right Search Tools (Beyond Google)

Google is great, but academic research requires specialized databases. Montclair State offers:

  • MontclairCat – For books, DVDs, and physical materials.
  • JSTOR – Peer-reviewed journals in humanities & social sciences.
  • ScienceDirect – STEM research papers.
  • ProQuest – Dissertations, newspapers, and business reports.

How to Search Effectively: ✅ Use Boolean operators (AND, OR, NOT) for precise results. ✅ Filter by date (e.g., "2015–Present") to get recent research. ✅ Check "Full Text" or "Peer Reviewed" to ensure credibility.

Real-World Example: *A history student researching the American Revolution used JSTOR’s advanced search with keywords like "Patriots AND taxation" and filtered by "1760–1780"—saving hours of irrelevant searches.*

2.2 Strategy 2: Leverage Librarians for Expert Help

Montclair State librarians are not just bookkeepers—they’re research experts. They can:

  • Help refine search queries.
  • Guide you to hidden databases.
  • Teach citation styles (APA, MLA, Chicago).

How to Access Help:

  • Chat with a Librarian (available 24/7 via the library website).
  • Schedule a Research Appointment (book a 1-on-1 session).
  • Visit the Research Help Desk (located in the University Library).

Common Mistake: Waiting until the last minute to ask for help. Solution: Book a session early—librarians can save you weeks of frustration.

2.3 Strategy 3: Master Interlibrary Loan (ILL) for Missing Books

Not every book is in Montclair’s collection. Interlibrary Loan (ILL) lets you borrow from other NJ libraries (and beyond).

How to Request an ILL:

  1. Search MontclairCat for the book.
  2. If unavailable, click "Request via Interlibrary Loan."
  3. Fill out the form (usually takes 3–7 business days).

Pro Tip: For urgent needs, check WorldCat—it connects to thousands of libraries worldwide.

2.4 Strategy 4: Use Zotero or RefWorks for Citation Management

Managing citations manually is error-prone and time-consuming. Zotero (free) and RefWorks (paid, but accessible via Montclair) help:

  • Automatically generate citations (APA, MLA, etc.).
  • Organize research papers in folders.
  • Create bibliographies in seconds.

How to Get Started:

  1. Download Zotero from zotero.org.
  2. Install the browser extension to save citations automatically.
  3. Use the Word/Google Docs plugin to insert citations.

Real-World Example: A psychology student using Zotero saved 10+ hours by avoiding manual citation formatting. Instead of spending hours adjusting APA references, they let Zotero do the work.

2.5 Strategy 5: Explore Open Access & Free Resources

Not all research requires a library subscription. Open Access (OA) resources are free and legally available. Montclair State curates a list:

  • Google Scholar (with library links for full text).
  • Directory of Open Access Journals (DOAJ).
  • Project MUSE (humanities & social sciences).
  • PubMed Central (health & medical research).

How to Find OA Resources:

  • Use the "Open Access" filter in MontclairCat.
  • Check the library’s Open Access Guide.

Common Mistake: Assuming "free" means "unreliable." Solution: Always verify sources via Google Scholar’s "Cited by" feature to check credibility.

2.6 Strategy 6: Utilize Study Spaces & Tech Tools

Montclair Libraries offer more than just books—they provide study environments and tech support:

Feature Location Best For
Group Study Rooms University Library (2nd Floor) Collaborative projects
Silent Study Zones Feliciano Library (1st Floor) Focused reading
Printing/Scanning All libraries (with student ID) Assignments & exams
Tech Workstations University Library (IT Help Desk) Troubleshooting laptops

Pro Tip: Book a group study room in advance via the LibApp to avoid last-minute conflicts.

2.7 Strategy 7: Attend Library Workshops & Events

Montclair State Libraries host free workshops on:

  • Research methods
  • Citation management
  • Data analysis (SPSS, R, Python)

How to Find Workshops:

  • Check the library calendar on the website.
  • Sign up via Montclair’s Eventbrite page.

Real-World Example: *A computer science student attended a Python for Data Analysis workshop and learned how to clean datasets efficiently—a skill that improved their final project grade by 15%.*

2.8 Strategy 8: Use the Library’s Digital Archives & Special Collections

Montclair State has unique historical and cultural collections, including:

  • New Jersey Digital Highway (state archives).
  • African American History Collection.
  • Women’s Studies Archives.

How to Access:

  • Search "Special Collections" on the library website.
  • Request digital scans of rare materials.

Common Mistake: Ignoring niche collections that could enhance a thesis or paper. Solution: Browse the Digital Collections page—you might find exclusive primary sources no one else is using.

2.9 Strategy 9: Optimize Your Search with Advanced Techniques

Beyond basic searches, advanced techniques can cut research time in half:

✅ Truncation (*) – Search **"womn" to find "woman," "women," "womanhood." ✅ Phrase Searching (" ") – Search "climate change impacts" (not just separate words). ✅ Limiting by Date/Publication Type – Narrow results to "2020–Present" or "Peer Reviewed." ✅ Using "Related Articles" – In JSTOR or Google Scholar, click "Cited by" to find similar research.

Real-World Example: A sociology student researching "youth mental health" used truncation ("youth" and "mental health") to find 100+ relevant studies in minutes—whereas a broad search would yield thousands of irrelevant results.*

2.10 Strategy 10: Avoid Plagiarism with Library Resources

Plagiarism is a serious academic offense, but the library helps prevent it with:

  • Turnitin integration (via Montclair’s online grading system).
  • Citation guides (APA, MLA, Chicago).
  • Plagiarism workshops (offered by the Writing Center).

How to Avoid Plagiarism: ✔ Use quotation marks for direct quotes. ✔ Paraphrase carefully (don’t just rewrite—change structure & vocabulary). ✔ Cite every source, even paraphrased ideas.

Common Mistake: Assuming "summarizing" means "paraphrasing." Solution: Use the library’s plagiarism guide to learn proper paraphrasing techniques.

Chapter 3: Common Mistakes New Students Make (And How to Avoid Them)

Even experienced students fall into traps. Here are the biggest pitfalls and how to steer clear.

3.1 Mistake #1: Not Using the Library’s Website Properly

Problem: Many students Google their research topics instead of using Montclair’s curated databases. Solution:

  • Bookmark the library homepage (lib.montclair.edu).
  • Use the A-Z Database List for subject-specific resources.

3.2 Mistake #2: Ignoring Librarian Help Until the Last Minute

Problem: Students wait until finals week to ask for research help, leading to stress and incomplete work. Solution:

  • Schedule a research appointment early in the semester.
  • Use the 24/7 chat service for quick questions.

3.3 Mistake #3: Overlooking Digital Resources

Problem: Some students only use physical books, missing e-books, journals, and databases. Solution:

  • Check the "E-Resources" tab on the library website.
  • Use the LibApp to access materials anytime, anywhere.

3.4 Mistake #4: Not Managing Citations Properly

Problem: Students forget to cite sources or mix up citation styles, leading to plagiarism or deductions. Solution:

  • Use Zotero or RefWorks for automated citations.
  • **Follow the Montclair Citation Guide for your discipline.

3.5 Mistake #5: Failing to Renew Overdue Books

Problem: Students lose access to books due to late fees or overdue notices. Solution:

  • Renew books online via MontclairCat.
  • Set up email alerts for due dates.

3.6 Mistake #6: Not Exploring Special Collections

Problem: Students miss unique research materials in archives and special collections. Solution:

  • **Browse the Digital Collections page.
  • Request rare books via Interlibrary Loan.
