8 Actionable Strategies to Master Montclair State University Libraries

Now that you know what’s available, let’s break down how to use these resources effectively.

1. Get Your Library Card & Access All Resources Immediately

First Step: Your student ID is your library card. Activate it online via the Montclair State Libraries Portal.

How to Do It:

  • Visit the Library’s "Get Started" page.
  • Enter your NetID and password to access digital resources.
  • Download the LibApps mobile app for off-campus access to databases.

Real-World Example: Imagine you’re researching for a psychology paper on sleep disorders. Without your library card, you’d struggle to access PsycINFO (a key database). But with it, you can search 2.5 million records instantly.

Common Mistake: Assuming you can’t access resources off-campus. Fix: Always use VPN (Montclair’s Secure VPN) or LibApps for remote access.

2. Use the Library Catalog Like a Pro (Beyond Basic Searches)

The Montclair State Library Catalog is your first tool for finding books, e-books, and more. But most students only use basic keyword searches—here’s how to unlock advanced features:

A. Boolean Operators for Precision Searching

  • AND → Narrows results (e.g., "climate change AND policy").
  • OR → Broadens results (e.g., "machine learning OR AI").
  • NOT → Excludes terms (e.g., "quantum physics NOT basics").

B. Filters & Advanced Search

  • Use subject filters (e.g., "Education – Curriculum").
  • Apply date ranges (e.g., "2015-2023" for recent research).
  • Sort by relevance, date, or popularity.

Real-World Example: A business student needs case studies on Amazon’s expansion in Africa. Instead of searching "Amazon Africa", they use:

  • "Amazon" AND "case study" AND "Africa"
  • Filter by "Book" and "2018-present"
  • Result: 12 highly relevant e-books in minutes.

Common Mistake: Ignoring e-books—many students only look for physical copies. Fix: Check the "Available Online" filter to find instant access to digital texts.

3. Leverage Databases for Academic Research (Not Just Google)

Google is not your research tool. Montclair State provides premium databases like:

  • JSTOR (humanities & social sciences)
  • ScienceDirect (STEM)
  • ProQuest (business & education)
  • EBSCOhost (multidisciplinary)

How to Find the Right Database:

  1. Go to Montclair Libraries Databases A-Z.
  2. Browse by subject (e.g., "Business" → "ProQuest").
  3. Use advanced search with peer-reviewed filters.

Real-World Example: A grad student in environmental science needs data on deforestation in Brazil. Instead of Googling, they:

  • Go to ScienceDirect.
  • Search "deforestation Brazil 2020-2023".
  • Apply "Full Text" and "Peer Reviewed" filters.
  • Result: 47 high-quality scholarly articles in seconds.

Common Mistake: Not checking "peer-reviewed" status. Fix: Always verify sources—librarians can help with citation checks.

4. Use Research Assistants & Librarians for One-on-One Help

Montclair State’s librarians are experts in their fields. Don’t hesitate to ask for help!

How to Get Assistance:

  • Chat with a Librarian (available 24/7 via the library website).
  • Schedule a Research Appointment (via LibCal).
  • Visit the Research Help Desk (Library North, 2nd floor).

Real-World Example: A first-year student struggles to find primary sources on the Civil Rights Movement. They:

  • Email a history librarian for guidance.
  • The librarian suggests Archives Unbound and ProQuest Historical Newspapers.
  • Result: The student finds 15 original documents in a week.

Common Mistake: Waiting until the last minute to ask for help. Fix: Book a session early—librarians can save you hours of wasted searching.

5. Access Free Calculators & Tools to Complement Your Research

While libraries are primarily for books and databases, they also connect you to free academic tools, including:

  • Statistical calculators (for research methods)
  • Citation generators (APA, MLA, Chicago)
  • Plagiarism checkers (Turnitin access via Montclair)
  • Math & science solvers (Wolfram Alpha, Desmos)

Where to Find Them:

  • Montclair’s "Tools & Resources" page (link).
  • LibGuides (subject-specific toolkits).

Real-World Example: A psychology student needs to calculate a t-test for their experiment. Instead of using a paid software, they:

  • Use Montclair’s "Statistics Tools" guide.
  • Find a free t-test calculator linked from the library.
  • Result: Instant results with step-by-step explanations.

Common Mistake: Not knowing these tools exist. Fix: Bookmark the library’s "Tools" page for future use.

6. Utilize Study Spaces & Group Work Features

Montclair’s libraries offer multiple study environments:

  • Silent Zones (for focused work)
  • Collaborative Tables (for group projects)
  • Tech Workstations (with printers, scanners, and laptops)
  • 24/7 Study Lounges (Library North, 2nd floor)

Pro Tips:

  • Reserve a group study room via LibCal.
  • Use the "Study Space Finder" to locate quiet areas.
  • Bring your own device—libraries provide charging stations.

Real-World Example: A group of business students needs to work on a case study presentation. They:

  • Reserve a 4-person study room via LibCal.
  • Use the projector and whiteboard available in the room.
  • Result: Efficient collaboration without distractions.

Common Mistake: Assuming all spaces are open. Fix: Check availability in real-time before heading to the library.

7. Explore Special Collections & Unique Resources

Montclair’s libraries hold rare and unique materials, including:

  • Archival collections (historical documents, oral histories)
  • Government documents (federal, state, and local records)
  • Specialized databases (e.g., Artstor for visual research)

How to Access Them:

  • Visit Special Collections.
  • Request interviews with archivists for deep dives.

Real-World Example: A history student researches Prohibition-era New Jersey. They:

  • Visit the Special Collections to find original newspaper clippings.
  • Access digitized government reports on alcohol regulation.
  • Result: Unique primary sources for their thesis.

Common Mistake: Not exploring beyond standard databases. Fix: Ask librarians about "hidden" collections—they know where the gold is.

8. Use Interlibrary Loan (ILL) for Books & Articles Not in Montclair’s Collection

If Montclair doesn’t have a book or article you need, Interlibrary Loan (ILL) gets it for you for free.

How to Request:

  1. Search the library catalog—if unavailable, click "Request via ILL".
  2. Fill out the online form (takes 1-5 business days).
  3. Receive a digital copy (PDF) or physical book via campus mail.

Real-World Example: A grad student needs a rare 19th-century novel not in Montclair’s collection. They:

  • Submit an ILL request.
  • Receive the scanned chapter in 48 hours.
  • Result: No need to travel to another library.

Common Mistake: Giving up if a book isn’t available. Fix: Always check ILL—it’s free and efficient.

Common Mistakes New Students Make (And How to Avoid Them)

Even experienced students fall into these traps. Here’s how to skip the pitfalls:

Mistake Why It’s Bad How to Fix It
Only using Google for research Leads to unreliable sources and plagiarism risks. Use Montclair’s databases first.
Ignoring librarians You’re paying for expert help—don’t leave it unused. Chat, email, or schedule a session.
Not checking e-book availability Wasting time searching for physical copies that aren’t needed. Filter for "Available Online" first.
Waiting until finals to ask for help Stress increases, and librarians may be busy. Get help early—it saves time.
Not using ILL for missing resources Missing key sources because you didn’t request them. Always check ILL before giving up.
Skipping citation tools Plagiarism risks and last-minute formatting errors. Use Zotero, RefWorks, or Montclair’s citation generator.
Not exploring study spaces Distractions or lack of focus in crowded areas. Reserve a quiet zone in advance.
