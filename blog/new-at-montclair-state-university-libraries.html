Chapter 1: Getting Started – Your First Steps at Montclair State University Libraries

1.1 How to Access the Library System: Physical & Digital Entry Points

Montclair State University Libraries consist of three main locations:

  • Library North (Main Campus) – The largest branch, housing books, journals, study spaces, and the Digital Media Lab.
  • Library South – Focuses on special collections, archives, and interdisciplinary research.
  • The Learning Commons (Library North) – A collaborative space with group study rooms, tech stations, and librarian drop-in hours.

Digital Access:

  • Online Catalog (Montclair OneSearch) – Your gateway to 3 million+ books, e-books, articles, and multimedia.
  • Library Website – https://www.montclair.edu/library (Bookmark this!)
  • Mobile App (LibApps) – Available on iOS & Android, allowing you to:
    • Renew loans
    • Search for books
    • Access 24/7 digital resources

Pro Tip: Enable Google Scholar alerts and sync them with Montclair’s library catalog to get full-text access automatically.

1.2 Your First Visit: What to Expect (And How to Prepare)

Your first trip to the library should be strategic, not just exploratory. Here’s how to make the most of it:

✔ Bring your Montclair ID – Required for checkouts, printing, and accessing restricted areas. ✔ Download the LibApps mobile app before arriving to pre-search for books. ✔ Visit the Research Help Desk – Librarians are trained to assist with:

  • Finding specific sources
  • Citing properly (APA, MLA, Chicago)
  • Using databases effectively

Real-World Example: A first-year psychology major, Jamie, needed sources for a paper on "Social Media and Mental Health." Instead of browsing randomly, she:

  1. Used Montclair OneSearch to filter by "peer-reviewed journals."
  2. Asked a librarian for help narrowing down results to recent (2020–2024) studies.
  3. Found 15 relevant articles in PsycINFO and JSTOR—far more than she could’ve discovered alone.

Result? Jamie submitted an A+ paper with credible, up-to-date sources—all thanks to proactive library use.

Key Policies to Know:

Common Mistake: Assuming all books are available for immediate checkout. Fix: Check availability in Montclair OneSearch before visiting—some books may be on hold or damaged.

Chapter 3: Research Strategies – How to Find What You Need (Fast)

3.1 The Montclair Research Method: A Step-by-Step Framework

Follow this proven 5-step process to efficiently find high-quality sources:

  1. Define Your Topic (Be specific!)

    • Bad: "The effects of social media."
    • Good: "How Instagram influences body image in Gen Z females (2020–2024)."

  2. Use Montclair OneSearch (But narrow your search with filters)

    • Keywords: "Instagram AND body image AND Gen Z"
    • Limit to: "Peer-reviewed," "Full text," "2020–2024"

  3. Check Subject Databases (For deeper research)

    • PsycINFO (for psychology studies)
    • JSTOR (for academic articles)

  4. Evaluate Sources (Use the CRAAP Test)

    • Currency (Is it recent?)
    • Relevance (Does it answer your question?)
    • Authority (Who wrote it? Credentials?)
    • Accuracy (Are claims backed by evidence?)
    • Purpose (Is it biased or neutral?)

  5. Cite Properly (Use Zotero or NoodleTools for automation)

Real-World Example: A history student needed primary sources on the Civil Rights Movement. Instead of searching randomly, she:

  1. Used Montclair’s Special Collections database.
  2. **Found declassified FBI files (via ProQuest Historical Newspapers).
  3. Cited them in APA format using Zotero’s browser extension.

Result? Her **thesis received praise for uncovering rare documents others missed.

3.2 Advanced Search Techniques (Beyond Basic Keywords)

A. Boolean Operators (AND, OR, NOT)

  • "Social media AND mental health" → Finds both terms together.
  • "Instagram OR Facebook" → Finds either term.
  • "Cyberbullying NOT teenagers" → Excludes irrelevant results.

B. Truncation & Wildcards

  • "Child AND education"* → Finds child, children, childhood.
  • "Climat AND policy"* → Finds climate, climatic, climatology.

C. Field Searching

  • Title: "The Great Gatsby" in:title
  • Author: "F. Scott Fitzgerald" in:author
  • Subject: "Postmodernism" in:subject

Pro Tip: Use Google Scholar’s "Cited by" feature to find related research on a topic.

3.3 How to Get Full-Text Access When a PDF Isn’t Available

Not every article is open-access. Here’s how to unlock paywalled research:

  1. Check Montclair’s Journal List (Link) – See if the journal is subscribed to.
  2. Use Interlibrary Loan (ILL) – Request the article within 24 hours (takes 1–3 days).
  3. Ask a Librarian – They can check for alternative sources.
  4. Use Google Scholar’s "Find it @ Montclair" button – Automatically checks for full-text access.

Common Mistake: Giving up if a PDF isn’t available immediately. Fix: Most articles can be requested via ILL—just plan ahead.
