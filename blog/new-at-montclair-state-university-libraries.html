Chapter 1: Getting Started with Montclair State University Libraries

Before you can use the library effectively, you need to understand its structure, services, and key resources. Montclair State’s libraries—including The Library at the Center for Academic Success (CAS) and The Library at the School of Business—are designed to support every discipline.

1.1 Understanding the Library’s Physical & Digital Layout

Montclair State’s main library, The Library at the Center for Academic Success (CAS), is a modern, multi-floor research hub with:

  • A 24/7 study lounge (perfect for late-night study sessions)
  • Group study rooms (bookable via the library’s website)
  • Specialized collections (e.g., rare books, government documents, and STEM research materials)
  • A makerspace (for 3D printing, coding, and prototyping)

Pro Tip: Use the library’s interactive floor map (available on their website) to locate sections like:

  • Reference Desk (for immediate help)
  • Circulation Desk (to check out books)
  • Digital Media Lab (for editing videos, podcasts, and presentations)

1.2 How to Access Your Library Account

Every Montclair State student, faculty, and staff member gets a library account with:

  • Borrowing privileges (books, DVDs, reserves)
  • Fine tracking (late fees, holds)
  • Access to digital loans (e-books, audiobooks)

Steps to Set Up Your Account:

  1. Go to Montclair State Library Website
  2. Click "My Account" (top-right corner)
  3. Log in with your Montclair State username and password
  4. Verify your personal details (name, email, ID number)

Real-World Example: A first-year biology major, Priya, struggled to find her reserved lab manuals. She didn’t realize she could check her account online to see which books were held for her. After logging in, she saw a notification: "Your reserve item ‘Molecular Biology Lab Guide’ is ready at the Circulation Desk!" She picked it up within minutes, saving her from a last-minute rush.

1.3 Exploring the Library’s Website: A Step-by-Step Guide

The library’s website is your gateway to all resources, but many users skip key sections. Here’s how to navigate it like a pro:

A. The Homepage Dashboard

  • Search Bar (Top Center): The most powerful tool. Try:
    • "Climate change policies 2023-2024"
    • "Psychology of procrastination PDF"
    • "Montclair State thesis on AI ethics"
  • Quick Links: Access databases, research guides, and FAQs in one click.

B. Databases & Journals

  • Subject-Specific Databases: Instead of a broad Google search, use:
    • JSTOR (humanities & social sciences)
    • ScienceDirect (STEM research)
    • ProQuest (business & education)
  • Open Access Journals: Free, peer-reviewed articles (e.g., PLOS ONE, arXiv).

C. Research Guides

Montclair State’s LibGuides are pre-made research roadmaps for different subjects. For example:

  • Business Research Guide (for finance, marketing, and entrepreneurship)
  • STEM Research Guide (with lab manuals and scientific databases)
  • Writing & Citation Guide (APA, MLA, Chicago styles)

Pro Tip: Bookmark your subject’s LibGuide—it’s your shortcut to credible sources.

D. Digital Collections & Archives

  • Montclair State Digital Collections: Theses, dissertations, and historical documents.
  • Newspaper Archives: Access The Montclair State University Times (since 1990s) and The Star-Ledger (NJ news).

Common Mistake: Assuming everything is available online. Fix: Check "Physical Collection" in the search results—some rare books and older journals are only in the library.

Chapter 2: 10 Actionable Strategies to Master Montclair State Libraries

Now that you know the basics, let’s dive into practical strategies to make the most of your library experience.

Strategy 1: Use the Library’s Advanced Search Filters

The default search on the library website is powerful, but advanced filters help you find exactly what you need.

How to Apply Filters:

  1. Type your search term (e.g., "machine learning algorithms")
  2. Click "Advanced Search" (or use the filter icons)
  3. Refine by:
    • Date Range (e.g., last 5 years for current research)
    • Resource Type (books, articles, videos, datasets)
    • Language (English, Spanish, etc.)
    • Peer-Reviewed Only (for academic papers)

Real-World Example: A computer science graduate student, Raj, needed a 2023 paper on "deep learning in healthcare." He searched broadly and got 100+ results. By applying the date filter (2023) and peer-reviewed filter, he narrowed it down to 12 relevant articles—saving hours of irrelevant reading.

Strategy 2: Leverage Librarian Research Help

Montclair State librarians are not just bookkeepers—they’re research experts. Many offer:

  • One-on-one consultations (book via Ask a Librarian)
  • Workshops (on citation, data analysis, and literature reviews)
  • Email & chat support (24/7 via the website)

How to Get the Most Out of Librarian Help:

  • Be specific in your request (e.g., "I need help finding primary sources on the Civil Rights Movement in NJ").
  • Prepare a topic outline before your session.
  • Follow up with their recommendations.

Pro Tip: Check the library’s calendar for drop-in hours—no appointment needed!

Strategy 3: Access Digital Loans & E-Books

Montclair State provides thousands of e-books through platforms like:

  • Ebrary (now ProQuest Ebook Central)
  • EBSCOhost
  • OverDrive (for audiobooks)

How to Borrow an E-Book:

  1. Search for your title in the library catalog.
  2. Click "Access Online" (if available digitally).
  3. Some e-books allow 24-hour loans, while others let you check out for 1-2 weeks.

Common Mistake: Assuming all books are available in print. Fix: Always check the "Available Copies" section—if it says "Digital Access," download it immediately.

Strategy 4: Use Interlibrary Loan (ILL) for Missing Resources

What if Montclair State doesn’t have the book or article you need? Interlibrary Loan (ILL) lets you request materials from other universities and libraries—for free.

How to Request via ILL:

  1. Search the library catalog.
  2. If the item says "Not Available," click "Request via Interlibrary Loan."
  3. Fill out the form (usually takes 3-7 business days).

Pro Tip: For urgent needs, check:

  • Google Scholar (some PDFs are open-access)
  • ResearchGate (academics often share preprints)

Strategy 5: Utilize the Library’s Writing & Citation Tools

Citations can be time-consuming, but Montclair State offers free tools to simplify them:

  • Zotero (free reference manager)
  • RefWorks (for advanced citation needs)
  • Purdue OWL (Online Writing Lab) (APA, MLA, Chicago guides)

How to Use Zotero:

  1. Download Zotero (browser extension).
  2. Save articles, books, and websites as you research.
  3. Auto-generate citations in any format.

Real-World Example: *A history major, Sofia, was writing a 15-page thesis on "Women in the Suffrage Movement." Instead of manually citing each source, she used Zotero to:

  • Collect 47 sources in one folder.
  • Generate APA citations in seconds.
  • Insert footnotes directly into her Word document.

How to Find Workshops:

Strategy 8: Use the Library’s Study Spaces & Tech Tools

Montclair State’s libraries offer more than just books—they provide:

  • Silent study rooms (for focused work)
  • Group study pods (with whiteboards & screens)
  • Tech stations (scanners, printers, laptops)
  • 3D printers & makerspace tools (for engineering & design projects)

How to Book a Study Room:

  1. Go to Library Study Rooms.
  2. Select your preferred time slot.
  3. Bring your Montclair ID to check in.

Pro Tip: For exams or group projects, reserve a room a week in advance—they fill up fast!

Strategy 9: Access Open Educational Resources (OER)

Open Educational Resources (OER) are free, legally shareable textbooks and course materials. Montclair State’s library curates OER collections in:

  • OpenStax (math, science, English)
  • MIT OpenCourseWare (engineering, computer science)
  • Project Gutenberg (classic literature)

Why Use OER? ✔ No copyright issues (unlike some PDFs online). ✔ Updated regularly (unlike old textbooks). ✔ Cost-effective (no need to buy expensive books).

Real-World Example: *A nursing student, Priya, needed a free anatomy textbook for her human physiology class. Instead of buying a $200 textbook, she found:

  • OpenStax Anatomy & Physiology (free online).
  • Khan Academy’s medical videos (for visual learners). She saved $200+ and still aced her exams.

Strategy 10: Stay Updated with Library News & Alerts

Montclair State’s library sends weekly newsletters with:

  • New database additions
  • Upcoming workshops
  • Special collections highlights

How to Subscribe:

  1. Go to Library News.
  2. Click "Subscribe" (email or RSS feed).

Pro Tip: Set a Google Alert for "Montclair State University Library" to get instant updates on new resources.

Chapter 3: Common Mistakes New Users Make (And How to Avoid Them)

Even experienced researchers make mistakes. Here are the most frequent pitfalls and how to fix them.

Mistake 1: Skipping the "Advanced Search" Feature

Problem: Many users rely on basic keyword searches, which return irrelevant results.

Solution:

  • Use Boolean operators (AND, OR, NOT).
    • Example: "climate change AND policy NOT 2010"
  • Apply filters (date, subject, full text).

Mistake 2: Not Checking for Full-Text Availability

Problem: You find a great article, but it says "PDF unavailable." You assume it’s gone.

Solution:

  • Click "Check for Full Text" (if available digitally).
  • Use Interlibrary Loan (ILL) if not available.
  • Try Google Scholar (some PDFs are open-access).
