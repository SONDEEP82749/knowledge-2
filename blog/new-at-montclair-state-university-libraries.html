Chapter 2: 10 Actionable Strategies to Master Montclair State Libraries

Now that you understand the layout and resources, let’s dive into practical strategies to make the most of them.

Strategy 1: Use the Library’s Search Bar Like a Pro

The Montclair State University Library Search is your first stop for research, but many users don’t maximize its features.

How to Search Effectively:

  1. Start broad, then refine – Begin with a general keyword (e.g., "climate change in New Jersey"), then use filters like:
    • Date range (e.g., last 5 years for current research)
    • Source type (books, articles, videos)
    • Subject terms (e.g., "environmental policy")
  2. Use Boolean operators – Combine terms with:
    • AND (e.g., "climate change AND policy")
    • OR (e.g., "global warming OR climate change")
    • NOT (e.g., "climate change NOT New Jersey")
  3. Leverage the "Advanced Search" – This lets you search multiple databases at once (e.g., JSTOR + ProQuest).

Real-World Example: A history student researching Cold War propaganda started with the term "Cold War media." The search returned 12,000 results, but by adding "AND newspapers" and filtering for 1950-1960, she narrowed it down to 50 relevant articles in minutes.

Strategy 2: Explore Subject-Specific Databases

Not all research databases are created equal. Some are generalist (like Google Scholar), while others are niche and far more useful for specific fields.

How to Access:

Common Mistake: Students often rely only on Google Scholar, missing exclusive databases like ERIC or CINAHL, which have high-quality, vetted sources.

Strategy 3: Master the Interlibrary Loan (ILL) System

When Montclair State doesn’t have a book or article you need, Interlibrary Loan (ILL) is your lifeline.

How to Use ILL Effectively:

  1. Check availability first – Before requesting, search the library catalog to confirm the item isn’t already available.
  2. Submit requests quickly – ILL takes 3-5 business days, so plan ahead.
  3. Set up email alerts – The library will notify you when your request arrives.
  4. Return items on time – Late fees apply, and some loans have strict return windows.

Real-World Example: A PhD student in American literature needed a rare 19th-century novel not held by Montclair. By submitting an ILL request, she received a digital scan within 48 hours, allowing her to analyze the text for her dissertation.

Strategy 4: Use Zotero for Free & Efficient Citation Management

Citing sources correctly and efficiently is crucial for academic success. Zotero, a free, open-source citation manager, integrates with Montclair State Libraries to automate citations in APA, MLA, Chicago, and more.

How to Set Up Zotero:

  1. Download Zotero from zotero.org.
  2. Install the browser extension (Chrome/Firefox).
  3. Save articles from library databases – When you find a source, click "Save to Zotero" (works with JSTOR, EBSCO, and Google Scholar).
  4. Generate citations automatically – Drag and drop references into your Word/Google Docs paper.

Pro Tip: Use Zotero’s "Find Full Text" feature to automatically link to Montclair’s licensed databases when writing papers.

Common Mistake: Students manually copy-paste citations, leading to formatting errors and plagiarism risks. Zotero eliminates this by auto-formatting.
