Chapter 1: Getting Started – Your First Steps at Montclair State Libraries

1.1 How to Access the Libraries: Physical & Digital Entry Points

Montclair State University Libraries consist of three main branches:

  • The Library at the Center for Academic Success (CAS) – The largest branch, housing millions of books, journals, and multimedia resources.
  • The Music Library – Specialized in sheet music, audio recordings, and music research databases.
  • The Special Collections & University Archives – Home to rare books, historical documents, and university records.

How to Get a Library Card (If You Don’t Have One)

  • For Students & Faculty: Your Montclair State ID (or student email) automatically grants you access to all physical and digital resources.
  • For Visitors & Alumni: A guest pass can be requested via the Library Services website.
  • Off-Campus Access: Use your Montclair State credentials to log in to e-books, journals, and databases from anywhere.

Pro Tip: Bookmark the Library Homepage and set up Google Alerts for new library announcements.

1.2 Navigating the Library Website: A Step-by-Step Tour

The Montclair State Libraries website is your one-stop shop for research. Here’s how to use it effectively:

Key Sections to Explore

  1. Find Resources

    • Search Bar: The main search tool (called "Montclair Search") lets you find books, articles, videos, and more in one go.
    • Advanced Search: Filter by date, subject, format (e.g., e-book, journal), and more.
    • Subject Guides: Curated lists of recommended databases and resources by discipline (e.g., Business, Psychology, Education).

  2. Databases A-Z

    • Over 500+ specialized databases (e.g., JSTOR for humanities, ScienceDirect for STEM, ProQuest for dissertations).
    • Example: If you’re researching climate change, try GreenFILE or Environment Complete.

  3. My Library Account

    • Track loans, renewals, and holds.
    • Set up automatic notifications for due dates.

  4. Research Help

    • 24/7 Chat & Email Support (via the "Ask a Librarian" button).
    • One-on-One Research Appointments (schedule via the "Book a Librarian" link).

Real-World Example: *A graduate student in Nursing needed access to peer-reviewed journal articles on telemedicine. Instead of searching Google, they used the "Nursing Research Guide" on the library website, which directed them to CINAHL Plus with Full Text—a database with 1,300+ nursing journals. This saved them hours of irrelevant searching.

Chapter 2: 10 Actionable Strategies to Master Montclair State Libraries

2.1 Strategy 1: Use the "Montclair Search" Like a Pro

What it is: The unified search tool that scans books, articles, videos, and more across all library resources.

How to optimize it:

  • Be specific with keywords (e.g., instead of "global warming", try "anthropogenic climate change impacts on Arctic ecosystems").
  • Use Boolean operators (AND, OR, NOT) to refine results.
    • Example: "machine learning" AND "healthcare" NOT "AI".
  • Check the filters on the left sidebar for date, format, and subject.

Common Mistake: Assuming Google is better for academic research. Google Scholar is useful, but Montclair Search provides full-text access to paid journals.

2.2 Strategy 2: Leverage Subject-Specific Databases

Why it matters: General search tools can be overwhelming. Specialized databases give you high-quality, curated content.

Top Databases by Discipline:

Real-World Example: *A History major needed sources on the American Revolution. Instead of using Google, they used America: History & Life, which provided 500+ relevant scholarly articles—many with full-text access.

2.3 Strategy 3: Master the Art of Interlibrary Loan (ILL)

What it is: If Montclair doesn’t have a book or article you need, ILL lets you borrow it from another library.

How to request:

  1. Search for the item in Montclair Search.
  2. If it says "Not Available", click "Request via Interlibrary Loan".
  3. Fill out the form with your Montclair email and delivery preferences.
  4. Turnaround time: Usually 3-7 business days (sometimes faster for articles).

Pro Tip: For urgent needs, check if the item is available at NJSHARE (a consortium of New Jersey libraries). Some items arrive in 24-48 hours.

Common Mistake: Giving up if an item isn’t immediately available. ILL is free and widely used—don’t hesitate to request!

2.4 Strategy 4: Utilize E-Books & Digital Resources

Montclair offers thousands of e-books accessible 24/7 from anywhere.

How to find them:

  • Use the "Books & eBooks" filter in Montclair Search.
  • Try eBook Collections:
    • EBSCO eBooks (business, health, science)
    • Project MUSE (humanities & social sciences)
    • O’Reilly for Higher Education (tech & coding)

Real-World Example: *A Computer Science student needed a reference book on algorithms for a project. Instead of buying a $100 textbook, they found "Introduction to Algorithms" in O’Reilly, which they could download, annotate, and cite directly.

2.5 Strategy 5: Attend Library Workshops & Research Help Sessions

The library offers free workshops on:

  • Citation management (Zotero, EndNote)
  • Literature reviews
  • Data analysis (SPSS, R, Python)
  • Thesis & dissertation support

How to sign up:

  • Check the Library Events Calendar.
  • Request a custom research consultation via the "Ask a Librarian" form.

Common Mistake: Waiting until the last minute to seek help. Proactive research support can prevent wasted time and improve paper quality.

2.6 Strategy 6: Use the Library’s Study Spaces Wisely

Montclair Libraries offer multiple study environments:

  • Group Study Rooms (reservable via the Library Study Room Booking System).
  • Silent Study Zones (for focused work).
  • Collaboration Spaces (with whiteboards & tech).
  • 24/7 Study Lounge (in the CAS Library).

Pro Tip:

  • Book a study room in advance for group projects.
  • Use the "Quiet Hours" (typically 2 PM - 4 PM) for deep work.

Real-World Example: *A Business student needed to collaborate on a case study with three teammates. They booked a group study room for 3 hours, which included a projector and whiteboard—perfect for brainstorming.

2.7 Strategy 7: Cite Your Sources Correctly (Avoid Plagiarism)

The library provides free citation tools to help you format references properly.

Tools to Use:

  • Zotero (free, open-source, great for group projects).
  • EndNote (paid, but powerful for large bibliographies).
  • EasyBib (simple, web-based).

Common Citation Styles:

Style When to Use
APA Psychology, Education, Social Sciences
MLA Humanities (Literature, History, Arts)
Chicago History, Business, some STEM fields
AMA Medicine & Healthcare

Real-World Example: *A Psychology student used Zotero to automatically generate APA citations for their 15-source literature review. This saved them hours of manual formatting.

2.8 Strategy 8: Explore Special Collections & Archives

For unique primary sources, check out:

  • Montclair State University Archives (historical documents, yearbooks, alumni records).
  • Special Collections (rare books, manuscripts, and New Jersey-specific materials).

How to Access:

  • Visit the Special Collections desk in the CAS Library.
  • Request materials in advance via email to specialcollections@montclair.edu.

Real-World Example: *A History PhD student researching 1960s civil rights movements found primary sources in the archives, including letters from local activists—materials not available in any other database.

2.9 Strategy 9: Use the Library’s Tech & Printing Services

The library offers:

  • Computer workstations (with Microsoft Office, SPSS, and Adobe Creative Suite).
  • Scanners & Printers (free for students, $0.10 per page).
  • 3D Printing & MakerSpace (for engineering, art, and design projects).

Pro Tip:

  • Print from your laptop using the library’s wireless network.
  • Scan documents to your Google Drive or email for free.

Common Mistake: Not checking printing limits—some accounts have daily/weekly limits.

2.10 Strategy 10: Stay Updated with Library News & Resources

Follow these channels to stay informed:

  • Library Blog (Montclair State Library Blog) – Features research tips, new databases, and events.
  • Social Media (Instagram, Twitter, Facebook) – Quick updates on workshops and new resources.
  • Library Newsletter – Sign up via the Library Homepage.

Real-World Example: *A Graduate student missed a **workshop on data visualization because they weren’t subscribed to the newsletter. After signing up, they received an alert and attended the next session, improving their thesis data presentation.

Chapter 3: Common Mistakes New Students Make (And How to Avoid Them)

3.1 Mistake #1: Not Using the Library’s Full Potential

What happens: Students think the library is just for borrowing books and don’t explore databases, research help, or study spaces.

How to fix it:

  • Take a virtual tour of the library’s Online Resources.
  • Ask a librarian for a personalized research guide based on your major.

3.2 Mistake #2: Ignoring Interlibrary Loan (ILL) Requests

What happens: If a book isn’t available, students give up instead of requesting it.

How to fix it:

  • Set up ILL notifications so you know when your request is ready.
  • Check NJSHARE first—some items arrive faster than ILL.

3.3 Mistake #3: Poor Search Strategies

What happens: Students use vague keywords (e.g., "climate change") and get too many irrelevant results.

How to fix it:

  • Use advanced search filters (date, subject, format).
  • Try different synonyms (e.g., "global warming" vs. "climate change").

3.4 Mistake #4: Not Using Citation Tools

What happens: Students manually cite sources, leading to errors and wasted time.

How to fix it:

  • Use Zotero or EndNote to auto-generate citations.
  • Double-check citations with the Purdue OWL Citation Guide.

3.5 Mistake #5: Overlooking Study Space Reservations

What happens: Students can’t find a quiet place to study during peak hours.

How to fix it:

  • Book a study room in advance via the Library Study Room Booking System.
  • Use silent hours (usually 2 PM - 4 PM) for focused work.
