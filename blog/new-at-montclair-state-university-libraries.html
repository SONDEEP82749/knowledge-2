Chapter 1: Understanding Montclair State University Libraries – A Closer Look

Before diving into strategies, it’s essential to familiarize yourself with the libraries’ structure and offerings. Montclair State University operates three main libraries, each with unique strengths:

1. The Library at the Center for Academic & Student Excellence (CASE)

  • Location: Lower Level of CASE (Student Center)
  • Specialties:
    • General academic research (humanities, social sciences, STEM)
    • Study spaces (group and individual)
    • Tech support (printers, scanners, laptops for checkout)
    • Workshops on research skills, citation tools, and data analysis

2. The Library at the School of Business (SBL)

  • Location: School of Business Building
  • Specialties:
    • Business, economics, and finance databases (IBISWorld, Mergent Online, Statista)
    • Case studies for management and marketing research
    • Career resources (resume reviews, LinkedIn optimization guides)

3. The Library at the School of Education (SEL)

  • Location: School of Education Building
  • Specialties:
    • Education research databases (ERIC, ProQuest Education Journals)
    • Curriculum development resources
    • Special education and teaching methodology guides

Digital & Remote Access: The Library Beyond Campus

Montclair State Libraries are not just physical spaces—they offer 24/7 digital access to:

  • Over 100,000 e-books (via Ebrary, JSTOR, and ProQuest)
  • Thousands of academic journals (ScienceDirect, IEEE Xplore, PsycINFO)
  • Specialized databases (depending on your field of study)
  • Interlibrary Loan (ILL) service – request books/articles from other libraries within days

Pro Tip: Bookmark the Montclair State University Libraries Website and set up a personal account to track checkouts, renewals, and saved searches.

Chapter 2: 8 Actionable Strategies to Master Montclair State University Libraries

Now that you know what’s available, let’s explore how to use it effectively. These strategies are tested by students, professors, and librarians—implement them, and you’ll save time, reduce stress, and produce higher-quality work.

Strategy 1: Start with the Library’s Research Guides – Your First Stop for Success

Problem: Many students waste hours searching for sources without a clear direction.

Solution: Montclair State Libraries provides subject-specific research guides—essentially curated roadmaps to the best databases, journals, and resources for your field.

How to Use Them:

  1. Go to Research Guides.
  2. Select your major or topic (e.g., "Psychology," "Business Management," "Computer Science").
  3. Follow the step-by-step instructions on:
    • Where to find peer-reviewed articles
    • How to use citation tools (Zotero, EndNote)
    • Recommended databases (e.g., PsycINFO for psychology, IEEE Xplore for engineering)

Real-World Example: A senior psychology major, Sarah, was struggling to find empirical studies on anxiety disorders. Instead of randomly searching Google Scholar, she used the Psychology Research Guide, which directed her to PsycINFO and PubMed Central. In one afternoon, she found 15 relevant studies—something she couldn’t achieve in a week of aimless searching.

Common Mistake: Skipping the research guides and jumping straight into Google Scholar or Wikipedia. This leads to low-quality sources, citation errors, and wasted time.

Strategy 2: Learn the Art of Advanced Searching (Beyond Basic Keywords)

Problem: Most students use simple keyword searches, which return irrelevant results or miss critical studies.

Solution: Advanced search techniques help you narrow down results to highly relevant, peer-reviewed sources.

Key Techniques:

Technique How to Apply It Example
Boolean Operators Use AND, OR, NOT to refine searches. "anxiety disorders AND cognitive behavioral therapy NOT case studies"
Field Searching Limit searches to titles, abstracts, or authors. Title: "stress management" AND Author: "Smith"
Truncation (*) Find variations of a word. "psycholog" = psychology, psychologist, psychological
Date Ranges Focus on recent research (2018–2024). "climate change" 2020..2024
Subject Headings Use controlled vocabulary from databases. In JSTOR, search under "Environmental Policy" instead of random terms.

Real-World Example: *A business student, James, needed market analysis reports on electric vehicle adoption in New Jersey. Instead of searching "electric cars market," he used:

  • "electric vehicles" AND "New Jersey" AND "2023"
  • Limited to "Reports" in the database (IBISWorld) This returned exactly 3 relevant reports—whereas a basic search yielded thousands of unrelated results.

Common Mistake: Ignoring peer-reviewed filters and settling for popular press articles (e.g., Forbes, Business Insider). Always check the "Peer-Reviewed" checkbox in databases like Google Scholar or ProQuest.

Strategy 3: Use Zotero or EndNote to Organize & Cite Like a Pro

Problem: Manually tracking sources leads to lost citations, formatting errors, and last-minute panic.

Solution: Reference managers like Zotero (free) or EndNote (paid) automatically store, organize, and format citations in APA, MLA, Chicago, and more.

How to Set It Up:

  1. Download Zotero (https://www.zotero.org/) and install the browser extension.
  2. Save articles directly from databases (e.g., JSTOR, ScienceDirect) into Zotero.
  3. Create folders by topic (e.g., "Thesis Chapter 2," "Literature Review").
  4. Generate citations in seconds when writing papers.

Real-World Example: *A graduate student, Priya, was working on a 150-page dissertation. Without Zotero, she would have spent weeks formatting citations manually. Instead, she:

  • Saved 200+ sources in Zotero.
  • Auto-generated APA citations as she wrote.
  • Avoided plagiarism by tracking exact page numbers.

Common Mistake: Waiting until the last week to organize citations. Start early—even if you only have 10 sources, set up Zotero now.

Strategy 4: Leverage Librarians as Your Free Research Partners

Problem: Many students don’t know that librarians offer one-on-one research help.

Solution: Librarians are experts in finding hidden gems in databases. They can: ✔ Help you refine search strategies ✔ Teach you advanced database features ✔ Point you to unpublished or gray literature (working papers, conference proceedings)

How to Access Help:

  • Book a research consultation (https://www.montclair.edu/library/research-help/)
  • Visit the Research Desk (CASE Library, Lower Level)
  • Chat with a librarian (available 24/7 via the website)

Real-World Example: *A computer science student, Raj, was researching quantum computing algorithms. He tried Google Scholar and IEEE Xplore for hours but couldn’t find recent conference papers. He scheduled a 30-minute consultation with a librarian, who:

  • Showed him how to search arXiv (a preprint server for physics/CS).
  • Taught him to use Google Scholar’s "Cited by" feature to find related work.
  • Found 5 key papers he hadn’t seen before.

Common Mistake: Assuming librarians are only for undergrads or basic help. Grad students and faculty use them all the time for specialized research.

Strategy 5: Explore Open Access & Free Resources (Beyond Paywalled Journals)

Problem: Some essential articles are behind paywalls, making research expensive or impossible.

Solution: Open Access (OA) resources provide free, legal access to scholarly work.

Where to Find Free Resources:

Resource Best For How to Access
Google Scholar Broad research https://scholar.google.com
arXiv Physics, CS, Math https://arxiv.org
Directory of Open Access Journals (DOAJ) Peer-reviewed OA articles https://doaj.org
Montclair State’s Open Access Repository MSU-affiliated research https://digitalcommons.montclair.edu

Real-World Example: *A biology major, Emily, needed genetic research on CRISPR gene editing. Most articles were paywalled, but her librarian suggested:

  • PubMed Central (free full-text articles).
  • arXiv (preprint versions of key studies). She found 12 free articles in one day—something she couldn’t do with paid databases alone.

Common Mistake: Giving up when you hit a paywall. Always check for free alternatives before paying.
