Chapter 1: Getting Started – Your First Steps at Montclair State Libraries

Before diving into research, familiarize yourself with the library’s structure, services, and resources. Here’s how to begin:

1.1 Locate the Libraries & Understand Their Layout

Montclair State University operates three main libraries:

  • John J. Cali School of Music Library (for music research)
  • University Library (Main Branch) (general research & study spaces)
  • Science & Mathematics Library (STEM-focused resources)

Pro Tip: Use the Montclair Libraries Map to plan your visits. The University Library is the largest, with study pods, silent floors, and group collaboration zones.

1.2 Get Your Library Card & Access Permissions

  • Current students/faculty automatically receive a library card via their Montclair email.
  • Alumni & community members can apply for a borrowing card (with restrictions).
  • Off-campus access? Use your Montclair username and password to log in to e-books, journals, and databases.

Real-World Example: A first-year biology student, Sarah, struggled to access her textbook online. She contacted the Library Help Desk, and within 24 hours, her proxy login was reset, allowing her full access to ScienceDirect and JSTOR.

1.3 Explore the Library Website & Key Features

The Montclair State University Libraries Website is your one-stop shop for: ✅ Catalog Search (find books, e-books, and media) ✅ Databases A-Z (subject-specific research tools) ✅ Research Guides (curated by librarians for your major) ✅ Interlibrary Loan (ILL) (borrow from other libraries) ✅ Library Hours & Events (workshops, study sessions)

Actionable Tip: Bookmark the Research Guides—they’re tailored by department, making it easier to find relevant sources for your field.

Chapter 2: 10 Proven Strategies to Master Montclair State Libraries

Now that you’re set up, let’s dive into actionable techniques to make the most of your library experience.

2.1 Strategy 1: Use Advanced Search Filters Like a Pro

The library catalog isn’t just for basic searches. Refine your results with:

  • Subject filters (e.g., "Psychology," "Computer Science")
  • Date ranges (limit to 2020–2024 for recent research)
  • Format (e-books, videos, datasets)
  • Availability (check in-library vs. online)

Example: *A history student researching Cold War propaganda could filter by "1950–1990" and "primary sources" to find declassified documents in ProQuest Historical Newspapers.

2.2 Strategy 2: Leverage Librarian-Curated Research Guides

Montclair’s Research Guides are goldmines of pre-organized resources. Each guide includes:

  • Recommended databases (e.g., PsycINFO for psychology, IEEE Xplore for engineering)
  • Citation styles (APA, MLA, Chicago)
  • Tutorials & how-to videos
  • Subject-specific tips

How to Find Yours:

  1. Go to Research Guides
  2. Search by your major (e.g., "Business," "Nursing," "Philosophy")
  3. Bookmark the guide for quick access

Common Mistake: Students often overlook these guides, wasting time searching randomly. Solution: Spend 10 minutes reviewing your guide before starting a project.

2.3 Strategy 3: Master the Art of Database Searching

Not all databases are created equal. Here’s how to pick the right one:

Subject Best Database Why?
Business Business Source Premier Full-text journals, market reports
Health Sciences CINAHL Nursing & allied health research
STEM ScienceDirect, IEEE Xplore Peer-reviewed papers, patents
Humanities JSTOR, Project MUSE Scholarly articles, literary criticism

Pro Tip: Use Boolean operators (AND, OR, NOT) to narrow or expand searches.

  • "Climate change AND policy" (fewer, more relevant results)
  • "Artificial intelligence OR AI" (broader search)

2.4 Strategy 4: Access E-Books & Digital Resources Efficiently

Montclair offers thousands of e-books via Ebsco, ProQuest, and OverDrive. To find them:

  1. Search the catalog → Filter by "eBook"
  2. Use the "Read Online" button (no download needed)
  3. **Check for 24/7 access (most e-books allow unlimited checkouts)

Real-World Example: *A graduate student in Education needed 30 e-books for a thesis. Instead of printing, she used Montclair’s e-book platform, saving $150+ and reducing carbon footprint.

2.5 Strategy 5: Utilize Interlibrary Loan (ILL) for Missing Resources

If Montclair doesn’t have a book or article, Interlibrary Loan (ILL) gets it for you—for free!

  • Turnaround time: 3–5 business days (sometimes faster)
  • Cost: No fees (covered by Montclair)
  • How to Request:
    1. Search the catalog → Click "Request via ILL"
    2. Fill out the form → Wait for notification

Warning: Some ILL requests take longer if the lending library is busy. Plan ahead!

2.6 Strategy 6: Attend Library Workshops & Research Help Sessions

Montclair offers free workshops on:

  • Citation management (Zotero, EndNote)
  • Data analysis (SPSS, R)
  • Academic writing & avoiding plagiarism

How to Join:

  • Check Library Events Calendar
  • Register via Montclair’s event portal

Example: *A computer science student attended a "Python for Data Science" workshop, which helped her automate literature reviews for her thesis.

2.7 Strategy 7: Use AI & Library Tools for Smarter Research

Montclair integrates AI-powered tools to enhance research:

  • RefWorks (automated citations)
  • Google Scholar + Montclair’s "Find It" button (links to full-text articles)
  • Librarian chatbots (instant Q&A via Ask a Librarian)

How to Use AI Effectively:

  • Summarize articles with JSTOR’s AI tools
  • Generate citations in Zotero’s browser extension
  • Ask librarians for AI-assisted literature reviews

2.8 Strategy 8: Optimize Your Study Spaces

Montclair’s libraries offer multiple study environments:

  • Silent Study Floors (University Library, 2nd floor)
  • Group Study Rooms (reservable via Room Scheduling)
  • Tech Zones (with scanners, 3D printers, and recording studios)

Pro Tip: Reserve a study room in advance during exam weeks to avoid last-minute stress.

2.9 Strategy 9: Preserve Your Research with Digital Archives

Montclair’s Digital Commons allows you to:

  • Upload your thesis/dissertation (permanent online archive)
  • Share research with the public
  • Gain visibility (Google Scholar citations)

How to Submit:

  1. Go to Montclair Digital Commons
  2. Follow the submission guidelines for your department

2.10 Strategy 10: Stay Updated with Library News & Alerts

Montclair sends weekly emails with:

  • New database additions
  • Upcoming workshops
  • Special collections (e.g., rare books, archives)

How to Subscribe:

  • Sign up via Library Newsletter

Chapter 3: Common Mistakes & How to Avoid Them

Even experienced researchers make mistakes. Here’s how to steer clear of them:

Mistake Why It’s Bad How to Fix It
Ignoring the Research Guide Wasting time on irrelevant sources Always check your department’s guide first
Not Using Boolean Operators Too many irrelevant results Use "AND," "OR," "NOT" to refine searches
Assuming All E-Books Are Free Some require Montclair login Check the "Access" tab before downloading
Skipping Interlibrary Loan Missing critical sources Request via ILL if Montclair doesn’t have it
Not Asking for Help Struggling alone when librarians can assist Use "Ask a Librarian" chat or email

Real-World Example: *A law student tried to download a legal journal without checking access. She realized it was only available on-campus—she visited the library instead of waiting until she could access it remotely.
