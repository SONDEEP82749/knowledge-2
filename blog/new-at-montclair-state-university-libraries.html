Chapter 1: Getting Started – How to Access Montclair State University Libraries

Before diving into strategies, you need to know how to access the libraries effectively. Here’s your step-by-step guide to setting up and navigating the system.

1.1 How to Get Your Library Card & Access Online Resources

Every Montclair State student, faculty, and staff member automatically receives library privileges, but you must activate your account to access resources.

Steps to Set Up Your Library Account:

  1. Log in to your Montclair State Portal (my.montclair.edu).
  2. Navigate to "Library & Learning Commons" under the "Resources" tab.
  3. Click "Library Account" and enter your Montclair State username and password.
  4. Verify your email for a confirmation link (this may take up to 24 hours).
  5. Once activated, you can:
    • Browse the catalog (Montclair State Libraries Catalog)
    • Check out books (both physical and digital)
    • Renew loans online
    • Access databases from anywhere

⚠️ Common Mistake: Many students forget to check their Montclair State email for library account activation emails. If you don’t receive one within 24 hours, contact the Library Help Desk at libhelp@montclair.edu.

1.3 How to Find & Use the Library Catalog

The Montclair State Libraries Catalog is your first stop for finding books, e-books, journals, and more.

Key Features of the Catalog:

  • Advanced Search Filters (by subject, date, format, etc.)
  • Hold & Recall System (request books from other libraries via Interlibrary Loan)
  • E-book Access (filter by "Available Online")
  • Subject Guides (pre-made research paths for your major)

Example Search Workflow:

Let’s say you’re researching "climate change policies in New Jersey" for a Political Science 101 paper.

  1. Go to the catalog (library.montclair.edu).
  2. Type in keywords: "climate change" AND "New Jersey" AND "policy".
  3. Apply filters:
    • Subject: "Environmental Policy"
    • Date: Last 5 years (2019-2024)
    • Format: Books + E-books
  4. Check for relevant titles like:
    • "New Jersey’s Climate Action Plan: A Case Study" (2023, e-book)
    • "Sustainable Governance in the Northeast" (2022, print)
  5. Click "Hold" on the e-book to access it immediately.

⚠️ Common Mistake: Not using Boolean operators (AND, OR, NOT) can lead to too many irrelevant results. Always refine your search with specific keywords.

Chapter 2: 10 Actionable Strategies to Master Montclair State Libraries

Now that you know how to access the libraries, let’s dive into practical strategies to maximize your research, study, and academic success.

Strategy 1: Leverage the Subject Guides for Your Major

Montclair State Libraries provides pre-made research guides for every academic department. These are curated by librarians to help you find reliable, discipline-specific sources.

How to Find Your Subject Guide:

  1. Go to Research Guides.
  2. Search by your major (e.g., "Business Administration," "Psychology," "Computer Science").
  3. Example: The Business Guide includes:
    • Recommended databases (IBISWorld, Statista, Business Source Premier)
    • Key journals (Harvard Business Review, Journal of Management)
    • Citation guides (APA, MLA, Chicago for business reports)

💡 Real-World Example: A Marketing student researching "brand loyalty in Gen Z" can use the Business Guide to find:

  • Market research reports from IBISWorld
  • Peer-reviewed articles on consumer behavior in Business Source Premier
  • Case studies from Harvard Business Review

⚠️ Common Mistake: Assuming Google Scholar is enough. While useful, librarian-curated guides provide high-quality, vetted sources that Google Scholar may not.

Strategy 2: Use Databases Effectively (Beyond Google Scholar)

Google Scholar is convenient, but university databases offer more reliable, full-text access to peer-reviewed journals, dissertations, and industry reports.

Top Databases at Montclair State:

Database Best For How to Access
JSTOR Humanities, Social Sciences, Arts Link (via Montclair login)
ProQuest Business, Health, Education Link
ScienceDirect STEM, Life Sciences Link
PsycINFO Psychology Research Link
Statista Market Data, Business Analytics Link

How to Search Like a Pro:

  1. Use advanced search (avoid vague terms like "climate change"—instead, try "renewable energy adoption in Europe").
  2. Filter by:
    • Full-text availability (ensures you can download the article)
    • Date range (limit to the last 5 years for current research)
    • Peer-reviewed only (for academic papers)
  3. Save & organize your results using Zotero, EndNote, or Mendeley (more on this in Strategy 6).

💡 Real-World Example: A Nursing student studying "telehealth adoption during COVID-19" can use:

  • CINAHL (for nursing-specific research)
  • PubMed (for medical studies)
  • ProQuest Nursing & Allied Health (for industry reports)

⚠️ Common Mistake: Not checking "Full Text" before clicking. Many database results are abstracts only—you must pay or request via Interlibrary Loan for the full article.

Strategy 3: Master Interlibrary Loan (ILL) for Books & Articles You Can’t Find

Not every resource is available at Montclair State. Interlibrary Loan (ILL) lets you borrow books and request articles from other universities and libraries nationwide.

How to Use ILL:

  1. Search the Montclair Catalog—if a book isn’t available, click "Request via Interlibrary Loan."
  2. For articles, use Google Scholar or a database, then click "Get It @ Montclair" (if available) or "Request via ILL."
  3. Check your ILL account (ILL Portal) for updates.
  4. Articles arrive in 1-3 days; books take 1-2 weeks.

💡 Real-World Example: *A Political Science student needs "The Rise of Populism in Europe" (2021), but Montclair doesn’t own it.

  • They request it via ILL and receive a PDF within 48 hours.
  • If they need the physical book, it arrives in 10 days (free shipping!).

⚠️ Common Mistake: Assuming ILL is slow. While books take time, articles usually arrive within 24-48 hours if requested early.

Strategy 4: Utilize E-Books & Digital Resources (No Late Fees!)

Montclair State offers thousands of e-books through Ebsco, ProQuest, and JSTOR, meaning you can access them anytime, anywhere—no late fees!

How to Find & Use E-Books:

  1. Go to the catalog and filter by "Available Online."
  2. Use Ebsco e-Books (Link) for subject-specific collections.
  3. Check out for 1-7 days (depending on the platform).
  4. Download or read online (some allow offline access via apps like Ebsco Mobile).

💡 Real-World Example: *A Business student needs "Digital Marketing Strategy" for a case study.

  • They find the e-book in Ebsco, check it out for 7 days, and download it to their tablet for offline reading.

⚠️ Common Mistake: Not checking concurrent user limits. Some e-books only allow 1-3 users at a time—if someone else has it, try a different source.

Strategy 6: Use Citation Managers (Zotero, EndNote, Mendeley)

Manually citing sources is time-consuming and error-prone. Citation managers help you organize, annotate, and format references automatically.

Top Citation Managers at Montclair State:

Tool Best For How to Access
Zotero Free, easy to use Zotero Guide
EndNote Advanced researchers EndNote Guide
Mendeley Collaborative research Mendeley Guide

How to Set Up Zotero (Step-by-Step):

  1. Download Zotero (zotero.org).
  2. Install the browser extension (Chrome/Firefox).
  3. Save references by clicking the Zotero icon while browsing.
  4. Organize into folders (e.g., "Thesis Research," "Class Papers").
  5. Generate citations in APA, MLA, Chicago, etc. with one click.

💡 Real-World Example: *A History student is writing a 15-page thesis on "The American Revolution."

  • They use Zotero to collect 50+ sources from JSTOR, Google Scholar, and Montclair’s catalog.
  • When writing, they drag sources into their paper and auto-generate citations in Chicago Style.

⚠️ Common Mistake: Not backing up your Zotero library. Sync with Zotero Cloud or export regularly to avoid losing work.
