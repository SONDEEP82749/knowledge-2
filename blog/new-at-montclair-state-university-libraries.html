1. Start with the Library Website: Your Digital Gateway

The Montclair State University Libraries website is your first stop—it’s not just a catalog; it’s a research powerhouse.

✅ How to Use It Effectively:

  • Search the Library Catalog (use advanced search filters for format, date, or subject).
  • Explore Databases by Subject—instead of searching Google Scholar blindly, use pre-curated academic databases like:
    • JSTOR (humanities & social sciences)
    • ScienceDirect (STEM)
    • ProQuest (business & education)
  • Check for Full-Text Availability—many articles are directly accessible via the "PDF" or "HTML" links.

Real-World Example: A graduate student in psychology was struggling to find peer-reviewed articles on "cognitive behavioral therapy in adolescents." Instead of searching Google, they used the Psychology Databases section on the library website and found 12 relevant studies in under 10 minutes—all with full-text access.

3. Use Zotero or RefWorks for Citation Management (No More Last-Minute Panic!)

Citations are every student’s nightmare—but Montclair State Libraries support citation tools like Zotero (free) and RefWorks (paid but accessible via the library).

✅ Why You Need It:

  • Automatically generates citations in APA, MLA, Chicago, and more.
  • Organizes sources in folders (e.g., "Thesis Sources," "Coursework").
  • Syncs across devices (great for remote work).

Real-World Example: A senior thesis writer was overwhelmed by 50 sources and risked plagiarism by mixing formats. After setting up Zotero, they automatically formatted all citations in APA 7th edition and exported a bibliography in one click.

Pro Tip:

  • Download the Zotero browser extension to auto-save articles from databases.
  • Use the "Save to Zotero" button in library databases (many have this feature).

7. Use Interlibrary Loan (ILL) for Books & Articles Not in Montclair’s Collection

Not every resource is in Montclair’s catalog—but that doesn’t mean you’re out of luck. Interlibrary Loan (ILL) lets you borrow books from other libraries (usually within 3–5 business days).

✅ How to Request ILL:

  1. Search the Montclair catalog—if the item says "Not Available," request it via ILL.
  2. Fill out the online form with your student ID and contact info.
  3. Track your request via your library account.

Real-World Example: A business student needed a rare 1980s economic report not held by Montclair. Via ILL, they received a scanned PDF in 48 hours—no wait for shipping!

Pro Tip:

  • Check the "Availability" tab in the catalog before requesting—sometimes items are held by another campus library and can be transferred instantly.

8. Learn How to Evaluate Sources (Avoiding Misinformation Traps)

With endless information online, knowing how to spot credible sources is critical. Montclair’s librarians teach the CRAAP Test (Currency, Relevance, Authority, Accuracy, Purpose).

✅ How to Apply It:

Factor What to Look For Example
Currency Is the info up-to-date? A 2020 study on climate change is better than a 2005 report.
Relevance Does it answer your research question? A general psychology textbook won’t help with neuropsychology.
Authority Who wrote it? Peer-reviewed? A .gov or .edu source is more reliable than a random blog.
Accuracy Are the facts verifiable? Cross-check with multiple sources.
Purpose Is it biased or neutral? A corporate report on renewable energy may downplay fossil fuels.

Common Mistake & Fix: ❌ Mistake: Using Wikipedia as a primary source. ✅ Fix: Use Wikipedia to find key terms, then search academic databases for peer-reviewed articles.
