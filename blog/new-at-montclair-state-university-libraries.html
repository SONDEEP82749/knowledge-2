Chapter 1: Understanding Montclair State University Libraries – Structure & Key Features

Montclair State University Libraries consist of three main branches, each serving distinct academic needs:

  1. The Library at the Center for Academic & Student Excellence (CASE)

    • The primary research hub for undergraduate and graduate students.
    • Houses special collections, including rare books, archives, and digital humanities resources.
    • Offers group study rooms, silent study zones, and 24/7 access during peak semesters.

  2. The Library at the School of Business

    • Specializes in business, economics, and finance resources.
    • Features Bloomberg Terminals (for real-time financial data) and Wall Street Journal archives.
    • Ideal for business majors, entrepreneurs, and finance research.

  3. The Library at the School of Education

    • Focuses on educational theory, curriculum development, and teaching resources.
    • Includes ERIC (Education Resources Information Center) and ProQuest Education databases.
    • A must-visit for future teachers, counselors, and education researchers.

Key Digital & Physical Resources Available

Montclair State Libraries provide over 500,000 physical volumes and millions of digital articles, e-books, and streaming media. Some of the most valuable resources include:

  • JSTOR – A goldmine for humanities and social sciences research.
  • Project MUSE – High-quality literature, philosophy, and cultural studies journals.
  • ScienceDirect & IEEE Xplore – Essential for STEM and engineering students.
  • Statista – The best database for market research, statistics, and business analytics.
  • OverDrive & Hoopla – Free e-books and audiobooks for leisure reading.

Pro Tip: Bookmark the Montclair State Libraries Website and set up an account to access interlibrary loan (ILL) services, which allow you to request books from other institutions if Montclair doesn’t have them.

Chapter 2: 10 Actionable Strategies to Master Montclair State University Libraries

1. Start with the Library’s Search Portal (OneSearch)

Montclair State’s OneSearch is the first tool you should use when beginning research. It aggregates books, journal articles, videos, and primary sources into a single searchable database.

How to Use It Effectively:

  • Use Boolean operators (AND, OR, NOT) to refine searches.
    • Example: "climate change" AND "policy" NOT "economic"
  • Filter results by date, source type (peer-reviewed journals only), and subject.
  • Save frequently used searches as alerts to get new results automatically.

Real-World Example: A political science student researching "gun control legislation" could narrow their search to:

  • Peer-reviewed journals (for academic credibility)
  • Last 5 years (to stay current)
  • Full-text available (to avoid paywalls)

2. Leverage Librarians as Research Partners

Montclair State Libraries employ subject specialists—librarians who are experts in specific fields (e.g., psychology, engineering, history). They can:

  • Help you find the best databases for your topic.
  • Teach you advanced search techniques.
  • Review your annotated bibliographies and thesis outlines.

How to Access Them:

  • Visit the "Ask a Librarian" page on the library website.
  • Schedule a one-on-one consultation via email or Zoom.
  • Attend library workshops (often held during orientation and midterms).

Common Mistake: Assuming you can figure everything out alone. Many students waste hours searching ineffectively when a 15-minute consultation could save weeks of work.

3. Use Zotero or RefWorks for Citation Management

Manually tracking citations is time-consuming and error-prone. Zotero (free) and RefWorks (paid, but Montclair offers access) automatically:

  • Save citations from databases and websites.
  • Format references in APA, MLA, Chicago, and more.
  • Generate bibliographies in seconds.

How to Set It Up:

  1. Download Zotero from zotero.org.
  2. Install the Zotero browser connector (Chrome/Firefox).
  3. Drag-and-drop citations from Google Scholar or OneSearch into your library.
  4. Use the Word/Google Docs plugin to insert citations while writing.

Real-World Example: A history student writing a paper on the American Revolution could:

  • Find a primary source in the Library of Congress Digital Collections.
  • Add it to Zotero with a full citation.
  • Later, auto-generate an MLA-formatted works cited page without manual copying.

4. Explore Special Collections & Archives

Montclair State’s Special Collections hold rare books, manuscripts, and historical documents that are not available in standard databases. Some highlights:

  • New Jersey History Collections – Diaries, letters, and photographs from NJ’s past.
  • African American Studies Archives – Oral histories and civil rights documents.
  • Women’s Studies Collection – Feminist literature and activist materials.

How to Access Them:

  • Check the Special Collections Guide for available materials.
  • Request digital scans of fragile documents.
  • Attend exhibits and lectures hosted by the library.

Pro Tip: If you’re writing a senior thesis or independent study, these collections can provide unique primary sources that set your work apart.

5. Utilize the Library’s Study Spaces Strategically

Montclair State Libraries offer multiple study environments, each suited for different needs:

Study Space Best For Tips for Use
Silent Study Rooms Deep focus (exams, writing) Arrive early—these book quickly!
Group Study Rooms Collaborative projects Reserve in advance via the library website.
Computer Labs Digital research Check for printer/copier availability.
Quiet Zones (CASE Library) Reading & light studying Avoid loud conversations.
24/7 Study Lounge (CASE) Late-night work Bring a charger—outlets are limited.

Common Mistake: Assuming all study rooms are first-come, first-served. Many require advance reservations, especially during finals week.

6. Take Advantage of Digital Accessibility Tools

Montclair State Libraries provide assistive technologies to support students with disabilities, including:

  • Text-to-speech software (NaturalReader, Kurzweil).
  • Screen readers (JAWS, NVDA).
  • Alternative formats (large print, braille, audiobooks).

How to Access Them:

  • Visit the Accessibility Services page.
  • Request alternative formats for textbooks or research materials.
  • Use librarian consultations to explore tools tailored to your needs.

Real-World Example: A visually impaired student researching disability rights could:

  • Use Kurzweil 3000 to read PDFs aloud.
  • Request audio versions of key textbooks.
  • Access described videos from the library’s Kanopy streaming service.

7. Use Interlibrary Loan (ILL) for Books Not in Montclair’s Collection

If Montclair doesn’t have a book you need, Interlibrary Loan (ILL) lets you borrow it from other universities and libraries—for free.

How to Request an ILL:

  1. Search OneSearch for your book.
  2. If it’s not available at Montclair, click "Request via Interlibrary Loan."
  3. Fill out the form with your Montclair email and pickup location.
  4. Wait 1–2 weeks (sometimes longer for rare items).

Pro Tip: Check the status of your request regularly. Some ILLs take longer during peak semesters.

8. Attend Library Workshops & Research Bootcamps

Montclair State Libraries host free workshops on:

  • Advanced Google Scholar searches
  • Writing literature reviews
  • Using SPSS for data analysis
  • Open-access publishing

How to Find Workshops:

  • Check the Library Events Calendar.
  • Sign up via Montclair’s Canvas portal or the library website.
  • Some workshops are recorded for later viewing.

Real-World Example: A psychology student preparing for a statistics-heavy research project could attend:

  • "Introduction to SPSS" to learn data analysis.
  • "Finding Peer-Reviewed Articles" to refine their literature search.
  • "Avoiding Plagiarism" to ensure ethical writing.

While OneSearch is powerful, some specialized databases are better for niche topics. Here’s a breakdown:

Pro Tip: Don’t limit yourself to OneSearch—explore databases by subject to find hidden gems.

10. Prepare for Finals Week: Library Survival Guide

Finals week is chaotic, but Montclair State Libraries have strategies to help you stay productive:

✅ Extend your library card – Some branches offer 24/7 access during exams. ✅ Use the "Study Break" rooms – Short walks or quiet zones help prevent burnout. ✅ Print early – Copiers and printers run out of paper during peak times. ✅ Ask for extended checkout – Some books can be kept for 2–4 weeks instead of 2. ✅ Use the "Librarian Hotline" – Some branches offer last-minute research help until closing.

Common Mistake: Waiting until the last minute to print or request books. Plan ahead!

Chapter 3: Common Mistakes New Students Make (And How to Avoid Them)

Mistake #1: Not Using Subject-Specific Databases

Problem: Many students rely only on Google Scholar or OneSearch, missing high-quality, peer-reviewed sources in specialized databases.

Solution:

  • For literature: Use JSTOR, MLA International Bibliography.
  • For business: Use Business Source Premier, IBISWorld.
  • For science: Use PubMed, IEEE Xplore.

Example: A biology student researching cancer treatments would miss clinical trial data if they only searched Google Scholar.

Mistake #2: Ignoring Librarian Consultations

Problem: Students assume they can figure out research on their own, leading to wasted time and low-quality sources.

Solution:

  • Schedule a 15-minute consultation—librarians can save you hours of inefficient searching.
  • Ask for help with citations—they can spot plagiarism risks before you submit.

Example: A history student spent three days searching for primary sources on the Civil War—a librarian could have pointed them to the Library of Congress Digital Collections in 10 minutes.

Mistake #3: Not Setting Up Alerts for New Research

Problem: Students manually check databases weekly, missing recent studies that could strengthen their work.

Solution:

  • Set up email alerts in JSTOR, Google Scholar, or OneSearch.
  • Follow key researchers on ResearchGate or Academia.edu for new publications.

Example: A sociology student tracking gender studies trends could set an alert for new articles on feminist economics and stay updated without constant searching.

Mistake #4: Overlooking Digital Accessibility Tools

Problem: Students with disabilities or learning differences may struggle with PDFs, large texts, or audiobooks.

Solution:

  • Use text-to-speech tools (NaturalReader, Read&Write).
  • Request alternative formats (large print, audio) for textbooks.
  • Ask for screen reader assistance during research consultations.

Example: A student with dyslexia could use Kurzweil 3000 to read law journal articles aloud while taking notes.

Mistake #5: Not Reserving Study Rooms in Advance

Problem: Students arrive late to group study rooms, only to find them already booked.

Solution:

  • Reserve rooms via the library website at least 24 hours in advance.
  • Check availability during peak times (evenings, weekends).

Example: A business class preparing for a group presentation could reserve a study room for 4 hours instead of scrambling for a spot last-minute.

Mistake #6: Failing to Check for Full-Text Availability

Problem: Students click on a great article, only to find it’s behind a paywall.

Solution:

  • Check the "Full Text" filter in OneSearch.
  • Use Google Scholar’s "All Versions" to find free PDFs.
  • Request articles via Interlibrary Loan if Montclair doesn’t have them.

Example: A student found a groundbreaking study on renewable energy but couldn’t access it—ILLiad (Interlibrary Loan) got it for them in 48 hours.
