Chapter 1: Getting Started – Your First Steps at Montclair State Libraries

1.1 How to Access the Libraries: Physical & Digital Entry Points

Montclair State University Libraries consist of three main locations:

  • Library North (main campus, home to the Learning Commons)
  • Library South (special collections, archives, and quiet study spaces)
  • The Innovation Lab (for tech-savvy students needing 3D printing, coding, or VR resources)

Digital access is just as seamless:

  • Online Catalog (Montclair Search) – Your gateway to books, journals, and multimedia.
  • Databases (EBSCO, JSTOR, ProQuest) – Thousands of peer-reviewed articles at your fingertips.
  • LibGuides – Subject-specific research guides created by librarians.

Pro Tip: Bookmark Montclair State Libraries’ homepage—it’s your one-stop hub for everything from research help to event calendars.

1.2 Your First Visit: What to Expect

If you’re new to campus, your first stop should be the Library North desk (or the 24/7 Help Desk if visiting after hours). Here’s what happens next:

  1. Get Your Library Card – Most students receive one automatically via their Montclair email, but if not, ask at the desk.
  2. Explore the Layout – The Learning Commons is perfect for group study, while Library South has quiet carrels for deep focus.
  3. Ask a Librarian – Yes, they’re real people who love helping students. Drop by the Research Help Desk or schedule a one-on-one appointment.

Real-World Example: *A first-year biology student, Jamie, struggled to find peer-reviewed articles for her paper. She visited the Research Help Desk, where a librarian guided her to BioOne and PubMed, saving her hours of frustration.

Chapter 2: 10 Actionable Strategies to Master Montclair State Libraries

2.1 Strategy 1: Use Montclair Search Like a Pro

Montclair Search is your primary tool for finding books, articles, and more. But many students don’t use it effectively.

How to Optimize Your Searches: ✔ Use Boolean Operators – Combine keywords with AND, OR, NOT (e.g., "climate change AND policy NOT 2020"). ✔ Filter by Source Type – Need a book? A journal article? A dataset? Use the left-hand filters. ✔ Check Full-Text Availability – If an article isn’t available, use Interlibrary Loan (ILL) (more on this later).

Common Mistake: Searching with too few keywords (e.g., just "psychology"). Instead, try: ❌ "Psychology" ✅ "cognitive behavioral therapy AND depression"

2.2 Strategy 2: Leverage Databases for Academic Success

Montclair State provides over 200 databases, but most students only use Google Scholar or JSTOR. Here’s how to unlock hidden gems:

Real-World Example: *A graduate student in education policy needed government reports on school funding. Instead of searching Google, she used ProQuest’s Policy & Government database, finding exactly what she needed in 10 minutes—something she couldn’t find in a week of Google searches.

2.3 Strategy 3: Master Interlibrary Loan (ILL) for Off-Campus Resources

What if Montclair doesn’t have the book or article you need? Interlibrary Loan (ILL) is your lifeline.

How to Use ILL:

  1. Search Montclair Search – If the item says "Not Available," note the title.
  2. Submit an ILL Request – Fill out the form at Montclair ILL Portal.
  3. Wait (usually 3-7 days) – Some items arrive electronically, others via mail.
  4. Return on Time – Fines apply if you’re late!

Pro Tip: For urgent needs, check "Priority Request"—librarians will expedite it if possible.

Common Mistake: Not checking your ILL account regularly. Set a weekly reminder to avoid missing deliveries.

2.7 Strategy 7: Learn Citation Management with Zotero or EndNote

Citing sources manually is tedious. Zotero (free) and EndNote (paid) automate citations in APA, MLA, Chicago, and more.

How to Get Started with Zotero:

  1. Download Zotero from zotero.org.
  2. Browse the Web & Save Citations (Chrome/Firefox extension).
  3. Generate Bibliographies in seconds.

Common Mistake: Not backing up your library—Zotero stores data locally. Use Zotero’s cloud sync to avoid losing work.

Chapter 3: Common Mistakes & How to Avoid Them

3.1 Mistake #1: Relying Only on Google Scholar

Why It’s Bad:

  • No peer-review filter → You might cite unreliable sources.
  • Limited full-text access → Many articles are paywalled.

Solution:

  • Use Montclair Search first → It aggregates all library resources.
  • Combine Google Scholar with library databases for full-text access.

3.2 Mistake #2: Ignoring Interlibrary Loan (ILL) Deadlines

Why It’s Bad:

  • Late returns = fines (even for digital loans).
  • Missed deadlines = delayed research.

Solution:

  • Set calendar alerts for due dates.
  • Check ILL status weekly (via ILL Portal).

3.3 Mistake #3: Not Using Subject-Specific LibGuides

Why It’s Bad:

  • Wasting time searching irrelevant databases.
  • Missing key resources (e.g., business students ignoring Bloomberg).

Solution:

  • Bookmark your department’s LibGuide (e.g., Psychology LibGuide).
  • Follow updates for new databases in your field.

3.4 Mistake #4: Skipping Workshops Because You’re Busy

Why It’s Bad:

  • Missing out on time-saving tools (e.g., Zotero, RefWorks).
  • Struggling with citations until the last minute.

Solution:

  • Attend at least one workshop per semester—even if it’s virtual.
  • Watch recordings if you can’t attend live.

3.5 Mistake #5: Not Asking for Help When Stuck

Why It’s Bad:

  • Wasting hours on wrong sources.
  • Submitting weak papers due to poor research skills.

Solution:

  • Visit the Research Help Desk (Library North, 1st floor).
  • Email a librarian with specific questions (e.g., "How do I find case studies on X?").

Chapter 4: FAQs About Montclair State University Libraries

Here are five frequently asked questions with detailed answers to help you navigate the libraries like a pro.

❓ FAQ 1: How do I find books that Montclair doesn’t have?

Answer: If Montclair doesn’t own a book, use Interlibrary Loan (ILL). Here’s how:

  1. Search Montclair Search → If it says "Not Available," note the title.
  2. Go to ILL Portal → Click "Request Item."
  3. Fill out the form (include your Montclair email and due date needs).
  4. Wait 3-7 days → You’ll get an email notification when it arrives.
  5. Access digitally (PDF) or pick up physically (if mailed).

Pro Tip: For urgent needs, select "Priority Request"—librarians may expedite it.

❓ FAQ 2: Can I use Montclair Libraries if I’m not a current student?

Answer: Yes! Alumni, faculty, and community members can access some resources with a Montclair Library Pass. Here’s how:

  1. Apply for a pass at Montclair Library Pass.
  2. Show your ID when visiting.
  3. Access digital resources (e-books, databases) off-campus with your Montclair credentials.

Limitation: Physical book checkout is student/faculty-only, but digital access is available to all pass holders.
