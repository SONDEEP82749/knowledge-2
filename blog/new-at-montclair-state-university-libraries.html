8 Actionable Strategies to Master Montclair State University Libraries

Now that you understand the value of these libraries, let’s break down practical, step-by-step strategies to help you maximize your experience.

Strategy 1: Start with the Library’s Homepage & Discover Portal

Why it matters: The Montclair State University Libraries homepage is your first stop for all digital resources, but many students overlook its full potential.

How to Use It Effectively:

  1. Bookmark the Portal

    • Save Montclair State University Libraries Portal as your homepage or a favorite link in your browser.
    • This is where you’ll find:
      • OneSearch (a unified search bar for books, articles, and databases)
      • Subject Guides (curated research resources by discipline)
      • Library Hours & Location Maps

  2. Use OneSearch Like a Pro

    • Don’t just type keywords—use advanced search filters to narrow results:
      • Date range (e.g., "2015–Present" for recent research)
      • Source type (books, journal articles, videos)
      • Full-text availability (ensures you can read immediately without interlibrary loan delays)
    • Example Search:
      • Instead of searching "climate change," try:
        • "climate change AND policy AND United States"
        • "renewable energy trends 2020–2024"

  3. Explore Subject Guides

    • Each department (Business, Education, STEM, etc.) has a dedicated guide with:
      • Recommended databases (e.g., Business students should check Business Source Premier)
      • Key journals & e-books in their field
      • Research tutorials & citation guides
    • Example: A Psychology student researching cognitive behavior therapy should visit the Psychology Subject Guide to find PsycINFO, a gold-standard database for psychology research.

Real-World Example: *A Business Economics major was struggling to find reliable financial data for a case study. Instead of searching Google, they used OneSearch → Filtered by "Business" → Selected "Statista" (a database with real-time economic metrics). This saved them weeks of manual data collection and ensured high-quality sources.

Strategy 3: Master the Art of Interlibrary Loan (ILL) & Requesting Books

Why it matters: Not every book is available at Montclair. Interlibrary Loan (ILL) lets you borrow from other institutions—for free—but many students don’t know how to use it effectively.

Step-by-Step ILL Process:

  1. Check Availability in OneSearch

    • If a book is not at Montclair, click "Request via Interlibrary Loan."
    • Example: You need "The Psychology of Persuasion" by Robert Cialdini, but Montclair doesn’t own it. Request it via ILL.

  2. Submit Your Request

    • Fill out the online form with:
      • Your Montclair username & password
      • Book title, author, and ISBN
      • Your preferred pickup location (most libraries)

  3. Track Your Request

    • ILL requests typically take 3–10 business days.
    • Check your Montclair email for updates—don’t assume it’s lost if you don’t hear back immediately.

  4. Return the Book on Time

    • Late fees apply, and some institutions charge penalties.
    • Set a reminder in your calendar.

Pro Tip:

  • Request digital copies when possible (e.g., PDF scans) to avoid shipping delays.
  • For urgent needs, contact the Reference Desk—they may fast-track your request.

Common Mistake & Fix: ❌ Mistake: Requesting 10 books at once and forgetting about them. ✅ Fix: Limit to 3–5 requests at a time and check your ILL account weekly.

Strategy 4: Use Library Workshops & Research Consultations

Why it matters: Librarians are trained researchers—they can save you hours of trial and error by teaching you how to find the best sources.

How to Take Advantage:

  1. Attend Research Workshops

    • Topics include:
      • Citation management (Zotero, EndNote)
      • Avoiding plagiarism
      • Data analysis with SPSS or R
      • Writing literature reviews
    • Check the Library Events Calendar for upcoming sessions.

  2. Schedule a 1-on-1 Research Consultation

    • Book a 30–60 minute appointment with a subject specialist.
    • Example: A Political Science student struggling with quantitative research met with a **librarian who taught them how to use Statista for election data analysis.

  3. Ask for Help on Discussion Boards

    • Some online workshops have Q&A forums—participate actively to get personalized advice.

Real-World Example: *A Biology major was overwhelmed by PubMed (a massive medical database). During a research consultation, a librarian showed them how to:

  • Use MeSH terms (Medical Subject Headings) for precise searches
  • Filter by "Clinical Trial" to find high-impact studies
  • Export citations directly into Zotero This cut their research time in half.

Strategy 7: Save Time with Citation Management Tools

Why it matters: Manually citing sources is tedious—citation managers like Zotero, EndNote, or Mendeley automate the process and keep your references organized.

How to Set Up & Use Zotero (Free & Easy):

  1. Download & Install Zotero

    • Available for Windows, Mac, and Chrome/Firefox extensions.
    • Montclair provides free training—check the Library Workshops page.

  2. Add Sources to Your Library

    • Drag-and-drop PDFs from databases or websites into Zotero.
    • Auto-fill citations from Google Scholar, JSTOR, or OneSearch.

  3. Create Bibliographies in Seconds

    • Choose your citation style (APA, MLA, Chicago, etc.).
    • Insert citations into Word/Google Docs with one click.

  4. Collaborate with Group Projects

    • Share folders with classmates for group research.

Real-World Example: *A Philosophy student was struggling to keep track of 50+ sources for their seminar paper. After using Zotero, they:

  • Automatically generated an APA bibliography
  • Avoided duplicate citations
  • Saved 10+ hours of manual work

Alternative Tools:

  • EndNote (Paid, but better for large research projects)
  • Mendeley (Great for PDF annotation & collaboration)
