Chapter 1: Getting Oriented – Understanding the Library’s Structure & Services

Before diving into research or study sessions, it’s essential to familiarize yourself with the library’s layout and key services. Montclair State University Libraries consist of three main branches:

  1. The Library at the Center for Academic Success (CAS) – The largest branch, housing general collections, special collections, and the Writing Center.
  2. The Science & Engineering Library – Specialized in STEM fields, with advanced lab equipment and research databases.
  3. The Music Library – A treasure trove for music students, featuring sheet music, audio recordings, and MIDI files.

Key Services You Should Know About

  • Research Assistance – Librarians offer one-on-one consultations to refine search strategies and find credible sources.
  • Interlibrary Loan (ILL) – Need a book or article not available at Montclair? ILL borrows it from other institutions for free.
  • Study Spaces – From quiet carrels to collaborative group rooms, the libraries provide flexible study environments.
  • Technology Lending – Access to laptops, cameras, audio recorders, and more for short-term use.
  • Digital Scholarship Lab – A space for data analysis, 3D printing, and multimedia projects.

Pro Tip: Use the Library Website’s "Find a Librarian" tool to connect with subject specialists who can guide you in your field of study.

Chapter 2: 10 Actionable Strategies to Master Montclair State University Libraries

1. Start with the Library’s Homepage & Search Tools

The Montclair State University Libraries homepage is your first stop for research. Instead of guessing where to look, use:

  • OneSearch – A unified search bar that pulls results from books, journals, articles, and databases in one place.
  • Advanced Search Filters – Narrow results by date, source type, or subject to save time.

Real-World Example: A history student searching for "Cold War propaganda" could use OneSearch to filter results by "peer-reviewed journals" and "1950-1990" to find the most relevant sources quickly.

2. Leverage Subject Guides for Research Efficiency

Each department has a dedicated research guide created by librarians. These guides include:

  • Recommended databases (e.g., JSTOR for humanities, IEEE Xplore for engineering)
  • Key reference works (dictionaries, encyclopedias)
  • Citation guides (APA, MLA, Chicago)

Where to Find Them: 🔗 https://www.montclair.edu/library/subject-guides

Pro Tip: Bookmark your subject guide for quick access during research projects.

3. Use Zotero or RefWorks for Citation Management

Manually formatting citations is time-consuming and error-prone. Instead, use free citation tools like:

  • Zotero (open-source, integrates with Word)
  • RefWorks (paid but robust for large projects)

How to Set It Up:

  1. Install the browser extension (Chrome/Firefox).
  2. Save citations as you find them.
  3. Generate formatted bibliographies in seconds.

Common Mistake: Waiting until the last minute to organize citations—this leads to stress and mistakes. Start early!

4. Explore Digital Collections & Open Access Resources

Montclair Libraries provide free access to thousands of e-books, journals, and primary sources, including:

  • Project MUSE (humanities & social sciences)
  • ScienceDirect (STEM research)
  • JSTOR (archival journals)

Example: A political science student researching "gender politics in Latin America" could access full-text articles from the 1990s in JSTOR without leaving the library’s website.

5. Attend Library Workshops & Research Bootcamps

The libraries offer free workshops on:

  • Advanced Google Scholar searches
  • Evaluating sources for credibility
  • Data visualization with Tableau

How to Register: Check the Library Events Calendar for upcoming sessions.

6. Utilize the Writing Center for Academic Support

Struggling with thesis structure, citations, or editing? The Writing Center (located in the CAS Library) offers:

  • One-on-one tutoring (in-person or virtual)
  • Workshops on academic writing
  • Feedback on drafts

Real-World Example: A graduate student working on a dissertation proposal could meet with a Writing Center tutor to refine their argument flow and citations, saving hours of revision time.

7. Take Advantage of Study Spaces & Quiet Hours

Montclair Libraries offer different study environments to suit your needs:

  • Group Study Rooms (reservable via Room Scheduler)
  • Silent Study Zones (for focused work)
  • Collaborative Tables (for group projects)

Pro Tip: Use Montclair’s "Study Space Finder" tool to locate the best spot based on your needs.

8. Use Interlibrary Loan (ILL) for Missing Resources

If a book or article isn’t available at Montclair, ILL can get it for you in 3-5 business days. Here’s how:

  1. Search OneSearch and click "Request via Interlibrary Loan."
  2. Fill out the form with your Montclair ID and email.
  3. Receive a digital copy or physical loan via mail.

Common Mistake: Assuming ILL is slow—it’s actually faster than waiting for a book to arrive via regular mail!

9. Explore Special Collections for Unique Research Opportunities

Montclair’s Special Collections hold rare books, archives, and historical documents, including:

  • Civil Rights Movement materials
  • Local New Jersey history
  • Literary manuscripts

Example: *A history student researching "Montclair’s role in the 1960s civil rights movement" could access primary sources from the Montclair State University Archives, providing unmatched depth to their paper.

10. Stay Updated with Library News & Alerts

The libraries send weekly newsletters with:

  • New database additions
  • Upcoming workshops
  • Special exhibits

How to Subscribe: Sign up via Montclair’s Library Newsletter.

Chapter 3: Common Mistakes & How to Avoid Them

Mistake #1: Not Using Subject-Specific Databases

Problem: Many students rely only on Google Scholar, which can lead to irrelevant or low-quality sources.

Solution:

  • Use subject guides to find specialized databases (e.g., PsycINFO for psychology, PubMed for medicine).
  • Combine keywords (e.g., "climate change AND policy" instead of just "climate change").

Mistake #2: Ignoring the Writing Center Until the Last Minute

Problem: Waiting until two days before a paper is due to get feedback means missed opportunities for improvement.

Solution:

  • Schedule a Writing Center appointment early—even for outlines or drafts.
  • Use their online resources for self-editing tips.

Mistake #3: Overlooking Digital Accessibility Tools

Problem: Some students struggle with reading e-books or PDFs due to visual impairments or dyslexia.

Solution:

  • Use text-to-speech tools (like NaturalReader) for digital books.
  • Adjust font size and contrast in library databases.

Mistake #4: Not Taking Advantage of Librarian Consultations

Problem: Many students don’t know librarians offer free help with research strategies.

Solution:

  • Book a 15-minute consultation via Ask a Librarian.
  • Ask for help refining search terms or finding peer-reviewed sources.

Mistake #5: Failing to Check for Updated Editions

Problem: Older books or articles may contain outdated information.

Solution:

  • Use Google Scholar’s "Cited by" feature to find more recent discussions on a topic.
  • Check library catalogs for newer editions of key texts.
