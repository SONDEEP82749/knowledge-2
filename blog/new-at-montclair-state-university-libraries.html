Chapter 1: Understanding the Montclair State University Libraries System

Before you can use the libraries effectively, you need to understand their structure, resources, and unique offerings.

1.1 The Three Main Libraries on Campus

Montclair State University operates three primary libraries, each serving different academic needs:

  1. The Library at the Center for Academic Success (CAS Library)

    • Location: Center for Academic Success (CAS), Room 102
    • Focus: Undergraduate research, study spaces, and general academic support.
    • Key Features:
      • Quiet study zones with group and individual seating.
      • 24/7 computer labs with high-speed internet.
      • Printing, scanning, and photocopying services.
      • Librarian-led workshops on research methods.

  2. The Library at the School of Business (SB Library)

    • Location: School of Business, Room 201
    • Focus: Business, economics, finance, and management research.
    • Key Features:
      • Specialized business databases (e.g., IBISWorld, Mergent Online, Bloomberg Terminal).
      • Case study collections for business strategy courses.
      • Career resources like LinkedIn Learning and resume workshops.

  3. The Library at the School of Arts & Sciences (SAS Library)

    • Location: School of Arts & Sciences, Room 305
    • Focus: Humanities, social sciences, STEM, and interdisciplinary research.
    • Key Features:
      • Rare books & archives (including historical Montclair documents).
      • Digital humanities tools (e.g., Zotero, Omeka).
      • STEM-specific resources like ScienceDirect, IEEE Xplore.

1.2 Digital & Remote Access: Libraries Beyond the Physical Space

Montclair State Libraries are not just brick-and-mortar—they offer robust digital resources that work for you anytime, anywhere.

  • MSU Libraries Online Portal (libraries.montclair.edu)

    • One-stop access to 300+ databases, e-books, journals, and streaming media.
    • Interlibrary Loan (ILL) service—borrow books from other universities in 3-5 business days.
    • Librarian chat & email support—get help instantly without visiting in person.

  • Mobile-Friendly Apps

    • LibApps (by Montclair State) – Scan QR codes on books, check due dates, and renew loans.
    • Libby & Hoopla – Borrow e-books and audiobooks for free.

  • Open Access & Free Resources

    • Google Scholar integration (via library links).
    • Preprint servers (e.g., arXiv for STEM, SSRN for business).

Real-World Example: A junior studying Environmental Science needed access to a 2022 peer-reviewed journal article on climate policy. Instead of paying $30 for a PDF, she used the MSU Libraries’ database search to find the free full-text version through JSTOR. She also submitted an Interlibrary Loan request for a hard-to-find book from Rutgers University, receiving it in 4 days—all without leaving her dorm room.

Chapter 2: 10 Actionable Strategies to Master Montclair State University Libraries

Now that you know what the libraries offer, let’s explore how to use them effectively.

Strategy 1: Start with the Library’s Research Guides

Montclair State Libraries provides pre-made research guides tailored to specific courses, majors, and topics.

How to Use Them:

  1. Visit Montclair State Research Guides.
  2. Search by subject (e.g., "Psychology," "Computer Science") or course name (e.g., "ENG 101").
  3. Follow the step-by-step guides on:
    • Where to find credible sources.
    • How to use databases effectively.
    • Citation tools (APA, MLA, Chicago).

Real-World Example: *A first-year English major was assigned a literary analysis paper on F. Scott Fitzgerald’s The Great Gatsby. Instead of searching Google, she used the Literature Research Guide, which directed her to:

  • JSTOR for critical essays.
  • Project MUSE for academic journals.
  • Gale Literature Criticism for book reviews. This saved her hours of wasted searching and ensured she found peer-reviewed sources from the start.*

Strategy 2: Use Advanced Database Searching Techniques

Generic Google searches often lead to irrelevant or low-quality sources. Montclair State’s library databases are curated for academic rigor, but you must search them correctly.

Key Techniques: ✅ Boolean Operators (AND, OR, NOT)

  • "Climate change AND policy" (narrows results).
  • "Renewable energy OR solar power" (broadens results).
  • "Global warming NOT opinion" (excludes non-academic sources).

✅ Truncation & Wildcards

  • **"Climat*" → Finds climate, climactic, climatology.
  • **"Psycholog*" → Finds psychology, psychological, psychotherapist.

✅ Field Searching

  • Title: title:"machine learning"
  • Author: author:"Susan Blackmore"
  • Subject: subject:"artificial intelligence"

✅ Limiters & Filters

  • Peer-reviewed only (check the filter).
  • Date range (e.g., 2018–2024).
  • Full-text available (ensures you can read it immediately).

Common Mistake & Fix: ❌ Mistake: Typing a single-word query (e.g., "AI"). ✅ Fix: Use phrases (e.g., "artificial intelligence trends") and limit to peer-reviewed journals.

Strategy 3: Leverage Librarian Workshops & One-on-One Help

Montclair State Libraries offers free workshops and individual research consultations—you’re not alone in this!

How to Access Help:

  • Workshops:
    • Check Library Events Calendar for topics like:
      • "How to Find Credible Sources" (for freshmen).
      • "Advanced Database Searching" (for upperclassmen).
      • "Avoiding Plagiarism with Zotero" (for grad students).
  • One-on-One Sessions:
    • Book a 30-minute appointment with a librarian via Calendly.
    • Great for thesis research, capstone projects, or complex topics.

Real-World Example: *A graduate student in Public Administration was struggling to find government datasets for her policy analysis paper. She scheduled a librarian consultation, who taught her how to:

  • Access ProQuest Statistical Databases.
  • Use Census.gov and ICPSR (Inter-University Consortium for Political and Social Research).
  • Request custom data tables from Montclair’s Data Services. Within two weeks, she had all the data she needed—something she couldn’t find on her own.*

Strategy 4: Use Zotero for Efficient Citation Management

Citing sources correctly is non-negotiable in academia. Zotero, a free citation manager, integrates with Montclair State Libraries to automate citations in APA, MLA, Chicago, and more.

How to Set Up Zotero:

  1. Download Zotero (https://www.zotero.org/).
  2. Install the browser connector (for Chrome/Firefox).
  3. Search Montclair’s databases (e.g., JSTOR, EBSCO) and auto-save citations.
  4. Drag-and-drop PDFs into Zotero for full-text storage.
  5. Generate bibliographies in seconds.

Pro Tip:

  • Use Zotero’s "Related Items" feature to find additional sources on your topic.
  • Share libraries with group members for collaborative research.

Common Mistake & Fix: ❌ Mistake: Manually copying citations from Google Scholar (often incorrect). ✅ Fix: Use Zotero’s Google Scholar connector to auto-fill citations accurately.

Strategy 8: Take Advantage of Interlibrary Loan (ILL) for Hard-to-Find Books

Not every book you need is in Montclair’s collection—but that doesn’t mean you’re out of luck.

How ILL Works:

  1. Search Montclair’s catalog (https://montclair.libcat.montclair.edu/).
  2. If a book is not available, click "Request via Interlibrary Loan".
  3. Fill out the simple form (usually takes <2 minutes).
  4. Wait 3–10 days (depending on availability).
  5. Pick up at the CAS Library or request PDF delivery (for e-books).

Pro Tip:

  • Check processing times—some rare books take weeks.
  • Renew loans before they expire to avoid fines.

Common Mistake & Fix: ❌ Mistake: Not requesting ILL early (last-minute requests take longer). ✅ Fix: Submit ILL requests 2–3 weeks before your deadline.
