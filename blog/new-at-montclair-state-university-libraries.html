8 Actionable Strategies to Succeed at Montclair State University Libraries

1. Master the Library Catalog (MontclairSearch) Like a Pro

Montclair’s MontclairSearch is your gateway to books, articles, and multimedia—but many students don’t use it efficiently.

How to Optimize It:

  • Use advanced search filters (e.g., "Peer-Reviewed," "Full Text," "Date Range").
  • Try Boolean operators (AND, OR, NOT) for precise searches. Example:
    • "climate change AND policy" (narrows results)
    • "machine learning OR AI" (broadens results)
  • Save searches & set alerts for new publications in your field.

Real-World Example: A sociology major struggling with a thesis on urban gentrification used MontclairSearch with the terms: "gentrification NOT New York" (to avoid irrelevant NYC studies) + "affordable housing" (to focus on policy solutions). This saved hours of manual searching and provided highly relevant sources.

2. Leverage the Research Help Desk (Before You Get Stuck)

Montclair’s Research Help Desk (available in-person and via chat, email, or Zoom) is free and underutilized.

How to Use It Effectively:

  • Ask for a "Research Consultation"—librarians can help refine your topic.
  • Use the "Citation Help" tool to format references in APA, MLA, or Chicago styles.
  • Request interlibrary loans (ILL) for books not in Montclair’s collection—most arrive in 3-5 days.

Common Mistake: Waiting until the last minute to ask for help. Solution: Book a session early—librarians can guide you on where to find credible sources before you start writing.

4. Find the Perfect Study Space (No More Distractions!)

Montclair Libraries offer multiple study environments, but choosing the right one can double your productivity.

Study Space Breakdown:

  • Silent Study (Main Library, 2nd Floor) – Ideal for exams and deep focus.
  • Collaborative Pods (Science & Engineering Library) – Great for group projects.
  • Quiet Zones (Library West) – Perfect for reading and note-taking.
  • Tech Lounge (Library East) – For laptops, chargers, and whiteboards.

Real-World Example: A business student preparing for finals used the Silent Study area during peak hours (8 AM–10 AM) to avoid distractions. Meanwhile, a computer science team worked in the Tech Lounge to debug code together.

Common Mistake: Assuming all spaces are open 24/7. Solution: Check the Library Hours page—some areas close earlier on weekends.

5. Use the "Ask a Librarian" Chat for Instant Help

Montclair’s live chat service (available on the library website) connects you to real librarians in minutes.

How to Get the Most Out of It:

  • Be specific in your question (e.g., "How do I find primary sources on the Civil Rights Movement?" instead of "Help me with my paper").
  • Save chat transcripts—some librarians can email you follow-up resources.
  • Use it for quick citations (e.g., "How do I cite a podcast in APA?").

Pro Tip: If chat is slow, email the library at reference@montclair.edu—they often respond within 24 hours.

6. Access E-Books & Audiobooks for On-the-Go Learning

Montclair’s e-book collection (via OverDrive, EBSCO, and ProQuest) lets you study anywhere.

How to Download:

  1. Search MontclairSearch for e-books.
  2. Click "Available Online" and select a platform (e.g., Libby for OverDrive).
  3. Check out with your Montclair ID (borrow for 28 days).

Best E-Book Platforms at Montclair:

  • Libby (OverDrive) – Free audiobooks & e-books.
  • EBSCOhost eBooks – Subject-specific collections.
  • Project MUSE – Humanities & social sciences.

Common Mistake: Not renewing e-books before they expire. Solution: Set a reminder or use the "Renew" option in your account.

7. Attend Library Workshops & Events (Free & Valuable!)

Montclair Libraries host workshops on research, citation, and tech skills—many students miss them!

Upcoming Workshops (Check the Events Calendar):

  • "How to Find Primary Sources" (Great for history papers)
  • "Zotero for Citation Management" (Avoid last-minute formatting stress)
  • "Data Visualization with Tableau" (For STEM students)

Real-World Example: A psychology graduate student attended a "Literature Review Workshop" and learned how to systematically search databases, which cut her research time in half.

Pro Tip: Sign up early—some workshops fill up!

8. Use the "Library Guides" for Subject-Specific Help

Montclair’s "Research Guides" are curated by librarians to help with specific subjects.

How to Use Them:

  • Browse by subject (e.g., "Business Research," "Nursing," "Literature").
  • Find recommended databases, books, and tools tailored to your major.
  • Use the "Citation Help" section to format references correctly.

Example: A nursing student used the Nursing Research Guide to find peer-reviewed journals on telehealth, saving her weeks of manual searching.

2. Can I print or scan documents at Montclair Libraries?

Answer: Yes! Montclair Libraries offer free printing and scanning:

  • Printing: Use your Montclair student account (funds can be added via MyMontclair).
  • Scanning: Use the self-service scanners (PDF or email copies).
  • Photocopying: Available at Library West (costs apply).

Pro Tip: Check the Library Services page for updated rates.

3. How do I find primary sources for my research?

Answer: Primary sources (original documents) can be found in:

  • Montclair’s Special Collections (letters, diaries, historical papers).
  • JSTOR, ProQuest, and EBSCOhost (filter by "Primary Sources").
  • Google Scholar (search "primary sources [your topic]").

Example Search: "Primary sources on the Women’s Suffrage Movement" → Use Montclair’s Research Guide for History.

4. Are there quiet study spaces for exams?

Answer: Yes! The Silent Study area (Main Library, 2nd Floor) is reserved for focused work during exams. Other quiet options:

  • Library West (3rd Floor) – Low-traffic zone.
  • Science & Engineering Library (4th Floor) – Great for STEM students.

Tip: Arrive early—these spaces fill up during peak exam weeks.

5. How do I cite sources correctly?

Answer: Montclair Libraries offer multiple citation tools:

  • Zotero (free, browser-based reference manager).
  • EasyBib (web-based citation generator).
  • Librarian consultations (ask for APA/MLA/Chicago help).

Quick Citation Example (APA):

  • Book: Author, A. A. (Year). Title. Publisher.
  • Journal Article: Author, A. A., & Author, B. B. (Year). Title. Journal Name, Volume(Issue), pages.
