Chapter 1: Getting Started – Understanding Montclair State University Libraries

Before you can maximize the libraries’ potential, you need to understand their structure, resources, and unique offerings. Montclair State’s library system consists of three main locations, each with specialized strengths:

1. The Library at the Center for Academic Success (CAS)

  • Primary location for undergraduate students, offering 24/7 study spaces, group collaboration areas, and one-on-one research consultations.
  • Special features:
    • The Writing Center – Free editing and feedback for papers.
    • The Math & Science Learning Center – Tutoring for STEM disciplines.
    • The Digital Scholarship Lab – Access to 3D printers, scanning equipment, and digital humanities tools.

2. The Library at the School of Business

  • Tailored for business, economics, and management students, with a focus on financial databases, market research, and industry reports.
  • Key resources:
    • Bloomberg Terminal (for finance students)
    • IBISWorld (industry analysis)
    • Statista (market data)

3. The Library at the School of Education

  • Designed for education majors, with early childhood, special education, and curriculum resources.
  • Unique offerings:
    • ERIC Database (education research)
    • Teacher resource collections (books, lesson plans, and professional development materials)

How to Access These Libraries

  • Physical access: All three libraries are open to current Montclair State students, faculty, and staff with a valid ID.
  • Online access: 24/7 digital access via the Montclair State University Libraries Website.
  • Mobile app: The LibApps mobile app allows you to search for books, renew loans, and access e-resources on the go.

Pro Tip: Bookmark the LibGuides—a subject-specific research guide created by librarians to help you find the best resources for your field.

Chapter 2: 10 Actionable Strategies to Master Montclair State University Libraries

Now that you know the lay of the land, let’s explore practical, step-by-step strategies to make the most of your library experience.

Strategy 1: Use the Library Catalog Like a Pro

The Montclair State University Libraries Catalog is your first stop for finding books, journals, and digital media. Many students underuse this tool because they don’t know its advanced features.

How to Search Effectively:

  1. Start with a broad keyword search (e.g., "climate change and policy").
  2. Refine with filters:
    • Publication date (limit to the last 5 years for current research).
    • Format (books, e-books, videos, datasets).
    • Availability (check "Available Online" for instant access).
  3. Use Boolean operators (AND, OR, NOT) for precise results:
    • "global warming" AND "economic impact"
    • "machine learning" NOT "introduction"
  4. Check the "Recommended" section—librarians curate high-quality sources based on your search.

Real-World Example: *A graduate student in Environmental Science was struggling to find peer-reviewed studies on renewable energy in New Jersey. Instead of searching Google Scholar, they used the library catalog with the terms "New Jersey renewable energy" AND "policy analysis"—resulting in 12 relevant e-books and 40 journal articles in minutes.*

Strategy 2: Leverage the Library’s Database Collection

Montclair State’s databases are goldmines of academic content, but many students don’t know how to navigate them efficiently.

Top Databases by Discipline:

Discipline Key Databases Best For
Business Business Source Premier, ABI/INFORM Market research, company reports
STEM ScienceDirect, IEEE Xplore Peer-reviewed research, technical papers
Humanities JSTOR, Project MUSE Historical analysis, literary criticism
Social Sciences PsycINFO, Sociological Abstracts Psychology, sociology research

How to Find the Right Database:

  • Use the Databases A-Z List.
  • Narrow by subject (e.g., "Business" → "Economics").
  • Check the "Most Used" section for popular resources in your field.

Pro Tip: If a database doesn’t load, try clearing your browser cache or using Montclair’s VPN for off-campus access.

Real-World Example: *A marketing student needed recent consumer behavior trends for their capstone project. Instead of searching Google, they used Business Source Premier with the search term "post-pandemic shopping habits"—finding 15 industry reports from Nielsen and McKinsey that directly informed their analysis.*

Strategy 3: Request Books & Materials Through Interlibrary Loan (ILL)

Not every book you need is available at Montclair State. That’s where Interlibrary Loan (ILL) comes in—a free service that borrows materials from other institutions.

How to Use ILL:

  1. Search the catalog for your desired book.
  2. If it’s not available, click "Request via Interlibrary Loan."
  3. Fill out the simple form (usually takes 3-7 business days).
  4. Track your request in your ILL account.

Pro Tip:

  • Request early—some books take up to two weeks to arrive.
  • Check the "Hold Shelf" in the library—sometimes books are already available but not checked out.

Common Mistake: Students often forget to renew ILL books before they’re due back. Set a calendar reminder for 3 days before the due date.

Strategy 4: Use the Writing & Research Help Desk

Montclair State Libraries offer free, one-on-one research assistance from librarians who specialize in your field.

How to Get Help:

  • Stop by the Research Help Desk (located in the CAS Library).
  • Chat with a librarian via LibAnswers (available 24/7).

What They Can Help With: ✔ Narrowing down a research topic ✔ Finding primary sources ✔ Evaluating sources for credibility ✔ Citing in APA, MLA, or Chicago style

Real-World Example: *A history student was overwhelmed by too many sources for their thesis on Cold War propaganda. A librarian helped them refine their search terms and identify the most relevant declassified documents from the National Archives, saving them weeks of wasted research.

Strategy 8: Use the Library’s Citation & Plagiarism Tools

Plagiarism and improper citations are common academic pitfalls—but the library offers tools to help you avoid them.

Key Tools:

  • Zotero (free reference manager)
  • RefWorks (for advanced citation needs)
  • Turnitin (built into Canvas for originality checks)

How to Use Zotero (Step-by-Step):

  1. Download Zotero from zotero.org.
  2. Browse the web—Zotero auto-saves citations from databases and websites.
  3. Organize sources into folders and tags.
  4. Generate citations in APA, MLA, or Chicago format.

Real-World Example: *A psychology student was struggling to keep track of 50+ sources for their literature review. By using Zotero, they automatically generated citations and avoided accidental plagiarism, saving hours of manual work.

Strategy 10: Advocate for Your Research Needs

Librarians want to help you succeed—but they can’t know your needs unless you tell them.

How to Get Personalized Help:

  • Email a subject librarian (find them via LibGuides).
  • Request a research consultation for complex projects.
  • Suggest new resources—librarians add books and databases based on student demand.

Real-World Example: *A computer science student noticed a gap in the library’s database collection for AI ethics research. They emailed the STEM librarian, who purchased access to the "Ethics of AI" database—now available to all Montclair students.

Chapter 3: Common Mistakes & How to Avoid Them

Even the most dedicated students make avoidable mistakes when using the library. Here’s how to spot and fix them.

Mistake 1: Not Using the Library’s Website Effectively

Problem: Many students search Google first instead of using the Montclair Libraries portal. Solution:

  • Bookmark the Montclair Libraries Homepage.
  • Use the search bar—it prioritizes Montclair’s resources over Google.
  • Check the "Recommended" section—librarians curate the best sources for you.

Mistake 2: Ignoring the "Ask a Librarian" Service

Problem: Students don’t ask for help when they’re stuck. Solution:

  • Use LibAnswers (24/7 chat).
  • Schedule a research consultation—librarians save hours of wasted time.
  • Email a subject specialist—they know your field’s best resources.

Mistake 3: Not Checking for Full-Text Access

Problem: Students find a great article but can’t access the full text. Solution:

  • Click "Check for Full Text" in the catalog.
  • Use Google Scholar and click "Montclair State University" for full-text links.
  • Request via Interlibrary Loan if the article isn’t available.

Mistake 4: Overlooking Digital & Special Collections

Problem: Students only look for books and miss unique digital resources. Solution:

  • Browse Digital Collections.
  • Search for primary sources
