Chapter 1: Getting Oriented – The Basics of Montclair State University Libraries

Before diving into advanced research techniques, it’s crucial to familiarize yourself with the library’s structure, services, and key tools. Here’s what you need to know:

1.1 The Library System: Branches & Locations

Montclair State University Libraries consist of three main branches, each serving different academic needs:

  • The Library at the Center for Academic & Student Excellence (CASE)

    • Located in the CASE building, this is the main research hub with study spaces, group rooms, and a 24/7 computer lab.
    • Features specialized collections in business, education, and social sciences.

  • The Library at the School of Business

    • A dedicated space for business students, offering financial databases, case studies, and industry reports.
    • Ideal for marketing, accounting, and economics research.

  • The Library at the School of Arts & Sciences

    • Houses humanities, sciences, and interdisciplinary collections, including rare books and archives.
    • Perfect for literature, history, psychology, and STEM research.

💡 Pro Tip: Use the Montclair State Libraries Website to explore virtual tours, floor maps, and branch-specific resources.

1.2 Your Library Card & Access

Every Montclair State student, faculty, and staff member receives a free library card upon enrollment. This card grants access to: ✅ Physical books, journals, and media (DVDs, CDs) ✅ Online databases (JSTOR, ProQuest, EBSCO) ✅ Interlibrary Loan (ILL) services (borrowing from other institutions) ✅ Printing, scanning, and photocopying (with credit limits)

🔹 How to Get Your Card:

  1. Visit the Circulation Desk at any library branch.
  2. Present your Montclair State ID.
  3. Receive your library card (valid for the duration of your enrollment).

⚠️ Common Mistake: Assuming your student ID is your library card. Always carry both—some services require separate authentication.

Strategy 1: Use the Library Catalog Effectively (Beyond Google Searches)

Problem: Many students rely on Google Scholar or Wikipedia instead of the Montclair State Library Catalog, missing out on peer-reviewed, credible sources.

Solution:

  • Search the Montclair State Library Catalog for books, e-books, and journal articles.
  • Use advanced search filters (author, title, subject, date range).
  • Check the "Available Online" option to access e-books instantly.

Real-World Example: A political science student researching "Climate Policy in New Jersey" could:

  1. Search "climate policy New Jersey" in the catalog.
  2. Filter by "Books & eBooks" and "2015-Present".
  3. Find "New Jersey’s Energy Future" (2022) available online—saving hours of searching.

Strategy 3: Master the Interlibrary Loan (ILL) Service

Problem: You need a book or article that Montclair State doesn’t own.

Solution:

  • Submit an ILL request via Montclair State ILL Portal.
  • Turnaround time: 3-7 business days (faster for digital articles).
  • Cost: Free for students (unlike some university libraries).

Real-World Example: *A history student researching "The Vietnam War in Montclair" finds a rare local newspaper article not in the catalog. They submit an ILL request, and within 5 days, receive a digital copy via email.

Strategy 8: Learn How to Evaluate Sources (Not All Information Is Equal!)

Problem: You find a quick Google result, but is it credible?

Solution: Use the CRAAP Test (Currency, Relevance, Authority, Accuracy, Purpose):

Factor What to Check Example
Currency Is the info up-to-date? A 2010 study on "Social Media Trends" is outdated.
Relevance Does it answer your question? A book on "General Psychology" won’t help with "Sports Psychology."
Authority Who wrote it? A .edu or .gov source is more reliable than a random blog.
Accuracy Are facts verifiable? Check citations—if none exist, be skeptical.
Purpose Why was it written? A news article is different from a peer-reviewed journal.

Real-World Example: *A business student finds a LinkedIn post on "AI in Marketing." They apply the CRAAP Test:

  • Currency? Posted 3 months ago (still relevant).
  • Authority? Written by a marketing consultant (not a scholar).
  • Conclusion: Useful for industry insights, but supplement with a journal article for academic rigor.

How to Get Started:

  1. Download Zotero (browser extension).

Real-World Example: *A history student collects 15 sources for a paper. Instead of manually formatting APA citations, they:

  1. Import all PDFs into Zotero.
  2. Drag-and-drop citations into their Word doc.
  3. Export a full bibliography in one click.

Chapter 3: Common Mistakes & How to Avoid Them

Even experienced students make avoidable errors when using academic libraries. Here’s how to steer clear of pitfalls:

Mistake 1: Ignoring the Library’s Website

Problem: Relying on Google searches instead of the official library portal leads to misinformation and wasted time.

Solution: ✅ Bookmark the Montclair State Library Homepage. ✅ Check the "Databases" and "Research Guides" sections first.

Mistake 2: Not Using Subject-Specific Databases

Problem: Searching Google Scholar for everything results in irrelevant or low-quality sources.

Solution: ✅ Match your topic to the right database (e.g., JSTOR for humanities, ScienceDirect for STEM). ✅ Use the "Advanced Search" feature in databases for precise results.

Mistake 3: Overlooking Interlibrary Loan (ILL)

Problem: Giving up on a book or article because Montclair State doesn’t own it.

Solution: ✅ Submit an ILL request—it’s free and fast for students. ✅ Check the "Available Online" option before requesting.

Mistake 4: Skipping Workshops & Research Help

Problem: Missing out on free expert guidance that could save hours of work.

Solution: ✅ Attend at least one workshop per semester (e.g., "Citation Basics"). ✅ Schedule a research consultation if stuck on a topic.

Mistake 5: Not Evaluating Sources Properly

Problem: Using any source without checking credibility and relevance.

Solution: ✅ Apply the CRAAP Test before using a source. ✅ Prefer peer-reviewed journals over blogs or Wikipedia.

Mistake 6: Forgetting to Renew Books

Problem: Facing late fees because you didn’t renew a book in time.

Solution: ✅ Check due dates via the library catalog. ✅ Renew books online before the due date.
