Chapter 1: Getting Oriented – Montclair State University Libraries at a Glance

Before you can use the library effectively, you need to understand its structure. Montclair State’s libraries are divided into three main branches, each serving different academic needs:

1. The Library at the Center (Main Campus – Main Library)

📍 Location: 1 Normal Avenue, Montclair, NJ 📚 Key Features:

  • 24/7 access (via LibKey for e-books and journals)
  • Special Collections (rare books, archives, and primary sources)
  • Group study rooms (reservable via the Library App)
  • Tech zones with scanners, laptops, and 3D printers

2. The Library at the School of Business (SBS Library)

📍 Location: 1 Normal Avenue (inside the School of Business building) 📚 Best For:

  • Business, economics, and finance students
  • Market research databases (IBISWorld, Mergent Online)
  • Career resources (resume workshops, LinkedIn tutorials)

3. The Library at the School of Education (SOE Library)

📍 Location: 1 Normal Avenue (inside the School of Education building) 📚 Best For:

  • Education majors (ERIC, Education Source databases)
  • Curriculum development tools
  • Teacher resource collections (children’s books, lesson plans)

How to Access the Libraries

  • Physical Access: Show your Montclair State ID at any entrance.
  • Online Access: Use your NetID and password to log in to Montclair State’s Library Portal (libguides.montclair.edu).
  • Mobile App: Download the Montclair State Library App for real-time availability checks, renewals, and alerts.

Pro Tip: Bookmark Montclair State’s Library Homepage—it’s your one-stop shop for all research needs.

Chapter 2: 10 Actionable Strategies to Master Montclair State University Libraries

Now that you know the layout, let’s get practical. These 10 strategies will help you find sources faster, avoid plagiarism, and impress your professors.

Strategy 1: Use the Library’s Advanced Search Filters (Like a Pro)

Most students just type keywords into Google Scholar or the library catalog—but that’s inefficient. Montclair State’s LibGuides and OneSearch offer powerful filters to narrow down results.

How to Do It:

  1. Go to OneSearch (Montclair’s main search tool).
  2. Instead of searching for "climate change," try:
    • "Climate change AND policy" (for political science)
    • "Climate change AND economics" (for business students)
    • "Climate change AND health impacts" (for nursing/medical students)
  3. Use advanced search filters (under the search bar):
    • Date range (e.g., "2015–2024" for recent research)
    • Source type (peer-reviewed journals, books, news articles)
    • Subject terms (e.g., "Renewable Energy" under "Subject")

Real-World Example: A business student researching "sustainable packaging" could filter for:

  • Subject: "Packaging Industry"
  • Date: "Last 5 years"
  • Source Type: "Peer-reviewed journals" This reduces 100 irrelevant results to just 10 high-quality articles.

Strategy 2: Leverage LibGuides for Subject-Specific Research

Montclair State’s LibGuides are curated research guides created by librarians for each major. Instead of guessing where to look, use these pre-made roadmaps.

How to Find Them:

  1. Go to LibGuides.
  2. Click on your major (e.g., "Psychology," "Computer Science," "Nursing").
  3. Follow the step-by-step research process (e.g., "Find Articles," "Evaluate Sources").

Real-World Example: A psychology student writing on "social media and mental health" would:

  1. Go to the Psychology LibGuide.
  2. Click "Find Articles" → "Psychology Databases" (e.g., PsycINFO, PubMed).
  3. Use the pre-selected keywords ("social media," "anxiety," "depression") to find reliable studies.

Why It Works: LibGuides save time by eliminating dead-end searches—you get only the best databases for your topic.

Strategy 3: Use Zotero or Mendeley for Free Citation Management

Plagiarism is a real risk, and manually citing sources is tedious. Zotero (free) and Mendeley (free for students) automatically organize and cite your sources.

How to Set It Up:

  1. Download Zotero (Chrome/Firefox extension).
  2. Install the ZoteroBib extension for quick citations.
  3. Drag-and-drop PDFs from Montclair’s databases into Zotero.
  4. Generate APA, MLA, or Chicago citations in seconds.

Real-World Example: A history student researching "Cold War propaganda" could:

  1. Find 5 articles in JSTOR (via Montclair’s databases).
  2. Add them to Zotero with one click.
  3. Auto-generate a works cited page in MLA format.

Pro Tip: Use Zotero’s "Save to Cloud" feature to sync across devices.

Strategy 4: Master Interlibrary Loan (ILL) for Books Not in Montclair’s Collection

Montclair’s collection is impressive, but not everything is available. Interlibrary Loan (ILL) lets you borrow books from other universities for free.

How to Request:

  1. Go to ILL Request Form.
  2. Search for the book in WorldCat (if not in Montclair’s catalog).
  3. Submit the request—most books arrive in 3–7 days.

Real-World Example: *A philosophy student needing "Nietzsche’s Complete Works" (not in Montclair’s collection) would:

  1. Search WorldCat → Find it at Princeton University.
  2. Request via ILL → Get a digital scan in 2 days.

Why It’s Worth It:

  • No late fees (unlike Amazon).
  • Faster than waiting for a used copy.

Strategy 5: Use Montclair’s Research Consultations (Free Expert Help)

Librarians are your secret weapon. Many students don’t know they can get one-on-one help from research experts.

How to Book a Session:

  1. Go to Research Consultations.
  2. Click "Schedule an Appointment" (available daily, including weekends).
  3. Meet via Zoom or in-person for customized research guidance.

Real-World Example: *A biology student struggling with "genetic engineering ethics" could:

  1. Book a 30-minute consultation.
  2. Get tips on finding ethical debates in BioEthics journals.
  3. Learn how to evaluate controversial sources.

Why It’s a Game-Changer:

  • Saves hours of trial-and-error searching.
  • Helps you find sources professors actually respect.

Strategy 6: Explore Montclair’s Digital Archives & Special Collections

Montclair’s Special Collections hold rare books, manuscripts, and primary sources that most students never use.

What’s Available?

  • New Jersey History Archives (old newspapers, government documents)
  • African American Studies Collection (letters, oral histories)
  • Women’s Studies Archives (feminist manifestos, diaries)

How to Access:

  1. Go to Special Collections.

Real-World Example: *A history student writing on "Women’s Suffrage in NJ" could:

  1. Find original suffrage petitions in the archives.
  2. Use them as primary sources in their paper.

Why It’s Unique:

  • No other library has these exact documents.
  • Proves deep research to professors.

Strategy 7: Optimize Your Searches with Boolean Operators (AND, OR, NOT)

Boolean operators supercharge your searches by refining results.

Operator Example What It Does
AND "climate change AND policy" Finds both terms (narrows results)
OR "global warming OR climate change" Finds either term (broadens results)
NOT "social media NOT TikTok" Excludes TikTok from results

Real-World Example: *A political science student researching "gun control laws" could:

  1. Search: "gun control AND California" (finds state-specific laws).
  2. Exclude irrelevant terms: "gun control NOT hunting" (removes unrelated results).

Pro Tip: Use quotation marks for exact phrases ("net neutrality").

Strategy 9: Take Advantage of Montclair’s Open Access & Free Resources

Not all great research is behind paywalls. Montclair provides free access to:

  • Google Scholar (with Montclair’s full-text links)
  • JSTOR Open Access (free articles)
  • Project MUSE (humanities & social sciences)

How to Find Them:

  1. Go to Montclair’s Databases A-Z.
  2. Filter by "Free" or "Open Access".

Real-World Example: *A sociology student needing "free peer-reviewed articles" on "urban poverty" could:

  1. Search JSTOR Open Access.
  2. Find 5 free articles (no library card needed).

Why It’s Smart:

  • Saves money (no need for Sage Journals subscriptions).
  • Ethical research (avoids paywalled sources).

Strategy 10: Use Montclair’s Library App for Real-Time Updates

The Montclair State Library App (available on iOS & Android) lets you: ✅ Check book availability (no more wasted trips). ✅ Renew books (avoid late fees). ✅ Get alerts when your favorite books are back in stock. ✅ Scan QR codes on books for quick access.

How to Set It Up:

  1. Download Montclair Library App.

  • Keeps you updated on new resources.

Chapter 3: Common Mistakes New Students Make (And How to Avoid Them)

Even with the best strategies, many students still struggle. Here are the biggest pitfalls and how to fix them.

Mistake 1: Relying Only on Google Scholar (Without Montclair’s Full-Text Links)

Problem: Google Scholar doesn’t always provide full-text articles. Students paywall-trap themselves by clicking "PDF" without checking if Montclair has access.

Solution:

  1. Search Google Scholar → Find the article.
  2. Click "Montclair State University" (if available) → Instant free access.
  3. If not, use Montclair’s databases (e.g., JSTOR, EBSCO) first.

Real-World Fix: *A student finds a great article in Google Scholar but can’t access it. Instead of paying $30, they:

  1. Go to Montclair’s Databases.
  2. Search the title → Find it in JSTOR (free via Montclair).
