Before diving into strategies, it’s crucial to know what’s available at your fingertips. Montclair State University Libraries provide far more than just books—they offer a digital ecosystem designed to support research, creativity, and academic excellence.

Montclair State has three main library locations, each with unique strengths:

  • John J. Wilcox, Jr. Library (Main Campus – Upper Montclair)

    • Hours: Mon-Thu (8:00 AM – 12:00 AM), Fri (8:00 AM – 6:00 PM), Sat (10:00 AM – 6:00 PM), Sun (12:00 PM – 12:00 AM)
    • Key Features:

  • Library at the School of Business (Lower Montclair Campus)

    • Hours: Mon-Fri (8:00 AM – 10:00 PM), Sat (10:00 AM – 6:00 PM)
    • Key Features:
      • Business-focused databases (IBISWorld, Mergent Online, Statista)
      • Case study collections for management and finance students
      • Collaborative workspaces ideal for group projects

  • Library at the School of Education (Upper Montclair Campus)

    • Hours: Mon-Fri (8:00 AM – 8:00 PM), Sat (10:00 AM – 6:00 PM)
    • Key Features:
      • Education-specific databases (ERIC, ProQuest Education Journals)

1.2 Digital & Online Resources: Beyond the Physical Shelves

Montclair State Libraries don’t just stop at physical books—they provide a vast digital library accessible 24/7 from anywhere with an internet connection.

A. Databases & Journals (Accessible via Calculator-Library-Portal)

Montclair subscribes to hundreds of premium databases, including:

  • JSTOR – Full-text articles from thousands of academic journals (arts, humanities, sciences).
  • ProQuest – Business, health, and social sciences research.
  • ScienceDirect – Peer-reviewed scientific papers (ideal for STEM students).

Strategy 2: Use Boolean Operators to Refine Your Searches

Problem: Students often get overwhelmed by too many (or irrelevant) search results.

Solution: Boolean operators (AND, OR, NOT) help narrow down your searches.

How to Apply Them:

Operator Example Search What It Does
AND "climate change" AND "New Jersey" Finds both terms (more specific results).
OR "global warming" OR "climate change" Finds either term (broader results).
NOT "AI" NOT "machine learning" Excludes machine learning from results.

💡 Pro Tip: Combine with quotation marks for exact phrases (e.g., "social media impact").

📌 Real-World Example: *A psychology student researching "social media and anxiety" initially got 10,000 results. By using: "social media" AND "anxiety" AND "college students" They reduced results to 200—all highly relevant to their paper.
