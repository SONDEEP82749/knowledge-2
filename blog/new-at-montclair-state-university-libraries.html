Chapter 1: Getting Started – Your First Steps at Montclair State University Libraries

1.1 How to Access the Libraries: Physical & Digital Entry Points

Montclair State University Libraries consist of three main campus locations, each with unique strengths:

A. The Library at the Center for Academic Success (CAS Library)

📍 Location: 1 Normal Avenue, Montclair, NJ 🔹 Best for: First-year students, general research, study groups, and tech access 🔹 Key Features:

  • 24/7 computer labs with high-speed Wi-Fi
  • Group study rooms (reservable via the Library App)
  • Printing, scanning, and copying (with student discounts)
  • Accessibility services, including screen readers and adaptive tech

B. The Library at the School of Business (SB Library)

📍 Location: 1 Normal Avenue, Montclair, NJ (inside the Business School building) 🔹 Best for: Business, economics, and marketing students 🔹 Key Features:

  • Specialized business databases (IBISWorld, Mergent Online, Statista)
  • Financial modeling software (Excel, Bloomberg Terminal access for qualified students)
  • Career resources, including resume workshops and LinkedIn training

C. The Library at the School of Arts & Sciences (AS Library)

📍 Location: 1 Normal Avenue, Montclair, NJ (upper level of CAS) 🔹 Best for: Humanities, social sciences, and STEM researchers 🔹 Key Features:

  • Rare books & archives (including historical Montclair documents)
  • Digital humanities lab (for text analysis, GIS mapping, and multimedia projects)
  • Quiet study zones for deep focus

💡 Pro Tip: Download the Montclair State University Libraries App (available on iOS & Android) for real-time availability checks, reserve study rooms, and push notifications for new resources.

1.2 Your First Visit: What to Expect (And How to Prepare)

Your first trip to the library should be strategic, not stressful. Here’s how to make the most of it:

Step 1: Get Your Student ID & Library Card

  • Your Montclair State ID (issued at orientation) doubles as your library card.
  • Activate it online via the Library Website under "Library Account."
  • Borrowing limits:
    • Physical books: Up to 50 items (with a 30-day loan period, renewable for 30 more days if not requested by another user).
    • Reserve items (textbooks, high-demand books): 3-day loan with no renewals.

Step 2: Locate the Research Help Desk

  • Where to find it: Near the entrance of the CAS Library (look for the "Ask a Librarian" sign).
  • What they offer:
    • One-on-one research consultations (book via Calendly)
    • Database tutorials (e.g., how to use JSTOR, ProQuest, or Google Scholar effectively)
    • Citation help (APA, MLA, Chicago—we’ll cover this in Section 3)

Step 3: Explore the Library Layout (Without Getting Lost)

Montclair’s libraries use a modified Dewey/LC classification system. Here’s a quick breakdown:

Section Location What You’ll Find
Reference (Non-Circulating) Near the front desk Encyclopedias, handbooks, and one-day loan books
General Collection Stacks (2nd & 3rd floors) Fiction, textbooks, and 30-day loan books
Reserves Behind the front desk Professor-recommended textbooks (3-day loan)

2.2 How to Access Journal Articles & Full Text

Strategy 3: The "3-Click Rule" for Getting Full Articles

Many students get stuck on paywalls. Here’s how to bypass them:

  1. Search in Google Scholar → Click "@ Montclair State University" (if available).
  2. Use the "Find It @ Montclair" button (looks like a magnifying glass).
  3. If blocked, try:
    • Interlibrary Loan (ILL) – Request articles not available digitally (takes 24-72 hours).
    • Google "PDF" + article title (sometimes preprints are available).

🚨 Common Mistake: Assuming "free" PDFs online are credible. ✅ Solution: Always check the publisher (Springer, Elsevier, Sage) and citation details before using.

Chapter 3: Research & Study Strategies – How to Work Smarter, Not Harder

3.1 How to Find Primary Sources (Beyond Google)

Primary sources are firsthand accounts (letters, speeches, original research) that elevate your papers from "good" to "standout."

Where to Find Them at Montclair:

Source Type Where to Look Example
Archives AS Library (Special Collections) Montclair’s 1960s civil rights protest letters
Government Docs GPO Monthly Catalog Federal budget reports from the 1980s
Newspapers NewspaperARCHIVE, ProQuest Historical Newspapers 1920s New York Times coverage of jazz age culture
Interviews Academic databases (e.g., Ethnic NewsWatch) Oral histories of NJ immigrants

🔹 How to Cite Primary Sources:

  • APA: (Author, Date) – "Smith (1985) argued..."
  • MLA: (Author Date) – "Smith 1985"
  • Chicago: Footnote + Bibliography – "1. John Smith, 'Title' (Montclair, 1985), 45."

3.2 How to Evaluate Sources (The CRAAP Test)

Not all sources are created equal. Use the CRAAP Test to assess credibility:

C – Currency Is the source up-to-date? (Check publication date.)
R – Relevance Does it directly answer your research question?
A – Authority Who wrote it? (Look for affiliations, credentials).
A – Accuracy Are the facts verifiable? (Cross-check with other sources.)
P – Purpose Is it biased or objective? (Check the publisher’s agenda).

🚨 Common Mistake: Using Wikipedia as a primary source. ✅ Solution: Use Wikipedia to find keywords, then search Montclair’s databases for peer-reviewed articles.

3.3 How to Take Effective Notes & Avoid Plagiarism

Strategy 4: The Cornell Note-Taking System

  1. Record key points in the right column.
  2. Summarize in the left column.
  3. Add questions in the bottom section for discussion.

📌 Example:

Right Column (Notes) Left Column (Summary)
"Study found 68% of students procrastinate due to anxiety (Smith, 2022)" Main point: Procrastination linked to mental health.

Strategy 5: Use Zotero or Mendeley to Avoid Plagiarism

  • Automatically formats citations in APA, MLA, Chicago.
  • Tracks PDFs and prevents duplicate sources.
  • Free for students (no need for expensive tools like EndNote).

💡 Real-World Example: *A history student used Zotero to organize 50 sources for their thesis. When they accidentally misquoted, Zotero flagged the error and suggested corrections.
