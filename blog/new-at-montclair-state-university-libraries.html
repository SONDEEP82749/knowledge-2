Understanding Montclair State University Libraries: A Quick Overview

Montclair State University Libraries consist of three main locations:

  1. The Library at the Center for Academic Success (CAS) – The largest branch, housing millions of books, journals, and digital resources.
  2. The Library at the School of Business – Specialized collections for business, economics, and finance research.
  3. The Library at the School of Education – Focused on education, psychology, and social sciences.

Each library offers: ✅ 24/7 online access to e-books, databases, and streaming media. ✅ Research assistance via live chat, email, and in-person help. ✅ Study spaces, including group rooms, silent zones, and laptop lending. ✅ Special collections, such as rare books, archives, and government documents.

Fun Fact: In 2022-2023, Montclair State students accessed over 5 million digital resources through the library’s databases (Montclair State University Libraries Annual Report, 2023). That’s 5 million opportunities to enhance your research!

8 Actionable Strategies to Succeed at Montclair State University Libraries

1. Start with the Library’s Homepage & Discover Tools

The Montclair State University Libraries homepage is your first stop for research. Instead of guessing where to look, use these key features:

  • Search Box (OneSearch): This is your primary tool for finding books, articles, and more. Try advanced search filters (e.g., date range, full-text availability).
  • Databases A-Z: Not everything is in OneSearch. For niche topics, browse subject-specific databases like:
    • JSTOR (humanities & social sciences)
    • ScienceDirect (STEM fields)
    • PsycINFO (psychology research)
  • Library Guides: These are curated research roadmaps for different subjects. For example:
    • "Business Research Guide" (for MBA students)
    • "Education Research Guide" (for future teachers)

Real-World Example: A history student researching the American Civil War could start with OneSearch but then switch to America’s Historical Newspapers (a specialized database) to find primary sources like old newspapers.

2. Master the Art of Boolean Searching (AND, OR, NOT)

Boolean operators help you refine searches for more precise results. Here’s how to use them:

Operator Meaning Example
AND Both terms must appear "climate change AND policy"
OR Either term works "global warming OR climate crisis"
NOT Excludes a term "artificial intelligence NOT ethics"

Pro Tip:

  • Use quotation marks for exact phrases (e.g., "machine learning algorithms").
  • Avoid stop words like "the," "and," or "of" unless they’re critical to your search.

Common Mistake: Searching too broadly (e.g., just "psychology") leads to irrelevant results. Instead, try: "cognitive behavioral therapy AND depression" for focused findings.

3. Leverage the Library’s Research Help Services

You don’t have to figure everything out alone! Montclair State offers multiple ways to get help:

  • Live Chat (24/7): Available through the library website—instant answers to your questions.
  • Email Reference: Submit questions via the "Ask a Librarian" form.
  • In-Person Help: Visit the Research Help Desk during library hours.
  • Workshops & Tutorials: The library hosts seminars on citation, database use, and research strategies.

Real-World Example: *A graduate student struggling with peer-reviewed article searches could attend a "Finding Scholarly Sources" workshop and learn how to evaluate sources like a pro.

4. Use Zotero or RefWorks for Citation Management

Manually tracking citations is time-consuming and error-prone. Instead, use free citation managers like:

  • Zotero (open-source, easy to use)
  • RefWorks (paid, but integrated with Montclair’s library)

How to Set It Up:

  1. Download & install the browser extension.
  2. Save citations as you find them.
  3. Generate bibliographies in APA, MLA, Chicago, or IEEE format.

Common Mistake: Waiting until the last minute to organize citations. Start early!

5. Explore Special Collections & Archives

Montclair State’s special collections hold unique, historical materials that general searches won’t uncover. Some highlights:

  • Rare Books Collection – Includes first editions, manuscripts, and rare texts.
  • University Archives – Documents the history of Montclair State, including old yearbooks and faculty papers.
  • Government Documents – Federal, state, and local records (great for political science and public policy research).

Real-World Example: *A political science student researching New Jersey’s labor laws could find primary sources in the Government Documents section that aren’t available online.

6. Take Advantage of Study Spaces & Tech Lending

The library isn’t just for books—it’s a productivity hub. Some lesser-known perks:

  • Group Study Rooms – Bookable for collaborative projects.
  • Silent Study Zones – For deep focus (no talking allowed).
  • Tech Lending – Borrow laptops, cameras, and audio recorders for free.
  • 3D Printing & Scanning – Some branches offer on-site printing services.

Pro Tip: Check the library’s calendar for quiet hours—some branches have noise restrictions during exam weeks.

7. Learn How to Evaluate Sources (The CRAAP Test)

Not all sources are equal. Use the CRAAP Test to assess credibility:

C Currency – Is the information up-to-date?
R Relevance – Does it answer your research question?
A Authority – Who wrote it? Are they experts?
A Accuracy – Are the facts verifiable?
P Purpose – Why was it created? (To inform? Sell? Persuade?)

Common Mistake: Relying only on Wikipedia for research. While it’s useful for background info, peer-reviewed journals should be your primary sources.

8. Use Interlibrary Loan (ILL) for Missing Resources

Can’t find a book or article in Montclair’s collection? Interlibrary Loan (ILL) lets you borrow from other libraries for free.

How to Request:

  1. Search OneSearch for the item.
  2. Click "Request via Interlibrary Loan."
  3. Fill out the form—most requests arrive in 3-5 business days.

Real-World Example: *A film student needing a rare movie script not in Montclair’s collection could request it via ILL and receive it digitally or physically within a week.

Common Mistakes New Students Make (And How to Avoid Them)

Mistake #1: Not Using the Library’s Databases Effectively

Problem: Many students rely only on Google Scholar, which can lead to low-quality or irrelevant sources.

Solution:

  • Start with OneSearch, then narrow down using databases.
  • Check the library’s "Recommended Databases" list for your field.

Mistake #2: Ignoring Research Help Services

Problem: Students don’t ask for help until the last minute, leading to stress and poor-quality work.

Solution:

  • Attend workshops early in the semester.
  • Use live chat for quick questions.

Mistake #3: Failing to Save & Organize Citations

Problem: Students forget to cite sources or mix up formats, leading to plagiarism risks.

Solution:

  • Use Zotero or RefWorks from day one.
  • Double-check citations before submitting papers.

Mistake #4: Overlooking Special Collections

Problem: Students don’t explore rare books or archives, missing unique research opportunities.

Solution:

  • Check the library’s "Special Collections" page for hidden gems.
  • Ask librarians about unpublished manuscripts relevant to your topic.

Mistake #5: Not Taking Advantage of Study Spaces

Problem: Students study in noisy areas or don’t book group rooms, reducing productivity.

Solution:

  • Use the library’s "Study Space Finder" to locate quiet zones.
  • Book group rooms in advance for collaborative work.

FAQ: Your Most Pressing Questions About Montclair State Libraries

❓ How do I access e-books and digital resources from off-campus?

Answer: Montclair State provides off-campus access to most digital resources. Simply:

  1. Go to the library website.
  2. Log in using your Montclair State username and password.
  3. Click on the e-book or database link—you’ll be automatically authenticated.

Pro Tip: Bookmark the library’s "Off-Campus Access" guide for troubleshooting.

❓ What if I can’t find a book in the library?

Answer: If a book isn’t available at Montclair, use Interlibrary Loan (ILL). It’s free and easy:

  1. Search OneSearch for the book.
  2. Click "Request via Interlibrary Loan."
  3. Fill out the form—most requests arrive in 3-5 days.

Alternative: Check WorldCat (a global library catalog) to see if another nearby university has it.

❓ How can I find peer-reviewed articles?

Answer: Peer-reviewed articles are essential for academic research. To find them:

  1. Use OneSearch and filter by "Peer-Reviewed Journals."
  2. Browse subject-specific databases like:
    • JSTOR (humanities)
    • PubMed (health sciences)
    • IEEE Xplore (engineering)
  3. Check the "Scholarly Articles" tab in Google Scholar.

Warning: Avoid predatory journals—always verify the publisher’s reputation.

❓ Can I print or scan documents at the library?

Answer: Yes! Most Montclair State libraries offer:

  • Printing (from your Montclair email account).
  • Scanning (to email or USB drive).
  • Photocopying (limited quantities).

Cost: Printing is free for students, but scanning may have a small fee.

❓ How do I cite sources correctly?

Answer: Citation styles vary by discipline. Montclair recommends:

  • APA (Psychology, Education, Social Sciences)
  • MLA (Humanities, Literature)
  • Chicago (History, Business)
  • IEEE (Engineering, Computer Science)

Tools to Help:

  • Purdue OWL (free citation guides)
  • Zotero/RefWorks (automated citations)
  • Library’s Citation Workshop (hands-on practice)

Common Error: Mixing citation styles in one paper. Stick to one format per assignment.
