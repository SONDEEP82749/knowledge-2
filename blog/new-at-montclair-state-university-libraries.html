Chapter 1: Getting Started – Your First Steps at Montclair State University Libraries

1.1 How to Access the Libraries: Physical & Digital Options

Montclair State University Libraries consist of three main branches, each with unique strengths:

  • John J. Cali School of Music Library – Specializes in musicology, audio recordings, and sheet music.
  • Library South (Main Campus) – The largest branch, housing general collections, archives, and special collections (including rare books).
  • Library North (Main Campus) – Focuses on STEM, business, and interdisciplinary research.

Digital Access: Your 24/7 Backpack

You don’t need to be on campus to use the libraries! Here’s how to access everything remotely:

✔ Montclair State University Portal – Log in via my.montclair.edu to access e-books, databases, and interlibrary loan requests. ✔ LibGuides – Subject-specific research guides (e.g., Psychology Research Guide, Business Data Sources). ✔ LibAnswers (FAQ & Chat Support) – Get instant help via live chat or pre-written answers. ✔ Mobile App (LibCal) – Reserve study rooms, check book availability, and receive library alerts.

Pro Tip: Bookmark lib.montclair.edu—your one-stop portal for all library services.

1.2 Your First Visit: What to Expect

Your first trip to the library should be strategic, not overwhelming. Here’s what to do:

  1. Visit the Research Help Desk – Librarians are trained to help with:
    • Finding peer-reviewed articles
    • Citing sources (APA, MLA, Chicago)
    • Using specialized databases (e.g., JSTOR, ProQuest)
  2. Take a Library Tour – Many first-year students skip this, but a 15-minute tour can save you weeks of trial and error.
  3. Check Out a Library Card – Your student ID is your library card—no extra fees!
  4. Explore the "New Books" Shelf – Located near the main entrance, this is where cutting-edge research in your field is displayed.

Real-World Example: A sophomore in the Nursing program, Sarah, struggled to find evidence-based practice articles for her capstone project. After visiting the Research Help Desk, a librarian introduced her to CINAHL Plus, a nursing-specific database she hadn’t known existed. Within 2 hours, she found 10 relevant studies—something she couldn’t locate on Google Scholar.

Chapter 2: 10 Actionable Strategies to Master Montclair State University Libraries

2.1 Strategy 1: Use the Library’s Advanced Search Features (Beyond Google)

Problem: Many students default to Google Scholar or Google Search, which often return low-quality, outdated, or irrelevant sources.

Solution: Montclair’s library catalog (OneSearch) and specialized databases are curated for academic rigor.

How to Search Like a Pro:

  1. Use Boolean Operators (AND, OR, NOT) to refine searches.
    • Example: "climate change AND policy NOT 2010"
  2. Filter by Source Type – Look for:
    • Peer-reviewed journals (gold standard for research)
    • Books & e-books (for in-depth analysis)
    • Datasets & Theses (for original research)
  3. Leverage Subject-Specific Databases – Instead of OneSearch, try:
    • Business: IBISWorld, Business Source Premier
    • STEM: ScienceDirect, IEEE Xplore
    • Humanities: JSTOR, Project MUSE

Common Mistake: Students often ignore the "Limit To" filters, leading to hundreds of irrelevant results.

2.2 Strategy 2: Master Interlibrary Loan (ILL) for Articles & Books You Can’t Find

Problem: You need a specific journal article or book, but Montclair doesn’t have it.

Solution: Interlibrary Loan (ILL) lets you borrow from other institutions for free.

How to Request an ILL:

  1. Go to ILL Request Form.
  2. Search for the item in OneSearch first—if it’s unavailable, request it.
  3. Turnaround Time:
    • Articles: Usually 1-3 days
    • Books: Usually 5-10 days
  4. Renewals: Most ILL items can be extended for 30 days.

Real-World Example: A graduate student in Environmental Science needed a 2022 report from the U.S. Geological Survey that Montclair didn’t have. Using ILL, she received a PDF within 48 hours—something she couldn’t find on Google.

2.3 Strategy 3: Use LibGuides for Subject-Specific Research Help

Problem: You’re not sure where to start for your psychology paper, business case study, or engineering project.

Solution: LibGuides are pre-made research roadmaps created by librarians.

How to Use LibGuides:

  1. Browse LibGuides by Subject.
  2. Look for:
    • Best Databases (e.g., PsycINFO for Psychology)
    • Citation Guides (APA, MLA, etc.)
    • Research Tutorials (e.g., How to Evaluate Sources)
  3. Bookmark your favorite guides for quick access.

Common Mistake: Students assume they must figure out research on their own—LibGuides are free expert help.

2.4 Strategy 4: Attend Library Workshops & Research Help Sessions

Problem: You’re self-teaching research skills, leading to inefficient searches and poor citations.

Solution: Montclair offers free workshops on:

  • Citation Management (Zotero, EndNote)
  • Data Analysis (SPSS, R)
  • Open Access Publishing

How to Find Workshops:

  1. Check Library Events Calendar.
  2. Popular Workshops:
    • "Citing Like a Pro: APA & MLA Made Easy"
    • "Finding Primary Sources for History Projects"
    • "Using Qualtrics for Surveys"

Real-World Example: A business student, James, attended a Zotero workshop and learned how to automatically cite sources in his marketing research paper. This saved him 10+ hours of manual formatting.

2.5 Strategy 5: Utilize the Writing & Academic Support Center (WASC)

Problem: You’ve found your sources, but writing and structuring your paper is overwhelming.

Solution: The Writing & Academic Support Center (WASC) offers:

  • One-on-one tutoring (in-person or virtual)
  • Thesis editing
  • Resume & cover letter reviews

How to Access WASC:

  1. Visit WASC Website.
  2. Book an appointment via LibCal.
  3. Drop-in hours available for quick questions.

Common Mistake: Students wait until the last minute—WASC is best used early in the writing process.

2.6 Strategy 6: Explore Special Collections & Archives

Problem: You need primary sources (letters, historical documents, rare books) for a history or literature project.

Solution: Montclair’s Special Collections include:

  • Montclair Historical Society Archives (local history)
  • African American Cultural Center (civil rights documents)
  • Rare Books Collection (first editions, manuscripts)

How to Access Special Collections:

  1. Request an appointment via Special Collections Page.
  2. Popular Use Cases:
    • History students analyzing 19th-century newspapers.
    • Literature majors studying original manuscripts.

Real-World Example: A PhD candidate in American Literature used Montclair’s rare book collection to find unpublished letters by Flannery O’Connor. This elevated her dissertation from "good" to "groundbreaking."

2.7 Strategy 7: Use the Library’s Tech & Study Spaces

Problem: You need a quiet place to study, but classrooms and dorms are noisy.

Solution: Montclair Libraries offer:

  • Group Study Rooms (reservable via LibCal)
  • Silent Study Zones (no talking allowed)
  • Tech Lending (laptops, cameras, audio recorders)

How to Reserve a Study Room:

  1. Go to LibCal.
  2. Search for "Group Study Room" and select availability.
  3. Pro Tip: Reserve 24-48 hours in advance for peak times.

Common Mistake: Students don’t check room capacities—some seats fewer people than advertised.

2.8 Strategy 8: Leverage the Library’s Career & Internship Resources

Problem: You’re struggling to find internships or networking opportunities.

Solution: The library provides:

  • LinkedIn Learning (free courses on resume writing, interview skills)
  • Career Guides (how to network with alumni)
  • Job Postings (via Montclair Career Services)

How to Use Career Resources:

  1. Access LinkedIn Learning via the library portal.
  2. Popular Courses:
    • "How to Write a Standout Resume"
    • "Negotiating Your Salary"
  3. Attend Career Fairs (announced via Montclair email & LibGuides).

Real-World Example: A senior in Marketing took a LinkedIn Learning course on cold emailing. She used the templates provided to secure a summer internship at a Fortune 500 company—something she couldn’t have done without the library’s resources.

2.9 Strategy 9: Participate in Research Assistantships & Undergraduate Fellowships

Problem: You want hands-on research experience, but don’t know where to start.

Solution: Montclair Libraries connect students with faculty researchers through:

  • Undergraduate Research Fellowships
  • Library Research Assistantships (paid positions)

How to Get Involved:

  1. Check Office of Undergraduate Research.
  2. Popular Opportunities:
    • Data Analysis Assistantships (for STEM students)
    • Digital Humanities Projects (for humanities majors)

Common Mistake: Students assume research is only for grad students—undergrads can lead projects too.

2.10 Strategy 10: Take Advantage of the Library’s Open Access & Digital Scholarship Support

Problem: You want to publish your work, but paywalls and copyright issues hold you back.

Solution: Montclair supports:

  • Open Access Journals (free to publish)
  • Digital Commons @ Montclair (institutional repository)
  • Copyright & Fair Use Guidance

How to Publish Open Access:

  1. Use DOAJ (Directory of Open Access Journals) to find reputable open-access journals.
  2. Submit your work to Digital Commons @ Montclair for free, permanent storage.

Real-World Example: A graduate student in Education published her dissertation in Digital Commons, making it accessible worldwide. This increased her academic profile and led to collaboration opportunities.

Chapter 3: Common Mistakes at Montclair State University Libraries (And How to Avoid Them)

3.1 Mistake #1: Not Using the "Ask a Librarian" Service

What Happens: Students struggle alone with complex searches, leading to wasted time and poor research.

How to Fix It:

  • Use LibAnswers for instant answers.
  • Chat with a librarian during office hours (listed on the website).

Real-World Fix: *A biology student, Michael, spent 3 hours searching for genetic research papers without success. After asking a librarian, he learned about PubMed Central, which gave him 100+ relevant articles in minutes.

3.2 Mistake #2: Ignoring the "New Books" & "Featured Resources" Sections

What Happens: Students miss out on the latest research in their field.

How to Fix It:

  • Check the "New Books" shelf near the main entrance.
  • Follow Montclair Libraries on Social Media for curated recommendations.

Real-World Fix: *A sociology major, Priya, found a 2023 book on racial disparities in education in the "New Books" section—something she couldn’t find on Google.

3.3 Mistake #3: Overlooking the Library’s Tech & Equipment Lending

What Happens: Students pay for equipment (laptops, cameras) when the library offers free alternatives.

How to Fix It:

    • Laptops & tablets
