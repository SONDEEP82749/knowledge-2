Chapter 1: Understanding Montclair State University Libraries – A Quick Overview

Before we jump into strategies, let’s get familiar with the core offerings of Montclair State’s library system.

1.1 The Main Libraries & Their Specializations

Montclair State operates three primary libraries, each with unique strengths:

  • Earl & Louise Delaney Library (Main Campus – Upper Montclair)

    • Largest academic library on campus, housing books, journals, and multimedia collections.
    • Specialized in: Education, psychology, business, and humanities.
    • Features:
      • 24/7 study spaces (including group rooms and silent zones).
      • Digital Scholarship Lab for 3D printing, scanning, and multimedia projects.
      • Rare Book & Special Collections (historical documents, archives).

  • Library at the School of Business (SBL)

    • Focused on finance, marketing, and management resources.
    • Key tools:
      • Bloomberg Terminal (for finance students).
      • IBISWorld & Statista (business databases).
      • Case study collections for real-world business analysis.

  • Library at the School of Arts & Communication (SACL)

    • Designed for film, media, and performing arts students.
    • Special features:
      • Film archives (classic and contemporary cinema).
      • Sound recording studio access.
      • Digital media production tools.

1.2 Digital vs. Physical Resources: What’s the Difference?

Montclair State Libraries offer both physical and digital resources, each with its own advantages:

1.3 Key Services You Didn’t Know You Needed

Beyond books, Montclair State Libraries provide hidden gems that can save you time and stress:

  • Interlibrary Loan (ILL) – Borrow books from other NJ libraries (usually arrives in 3-5 business days).
  • Research Consultations – Free one-on-one help from librarians (schedule via Ask a Librarian).
  • Citation Help – Tools like Zotero & RefWorks to organize and format citations automatically.
  • Study Rooms & Group Workspaces – Bookable rooms for collaborative projects (reserve via the library app).
  • Printing & Scanning – $0.10 per black & white page, $0.25 per color page (bring your own USB drive).

Real-World Example: A senior education major, Jamal, was struggling to find primary sources for his thesis on urban school reform. He scheduled a research consultation with a librarian, who introduced him to ProQuest’s Education Journals and NJ State Archives. Within two weeks, Jamal had 50 relevant sources—something he couldn’t find on Google Scholar alone.

Chapter 2: 8 Actionable Strategies to Master Montclair State Libraries

Now that you know what’s available, let’s get practical. These 8 strategies will turn you from a library newbie into a research powerhouse.

Strategy 1: Start with the Library’s Homepage & Search Like a Pro

Problem: Many students go straight to Google instead of using the library’s search tools, missing high-quality, academic-only sources.

Solution:

  1. Bookmark the Montclair State Library Homepage (https://library.montclair.edu)—it’s your one-stop shop.
  2. Use the Advanced Search (instead of basic) to refine by:
    • Date range (e.g., "2015–Present" for recent research).
    • Source type (books, journals, videos).
    • Subject keywords (e.g., "climate change AND policy").
  3. Try Boolean operators (AND, OR, NOT) for precise searches:
    • "teaching methods AND special education" (finds both terms together).
    • "AI NOT ethics" (excludes ethics-focused results).

Real-World Example: A graduate student in business analytics was researching "machine learning in retail." Instead of searching broadly, she used: "machine learning" AND "retail sales" AND "case study" NOT "theory" This gave her 12 highly relevant case studies in minutes—something a Google search would’ve buried under 100,000 results.

Strategy 2: Leverage Databases for Academic Gold (Not Just Google Scholar)

Problem: Google Scholar is great for quick answers, but Montclair’s databases contain peer-reviewed, vetted research.

Solution: Montclair provides over 200 databases, but these are the top 5 for most students:

Pro Tip: If a database doesn’t load, try the VPN (Montclair provides one via the IT portal) to ensure off-campus access.

Common Mistake: Assuming all databases are the same—each has unique filters. For example, JSTOR is great for full-text articles, while ProQuest has more business case studies.

Strategy 3: Use Zotero or RefWorks to Organize Your Research

Problem: Losing track of sources, PDFs, and citations is a major productivity killer.

Solution:

  • Zotero (Free & Easy) – A browser extension that:
    • Auto-saves articles from databases.
    • Tags & annotates sources.
    • Generates citations in APA, MLA, Chicago styles.
  • RefWorks (Paid, but Integrated) – Better for large projects, with collaboration tools.

Step-by-Step:

  1. Install Zotero (https://www.zotero.org).
  2. Create a folder for your paper (e.g., "Final Paper – Sociology").
  3. Drag & drop PDFs from databases into Zotero.
  4. Use the "Add On" for Word/Google Docs to auto-format citations.

Real-World Example: *A history major, Priya, was writing a 50-page thesis on the American Revolution. Instead of manually citing, she used Zotero to:

  • Store 150+ sources in one place.
  • Generate citations in Chicago style with one click.
  • Avoid plagiarism by tracking exact page numbers.

Result: She submitted her paper 2 days early and spent half the time on citations.*

Strategy 4: Master the Interlibrary Loan (ILL) System

Problem: Your favorite book isn’t at Montclair—but you don’t want to wait weeks for it.

Solution: Montclair’s Interlibrary Loan (ILL) system lets you borrow books from other NJ libraries in 3-5 business days.

How to Use ILL:

  1. Search the library catalog—if the book says "Not Available," note the title.
  2. Submit an ILL request via https://library.montclair.edu/ill.
  3. Check your email for updates (usually arrives in 3-7 days).
  4. Pick up at the circulation desk (or request digital scanning if you only need a chapter).

Pro Tip: For urgent needs, check if the book is available at Rutgers or NJIT—they often have faster turnaround.

Common Mistake: Assuming ILL is only for books—you can also request articles if Montclair doesn’t have them.

Strategy 5: Utilize the Digital Scholarship Lab for Advanced Projects

Problem: You need 3D printing, scanning, or video editing for a capstone project, but don’t know where to start.

Solution: Montclair’s Digital Scholarship Lab (located in the Delaney Library) offers:

  • 3D printing & scanning (for engineering, art, or research projects).
  • Audio/Video recording & editing (for media students).
  • GIS mapping tools (for geography & urban planning).
  • E-book creation & publishing (for digital humanities).

How to Access:

  1. Book a session via https://library.montclair.edu/digital-scholarship.
  2. Bring your project files (USB drive recommended).
  3. Get hands-on help from library staff.

Real-World Example: *A computer science student, Carlos, needed to 3D print a prototype for his robotics final project. He visited the lab, where staff:

  • Helped him design the model in Tinkercad.
  • Printed it in 2 hours (instead of waiting for a local maker space).
  • Provided post-processing tips (sanding, painting).

Result: His project won first place in the Engineering Expo.*

Strategy 6: Attend Library Workshops & Research Help Sessions

Problem: You’re not sure how to use databases or format citations correctly.

Solution: Montclair offers free workshops on:

  • Research strategies (how to find primary sources).
  • Citation management (Zotero, RefWorks).
  • Data analysis (SPSS, R, Python basics).
  • Open Access publishing (how to share your work for free).

How to Find Workshops:

  1. Check the library calendar (https://library.montclair.edu/events).
  2. Sign up via email (usually 1-2 sessions per month).
  3. Attend or watch recordings if you can’t make it.

Pro Tip: Even if you’re not a beginner, these workshops often reveal hidden features in databases you didn’t know existed.

Strategy 7: Use the Library App for On-the-Go Access

Problem: You’re off-campus and need quick access to books, databases, or study rooms.

Solution: Montclair’s library app (available on iOS & Android) lets you:

  • Search the catalog from your phone.
  • Renew books without visiting.
  • Find & book study rooms.
  • Get push notifications for new database updates.

How to Download:

  1. Search "Montclair State University Libraries" in the App Store/Google Play.
  2. Log in with your MSU credentials.
  3. Enable notifications for new research alerts.

Real-World Example: *A nursing student, Maya, was studying for finals on her phone. She used the app to:

  • Find a nearby study room (booked it for 2 hours).
  • Check if her textbook was available (it was—she picked it up later).
  • Get a citation reminder for her APA-formatted paper.

Result: She studied more efficiently and avoided last-minute library runs.*
