Chapter 1: Getting Acquainted with Montclair State University Libraries

Before diving into strategies, let’s explore the core components of the library system.

1.1 The Library System: More Than Just Books

Montclair State Libraries consist of three main branches, each catering to different academic needs:

  • The Library at the Center for Academic Success (CAS) – The hub for general research, featuring:

    • A 24/7 study lounge with silent and collaborative spaces
    • Over 500,000 physical books, including rare collections
    • Specialized research guides for STEM, humanities, and social sciences
    • Interlibrary Loan (ILL) services to borrow books from other institutions

  • The Music Library – A treasure trove for musicians, composers, and music scholars, housing:

    • Sheet music, audio recordings, and MIDI files
    • Digital archives of North Jersey music history
    • Recording studios and practice rooms (available for student use)

  • The Art Library – Supporting visual arts, design, and media studies, it includes:

    • Art books, catalogs, and rare prints
    • Digital image databases (e.g., Artstor, JSTOR Art)
    • 3D printing and scanning services for student projects

Pro Tip: Use the Montclair State Libraries’ website to explore each branch’s unique offerings. The "Find Your Library" tool helps you locate resources by subject.

Chapter 2: 8 Actionable Strategies to Succeed at Montclair State Libraries

Now that you know what’s available, let’s dive into how to use it effectively.

Strategy 1: Master the Library Catalog (Montclair OneSearch)

Problem: Many students waste time searching for books manually instead of using the unified catalog.

Solution: Montclair State’s Montclair OneSearch is a single search bar that pulls results from books, journals, articles, and digital collections.

How to Use It Like a Pro:

  1. Use advanced search filters (e.g., "Available at CAS Library," "Full Text Online").
  2. Check the "Peer-Reviewed" filter for academic journals.
  3. Save searches for recurring research (e.g., tracking new publications in your field).
  4. Set up alerts for new books on your topic (via LibraryThing Alerts).

Common Mistake: Searching only for books when articles or primary sources might be more relevant.

Fix: Try combining keywords with operators like:

  • "climate change" AND "New Jersey" AND "2020-2023"
  • filetype:pdf "research paper" "Montclair State" (for open-access theses)

Strategy 2: Leverage Database Subscriptions for Premium Research

Problem: Free Google Scholar searches often lead to paywalled articles.

Solution: Montclair State provides exclusive access to 100+ premium databases, including:

Database Best For How to Access
JSTOR Humanities & social sciences Via Montclair Libraries
ScienceDirect STEM research Linked from course readings
ProQuest Business, education, and news Search via OneSearch
EBSCOhost Nursing, psychology, and more Available 24/7

Real-World Example: James, a Computer Science major, needed a peer-reviewed paper on AI ethics. Instead of paying $30 for a PDF, he used Montclair’s JSTOR subscription to find the same article for free. He later cited it in his senior thesis—something he wouldn’t have done without library access.

Pro Tip: Bookmark the Montclair Databases A-Z List for quick access.

Strategy 3: Use LibGuides for Subject-Specific Help

Problem: Students often don’t know where to start when researching a niche topic.

Solution: Montclair’s LibGuides are curated research guides created by librarians for every major and minor.

How to Use Them:

  • Find your department’s guide (e.g., Psychology or Business).
  • Look for:
    • Recommended databases (e.g., PsycINFO for psychology students)
    • Citation examples (avoid plagiarism!)
    • Workshop links (e.g., Excel for Data Analysis)

Common Mistake: Ignoring LibGuides because they seem "too basic."

Fix: Even PhD students use them—LibGuides often include advanced tools like Zotero tutorials or statistical software guides.

Strategy 4: Attend Workshops & Trainings (Free!)

Problem: Many students don’t know workshops exist—or assume they’re only for freshmen.

Solution: Montclair Libraries offer free, expert-led workshops on:

  • Citation management (Zotero, EndNote)
  • Data visualization (Tableau, R Studio)
  • Critical thinking & research ethics
  • Open-access publishing

How to Sign Up:

  1. Check the Library Events Calendar.
  2. Virtual workshops are often recorded—great for last-minute prep!
  3. In-person sessions are best for hands-on practice (e.g., 3D modeling in the Art Library).

Real-World Example: Ana, a Public Health graduate student, attended a workshop on SPSS for statistical analysis. She later used the skills to analyze survey data for her master’s thesis, saving her weeks of trial-and-error learning.

Strategy 5: Use Interlibrary Loan (ILL) for Books Not in Montclair’s Collection

Problem: Your professor assigns a book Montclair doesn’t own.

Solution: Interlibrary Loan (ILL) lets you borrow books from other NJ colleges (Rutgers, Princeton, etc.) for free.

How to Request:

  1. Search Montclair OneSearch—if the book says "Not Available," click "Request via ILL."
  2. Fill out the form (takes 2-5 business days).
  3. Pick up the book at the Circulation Desk (or have it emailed as a PDF if available).

Common Mistake: Assuming ILL is only for books—it also works for articles and DVDs.

Fix: Check the ILL request form for all available formats.

Strategy 6: Explore Digital Scholarship & Open Access

Problem: Students want to publish their work but don’t know where to start.

Solution: Montclair’s Digital Commons is a free, open-access repository where students can:

  • Upload theses, research papers, and creative projects
  • Increase visibility (Google Scholar indexes these works!)
  • Meet tenure requirements (for faculty)

How to Contribute:

  1. Visit Montclair Digital Commons.
  2. Contact the Digital Scholarship Librarian (libref@montclair.edu) for help.
  3. Tag your work with keywords (e.g., "#MontclairResearch," "#ClimateJustice").

Real-World Example: Ethan, a Political Science senior, uploaded his independent study on voter suppression to Digital Commons. A professor from NYU reached out, offering him a research assistant position—something he never would have gotten without open-access exposure.

Strategy 7: Optimize Your Study Spaces (Beyond the Library)

Problem: The library is too noisy or too crowded for focused work.

Solution: Montclair Libraries offer multiple study environments:

Space Best For How to Reserve
Silent Study Rooms Deep focus (exams, papers) First-come, first-served (or reserve via Montclair Study Spaces)
Collaborative Tables Group projects, discussions No reservation needed
24/7 Computer Labs Late-night work, coding Available every day (no ID required after hours)

Pro Tip: Use the Montclair Library App to check real-time occupancy and find empty spaces.

Strategy 8: Build a Personal Research Toolkit

Problem: Students scatter their sources across Google Docs, emails, and sticky notes.

Solution: Montclair Libraries recommend three must-have tools:

  1. Zotero (Free Citation Manager)

    • Automatically saves PDFs, notes, and citations
    • Generates bibliographies in APA, MLA, etc.
    • Works with Word & Google Docs
    • Download: https://www.zotero.org/

  2. RefWorks (For Advanced Researchers)

    • Better for large-scale projects (e.g., dissertations)
    • Integrates with Montclair’s databases

  3. Notion or Evernote (For Organization)

    • Tag sources by topic (e.g., #ClimateChange, #LiteratureReview)
    • Link to PDFs stored in Zotero

Common Mistake: Waiting until the last week to organize sources.

Fix: Start Day 1 of your research project. A well-organized toolkit saves hours of stress.

Mistake 1: Ignoring Librarians

Why It’s Bad: Librarians are experts—they can save you hours of wrong-turn research.

How to Fix It:

  • Email libref@montclair.edu with specific questions.
  • Attend "Ask a Librarian" hours (virtual or in-person).
  • Follow @MontclairLibraries on Twitter for research tips.

Real-World Example: Lena, a Biology major, thought she could find genetic research papers on her own. After three failed Google Scholar searches, she asked a librarian, who taught her to use PubMed—a specialized biology database she didn’t know existed. She found 10 relevant studies in 10 minutes.

Mistake 2: Overlooking Digital Collections

Why It’s Bad: Many students only search physical books, missing e-books, audiobooks, and multimedia.

How to Fix It:

  • Use the "eBooks" filter in Montclair OneSearch.
  • Explore Montclair’s Digital Collections for primary sources (e.g., historical newspapers, oral histories).

Example: A History student researching Prohibition found original 1920s newspaper clippings in the digital archives—something no textbook could provide.
