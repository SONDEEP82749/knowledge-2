Chapter 1: Getting Oriented – Your First Steps at MSU Libraries

Before diving into research, familiarizing yourself with the physical and digital layout of MSU’s libraries will set you up for success.

1.1 The Two Main Libraries You Need to Know

Montclair State University operates two primary libraries, each serving different academic needs:

A. Earl G. Graves Library (Main Campus – Upper Montclair)

  • Location: 1 Normal Avenue, Upper Montclair
  • Specialties:
    • General academic research (humanities, social sciences, STEM)
    • Digital collections (e-books, journals, databases)
    • Study spaces (group rooms, silent zones, tech-equipped areas)
    • Special collections (rare books, archives, local history)
  • Hours: Varies by semester (check MSU Libraries’ official hours for updates)
  • Unique Feature: The "Library Lab"—a space for 3D printing, VR, and maker projects—is perfect for STEM and design students.

B. School of Business Library (Lower Montclair – Business Building)

  • Location: 1 Normal Avenue (Lower Level, Business Building)
  • Specialties:
    • Business, economics, and finance databases (Bloomberg Terminal, IBISWorld, Mergent)
    • Case studies & industry reports
    • Career resources (resume workshops, LinkedIn access)
    • Quiet study zones (ideal for exams and group projects)
  • Hours: Typically 8:00 AM – 10:00 PM (Mon-Thu), 8:00 AM – 6:00 PM (Fri), closed weekends
  • Unique Feature: Exclusive access to the Bloomberg Terminal, a $24,000+ per year tool used by Wall Street analysts—free for MSU students.

1.2 How to Navigate the Physical Library (Without Getting Lost)

Even if you’re a digital research powerhouse, knowing your way around the physical library can save you hours of frustration.

Key Areas to Locate Immediately:

  1. Circulation Desk – Where you check out books, renew loans, and get help with lost items.
  2. Reference Desk – Staffed by librarians who can guide you on research strategies (ask for help with APA, MLA, or Chicago citations).
  3. Study Rooms – Reserve via the Library Study Room Scheduler (first-come, first-served for walk-ins).
  4. Tech Zones – Laptops, scanners, and printers are available for free (bring your MSU ID).
  5. Special Collections – If you’re working on a history, literature, or archival project, this is where rare books and primary sources live.

Real-World Example: A first-year history student named Jamie was assigned a paper on 19th-century New Jersey politics. Instead of searching online, she visited the Special Collections and found original newspaper clippings from the 1880s—giving her exclusive primary sources that most students couldn’t access. Her professor praised her "unexpected depth of research."

Chapter 2: 10 Actionable Strategies to Master MSU Libraries

Now that you’re oriented, let’s dive into practical strategies to make the most of your library resources.

2.1 Strategy #1: Use the Library’s Search Portal (Beyond Google)

Google is great, but MSU’s library databases contain peer-reviewed journals, e-books, and restricted content that Google can’t access.

How to Search Effectively:

  1. Start at MSU Libraries’ Search Portal (not Google Scholar).
  2. Use advanced filters (by date, subject, full-text availability).
  3. Try Boolean operators (AND, OR, NOT) for precise searches:
    • "climate change AND policy" (finds articles with both terms)
    • "machine learning NOT beginner" (excludes introductory guides)
  4. Save your searches in EndNote or Zotero (more on this later).

Common Mistake: Students often search Google first and miss paywalled articles that MSU subscribes to. Always check the library portal first!

2.2 Strategy #2: Leverage Database-Specific Tools

Not all databases are created equal. Some (like JSTOR or ScienceDirect) have special features that can cut your research time in half.

Top Databases for MSU Students & How to Use Them:

Database Best For Pro Tip
JSTOR Humanities, social sciences Use "Advanced Search" to filter by article type (reviews, research articles)
ProQuest Business, education, health Try "Thesaurus" to find synonyms for weak keywords
ScienceDirect STEM, medicine Use "Cited By" to find related research
Google Scholar Broad searches Set up MSU’s proxy access (via Library Guide) to unlock paywalled papers

Real-World Example: A biology major named Priya needed recent studies on CRISPR gene editing. Instead of searching Google, she used ScienceDirect’s "Cited By" feature to find 2023 papers that cited a 2020 foundational study—giving her cutting-edge research in minutes.

2.3 Strategy #3: Master Zotero for Citation Management

Forget manual citations—Zotero (a free, browser-based tool) automatically formats references in APA, MLA, Chicago, and more.

How to Set Up Zotero in 5 Steps:

  1. Download Zotero from https://www.zotero.org/
  2. Install the browser connector (Chrome/Firefox/Edge)
  3. Search MSU databases and drag articles into Zotero
  4. Create a bibliography in your preferred style
  5. Export directly into Word/Google Docs

Pro Tip: Use Zotero’s "Related Items" feature to find additional sources based on what you’ve already collected.

Common Mistake: Students wait until the last minute to organize citations, leading to plagiarism risks and late penalties. Start early!

2.4 Strategy #4: Find E-Books & Audiobooks for On-the-Go Learning

MSU’s e-book collection (via Ebsco, ProQuest, and OverDrive) lets you study anywhere—no library trip needed.

How to Access E-Books:

  1. Go to MSU E-Books
  2. Search by title, subject, or keyword
  3. Download via OverDrive (for audiobooks) or read online (for PDFs)
  4. **Use Libby (by OverDrive) for free audiobooks on your phone

Real-World Example: A nursing student named Carlos used MSU’s e-book collection to study pharmacology on his commute to clinical rotations, saving hours of library time and boosting his exam scores.

2.5 Strategy #5: Use the Library’s Research Guides

MSU’s librarians have already done the hard work—they’ve created subject-specific research guides to help you find the best sources quickly.

How to Find & Use Research Guides:

  1. Visit MSU Library Guides
  2. Browse by subject (e.g., "Psychology Research Guide", "Business Analytics Guide")
  3. Follow the step-by-step instructions (e.g., "Best Databases for Literature Reviews")
  4. Bookmark your favorite guides for future use

Pro Tip: The "How to Write a Literature Review" guide is a game-changer for grad students!

2.6 Strategy #6: Attend Library Workshops & Tutorials

MSU offers free workshops on research, citations, and data analysis—you don’t want to miss these!

Upcoming Workshops (Check MSU Events Calendar):

  • "Citation Management with Zotero" (Weekly)
  • "Advanced Google Scholar Search" (Biweekly)
  • "Data Visualization with Tableau" (For STEM students)

Real-World Example: A computer science student named Leila attended a "Data Analysis Workshop" and learned how to clean datasets in Python—a skill that landed her a research assistant position in her second year.

2.7 Strategy #7: Request Interlibrary Loan (ILL) for Missing Books

Not every book is in MSU’s collection—but Interlibrary Loan (ILL) lets you borrow from other NJ libraries in 3-5 business days.

How to Request ILL:

  1. Go to MSU ILL Page
  2. Search for the book—if it’s not available, click "Request via ILL"
  3. Fill out the form (takes 5 minutes)
  4. Check your MSU email for updates

Pro Tip: For urgent needs, request PDF scans (if available) instead of physical books.

Common Mistake: Students forget to renew ILL books on time, leading to late fees. Set a calendar reminder for due dates.

2.8 Strategy #8: Use the Library’s 3D Printing & Maker Lab

If you’re in engineering, design, or business, MSU’s Library Lab offers 3D printing, laser cutting, and prototyping tools.

How to Access the Maker Lab:

  1. Book a session via Library Lab Scheduler
  2. **Attend a 1-hour training (required for first-time users)
  3. Upload your design (STL files for 3D printing)
  4. Print for free (with material limits)

Real-World Example: A mechanical engineering student named Raj 3D-printed a prototype for his capstone project—saving $500+ on external prototyping services.

2.9 Strategy #9: Ask a Librarian for 1-on-1 Help

MSU’s librarians are experts—they can save you weeks of wasted research.

How to Get Help:

  • Chat with a Librarian (via Ask a Librarian)
  • Schedule a Research Appointment (for deep dives on complex topics)
  • Visit the Reference Desk (for quick questions)

Pro Tip: Librarians can teach you how to find primary sources, evaluate sources for credibility, and avoid predatory journals.

2.10 Strategy #10: Use the Library’s Career & Job Resources

The School of Business Library isn’t just for research—it’s also a career powerhouse.

Chapter 3: Common Library Mistakes & How to Avoid Them

Even the best students make avoidable mistakes—here’s how to steer clear of them.

3.1 Mistake #1: Not Checking Loan Policies

Problem: Some books don’t renew, some have hold limits, and overdue fines add up fast.

Solution:

  • Check due dates in your MSU Library Account (https://www.montclair.edu/library/account)
  • Renew books early (via the same portal)
  • Return books on time (or use the "Hold Shelf" to avoid fines)

Pro Tip: If a book is checked out, use Interlibrary Loan (ILL) instead of waiting.

3.2 Mistake #2: Ignoring the Library’s Digital Collections

Problem: Students only use Google and miss exclusive MSU databases.

Solution:

  • Bookmark MSU’s Digital Collections
  • Search for primary sources (e.g., historical NJ newspapers)
  • **Use e-books for off-campus study

3.3 Mistake #3: Not Using Subject-Specific Databases

Problem: A business student searches JSTOR (humanities) instead of ProQuest (business).

Solution:

  • Match databases to your major:
    • Business? → ProQuest, Bloomberg
    • STEM? → ScienceDirect, IEEE Xplore
    • Humanities? → JSTOR, Project MUSE
