Chapter 1: Understanding the Montclair State University Libraries System

Before diving into strategies, it’s essential to know the layout and offerings of MSU’s library system. The university operates three main libraries, each with unique strengths:

1. The Library at the Center (Main Campus – Montclair)

  • Location: 1 Normal Avenue, Montclair, NJ 07043
  • Key Features:
    • 24/7 study spaces (including group rooms and silent zones)
    • Special collections (rare books, archives, and NJ-specific resources)
    • The Innovation Lab (3D printing, VR, and digital fabrication)
    • The Writing Center & Research Help Desk (free consultations)
    • Over 1 million physical volumes, including textbooks, journals, and multimedia

2. The Library at the School of Business (SBS Library)

  • Location: 1 Normal Avenue (inside the School of Business building)
  • Key Features:
    • Business-specific databases (IBISWorld, Mergent Online, Statista)
    • Case study collections (ideal for MBA and finance students)
    • Financial research tools (Bloomberg Terminal access for qualified students)
    • Quiet study pods for focused work

3. The Library at the School of Education (SOE Library)

  • Location: 1 Normal Avenue (inside the School of Education building)
  • Key Features:
    • Education-focused databases (ERIC, Education Full Text)
    • Curriculum materials & teacher resource collections
    • Early childhood education books & research papers
    • Collaboration spaces for future educators

Digital Access: Beyond the Physical Libraries

MSU Libraries also provide remote access to:

  • E-books & audiobooks (via Ebrary, OverDrive, and Libby)
  • Journals & articles (via JSTOR, ProQuest, and ScienceDirect)
  • Open-access repositories (like Montclair State’s Digital Commons)
  • Interlibrary Loan (ILL) – Borrow books from other institutions for free

Pro Tip: Bookmark the MSU Libraries’ website and set up a library account (via MyMSU Portal) to track checkouts, renewals, and fines.

Chapter 2: 10 Actionable Strategies to Master Montclair State University Libraries

Now that you know what’s available, let’s explore how to use it effectively. These strategies are tested by upperclassmen and librarians—implement them, and you’ll save hours of research time while improving your work quality.

Strategy 1: Start with the Library’s Research Guides (Subject-Specific Roadmaps)

Problem: Many students waste time searching for sources without knowing where to begin.

Solution: MSU Libraries provides pre-made research guides for every major, minor, and even general topics like thesis writing and APA formatting.

How to Use Them:

  1. Go to MSU Library Research Guides
  2. Select your major (e.g., "Psychology," "Computer Science," "English Literature")
  3. Follow the step-by-step database recommendations tailored to your needs.

Real-World Example: A sophomore in Environmental Science was struggling to find peer-reviewed articles on climate change policies. Instead of searching Google Scholar randomly, they used the Environmental Science Research Guide, which directed them to GreenFILE and ScienceDirect—where they found exactly the sources their professor required.

Common Mistake: Skipping the research guides and relying on Google searches—which often lead to outdated, biased, or non-academic sources.

Strategy 2: Use the Advanced Search Function (Not Just Basic Keywords)

Problem: Basic keyword searches return thousands of irrelevant results, making it hard to find credible sources quickly.

Solution: Advanced search filters help narrow down results by:

  • Date range (e.g., "2015–Present")
  • Source type (peer-reviewed journals, books, dissertations)
  • Subject terms (e.g., "artificial intelligence," "social justice")
  • Full-text availability (only show sources you can read immediately)

How to Apply This:

  1. In JSTOR, ProQuest, or Google Scholar, click "Advanced Search".
  2. Use Boolean operators (AND, OR, NOT) for precise queries.
    • Example: "climate change" AND "policy" NOT "opinion"
  3. Save searches for future reference.

Real-World Example: *A junior in Political Science needed empirical studies on gun control laws. Instead of searching "gun laws", they used advanced filters to:

  • Limit to "peer-reviewed journals"
  • Select "2018–2023"
  • Exclude "news articles" This reduced their results from 5,000 to 47 highly relevant studies in minutes.*

Common Mistake: Using too broad terms (e.g., "technology") without refining searches—leading to information overload.

Strategy 3: Leverage the Library’s Database A-Z List (Don’t Rely on Google Scholar Alone)

Problem: Google Scholar is convenient, but it lacks MSU’s paid subscriptions, meaning you can’t always access full texts.

Solution: MSU Libraries provides over 500 databases, many of which are exclusive to students. The A-Z Database List is your goldmine.

How to Use It:

  1. Go to MSU Databases A-Z
  2. Browse by subject (e.g., "Business," "Health Sciences," "Arts & Humanities")
  3. Try these top databases for different fields:
    • Business: Business Source Premier, IBISWorld
    • Health Sciences: CINAHL, MEDLINE
    • Humanities: JSTOR, Project MUSE
    • STEM: ScienceDirect, IEEE Xplore

Real-World Example: A graduate student in Nursing needed evidence-based practice articles on diabetes management. Instead of searching Google Scholar, they used CINAHL, which provided direct PDF access to 120 relevant studies—all peer-reviewed and free through MSU.

Common Mistake: Assuming Google Scholar is enough—many students can’t access full texts, leading to frustration and incomplete research.

Strategy 4: Use Zotero for Free Citation Management (No More Lost References)

Problem: Manually tracking citations is tedious, and APA/MLA formatting errors cost points.

Solution: Zotero is a free, browser-based citation manager that:

  • Automatically saves sources from databases
  • Generates citations in any style (APA, MLA, Chicago)
  • Creates bibliographies with one click
  • Syncs across devices (via Zotero account)

How to Set It Up:

  1. Download Zotero (works with Zotero Connector for easy saving)
  2. Save articles from JSTOR/ProQuest by clicking "Save to Zotero"
  3. Drag and drop PDFs into your library
  4. Insert citations into Word/Google Docs via Zotero’s Word plugin

Real-World Example: *A freshman in History was writing a 10-page paper on the American Revolution. Instead of copy-pasting citations manually, they used Zotero, which:

  • Saved 50+ sources in one place
  • Auto-formatted APA citations
  • Generated a bibliography in 30 seconds This saved them hours of work and eliminated formatting errors.

Common Mistake: Waiting until the last minute to organize citations—leading to rushed, error-prone work.

Strategy 5: Master Interlibrary Loan (ILL) for Books Not in MSU’s Collection

Problem: MSU doesn’t have every book you need, and buying them is expensive.

Solution: Interlibrary Loan (ILL) lets you borrow books from other universities for free (with a 1–2 week turnaround).

How to Request an ILL:

  1. Go to MSU ILL Page
  2. Search MSU’s catalog—if a book isn’t available, click "Request via ILL"
  3. Fill out the form with your MSU email and pickup location
  4. Check your email for updates (books arrive via campus mail or library pickup)

Real-World Example: *A senior in Philosophy needed a rare 19th-century text on existentialism that MSU didn’t own. They requested it via ILL, and within 5 days, the book arrived at The Library at the Center—no cost, no hassle.

Common Mistake: Assuming MSU has everything—many students give up when they can’t find a book, when ILL could solve the problem in days.

Strategy 6: Use the Library’s Research Help Desk (Free Expert Consultations)

Problem: You’re stuck on a research topic but don’t know where to turn.

Solution: MSU Libraries offers free research consultations with librarians who specialize in your field.

How to Book an Appointment:

  1. Go to Research Help Desk
  2. Click "Schedule an Appointment"
  3. Select a librarian based on your subject (e.g., Business, Education, STEM)
  4. Meet via Zoom or in-person for personalized guidance

Real-World Example: *A graduate student in Public Health was struggling to find statistically valid data on childhood obesity trends. They booked a 30-minute consultation with a Health Sciences librarian, who:

  • Taught them how to use CDC databases effectively
  • *Showed them how to filter for reliable, recent studies
  • *Saved them weeks of trial-and-error searching

Common Mistake: Assuming you can figure it out alone—librarians save time and improve research quality.

Strategy 7: Explore the Innovation Lab (For STEM, Design, and Creative Projects)

Problem: You need access to 3D printers, VR, or digital fabrication tools but don’t know where to go.

Solution: The Innovation Lab (inside The Library at the Center) offers:

  • 3D printing & scanning
  • Virtual reality (VR) headsets
  • Digital fabrication (laser cutting, CNC machines)
  • **Workshops on Adobe Creative Suite, Python, and more

How to Access It:

  1. Visit Innovation Lab
  2. Book a session (some tools require training)
  3. Bring your project files (STL, CAD, etc.)

Real-World Example: *A Computer Science major was working on a robotics project for their capstone course. Instead of buying expensive tools, they used:

  • The 3D printer to create custom parts
  • VR headsets to test simulations
  • Adobe Illustrator (available in the lab) to design circuit diagrams This cut costs by 70% while improving their project quality.

Common Mistake: Ignoring free campus resources—many students pay out-of-pocket for tools they could use for free at the library.

Strategy 8: Use the Library’s Study Spaces Strategically (Avoid Distractions)

Problem: You’re trying to study in a noisy café or dorm room, leading to poor focus and lower retention.

Solution: MSU Libraries offer multiple study environments, each designed for different needs:

Study Space Best For Location
Silent Study Zones Deep focus (reading, writing) Main Library (2nd floor)
Group Study Rooms Collaborative projects SBS Library (book ahead)
Computer Labs Typing papers, coding The Library at the Center (1st floor)
Quiet Pods Individual work (no talking) SOE Library
Outdoor Study Areas Fresh air & sunlight Library courtyard (weather permitting)

Pro Tip:

  • Book group rooms in advance (via Library Study Rooms)
  • Use the "Quiet Hours" (typically 10 PM–8 AM on weekdays)

Real-World Example: *A pre-law student was struggling with LSAT prep in their dorm. They switched to the Silent Study Zone in the Main Library, where:

  • No distractions allowed
