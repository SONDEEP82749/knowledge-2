New at Montclair State University Libraries: A Complete Guide to Maximizing Your Research & Study Success

Chapter 1: Understanding the Montclair State University Libraries System

Before diving into strategies, it’s crucial to understand the structure of the libraries. Montclair State operates three main campus libraries, each with unique strengths:

1. The Library at the Center for Academic & Creative Excellence (CACE)

  • Location: Main campus (near the Student Center)
  • Specialties:
    • General academic research (humanities, social sciences, STEM)
    • Study spaces (group rooms, silent zones, tech-equipped pods)
    • 24/7 access (via keycard or online)
    • Special collections (rare books, archives, local history)

2. The Library at the School of Business

  • Location: School of Business building
  • Specialties:
    • Business & economics databases (IBISWorld, Mergent, Statista)
    • Financial modeling software (Bloomberg Terminal, S&P Capital IQ)
    • Career resources (resume workshops, LinkedIn training)

3. The Library at the School of Education

  • Location: School of Education building
  • Specialties:
    • Education research databases (ERIC, ProQuest Education)
    • Teacher resource center (curriculum materials, lesson plans)
    • K-12 educator support (book sets, professional development guides)

Bonus: The Digital Library & Online Resources

  • Montclair State University Digital Commons – Open-access journals, theses, and faculty research.
  • Interlibrary Loan (ILL) – Borrow books from over 10,000 partner libraries worldwide.
  • LibGuides – Subject-specific research guides created by librarians.

Pro Tip: Bookmark the Montclair State University Libraries website and set up a personal account to track checkouts, renewals, and saved searches.

Chapter 2: 10 Actionable Strategies to Master Montclair State University Libraries

Now that you know the layout, let’s explore practical, step-by-step strategies to maximize your library experience.

Strategy 1: Set Up Your Library Account & Enable Alerts

Why it matters: A personalized library account gives you real-time updates on due dates, holds, and new resources.

How to do it:

  1. Log in to your account via Montclair State Libraries Online.
  2. Enable email alerts for:
    • Overdue notices (avoid fines!)
    • New book arrivals in your field
    • Database updates (e.g., new journal issues)
  3. Link your account to Google Drive or OneDrive for seamless document storage.

Real-World Example: *A senior economics major, Jamal, used library alerts to track new Statista reports on emerging markets. By setting up a weekly digest, he found a key dataset that became the core of his capstone project, earning him a distinction in research.

Strategy 2: Use the Advanced Search Function Like a Pro

Why it matters: The basic search bar is great, but advanced search filters help you find exactly what you need without sifting through irrelevant results.

How to do it:

  1. Go to the library homepage and click "Advanced Search."
  2. Refine by:
    • Date range (e.g., "2020–Present" for current research)
    • Resource type (books, journal articles, videos, datasets)
    • Subject keywords (e.g., "climate change AND policy")
  3. Use Boolean operators (AND, OR, NOT) for precise searches:
    • "Global warming AND renewable energy"
    • "Machine learning NOT beginner"

Common Mistake: Searching too broadly (e.g., just "psychology") leads to thousands of results. Instead, try: ❌ "Psychology" ✅ "Cognitive behavioral therapy AND depression treatment"

Real-World Example: *A nursing student, Priya, needed peer-reviewed studies on telehealth in pediatric care. She used advanced search filters to:

  • Select "Peer-Reviewed Journals"
  • Limit to "2018–2024"
  • Add "clinical trials" as a keyword This narrowed her results from 5,000 to 47 highly relevant articles in minutes.

Strategy 3: Leverage Librarian Research Consultations

Why it matters: Librarians at Montclair State are experts in research strategies—many have PhDs in their fields and can save you weeks of trial and error.

How to book a consultation:

  1. Visit the Research Help Desk.
  2. Schedule a virtual or in-person meeting (15–60 minutes).
  3. Prepare in advance with:
    • Your topic & research question
    • Any specific databases you’ve tried (and why they didn’t work)
    • Your deadline

What to expect:

  • Database recommendations tailored to your field
  • Citation guidance (APA, MLA, Chicago, etc.)
  • Help with complex searches (e.g., finding gray literature like government reports)

Real-World Example: *A graduate student in environmental science, Carlos, was struggling to find regional climate data for New Jersey. During his librarian consultation, he learned about:

  • NOAA’s regional datasets
  • Montclair’s subscription to Climate Data Store
  • How to request interlibrary loans for hard-to-find reports This single session cut his research time in half and gave him data he couldn’t find elsewhere.

Strategy 4: Master the Interlibrary Loan (ILL) System

Why it matters: The library doesn’t have every book or article—but ILL lets you borrow from other institutions for free.

How to use ILL:

  1. Search the library catalog—if a book isn’t available, click "Request via Interlibrary Loan."
  2. Submit your request (takes 3–10 business days).
  3. Check your email for updates (some articles arrive in 24 hours).
  4. Return items on time to avoid fees.

Pro Tips:

  • For articles, use PDF Drive or Google Scholar as a backup.
  • For books, check if a nearby NJ library (e.g., Rutgers, NJIT) has it via WorldCat.
  • Set reminders—ILL items auto-renew if no one else requests them.

Common Mistake: Assuming ILL only works for books. It also covers: ✅ Journal articles (even if Montclair doesn’t subscribe) ✅ Dissertations & theses ✅ Conference proceedings

Real-World Example: *A history major, Aisha, needed a rare 19th-century newspaper for her thesis. Since Montclair didn’t have it, she:

  1. Requested it via ILL
  2. **Got it from the New Jersey Historical Society in 5 days
  3. Digitized it and used it as primary source evidence This saved her from a dead-end research path.

  1. Go to Montclair LibGuides.

  3. Use the "Quick Start" guides for beginner-friendly research.

Top LibGuides to Bookmark:

  • Open Access Resources (free alternatives to paywalled journals)
  • Data & Statistics (where to find clean, reliable datasets)
  • Thesis & Dissertation Help

Real-World Example: *A biology student, Ethan, was researching CRISPR gene editing. Instead of randomly Googling, he used the Montclair LibGuide on "Genetics Research" to find:

  • The best databases (PubMed, ScienceDirect)
  • Key journals (Nature Genetics, Cell)
  • Ethics considerations (a critical part of his paper) This structured approach made his literature review 3x faster and more credible.
