Chapter 1: Understanding the Montclair State University Libraries System

Before you can maximize the libraries, you need to understand their structure and offerings. Montclair State University Libraries consist of three main branches, each with its own strengths:

1. The Library at the Center for Academic & Student Excellence (CASE)

  • Location: Lower Level, CASE Building
  • Specialties:
    • General academic research (humanities, social sciences, STEM)
    • Study lounges, group study rooms, and silent study zones
    • Tech support (printers, scanners, laptop rentals)
    • Workshops on research skills, citation management, and data analysis

2. The Library at the School of Business

  • Location: School of Business Building
  • Specialties:
    • Business, economics, and finance databases (IBISWorld, Statista, Bloomberg Terminal)
    • Case studies and industry reports
    • Career resources (resume reviews, LinkedIn tutorials)

3. The Library at the School of Arts & Communication

  • Location: School of Arts & Communication Building
  • Specialties:
    • Film, media, and performing arts archives
    • Creative writing and digital media resources
    • Exhibits and artist collaborations

4. Digital & Online Resources (Available 24/7)

  • Montclair State University Libraries’ Website: https://library.montclair.edu
  • Key Online Tools:
    • OneSearch (unified search for books, articles, and media)
    • EZProxy (remote access to paywalled journals)
    • LibGuides (subject-specific research guides)
    • LibCal (appointment scheduling for research help)

Pro Tip: Bookmark the LibGuides—they’re curated research roadmaps for every major at Montclair State. For example, the Psychology LibGuide lists the best databases for APA-style research, while the Engineering LibGuide highlights patent databases for STEM projects.

Chapter 2: 10 Actionable Strategies to Become a Library Pro

Now that you know where everything is, let’s get practical. These 10 strategies will turn you from a library novice into a research powerhouse.

Strategy 1: Master OneSearch – Your Gateway to All Library Resources

What it is: OneSearch is the unified search bar on the library website that pulls results from books, e-books, journal articles, videos, and more.

How to use it effectively:

  1. Start broad, then refine.
    • Example: Instead of searching "climate change," try "climate change solutions in New Jersey" for more relevant results.
  2. Use filters to narrow results.
    • Filter by date (2010–2024), source type (peer-reviewed journals), or format (e-book vs. print).
  3. Check the "Full Text" filter to ensure you’re getting immediately accessible articles.
  4. Save searches for later using your library account.

Real-World Example: A history student researching the 1960s civil rights movement could search:

  • "Freedom Riders New Jersey" → Filters to peer-reviewed articles → Finds a 2022 journal article from New Jersey History discussing local activism.

Common Mistake: Assuming OneSearch only finds books. It also indexes articles, newspapers, and multimedia—so always check the results type before clicking.

Strategy 2: Learn the Best Databases for Your Major

Not all databases are created equal. Some are subject-specific, while others are general. Here’s a quick breakdown:

Major Top 2 Databases to Use Why?
Business IBISWorld, Business Source Complete Industry reports & market analysis
Psychology PsycINFO, APA PsycArticles Peer-reviewed mental health research
Engineering IEEE Xplore, ScienceDirect Patent searches & technical papers
Education ERIC, JSTOR Research on teaching methods & policy
Film Studies Film & Television Literature Index, Kanopy Critiques & streaming documentaries

How to find the right database:

  • Go to the LibGuides for your major.
  • Use the "Databases by Subject" link on the library homepage.
  • Ask a librarian—they’re trained to recommend the best tools for your research.

Real-World Example: A nursing student writing a paper on diabetes management would use:

  • CINAHL (nursing-specific database) → Finds evidence-based practice guidelines.
  • PubMed → Discovers clinical trial results on insulin pumps.

Pro Tip: If a database isn’t working, clear your browser cache or try Incognito Mode—some institutions block certain sites.

Strategy 3: Access E-Books & Digital Resources Like a Pro

Montclair State Libraries offers thousands of e-books, but not all are equal. Here’s how to find and use them efficiently:

Where to find e-books:

  • OneSearch (filter by "e-book")
  • Ebrary, JSTOR, and ProQuest Ebook Central (direct links on the library website)

How to read & annotate:

  1. Download the OverDrive/Libby app for offline reading.
  2. Use Zotero or Mendeley to save and cite e-books.
  3. Check for DRM restrictions—some e-books allow limited printing/copying.

Real-World Example: A literature student analyzing Hemingway’s The Old Man and the Sea could:

  • Find the e-book in JSTOR → Highlight key passages → Export citations to Zotero.
  • Use Google Scholar to find critical essays on the novel.

Common Mistake: Assuming all e-books are free to print. Some have strict usage limits—always check the "Permissions" tab before printing.

Strategy 4: Use Interlibrary Loan (ILL) for Materials Not in Montclair’s Collection

What if the book or article you need isn’t available at Montclair? Interlibrary Loan (ILL) is your lifeline.

How it works:

  1. Search OneSearch—if the item says "Not Available," click "Request via Interlibrary Loan."
  2. Fill out the form (usually takes 3–7 business days).
  3. Receive a PDF (articles) or a physical book (via mail).

Pro Tips:

  • Start early—ILL requests can take weeks for physical books.
  • Check your ILL account regularly for updates.
  • Renew books before the due date to avoid fines.

Real-World Example: A political science student researching Vietnam War archives might find:

  • A rare document in the Library of Congress → Request via ILL → Receive a scanned copy in 5 days.

Common Mistake: Not checking your ILL account—some institutions cancel requests if they’re not monitored.

Montclair State Libraries offers free workshops on:

  • Citation management (Zotero, EndNote)
  • Data analysis (SPSS, R)
  • Academic writing & avoiding plagiarism
  • Finding primary sources

How to find them:

  • Check LibCal for upcoming events.
  • Sign up for email alerts from the library.

Real-World Example: A grad student struggling with APA citations could:

  • Attend a "Citation Workshop" → Learn how to auto-format references in Word.
  • Use Zotero’s browser extension to drag-and-drop citations into their paper.

Common Mistake: Assuming workshops are only for grad students—undergrads benefit too!

Strategy 6: Explore Special Collections for Unique Research Opportunities

Montclair State Libraries has rare books, archives, and historical collections that most students never explore. Some highlights:

  • New Jersey History Collection (manuscripts, photographs, oral histories)
  • African American Cultural Center (primary sources on civil rights)
  • Film & Media Archives (rare documentaries, screenplays)

How to access them:

  1. Visit the Special Collections desk (usually in the main library).
  2. Request materials in advance—some are fragile and require handling training.
  3. Ask for research assistance—librarians can help dig deeper into archives.

Real-World Example: A history student writing on Montclair’s role in the 1960s civil rights movement could:

  • Find unpublished letters from local activists in the New Jersey History Collection.
  • Interview a former librarian who worked in the 1970s.

Common Mistake: Assuming special collections are only for grad students—undergrads can (and should!) use them.

Strategy 7: Optimize Your Study Spaces

Montclair State Libraries offer multiple study environments, each with its own purpose. Here’s how to choose the right one:

Study Space Best For Rules
Silent Study Zones Focused reading, exams No talking, no groups
Group Study Rooms Collaborative projects Book in advance (via LibCal)

  • Reserve group rooms early—they fill up fast before exams.
  • Use the "Study Space Map" on the library website to find quiet areas.
  • Bring noise-canceling headphones for open study areas.

Real-World Example: A student preparing for a biology exam would:

  • Head to the Silent Study Zone in the CASE Library.
  • Use LibCal to reserve a study carrel for undisturbed focus.

Common Mistake: Assuming all study rooms are free—some require reservations.

Montclair State Libraries provides free access to:

  • Laptops & tablets (first-come, first-served)
  • Scanners & printers (with student accounts)
  • 3D printers & makerspace tools (for engineering/design students)

How to access them:

  1. Check the "Tech & Equipment" page on the library website.
  2. Bring your student ID—some services require account verification.
  3. Follow usage policies (e.g., printing limits).

Real-World Example: A film student editing a short documentary could:

  • Borrow a camera & lighting kit from the library.
  • Use the editing suite for post-production.

Common Mistake: Not checking equipment availability—some items must be reserved in advance.

Strategy 9: Leverage Librarian Research Help

Did you know? Montclair State librarians are trained researchers who can: ✔ Help you refine your thesis topic ✔ Teach you how to use advanced databases ✔ Review your citations for accuracy ✔ Guide you through primary source research

How to get help:

  • Chat with a librarian (live chat on the library website).
  • Schedule a research appointment (via LibCal).
  • Visit the Research Help Desk during library hours.

Real-World Example: A grad student struggling with quantitative data analysis could:

  • Book a 30-minute appointment with a data librarian.

Even the most experienced researchers make mistakes. Here are five pitfalls and how to steer clear:

Mistake 1: Ignoring the "Full Text" Filter in OneSearch

Problem: You find a great article, but it’s behind a paywall. Solution:

  • Click "Check for Full Text" (if available).
  • Use EZProxy (log in with your Montclair credentials).
  • Request via Interlibrary Loan if not available.

Mistake 2: Not Using Subject-Specific Databases

Problem: You search Google Scholar for everything, but miss key sources. Solution:
