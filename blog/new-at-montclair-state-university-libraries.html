New at Montclair State University Libraries: A Complete Guide to Maximizing Your Academic Success

This guide will walk you through everything you need to know to become a proactive library user, from finding hidden research databases to avoiding common pitfalls that waste time and effort.

Why Montclair State University Libraries Stand Out

Montclair State’s libraries offer more than just books—they provide: ✅ A vast digital archive (over 1.2 million e-books and 500+ research databases) ✅ Specialized collections (including rare manuscripts, government documents, and multimedia resources) ✅ Expert librarians ready to assist with APA, MLA, and Chicago citations ✅ Study spaces, including group rooms, silent zones, and tech-equipped workstations ✅ Interlibrary Loan (ILL) services to access materials from other institutions

Did you know? The Library’s Digital Commons hosts thousands of student research papers, making it a goldmine for inspiration and citation examples.

8 Proven Strategies to Master Montclair State University Libraries

1. Start with the Library’s Homepage & Discover Search

The Montclair State University Libraries homepage is your gateway to all resources. Instead of guessing where to look, use the unified search bar (located prominently on the homepage) to find books, articles, videos, and more in one place.

How to optimize your search:

  • Use Boolean operators (AND, OR, NOT) to refine results.
    • Example: "climate change AND policy NOT 2020"
  • Try advanced search filters (publication date, subject, format).
  • Save frequently used searches as alerts for new additions.

Real-world example: A political science major researching "voting rights legislation" might start broad but quickly narrow by adding "New Jersey" and filtering for peer-reviewed journals—saving hours of irrelevant browsing.

2. Leverage Subject Guides for Course-Specific Research

Each academic department has a customized research guide tailored to its needs. These guides include:

  • Recommended databases (e.g., JSTOR for humanities, ScienceDirect for STEM)
  • Key journals in the field
  • Citation tools and writing tips

Where to find them:

  • Visit Montclair State Libraries’ Subject Guides and select your major.

Pro tip: Bookmark your department’s guide—it’s your one-stop research hub.

3. Use Databases Strategically (Not Just Google Scholar)

While Google Scholar is useful, Montclair’s subscribed databases offer full-text access, peer-reviewed articles, and exclusive content.

Top databases by major:

Major Best Databases
Business Business Source Premier, ABI/INFORM
Nursing CINAHL, MEDLINE
Psychology PsycINFO, PsycARTICLES
Education ERIC, Education Research Complete
Engineering IEEE Xplore, ScienceDirect

Actionable tip: If a database doesn’t yield results, try related keywords (e.g., instead of "AI ethics," search "artificial intelligence moral dilemmas").

4. Access E-Books & Audiobooks Without Leaving Your Room

Montclair’s e-book collection (via Ebsco, ProQuest, and OverDrive) allows 24/7 access from anywhere.

How to find e-books:

  1. Use the library catalog and filter by "Available Online."
  2. For fiction/audiobooks, check Libby (OverDrive)—Montclair has a dedicated collection for students.

Common mistake: Assuming all e-books are free—some require Montclair login credentials.

5. Request Materials via Interlibrary Loan (ILL) for Free

Need a book not in Montclair’s collection? Interlibrary Loan (ILL) lets you borrow from other NJ libraries (including Rutgers and Princeton) for free.

How to request:

  1. Search the catalog—if unavailable, click "Request via ILL."
  2. Fill out the form (usually takes 3-7 business days).
  3. Pick up at Library Services Desk or have it emailed (for e-books).

Pro tip: Request multiple items at once to avoid last-minute scrambling.

6. Attend Library Workshops & Research Help Sessions

Montclair offers free workshops on:

  • Citation management (Zotero, EndNote)
  • Academic writing & avoiding plagiarism
  • Data analysis with SPSS or R

Where to find them:

  • Check Library Events Calendar or ask at the Research Help Desk.

Real-world example: A biology student struggling with SPSS for statistics attended a workshop and reduced their data analysis time by 50%.

7. Use the Library’s Tech & Printing Services

Montclair’s libraries provide:

  • Scanners & printers (with free printing for students—up to 500 pages/semester)
  • Audio recording booths (for presentations)
  • Tech support (for software like Matlab, Adobe Creative Suite)

How to print for free:

  1. Link your Montclair student account to the printer.
  2. Print from any library computer—no extra cost.

Warning: Some students forget to check their print balance, leading to unexpected fees.

8. Explore the Library’s Hidden Gems: Archives & Special Collections

Beyond textbooks, Montclair’s Special Collections hold:

  • Historical NJ documents (e.g., Civil Rights Movement records)
  • Rare books (first editions of The Great Gatsby)
  • Local history archives (Montclair’s role in WWII)

How to access:

  • Visit Special Collections or email specialcollections@montclair.edu.

Example use case: A history student researching "Montclair’s role in the 1960s civil rights movement" found primary sources in the archives that completely transformed their thesis.

Common Mistakes New Students Make (And How to Avoid Them)

❌ Mistake 1: Ignoring Librarians—They’re Free Experts!

Many students don’t ask for help until the last minute. Librarians can:

  • Help refine search strategies
  • Teach advanced database techniques
  • Review citations before submission

Solution: Visit the Research Help Desk or schedule a one-on-one appointment.

❌ Mistake 2: Relying Only on Wikipedia

While Wikipedia is useful for background info, it’s not a credible source for research papers.

Solution: Use Google Scholar or Montclair’s databases for peer-reviewed articles.

❌ Mistake 3: Not Saving Searches & Bookmarks

Students often forget their favorite databases or lose track of saved searches.

Solution:

  • Bookmark key guides (e.g., "Montclair Business Research Guide").
  • Set up Google Scholar alerts for new papers in your field.

❌ Mistake 4: Overlooking Open Access Resources

Some students don’t check for free, open-access articles before paying for PDFs.

Solution: Use Google Scholar’s "All Versions" filter to find free PDFs.

❌ Mistake 5: Waiting Until the Last Minute for ILL Requests

Interlibrary Loan (ILL) takes time—don’t wait until 3 days before a deadline.

Solution: Request materials at least 1 week in advance.

FAQ Section: Your Burning Questions Answered

❓ How do I access Montclair’s databases from off-campus?

Answer: All databases require a Montclair login (your student email and password). If prompted, select "Off-Campus Access" and enter your credentials.

❓ Can I renew books online?

Answer: Yes! Log in to Montclair’s Library Catalog and click "Renew" on your checked-out items. You can renew up to 3 times (unless the book is requested by another user).

❓ Are there quiet study spaces available?

Answer: Absolutely! The Library’s 2nd Floor has silent study zones, while the 1st Floor offers group study rooms (reservable via Room Scheduling).

❓ How do I cite sources using APA/MLA?

Answer: Montclair provides free citation tools:

  • Zotero (browser extension for saving & citing)
  • Purdue OWL (APA/MLA guides)
  • Librarian-led workshops (check the Workshops Calendar).

❓ What if I can’t find a book in the library?

Answer: Use Interlibrary Loan (ILL)—it’s free and fast! Request the book via the catalog, and it will arrive in 3-7 business days.
