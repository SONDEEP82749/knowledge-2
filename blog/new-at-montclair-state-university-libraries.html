Chapter 1: Understanding the Montclair State University Libraries System

Before diving into strategies, it’s essential to understand the structure and offerings of MSU’s libraries. The system consists of three main campuses, each with its own specialized collections:

1. The Library at the School of Business (SBL)

📍 Location: School of Business Building (SB) 🔹 Specialization: Business, economics, finance, and management research 🔹 Unique Features:

  • Bloomberg Terminals (for real-time financial data and market analysis)
  • Wall Street Journal & Financial Times subscriptions
  • Specialized business databases like IBISWorld and Mergent Online

2. The Library at the School of Education (SEL)

📍 Location: School of Education Building (SE) 🔹 Specialization: Education, psychology, counseling, and curriculum development 🔹 Unique Features:

  • ERIC (Education Resources Information Center) database
  • Counseling & therapy resources for research in mental health
  • Early childhood education materials for future teachers

3. The Main Library (ML) – The Heart of Research at MSU

📍 Location: University Center (UC) – Lower Level 🔹 Specialization: General academic research, STEM, humanities, and social sciences 🔹 Key Features:

  • Over 1 million physical books & 100,000+ e-books
  • 24/7 study spaces (including group rooms and silent zones)
  • Special collections (rare books, archives, and primary sources)
  • Interlibrary Loan (ILL) service for borrowing from other institutions
  • Research consultations with librarians specializing in your field

4. Digital & Online Resources

Since many students study remotely, MSU Libraries provide:

  • ProQuest, JSTOR, and EBSCOhost (for peer-reviewed articles)
  • Gale Databases (for historical and cultural research)
  • Statista (for market and consumer data)
  • O’Reilly for Higher Education (for tech and coding tutorials)

Fun Fact: In 2022 alone, MSU Libraries facilitated over 500,000 digital downloads of research materials, proving their vital role in modern academia.

Chapter 2: 10 Actionable Strategies to Master Montclair State University Libraries

Now that you know what’s available, let’s explore how to use it effectively. These strategies are backed by real student success stories and librarian recommendations.

Strategy 1: Start with the Library’s Homepage & Research Guides

Why it works: The MSU Libraries homepage is your first stop for finding resources, but many students skip the research guides—a huge missed opportunity.

How to do it:

  1. Bookmark the homepage: https://www.montclair.edu/library
  2. Explore the "Research Guides" (also called LibGuides)—these are curated collections of resources for specific subjects.
    • Example: If you’re studying environmental science, the Environmental Studies LibGuide links to Climate Change databases, EPA reports, and sustainability journals.
    • Example: For business students, the Finance LibGuide includes Bloomberg Terminal tutorials and stock market analysis tools.

Real-World Example: *A senior economics major, Jamal, was struggling to find real-time economic data for his capstone project. Instead of searching randomly, he used the Economics LibGuide, which directed him to FRED (Federal Reserve Economic Data) and World Bank Open Data. Within two hours, he compiled a dataset that saved him weeks of manual research.

Pro Tip: Bookmark your subject’s LibGuide and revisit it weekly—new databases and resources are added monthly.

Strategy 2: Use the Library Catalog (OneSearch) Like a Pro

Why it works: OneSearch is the unified search tool for books, articles, and media—but 90% of students don’t use advanced filters, leading to irrelevant results.

How to do it:

  1. Go to OneSearch and type your topic (e.g., "climate change policies in New Jersey").
  2. Apply filters to narrow results:
    • Date range (e.g., last 5 years for current research)
    • Resource type (books, journal articles, videos)
    • Subject terms (e.g., "environmental policy")
  3. Check "Peer-Reviewed" for academic articles (critical for grad students and research papers).
  4. Use "Citation Search" to find related works once you find a key article.

Common Mistake: Assuming all results are credible—always check the publisher (e.g., MIT Press vs. a random blog).

Real-World Example: *A PhD candidate in history, Dr. Elena, needed primary sources on 19th-century labor movements. Instead of just searching, she used OneSearch’s "Advanced Search" and filtered for "Archival Materials." She found digitized letters from the New Jersey Labor Archives, which became the core of her dissertation chapter.

Strategy 3: Leverage Librarian Research Consultations (Free & Valuable!)

Why it works: Many students don’t realize librarians offer free, one-on-one help—this is one of the best-kept secrets in academia.

How to do it:

  1. Schedule a consultation via:
    • Librarian Appointment System
    • Emailing your subject specialist (find yours here)
  2. Prepare in advance:
    • Bring a topic, paper prompt, or research question.
    • List keywords you’ve tried (e.g., "renewable energy subsidies").
  3. Ask for:
    • Database recommendations (e.g., "What’s the best database for my psychology experiment?")
    • Citation help (APA, MLA, Chicago)
    • Strategies for finding primary sources

Real-World Example: *A junior biology major, Mira, was stuck on finding peer-reviewed studies for her experiment on antibiotic resistance. She scheduled a 30-minute consultation with the Science Librarian, who taught her how to:

  • Use PubMed effectively
  • Find full-text articles via Google Scholar + MSU’s interlibrary loan
  • Avoid predatory journals (a common pitfall for undergrads)

Result: Mira completed her literature review in half the time and avoided plagiarism risks.

Strategy 4: Master Interlibrary Loan (ILL) for Books & Articles You Can’t Find

Why it works: MSU Libraries don’t have every book—but ILL lets you borrow from other institutions for free.

How to do it:

  1. Check OneSearch—if a book says "Not Available," try ILL.
  2. Submit a request via:
    • Interlibrary Loan Form
    • Email: ill@montclair.edu
  3. What to expect:
    • Books: Usually arrive in 5-10 business days.
    • Articles: Often arrive within 24-48 hours.
    • Loan periods: Typically 2-4 weeks (renewable if needed).

Real-World Example: *A graduate student in English Literature, Carlos, needed a rare 18th-century novel not held by MSU. He submitted an ILL request, and within 7 days, the book arrived from Princeton University. He cited it in his thesis and later thanked the librarian for saving his research.

Pro Tip: Set up ILL alerts so you never miss a delivery.

Strategy 7: Use Zotero or RefWorks for Citation Management

Why it works: Manually citing sources is tedious—and easy to mess up. Citation managers save time and reduce errors.

How to Set It Up:

  1. Download Zotero (free) or RefWorks (MSU provides access).
  2. Browse databases (JSTOR, Google Scholar) and drag citations into your library.
  3. Auto-generate citations in APA, MLA, Chicago, etc.
  4. Insert citations into Word/Google Docs with one click.

Real-World Example: *A history PhD student, Dr. Raj, was overwhelmed by citations for his book manuscript. He switched to Zotero, which:

  • Stored 500+ sources in one place
  • Auto-formatted citations in Chicago style
  • Synced across devices so he could work anywhere

Result: He submitted his chapter drafts on time without plagiarism worries.

Pro Tip: Use Zotero’s "Related Records" to find more sources on your topic.
