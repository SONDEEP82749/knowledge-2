Chapter 1: Getting Started – Your First Steps at Montclair State Libraries

1.1 Locating the Libraries & Understanding Their Layout

Montclair State University has three main library locations, each serving different academic needs:

  • John J. Cali School of Music Library – Specializes in music scores, audio recordings, and sheet music.
  • Library & Information Center (L&IC) – The hub for general research, housing books, journals, and multimedia resources.
  • Special Collections & University Archives – Houses rare books, historical documents, and university records.

Pro Tip: Use the Montclair State Libraries Map to plan your visits. The L&IC is the largest, with five floors dedicated to different subjects:

  • First Floor: General reference, study spaces, and the Information Commons.
  • Second Floor: Arts, Humanities, and Education books.
  • Third Floor: Social Sciences, Business, and Psychology resources.
  • Fourth Floor: STEM (Science, Technology, Engineering, Math) materials.
  • Fifth Floor: Government documents, microfilm, and archives.

1.2 Your Student ID = Your Library Pass

One of the first things you should do after enrolling is activating your student ID card. This grants you access to: ✅ Borrowing books (with loan periods ranging from 3 days to 1 year). ✅ Checking out laptops, tablets, and calculators (first-come, first-served). ✅ Printing, scanning, and photocopying (with a $100 annual printing allowance). ✅ Access to restricted collections (e.g., reference books, rare manuscripts).

⚠️ Common Mistake: Many students forget to renew books online before they expire. Solution: Set a calendar reminder for 3 days before your due date and use the My Account portal to extend loans.

Chapter 2: Finding Books & Journals – Beyond Google Search

2.1 Using the Library Catalog (Montclair OneSearch)

The Montclair OneSearch is your primary tool for discovering books, e-books, articles, and more. Here’s how to use it effectively:

Step-by-Step Search Strategy

  1. Start with a broad keyword (e.g., "climate change in Africa") and refine later.
  2. Use filters to narrow results:
    • Format: Books, e-books, journals, videos.
    • Date: Limit to last 5 years for current research.
    • Subject: Select specific categories (e.g., "Environmental Policy").
  3. Check the "Availability" tab to see if a book is in stock, checked out, or available online.

Real-World Example: A political science student researching "voting patterns in New Jersey" could:

  • Search "New Jersey elections 2020-2024"
  • Filter by "Books" and "Government Documents"
  • Find "The 2021 New Jersey Legislative Elections" (available as an e-book).

2.2 Accessing E-Books & Digital Resources

Montclair State offers thousands of e-books through platforms like:

  • Ebscohost (for academic journals)
  • JSTOR (humanities & social sciences)
  • ProQuest (business & education)
  • O’Reilly for Higher Education (tech & coding books)

Pro Tip: *Use Google Scholar (scholar.google.com) to find free PDFs of paywalled articles, then check if Montclair has access via Journal Finder.*

2.3 Requesting Books from Other Libraries (Interlibrary Loan)

If Montclair doesn’t have a book you need, Interlibrary Loan (ILL) is your lifeline. Here’s how it works:

  1. Search OneSearch and click "Request via Interlibrary Loan" if unavailable.
  2. Fill out the form (takes 3-7 business days).
  3. Receive a digital copy (PDF) or physical book via campus mail.

⚠️ Common Mistake: Students often abandon ILL requests if they don’t see immediate results. Solution: Check your email daily for updates and renew requests if needed.

3.2 Advanced Search Techniques

Most databases allow Boolean operators (AND, OR, NOT) to refine searches. Example:

  • "climate change AND policy" (finds articles with both terms)
  • "AI OR artificial intelligence" (broadens results)
  • "global warming NOT local" (excludes irrelevant terms)

Real-World Example: A business student analyzing "e-commerce trends in Latin America" could search:

  • "e-commerce" AND "Latin America" AND "2020-2024"
  • Then filter by "Peer-Reviewed Journals" for credibility.

3.3 Using Zotero for Citations & Research Management

Citing sources manually is error-prone and time-consuming. Zotero (a free tool) automatically saves, organizes, and formats citations in APA, MLA, Chicago, and more.

How to Set Up Zotero:

  1. Download Zotero (zotero.org).
  2. Install the browser extension (works with Chrome, Firefox, Edge).
  3. Drag-and-drop PDFs from databases into your Zotero library.
  4. Generate citations in seconds when writing papers.

⚠️ Common Mistake: Students forget to back up their Zotero library. Solution: Use Zotero’s cloud sync or export as a .zip file regularly.

Chapter 6: Avoiding Common Pitfalls & Maximizing Efficiency

6.1 Pitfall #1: Procrastinating Until the Last Minute

Problem: Many students wait until the night before to start research, leading to stress and incomplete work.

Solution: ✅ Break research into chunks (e.g., find 3 sources per day). ✅ **Use the Library’s "Research Guides" (Link) for pre-made topic overviews. ✅ Set Google Calendar reminders for deadlines and renewals.

6.2 Pitfall #2: Not Using Full-Text Access

Problem: Finding a great article but not being able to read it because it’s paywalled.

Solution:

  1. **Check Journal Finder to see if Montclair has access.
  2. **Use **Google Scholar’s "All Versions"** to find free PDFs.
  3. Request via Interlibrary Loan if unavailable.

6.3 Pitfall #3: Ignoring Study Spaces

Problem: Studying in noisy cafes or dorms leads to distraction and lower productivity.

Solution:

  • **Use the Silent Study Rooms for exams.

6.4 Pitfall #4: Not Leveraging Librarian Workshops

Problem: Missing out on free training that could save hours of research time.

Solution:

  • **Sign up for Library Workshops (e.g., "Advanced Google Search").
  • **Attend Research Bootcamps for first-year students.
  • **Watch Library Tutorials on YouTube.

6.5 Pitfall #5: Overlooking Digital Security

Problem: Downloading suspicious PDFs or using unsecured Wi-Fi can risk data breaches.

Solution:

  • **Only download files from trusted sources (Montclair databases).
  • Use a VPN when accessing off-campus resources.
  • Clear browser cache after using library databases.
