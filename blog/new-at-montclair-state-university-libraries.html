New at Montclair State University Libraries: A Complete Guide to Mastering Your Research & Study Tools

Introduction: Why Montclair State University Libraries Are Your Secret Weapon for Academic Success

Welcome to Montclair State University Libraries—your gateway to unlimited research resources, cutting-edge study tools, and expert support. Whether you're a first-year student, a graduate researcher, or a faculty member, the libraries offer a treasure trove of materials designed to elevate your academic journey.

According to the Montclair State University Libraries Annual Report (2023), over 50,000 students rely on library services annually, with 3.2 million physical and digital items available through their collections. Additionally, 92% of surveyed students reported that library resources significantly improved their research efficiency (Montclair State University Library Impact Study, 2022).

But here’s the catch: many students underutilize what the libraries offer. From hidden databases to advanced study spaces, there’s far more than meets the eye. This guide will walk you through everything you need to know—from navigating the Library Portal to mastering research strategies—so you can maximize your academic potential right from day one.

Chapter 1: Getting Started with Montclair State University Libraries

Before diving into research, it’s essential to understand the library’s structure, services, and tools. Let’s break it down systematically.

1.1 Understanding the Library’s Physical & Digital Spaces

Montclair State University Libraries consist of three main locations, each serving different academic needs:

  • The Library at the Center for Teaching and Learning (CTL) – The hub of student activity, featuring group study rooms, laptops, and 24/7 access during peak semesters.
  • The Library at the School of Business – Specializes in finance, economics, and business databases, ideal for MBA and undergraduate business students.
  • The Library at the School of Education – Houses education-specific journals, teacher resource collections, and early childhood education materials.

Beyond physical spaces, the Library Portal (accessible via montclair.edu/library) is your digital command center, offering:

  • OneSearch (a unified search engine for books, articles, and media)
  • Subject Guides (curated research resources by discipline)
  • Interlibrary Loan (ILL) services (borrowing materials from other institutions)

Pro Tip: Bookmark the Library Portal and set up Google Alerts for new library announcements—many students miss critical updates like database trials or extended hours.

1.2 Navigating the Library Portal: A Step-by-Step Guide

The Library Portal can feel overwhelming at first, but with the right approach, you’ll become a power user in no time. Here’s how to optimize your experience:

Step 1: Logging In & Accessing Your Account

  • Use your Montclair State username and password to log in.
  • Your library account tracks checked-out items, fines, and holds.
  • Reset your password via Montclair’s IT Support if needed.

Step 2: Using OneSearch Effectively

OneSearch is the most powerful tool for finding resources, but it requires strategic searching:

✅ Do:

  • Use Boolean operators (AND, OR, NOT) for precise results.
    • Example: "climate change" AND "policy" (narrows results)
  • Filter by date, format (books vs. articles), and peer-reviewed status.
  • Check "Full Text Online" to ensure immediate access.

❌ Don’t:

  • Rely solely on natural language queries (e.g., "Tell me about AI").
  • Ignore subject-specific databases (OneSearch is broad; some fields need specialized tools).

Real-World Example: A political science student searching for "voting rights legislation" might start with:

  • "voting rights" AND "legislation" AND "United States" (limits to U.S. focus)
  • Filters: Last 5 years, Peer-Reviewed Journals

1.3 Exploring Subject Guides: Your Research Roadmap

Each academic department has a dedicated Subject Guide, created by librarians who specialize in your field. These guides include:

  • Recommended databases (e.g., JSTOR for humanities, Business Source Premier for finance)
  • Citation styles (APA, MLA, Chicago)
  • Research tutorials (e.g., "How to Find Primary Sources in History")

How to Find Your Guide:

  1. Go to the Library Portal.
  2. Click "Subject Guides" in the top menu.
  3. Select your major or discipline.

Example: A psychology major should check the Psychology Research Guide, which highlights:*

  • PsycINFO (the gold standard for psychology articles)
  • APA citation templates
  • Ethics in research resources

Chapter 2: 10 Actionable Strategies to Master Montclair State University Libraries

Now that you’re familiar with the basics, let’s dive into practical strategies to maximize your library experience.

Strategy 1: Leverage the Library’s Database Collection (Beyond Google Scholar)

While Google Scholar is convenient, it lacks Montclair’s full access to paid journals. Instead, use specialized databases for deeper research.

Top Databases by Discipline:

Field Best Database Why It’s Useful
Business Business Source Premier Full-text business journals, company reports
Health Sciences CINAHL Nursing, medicine, allied health
Education ERIC Education research, teaching methods

Literature JSTOR Peer-reviewed articles, primary texts

Action Step:

  • Bookmark your top 3 databases for your field.
  • Set up RSS feeds in databases like JSTOR to get new article alerts.

Strategy 2: Use Interlibrary Loan (ILL) for Materials Not Available at Montclair

Not every book or article is in Montclair’s collection—but Interlibrary Loan (ILL) can get it for you in 3-5 business days.

How to Request via ILL:

  1. Find the item in OneSearch.
  2. Click "Request via Interlibrary Loan".
  3. Fill out the form with your Montclair email and preferred pickup location.

Pro Tip:

  • Check processing times—some articles arrive in 24 hours, while books take longer.
  • Renew loans before the due date to avoid fines.

Common Mistake: Students forget to check their Montclair email for ILL notifications. Set up email alerts in your ILL account.

Strategy 3: Attend Library Workshops & Research Help Sessions

Montclair’s Library Instruction Team offers free workshops on:

  • Citation management (Zotero, EndNote)
  • Advanced searching techniques
  • Evaluating sources for credibility

Where to Find Workshops:

  • Check the Library Calendar on the portal.
  • Request a custom workshop for your class via your librarian liaison.

Real-World Example: *A biology student struggling with peer-reviewed article searches attended a "Finding Scientific Literature" workshop, learning how to:

  • Use PubMed effectively.
  • Identify high-impact journals in their field.
  • Avoid predatory publishers.

Strategy 4: Utilize the Library’s Study Spaces & Tech Resources

The library isn’t just for books—it’s a tech-powered study hub. Here’s how to make the most of it:

Study Rooms & Group Work

  • Book a room via the Library Portal (some require a Montclair ID).
  • Silent vs. Collaborative Zones – The CTL Library has designated areas for different study needs.

Tech Equipment Available for Check-Out

  • Laptops, tablets, cameras, microphones
  • Audio-visual equipment (for presentations)
  • 3D printers & scanners (for engineering/design projects)

Action Step:

  • Check the "Equipment" tab in the Library Portal before visiting.
  • Reserve tech early—popular items (like cameras) get fully booked weeks in advance.

Primary sources (original documents, interviews, artifacts) deepen research but can be hard to locate. The library offers:

  • Digital Collections (e.g., Montclair’s Historical Newspapers)
  • Archival Databases (e.g., America’s Historical Newspapers)
  • Special Collections (rare books, manuscripts)

Example: A history student researching Prohibition could explore:

Strategy 7: Use the Library’s Open Access & Free Resources

Not all great research is behind paywalls. The library curates free, open-access materials, including:

  • DOAJ (Directory of Open Access Journals) – Peer-reviewed, no subscription needed.
  • Project MUSE – Humanities & social sciences.
  • Google Scholar (with Montclair access) – Set up library links to redirect to full texts.

How to Set Up Google Scholar with Montclair Access:

  1. Go to scholar.google.com.
  2. Click "Settings" > "Library Links".
  3. Search for "Montclair State University" and save.

Pro Tip:

  • **Use Unpaywall (a browser extension) to find legal PDFs of paywalled articles.

Every academic department has a dedicated librarian—don’t hesitate to ask for help!

How to Get Research Assistance:

Strategy 10: Stay Organized with Library Apps & Alerts

The library offers apps and notifications to keep you updated:

  • LibApps (Montclair State) – Check due dates, renew books, and track ILL requests.
  • RSS Feeds – Get new article alerts from databases like JSTOR.
  • Library Newsletter – Subscribe for workshop announcements, database trials, and updates.

How to Set Up Alerts:

  3. Choose frequency (weekly/daily).

Chapter 3: Common Mistakes & How to Avoid Them

Even the most diligent students make avoidable errors when using the library. Here’s how to steer clear of pitfalls.

Mistake 1: Ignoring the Library’s Subject Guides

Why It’s Bad:

  • You miss curated resources tailored to your field.
  • You waste time searching broad databases instead of specialized ones.

Solution:

  • Bookmark your Subject Guide and check it weekly.
  • Follow your librarian’s recommendations—they know what’s most relevant.

Mistake 2: Not Using Boolean Operators in Searches

Why It’s Bad:

  • Your searches return too many irrelevant results.
  • You miss key studies because your query was too vague.

Solution:

  • Learn Boolean basics:
    • AND = Both terms must appear ("AI" AND "ethics").
    • OR = Either term works ("climate change" OR "global warming").
    • NOT = Exclude terms ("AI NOT robotics").

Example: A student searching for "social media effects on teens" should use: "social media" AND ("mental health" OR "behavior")

Mistake 3: Failing to Check for Full-Text Availability

Why It’s Bad:

  • You click on an article, only to find it’s paywalled.
  • You waste time trying to access something Montclair doesn’t have.

Solution:

  • Always check the "Full Text Online" filter in OneSearch.
  • If an article isn’t available, use Interlibrary Loan (ILL).
