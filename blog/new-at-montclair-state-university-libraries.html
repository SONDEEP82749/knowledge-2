Chapter 1: Getting Started – Understanding Montclair State University Libraries

1.1 The Libraries You Need to Know

Montclair State University operates three main libraries, each serving a unique purpose:

  1. University Library (Main Campus – 1900 University Ave.)

    • The largest and most comprehensive library on campus, housing books, journals, DVDs, and multimedia resources.
    • Features study spaces, group rooms, and a rooftop terrace for quiet work.
    • Home to special collections, including rare books and historical archives.

  2. John J. Cali School of Music Library (Music Building – 1 Normal Ave.)

    • A specialized library for music students, with sheet music, audio recordings, and music theory resources.
    • Offers private practice rooms and digital music archives.

  3. Special Collections & University Archives (University Library, 2nd Floor)

    • Houses rare books, manuscripts, and historical documents related to New Jersey and Montclair State’s history.
    • Accessible by appointment only for researchers.

💡 Pro Tip: If you’re unsure which library has the resource you need, use the Montclair State Library Catalog to search across all locations.

Chapter 2: 10 Actionable Strategies to Master Montclair State University Libraries

2.1 Strategy 1: Use the Library Catalog Like a Pro

The Montclair State Library Catalog is your first stop for finding books, e-books, and media. Here’s how to use it effectively:

✔ Search by Keyword, Title, or Author – Instead of vague terms like "psychology," try "cognitive behavioral therapy" for more precise results. ✔ Use Filters – Narrow results by publication date, format (e-book vs. physical), or subject. ✔ Check Availability – If a book is checked out, use "Request Hold" to have it reserved for you. ✔ Explore Subject Headings – Clicking on a book’s subject tags (e.g., "Social Work – Case Studies") reveals related resources.

🚫 Common Mistake: Searching only by title without checking if the book is available. Always verify before visiting.

2.2 Strategy 2: Leverage Databases for Academic Research

Montclair State provides premium access to 100+ databases, including:

  • JSTOR (Humanities & Social Sciences)
  • ProQuest (Business, Health, and Science)
  • EBSCOhost (Multidisciplinary research)
  • ScienceDirect (STEM & Medical Research)

How to Search Effectively:

  1. Start with a focused question (e.g., "What are the effects of remote work on employee productivity?").
  2. Use Boolean operators (AND, OR, NOT) to refine searches.
    • Example: "remote work AND productivity NOT telecommuting"
  3. Check peer-reviewed filters to ensure credible sources.
  4. Save and export citations (APA, MLA, Chicago formats) using Zotero or RefWorks.

💡 Pro Tip: *If a full-text article isn’t available, use Interlibrary Loan (ILL)—it’s free and usually delivered within 24-72 hours.*

2.3 Strategy 3: Master Interlibrary Loan (ILL) for Missing Resources

Not every book or journal is in Montclair’s collection. Interlibrary Loan (ILL) lets you borrow from other universities—here’s how:

  1. Check if Montclair has the item first (via the catalog).
  2. Submit a request via ILL Portal.
  3. Monitor your account—you’ll get an email when it arrives.
  4. Return on time to avoid fines (most loans have 2-4 week limits).

⚠️ Warning: Some ILL requests take 1-2 weeks, so plan ahead!

🔍 Real-World Example: *A grad student in English Literature needed a 19th-century novel not held by Montclair. She used ILL to borrow it from Rutgers, allowing her to complete her analysis without delays.

2.9 Strategy 9: Organize Your Research with Zotero or RefWorks

Managing hundreds of citations manually is a nightmare. Use:

  • Zotero (Free, browser-based)
  • RefWorks (Paid, but integrated with Montclair’s databases)

How to Set It Up:

  1. Download Zotero (https://www.zotero.org).
  2. Create a free account and sync across devices.
  3. Drag-and-drop PDFs from databases into your library.
  4. Generate citations in seconds.

🔍 Real-World Example: *A history student used Zotero to organize 50+ sources for her thesis. She automatically formatted citations in Chicago Style, saving her 10+ hours of manual work.

Chapter 3: Common Library Mistakes & How to Avoid Them

Even experienced students make avoidable mistakes in the library. Here’s how to steer clear of them:

3.1 Mistake 1: Not Using Subject Headings Properly

❌ Wrong: Searching "climate change" and getting irrelevant results. ✅ Right: Using subject headings like "Global Warming – Policy" for focused research.

🔍 Fix: After finding a relevant book, check its subject tags and explore related terms.

3.2 Mistake 2: Ignoring Interlibrary Loan (ILL) Deadlines

❌ Wrong: Waiting until the night before a paper is due to request a book. ✅ Right: Request ILL materials at least 1 week in advance.

🔍 Fix: Set a reminder in your calendar for ILL requests.

3.3 Mistake 3: Not Asking for Help When Stuck

❌ Wrong: Struggling alone with complex database searches. ✅ Right: Chat with a librarian or schedule a research appointment.

🔍 Fix: Remember—librarians are paid to help you succeed!

3.4 Mistake 4: Overlooking Digital & Open Access Resources

❌ Wrong: Only searching physical books in the library. ✅ Right: Exploring e-books, journals, and open-access databases.

🔍 Fix: Check the Library Databases page for hidden gems.

3.5 Mistake 5: Not Organizing Citations Properly

❌ Wrong: Keeping random notes without citations. ✅ Right: Using Zotero/RefWorks to auto-format citations.

🔍 Fix: Start organizing early—don’t wait until the last minute!
