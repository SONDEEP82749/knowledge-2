Chapter 1: Getting Acquainted with Montclair State University Libraries

Before diving into strategies, it’s crucial to understand the structure, resources, and unique offerings of Montclair State’s libraries.

1.1 The Library System: A Brief Overview

Montclair State University operates three main library locations, each with distinct strengths:

  1. The Library at the Center for Academic Success (CAS)

    • Location: 1 Normal Avenue, Montclair, NJ
    • Focus: General academic research, study spaces, and student services
    • Key Features:
      • 24/7 study lounges (including silent study zones)
      • Group study rooms (bookable via the Montclair Libraries website)
      • Printing, scanning, and 3D printing services
      • Accessibility services (braille materials, screen readers, and adaptive technology)

  2. The Library at the School of Business

    • Location: 1 Normal Avenue (Business Building)
    • Focus: Business, economics, and entrepreneurship research
    • Key Features:
      • Specialized databases (Bloomberg Terminal, IBISWorld, Mergent Online)
      • Case study collections for business strategy projects
      • Career resources (resume workshops, LinkedIn training)

  3. The Library at the School of Education

    • Location: 1 Normal Avenue (Education Building)
    • Focus: Education research, curriculum development, and teacher resources
    • Key Features:
      • ERIC (Education Resources Information Center) database
      • Early childhood and special education collections
      • Professional development workshops for pre-service teachers

Pro Tip: Each library has its own "specialty," so if you're researching business ethics, head to the Business Library—if you're working on a psychology experiment, the CAS Library is your best bet.

1.2 Key Services You Should Know About

A. Research Assistance & Librarians

Montclair State’s librarians are not just bookkeepers—they’re research partners. Many hold master’s or doctoral degrees in their respective fields and can help you:

  • Refine your thesis topic
  • Find peer-reviewed articles (even in niche subjects)
  • Teach you how to use databases like JSTOR or ProQuest
  • Cite sources correctly (APA, MLA, Chicago, etc.)

🔹 How to Access Help:

  • Chat with a Librarian: Available 24/7 via the Ask a Librarian page.
  • Schedule a Research Appointment: Book a one-on-one session with a subject specialist.
  • Attend Workshops: The library offers research bootcamps, citation workshops, and data analysis sessions.

B. Interlibrary Loan (ILL) & Document Delivery

Stuck on a book or article that Montclair doesn’t own? No problem. The Interlibrary Loan (ILL) service lets you:

  • Borrow books from other NJ libraries (usually within 5–7 business days)
  • Request PDFs of journal articles (often delivered in 24–48 hours)
  • Access digitized rare materials (like historical newspapers)

🔹 Real-World Example: A graduate student in the History Department needed a rare 19th-century diary for her dissertation. Since Montclair didn’t have it, she submitted an ILL request through the Montclair Libraries ILL portal. Within a week, she received a high-quality scan of the primary source—saving her months of research time.

C. Digital & Open Access Resources

Montclair State is committed to open education, offering:

  • Open Access Journals: Free, peer-reviewed articles (via DOAB and Directory of Open Access Books).
  • Montclair Scholar: The university’s institutional repository, hosting theses, dissertations, and faculty research for public access.
  • Creative Commons Licensed Materials: Free-to-use images, videos, and texts (via Flickr Commons and Internet Archive).

🔹 Why This Matters: If you’re working on a budget-conscious project or need ethical, legally free sources, open access materials are a game-changer.

D. Technology & Maker Spaces

Beyond books, the libraries offer:

  • 3D Printing & Scanning: Design and print prototypes for engineering, art, or business projects.
  • Audio/Video Editing Stations: Use Adobe Creative Cloud, Final Cut Pro, or Audacity for multimedia projects.
  • Virtual Reality (VR) Labs: Explore 360-degree historical reconstructions or virtual field trips for anthropology and geography research.
  • Programming & Robotics Kits: Build and test Arduino, Raspberry Pi, or LEGO Mindstorms projects.

Problem: Many students begin research with Google Scholar or Google Search, which leads to irrelevant, low-quality, or paywalled sources.

Solution: Montclair State provides subject-specific databases that are curated, peer-reviewed, and full-text.

🔹 How to Choose the Right Database:

🔹 Pro Tip: If you’re unsure which database to use, ask a librarian or check the Montclair Libraries Database A-Z List.

Strategy 2: Use Advanced Search Techniques

Basic keyword searches often return thousands of irrelevant results. Instead, master these advanced techniques:

A. Boolean Operators (AND, OR, NOT)

  • AND → Narrows results (e.g., "climate change AND policy").
  • OR → Broadens results (e.g., "global warming OR climate crisis").
  • NOT → Excludes terms (e.g., "AI NOT ethics" to avoid ethical discussions).

B. Truncation & Wildcards

  • Use asterisks (*) to find variations:
    • "womn"* → Finds "woman," "women," "womyn"
    • "child"* → Finds "child," "children," "childhood"

C. Field Searching

  • Title: "The Impact of Social Media on Mental Health" title
  • Author: author: Freud
  • Subject: subject: "cognitive behavioral therapy"

🔹 Real-World Example: *A psychology major was researching "anxiety disorders in adolescents." Instead of a vague search, she used: ("anxiety disorder" OR "anxiety disorders") AND ("adolescent*" OR "teenager*") AND ("cognitive behavioral therapy" OR "CBT") This returned highly relevant, peer-reviewed studies in minutes, not hours.

Strategy 4: Use Zotero or RefWorks for Citation Management

Manually citing sources is time-consuming and error-prone. Zotero (free) and RefWorks (paid, but available via Montclair) help you: ✅ Automatically save citations from databases. ✅ Generate citations in any format (APA, MLA, Chicago). ✅ Create bibliographies with a click. ✅ Collaborate with group projects.

🔹 How to Set Up Zotero:

  1. Download Zotero.
  2. Install the Zotero Connector browser extension.
  3. Drag-and-drop PDFs into your library.
  4. Use the "Create Bibliography" feature to auto-generate references.

🔹 Common Mistake: Not backing up your Zotero library (use Zotero Sync or Google Drive).
