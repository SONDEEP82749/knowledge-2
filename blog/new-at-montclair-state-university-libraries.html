8 Actionable Strategies to Master Montclair State University Libraries

1. Start with the Library’s Search Portal: Beyond Google

Google is great, but Montclair State’s library search portal (accessible via the Library Homepage) is tailored to academic rigor. Here’s how to use it effectively:

  • Use Advanced Search Filters: Instead of a broad keyword search, refine your query by:
    • Date range (e.g., "2010–Present" for recent research)
    • Resource type (books, journal articles, dissertations, videos)
    • Subject terms (e.g., "climate change AND policy")
  • Boolean Operators Matter: Combine terms with AND, OR, NOT for precision.
    • Example: "artificial intelligence" AND "healthcare" NOT "review" (finds only original studies, not literature reviews).
  • Check the "Get It" Button: If an article isn’t immediately available, use interlibrary loan (ILL)—it’s free and fast (usually within 3–5 days).

Real-World Example: A biology major researching gene editing might start with a broad search for "CRISPR" but quickly narrow it down using:

  • "CRISPR" AND "cancer treatment" AND "clinical trials"
  • Filtering by "2020–2024" to focus on the latest studies.
  • Using "Get It" to access a paywalled journal article via ILL.

2. Leverage the "Research Guides" for Subject-Specific Help

Montclair State’s libraries don’t just hold books—they curate expert-created research guides for every major. These guides include:

  • Recommended databases (e.g., JSTOR for humanities, PubMed for health sciences)
  • Key journals in your field
  • Citation style guides (APA, MLA, Chicago)
  • Workshops and tutorials

How to Find Them:

  1. Go to the Library Research Guides.
  2. Select your major or discipline.
  3. Follow the step-by-step research process tailored to your needs.

Common Mistake: Ignoring these guides because they seem "too basic." In reality, they save hours of trial and error.

3. Explore Digital Collections & Primary Sources

Montclair State’s Digital Collections (accessible here) are goldmines for projects requiring original sources. Some standout collections include:

  • Newark Evening News Archives (for history and journalism students)
  • Montclair State University Historical Photographs (for alumni and local studies)

Actionable Tip:

  • For history papers, search for primary sources (e.g., letters, diaries, government documents).
  • For creative writing, use literary archives to study famous authors’ drafts.
  • For business students, explore historical market reports for case studies.

Real-World Example: A history student writing about Prohibition in New Jersey could:

  1. Search the Newark Evening News Archives for 1920s–1930s articles.
  2. Cross-reference with Montclair State’s Special Collections for local ordinances.
  3. Use Google Scholar to find academic analyses of the topic.

5. Attend Library Workshops & Events (They’re Free!)

Montclair State Libraries host weekly workshops on:

  • Citation management (Zotero, EndNote, Mendeley)
  • Data visualization (Excel, Tableau, R)
  • Open Access publishing
  • Thesis & dissertation support

How to Find Workshops:

  1. Check the Library Events Calendar.
  2. Sign up via Montclair Connect (the university’s event platform).
  3. Attend at least one per semester—they’re game-changers for research efficiency.

Real-World Example: A graduate student struggling with data analysis could:

  1. Attend a "Introduction to R for Social Sciences" workshop.
  2. Practice with library-provided datasets.
  3. Get one-on-one help from a data librarian.

6. Use the "Ask a Librarian" Service (Yes, They’re Real!)

Montclair State’s librarians are not just bookkeepers—they’re research partners. You can:

  • Chat live via the Ask a Librarian page.
  • Email with detailed questions (they respond within 24 hours).
  • Schedule a research consultation (great for theses, capstones, or complex projects).

How to Get the Most Out of This:

  • Be specific in your questions (e.g., "I need 5 peer-reviewed articles on renewable energy in urban planning—can you help me refine my search?").
  • Provide context (your assignment, deadline, and what you’ve already found).

Common Mistake: Assuming librarians are only for last-minute help. They can save you weeks of work by guiding you early in your research.

  • Attend "Tech Tuesdays" (monthly workshops on software like Adobe Creative Suite, SPSS, or MATLAB).

Real-World Example: A nursing student preparing for clinical simulations could:

  1. Book a collaboration space with a whiteboard.

  3. Print 3D models of anatomical structures for study.

8. Save Time with Citation Management Tools

Citing sources manually is error-prone and tedious. Montclair State recommends:

  • Zotero (free, user-friendly, great for beginners)
  • EndNote (powerful for large bibliographies)
  • Mendeley (good for PDF organization)

How to Set It Up:

  1. Download the software from the Library’s Citation Tools Guide.
  2. Create an account and import references from databases.
  3. Auto-generate citations in any style (APA, MLA, etc.).
  4. Sync across devices (Zotero works on mobile too).

Common Mistake: Waiting until the last minute to organize citations. Start early—it saves panic before deadlines.

Common Mistakes New Students Make (And How to Avoid Them)

Mistake #1: Not Using the Library’s Full Potential

What Happens: Students treat the library as just a place to study or borrow books, missing out on databases, workshops, and expert help.

How to Fix It: ✅ Explore all sections (Special Collections, Digital Archives, Tech Labs). ✅ Attend at least one workshop per semester. ✅ Ask librarians for help—they’re there to guide you, not just lend books.

Mistake #2: Relying Only on Google Scholar

What Happens: Google Scholar lacks Montclair’s subscription databases, meaning you might miss full-text articles or paywall traps.

How to Fix It: ✅ Use Montclair’s search portal first, then supplement with Google Scholar. ✅ Check the "Get It" button—it connects you to free full-text versions via the library.

Mistake #3: Ignoring Interlibrary Loan (ILL)

What Happens: Students give up when they can’t find an article instead of using ILL, which is free and fast.

How to Fix It: ✅ Submit ILL requests early (some take 3–5 days). ✅ Set up an ILL account via the Library Website.

Mistake #4: Not Taking Advantage of Study Spaces

What Happens: Students crash on their beds or study in noisy cafes, leading to distraction and burnout.

How to Fix It: ✅ Book a study room via the Library Study Space Finder. ✅ Use silent floors for exams or deep work.

Mistake #5: Procrastinating on Citation Management

What Happens: Students wait until the last week to organize citations, leading to plagiarism risks and formatting errors.

How to Fix It: ✅ Start citing early—use Zotero or EndNote from Day 1 of research. ✅ Set up auto-formatting to avoid last-minute headaches.

❓ Can I request books that aren’t in the library’s collection?

Answer: Yes! Use Interlibrary Loan (ILL) to borrow books from other universities and libraries. It’s free and usually takes 3–5 days. Submit requests via the ILL Form.

❓ Are there any special collections I should check out for my research?

Answer: Absolutely! Montclair State’s Special Collections include:

  • Newark Evening News Archives (for history and journalism)
  • Montclair State University Historical Photographs (for alumni and local studies)
  • Rare Books & Manuscripts (for literary and cultural research) Check them out here.

❓ How do I find peer-reviewed articles for my research paper?

Answer: Use Montclair’s search portal and filter by "Peer-Reviewed Journals". Alternatively:

  1. Go to JSTOR or ProQuest.
  2. Use advanced search filters to select "Scholarly (Peer-Reviewed) Journals".
