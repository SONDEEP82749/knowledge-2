Chapter 1: Getting Started – Your First Steps at Montclair State Libraries

1.1 How to Access the Library Portal (Calculator-Library-Portal Connection)

Montclair State’s Library Portal is your one-stop shop for all things research. Here’s how to log in and start exploring:

  • Go to Montclair State University Libraries and click "Library Portal."
  • Log in using your Montclair State username and password (same as for Blackboard, email, and Calculator).
  • Bookmark the portal—it’s your digital command center for:
    • E-books & journals (no need to visit the library in person!)
    • Research databases (JSTOR, ProQuest, ScienceDirect, etc.)
    • Interlibrary Loan (ILL) for books not available at Montclair
    • Library accounts (checkouts, renewals, fines)

Pro Tip: If you’re using Calculator (Montclair’s student portal), you can also access the Library Portal via the "Resources" tab.

Chapter 2: 10 Actionable Strategies to Master Montclair State Libraries

2.1 Strategy 1: Use the Advanced Search (Beyond Basic Keywords)

Most students only search by title or author, missing relevant articles and books. Instead, try:

  • Boolean operators (AND, OR, NOT) to refine searches.
    • Example: "climate change" AND "policy" (finds only results with both terms).
    • Example: "machine learning" OR "AI" (broadens results).
  • Truncation symbols (*) for variations.
    • Example: wom*n finds "woman," "women," "womyn."
  • Field-specific searches (e.g., search only in the abstract or subject headings).

Common Mistake: Using too many keywords (e.g., "artificial intelligence blockchain cryptocurrency") leads to zero results. Instead, start broad, then narrow down.

2.2 Strategy 2: Leverage the Library’s Research Guides

Montclair’s Research Guides are curated collections of the best resources for specific subjects. You’ll find them at: 🔗 https://www.montclair.edu/library/research-guides

Example Guides:

  • "Business Research" (includes IBISWorld, Statista, and Mergent Online)
  • "Psychology Research" (covers PsycINFO, PubMed, and APA Style)
  • "Open Access Resources" (free legal scholarship, preprints, and more)

Real-World Example: *A graduate student in environmental science could save hours by using the "Environmental Studies Research Guide" instead of randomly searching Google Scholar. The guide links directly to Montclair’s subscriptions, including ScienceDirect and GreenFILE, which are paywalled elsewhere.

2.3 Strategy 3: Master Interlibrary Loan (ILL) for Books Not at Montclair

Not every book you need is in Montclair’s collection. Interlibrary Loan (ILL) lets you borrow from other universities for free.

How to Use ILL:

  1. Search the Library Portal—if a book isn’t available, click "Request via Interlibrary Loan."
  2. Fill out the form (usually takes 3–7 business days).
  3. Check your email for updates—you’ll get a notification when it arrives.
  4. Pick it up at the Circulation Desk (or request digital scanning if you only need a chapter).

Pro Tip: If you need a book fast, check:

  • "Montclair State + NJ Shared Catalog" (books from Rutgers, NJIT, and others).
  • "WorldCat" (a global library catalog—some items can be requested via ILL).

Common Mistake: Assuming ILL is slow—while it takes time, many requests arrive in 24–48 hours, especially for e-books or digital scans.

2.4 Strategy 4: Use Zotero for Free Citation Management

Zotero (a free, open-source tool) helps you: ✅ Save citations from any database or website ✅ Organize references by folders and tags ✅ Generate citations in APA, MLA, Chicago, and more ✅ Insert citations into Word/Google Docs with one click

How to Set Up Zotero:

  1. Download from https://www.zotero.org/
  2. Sync with Montclair’s Zotero Group (ask librarians for access).
  3. Install the browser extension to auto-save citations.

Real-World Example: A senior thesis writer could spend 10 hours manually formatting citations without Zotero. With it, they save 5+ hours and reduce errors in their bibliography.

2.5 Strategy 5: Attend Library Workshops (Free & Highly Recommended)

Montclair offers free workshops on:

  • Citation & Plagiarism Avoidance
  • Data Management & Analysis
  • Open Access Publishing
  • Literature Reviews & Annotated Bibliographies

How to Find Workshops:

  • Check the Library Calendar at https://www.montclair.edu/library/events.

Common Mistake: Skipping workshops because they seem "too basic." Even PhD students benefit from advanced data analysis sessions.

2.6 Strategy 6: Use the Library’s Tech & Study Spaces

Montclair’s libraries offer more than just books—they have: 🖥 Tech Labs (with scanners, 3D printers, and audio recording equipment) 📊 Group Study Rooms (reservable via Calculator or the Library Portal) 🎧 Silent & Collaborative Study Zones 🔌 Charging Stations & Power Outlets

Real-World Example: *A music student recording a composition could use the Cali Music Library’s soundproof booths instead of renting an expensive studio. Similarly, STEM students can access lab equipment in the Science & Math Library.

2.7 Strategy 7: Ask a Librarian (Yes, They’re Free!)

Montclair’s librarians are experts—don’t hesitate to ask for help!

How to Get Help:

  • Chat with a Librarian (available 24/7 via the Library Portal).
  • Email (libref@montclair.edu).
  • In-Person Help (ask at the Circulation Desk or Research Help Desk).
  • Schedule a Research Appointment (great for thesis writers).

Common Mistake: Assuming librarians are "too busy"—they love helping students and can save you weeks of wasted research.

2.8 Strategy 8: Explore Open Access & Free Resources

Not everything you need is behind a paywall. Montclair provides free access to:

  • Google Scholar (with Montclair’s full-text links)
  • JSTOR Open Access (thousands of free articles)
  • Directory of Open Access Journals (DOAJ)
  • Project MUSE (Free Access Program)

How to Find Open Access:

  1. Search the Library Portal and filter by "Open Access."
  2. Use Google Scholar (set Montclair as your library in Settings > Library Links).
  3. Check the "Open Access Research Guide" for more options.

Real-World Example: *A student researching global health could find free, peer-reviewed articles on COVID-19 policies in JSTOR’s Open Access collection, saving them $30+ per article.

2.9 Strategy 9: Use the Library’s Data & Statistics Tools

Montclair provides premium data tools for free:

  • Statista (market research, consumer trends)
  • IBISWorld (industry reports)
  • SAGE Research Methods (for social science research)
  • RefWorks (alternative to Zotero for citation management)

How to Access:

  • Find them in the Library Portal under "Databases."
  • Watch tutorial videos (available on the Research Guides).

Common Mistake: Not exploring data tools—many students Google "free statistics" and end up with outdated or unreliable data.

2.10 Strategy 10: Take Advantage of Summer & Break Access

Montclair’s Library Portal is available 24/7, even during summer break and holidays. You can:

  • Access e-books & journals from anywhere.
  • Request ILL items for summer research.
  • Use Zotero & RefWorks to organize citations for fall semester papers.

Pro Tip: Bookmark the Library Portal and set up email alerts for new book arrivals in your field.

Chapter 3: Common Mistakes & How to Avoid Them

3.1 Mistake #1: Not Checking Your Library Account for Fines

  • Problem: Many students don’t realize they have overdue books until they try to check out more.
  • Solution:
    • Log in to your account (via the Library Portal).
    • Set up email notifications for due dates.
    • Renew books early (most can be renewed 3 times).

3.2 Mistake #2: Ignoring the "Full-Text" Button

  • Problem: Finding a great article but not being able to read it because it’s paywalled.
  • Solution:
    • Click "Full-Text"—if Montclair has access, it will link you directly.
    • If not, request via ILL or check Google Scholar (sometimes has free PDFs).

3.3 Mistake #3: Using Only Google Scholar (Without Montclair Links)

  • Problem: Google Scholar doesn’t always show Montclair’s subscriptions, leading to paywall traps.
  • Solution:
    • Set Montclair as your default library in Google Scholar Settings.
    • Use the Library Portal’s "Find It" button to check for full-text access.

3.4 Mistake #4: Not Using Subject Headings for Better Searches

  • Problem: Searching "climate change" gives millions of results—many irrelevant.
  • Solution:
    • Look at a book’s subject headings (in the Library Catalog) and use those terms.
    • Example: Instead of "global warming," try "climate change mitigation" or "greenhouse gas emissions."

3.5 Mistake #5: Forgetting About Digital Resources

  • Problem: Only visiting the library in person means missing e-books, online journals, and databases.
  • Solution:
    • Use the Library Portal for 24/7 access.
    • Download the LibApps app (for mobile access to catalogs and ILL requests).

Here are five of the most common questions new students have, answered in detail.

❓ FAQ 1: How do I renew books before they’re due?

Answer: You can renew books online via your Library Account:

  1. Go to the Library Portal → "My Account."
  2. Log in with your Montclair credentials.
  3. Find the book you want to renew and click "Renew."
  4. Most books can be renewed up to 3 times (unless someone else requests them).
