If you’re new to Montclair State, you might be wondering:

  • How do I navigate the library’s vast digital collections?
  • What tools can help me find high-quality research faster?
  • How can I avoid common pitfalls that waste time and effort?

This guide will walk you through everything you need to know—from library orientation tips to advanced research strategies—so you can save hours of frustration and boost your academic performance from day one.

8 Actionable Strategies to Succeed at Montclair State University Libraries

Now that you know what the libraries offer, let’s dive into how to use them effectively. These strategies will help you save time, reduce stress, and produce higher-quality work.

Strategy 1: Master the Library Portal (Your First Step to Success)

Problem: Many students waste time searching for resources because they don’t know how to use the Library Portal efficiently.

Solution: The Montclair State University Libraries Portal (link) is your one-stop shop for books, articles, and databases. Here’s how to use it like a pro:

A. Use the Advanced Search Function

  • Instead of typing vague terms like "global warming," try Boolean operators:
    • "climate change" AND "policy" (finds articles with both terms).
    • "renewable energy" NOT "solar" (excludes irrelevant results).
  • Filter by source type (peer-reviewed journals, books, news) to refine results.

B. Bookmark Key Databases

Save these high-impact databases to your browser for quick access:

  • JSTOR (humanities & social sciences)
  • ScienceDirect (STEM research)
  • ProQuest (business, education, and health)
  • Google Scholar (cross-disciplinary research)

C. Enable Alerts for New Research

Set up email alerts for:

  • New books in your field.
  • Latest journal articles on your topic.
  • Library event announcements (workshops, guest speakers).

Real-World Example: A political science major researching immigration policy sets up a JSTOR alert for new articles on "U.S. border security." Within a week, they receive three relevant studies, saving them weeks of manual searching.

Strategy 2: Learn the Difference Between Books, Journals, and Articles

Problem: Students often confuse books, book chapters, and journal articles, leading to irrelevant or outdated sources.

Solution:

Resource Type Best For How to Find It
Books Broad overviews, foundational knowledge Search the Library Catalog (use "Books" filter).
Book Chapters Specific case studies or subtopics Check "Book Chapters" in the catalog or Google Scholar.
Journal Articles Current, peer-reviewed research Use databases like JSTOR or ProQuest.
Conference Papers Cutting-edge, unpublished research Search Google Scholar or ResearchGate.

Real-World Example: A biology student needs a *recent study on CRISPR gene editing. They don’t rely on a 2010 textbook but instead search ScienceDirect for 2023-2024 journal articles, ensuring up-to-date, credible sources.

Strategy 3: Use Zotero or RefWorks to Avoid Plagiarism & Save Time

Problem: Students manually copy-paste citations, leading to plagiarism errors and last-minute formatting chaos.

Solution: Zotero (free) and RefWorks (paid, but available via Montclair) automate citations and organize sources.

How to Set Up Zotero in 5 Steps:

  1. Download Zotero (link) and install the browser extension.
  2. Save articles & books by clicking the Zotero icon in your browser.
  3. Organize by folders (e.g., "Thesis Research," "Class Papers").
  4. Generate citations in APA, MLA, or Chicago with one click.
  5. Insert footnotes directly into Word or Google Docs.

Real-World Example: A history student is writing a 15-page paper on the *American Revolution. Instead of manually citing 50 sources, they use Zotero to auto-format citations, reducing plagiarism risk and saving 10+ hours of work.

Strategy 4: Leverage Librarian Research Consultations (Free & Valuable)

Problem: Many students don’t know they can get free help from librarians, leading to poorly structured research.

Solution: Montclair’s librarians offer 1:1 consultations on:

  • Finding primary sources (archives, interviews).
  • Evaluating sources (is this a peer-reviewed journal or a blog post?).
  • Narrowing down topics (avoiding vague or too-broad research questions).

How to Book a Session:

  1. Visit Library Research Consultations.
  2. Select your subject area (e.g., "Business," "Psychology").
  3. Schedule a 30-minute Zoom call or in-person meeting.

Real-World Example: A sociology major struggles with finding qualitative studies on "youth mental health in urban areas." During a librarian consultation, they learn about:

  • PsycINFO (best for psychology research).
  • How to use keywords like "interview data" + "teenagers".
  • Where to find dissertations (ProQuest Dissertations & Theses).

Result: They find 12 relevant studies in 2 hours instead of weeks of trial-and-error.

Strategy 5: Use Interlibrary Loan (ILL) to Get Books & Articles Fast

Problem: A required book is checked out, and you can’t find it digitally.

Solution: Montclair’s Interlibrary Loan (ILL) system lets you borrow from other NJ libraries in 2-5 days.

How to Request an ILL:

  1. Search the Library Catalog—if the book is not available, click "Request via ILL."
  2. Fill out the form with your Montclair email.
  3. Check your email for updates (usually delivered in 3-5 days).

Real-World Example: A philosophy student needs *Heidegger’s "Being and Time" for a seminar. The Montclair copy is checked out, but they request it via ILL. Within 48 hours, it arrives at the Main Library’s circulation desk, ready for pickup.

Pro Tip: For articles, use Google Scholar’s "Get It" button—if Montclair doesn’t have it, ILL will digitally deliver it in 1-2 days.

Strategy 6: Attend Library Workshops & Events (Free Professional Development)

Problem: Students miss out on free training that could improve their research skills.

Solution: Montclair’s Library Workshops cover:

  • Citation management (Zotero, RefWorks).
  • Avoiding plagiarism (Turnitin deep dives).
  • Data analysis (SPSS, RStudio).
  • Open Access publishing (how to share your work for free).

How to Find Workshops:

  • Check Library Events Calendar.
  • RSVP via email or Zoom link.

Real-World Example: A grad student attends a "Writing Your Thesis Chapter" workshop. They learn:

  • How to structure a literature review.
  • **How to use EndNote for mass citations.
  • How to avoid procrastination traps in writing.

Result: They submit their first draft 2 weeks early instead of pulling an all-nighter.

Strategy 7: Use the Innovation Lab for Advanced Research & Projects

Problem: Students don’t know about the Innovation Lab, a hidden gem for multimedia projects.

Solution: The Innovation Lab offers:

  • 3D printing (for science fair projects, architecture models).
  • Audio/Video editing (for documentaries, presentations).
  • Coding & app development (Python, JavaScript).
  • Virtual Reality (VR) design (for psychology studies, marketing simulations).

Real-World Example: A computer science student needs to 3D print a prototype for their capstone project. They:

  1. Design the model in Tinkercad (free online software).
  2. Submit the file to the Innovation Lab.
  3. Pick up the printed part in 24 hours.

Bonus: The lab also has VR headsets for psychology experiments (e.g., studying virtual anxiety responses).

Strategy 8: Optimize Your Searches with Advanced Google Scholar Tricks

Problem: Students waste time on irrelevant Google Scholar results.

Solution: Use these Google Scholar hacks to find high-quality, recent research:

A. Use Quotation Marks for Exact Phrases

  • ❌ "climate change" (returns millions of results).
  • ✅ "climate change mitigation policies" (narrows to relevant studies).

B. Filter by Date (2019-Present)

  • Click "Since 2019" to avoid outdated sources.

C. Check the "Cited by" Section

  • If an article has 100+ citations, it’s likely influential.
  • Example: Search "AI in healthcare" → Click a highly cited article → See who cited it.

D. Use the "Get It" Button

  • If Montclair doesn’t have the article, click "Get It" → ILL will deliver it in 1-2 days.

Real-World Example: A marketing student searches "social media algorithms" in Google Scholar. Instead of scrolling through 500 results, they:

  1. Add quotation marks: "social media algorithms 2024".
  2. Filter by "Since 2023".
  3. Click "Cited by 50+" to find trusted sources.

Result: They find 8 relevant articles in 10 minutes instead of hours.

Common Mistakes at Montclair State University Libraries (And How to Avoid Them)

Even the most motivated students make avoidable mistakes that waste time and lower grades. Here’s how to steer clear of them:

Mistake 1: Ignoring the Library’s "How-To" Guides

Problem: Students reinvent the wheel by not using pre-made research guides.

Solution: Montclair’s Research Guides (link) cover:

  • How to find primary sources (archives, interviews).
  • Best databases for your major (e.g., Business students → ProQuest).
  • How to evaluate websites (is this a peer-reviewed journal or a blog?).

Fix: Bookmark your major’s guide and refer to it before starting research.

Mistake 2: Not Using "Persistent Links" for Citations

Problem: Students copy-paste URLs from Google Scholar, which break over time.

Solution: Always use Montclair’s "Persistent Links" (PURLs) for:

  • Journal articles (via JSTOR, ProQuest).
  • E-books (via the Library Catalog).

How to Get a PURL:

  1. Find the article/book in a database.
  2. Click "Permalink" or "Cite" → Copy the full URL.
  3. Use this in Zotero/RefWorks to ensure it never breaks.

Real-World Example: *A student copies a Google Scholar URL for their paper. Later, the link stops working. But if they had used the JSTOR permalink, it would still work forever.

Mistake 3: Overlooking Open Access Resources

Problem: Students pay for articles they could get for free.

Solution: Use Montclair’s Open Access Databases:

  • DOAJ (Directory of Open Access Journals) (link).
  • PubMed Central (for health sciences).
  • arXiv (for physics, math, computer science).

Fix: Before paying for an article, search "free PDF" in Google.
