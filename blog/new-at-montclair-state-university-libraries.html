Chapter 1: Understanding Montclair State University Libraries – What You Need to Know

Montclair State University Libraries consist of three main branches:

  1. The Library at the Center for Academic & Student Excellence (CASE) – The primary research hub, housing millions of books, journals, and digital resources.
  2. The Music Library – A specialized collection for music theory, performance, and composition, with rare scores and audio recordings.
  3. The Special Collections & University Archives – A treasure trove of historical documents, manuscripts, and rare books, including New Jersey-related archives.

Key Features of Montclair State Libraries

  • Over 500,000 physical and digital items (books, e-books, databases, streaming media).
  • 24/7 access to e-resources via the library website.
  • Interlibrary Loan (ILL) service – Borrow materials from other institutions if Montclair doesn’t have them.
  • Study spaces, including group rooms, silent zones, and tech-equipped carrels.
  • Research assistance via librarians, workshops, and one-on-one consultations.

Pro Tip: The library’s LibGuides (subject-specific research guides) are a goldmine for finding curated resources in your field.

Chapter 2: 8 Actionable Strategies to Master Montclair State University Libraries

Strategy 1: Start with the Library Website – Your Digital Command Center

The Montclair State University Libraries website is your first stop for any research project. Instead of wandering aimlessly, use these tools:

  • Search Box (OneSearch) – The primary discovery tool that searches books, articles, journals, and databases in one place.
  • Databases A-Z – For subject-specific research, such as:
    • JSTOR (humanities & social sciences)
    • ScienceDirect (STEM fields)
    • ProQuest (business, education, and health)
  • E-Journals & E-Books – Instant access to thousands of full-text articles and books.

Real-World Example: A political science major researching climate policy would start with OneSearch, then narrow to ProQuest Political Science Database for peer-reviewed articles. They’d also check JSTOR for historical context.

Strategy 2: Use Librarians as Your Research Partners

Montclair State librarians are highly trained experts who can:

  • Help you refine search strategies.
  • Teach you how to evaluate sources.
  • Point you to hidden gems in your field.

How to Access Help:

  • Chat with a Librarian (available 24/7 via the library website).
  • Schedule a Research Appointment (in-person or virtual).
  • Attend Workshops (e.g., Citation Management, Data Analysis, or Primary Source Research).

Common Mistake: Waiting until the last minute to ask for help. Proactive research assistance saves time and improves quality.

Strategy 3: Master the Art of Boolean Searching

Boolean operators (AND, OR, NOT) help you narrow or expand your search results.

Operator Purpose Example
AND Finds both terms "climate change" AND "policy"
OR Finds either term "global warming" OR "climate crisis"
NOT Excludes a term "climate change" NOT "economics"

Real-World Example: A history student researching Revolutionary War propaganda would search: "American Revolution" AND "propaganda" NOT "music" This ensures they avoid irrelevant results about musical propaganda.

Strategy 4: Leverage E-Resources & Digital Collections

Montclair State offers thousands of e-books, streaming videos, and databases—no need to visit the library physically for most research.

Top E-Resources to Explore:

  • E-Books (EBSCOhost, ProQuest Ebook Central) – Instant access to textbooks and scholarly monographs.
  • Streaming Media (Kanopy, Swank) – Documentaries, films, and educational videos for research or entertainment.
  • Primary Sources (Archives Unbound, Early American Imprints) – Historical documents, newspapers, and manuscripts.

Pro Tip: Bookmark your favorite databases in your browser for quick access.

Strategy 5: Utilize Study Spaces & Group Work Tools

The library offers multiple study environments to suit different needs:

  • Silent Study Zones (for focused work).
  • Group Study Rooms (reservable via the library website).
  • Tech Carrels (with computers, printers, and scanners).
  • Collaboration Spaces (for group projects and discussions).

Real-World Example: A business student working on a group presentation would reserve a group study room with projectors and whiteboards to brainstorm effectively.

Strategy 6: Take Advantage of Interlibrary Loan (ILL) for Missing Materials

If Montclair doesn’t have a book or article you need, Interlibrary Loan (ILL) lets you borrow from other institutions—for free.

How to Use ILL:

  1. Search OneSearch for the item.
  2. If unavailable, request via ILL (takes 3-7 business days).
  3. Receive digital copies of articles or physical books via mail.

Common Mistake: Assuming the library has everything. ILL is a lifesaver for rare or specialized materials.

Strategy 7: Organize Your Research with Citation Managers

Manually citing sources is time-consuming and error-prone. Citation managers like Zotero, Mendeley, and EndNote help you:

  • Save and organize references.
  • Generate citations in APA, MLA, Chicago, etc.
  • Create bibliographies automatically.

Real-World Example: *A graduate student writing a thesis would use Zotero to:

  • Store 500+ sources in folders by topic.
  • Auto-generate citations as they write.
  • Collaborate with advisors via shared libraries.*

Strategy 8: Explore Special Collections for Unique Research Opportunities

Montclair’s Special Collections & University Archives hold rare and historical materials, including:

  • New Jersey-related manuscripts (e.g., early 20th-century labor documents).
  • Rare books (e.g., first editions of Mark Twain).
  • University archives (e.g., historical student newspapers).

How to Access Special Collections:

  • Request materials in advance (some items require special handling).
  • Attend workshops on primary source research.
  • Consult archivists for guidance on uncovering hidden histories.

Pro Tip: Great for independent research projects, theses, or creative writing.

Chapter 3: Common Mistakes New Users Make (And How to Avoid Them)

Mistake 1: Not Using the Library Website Effectively

Problem: Many students only search Google instead of leveraging Montclair’s specialized databases. Solution:

  • Start with OneSearch, then drill down into subject-specific databases.
  • Bookmark key resources (e.g., JSTOR, ProQuest, EBSCO).

Mistake 2: Ignoring Librarian Help

Problem: Students wait until the last minute to ask for research help, leading to rushed, lower-quality work. Solution:

  • Schedule a research appointment early in the semester.
  • Attend workshops to build long-term skills.

Mistake 3: Overlooking E-Resources

Problem: Some students only use physical books, missing digital journals, streaming media, and primary sources. Solution:

  • Check the library’s e-resources page regularly.
  • **Use Google Scholar and set Montclair as your library link to access full-text articles.

Mistake 4: Poor Search Strategies

Problem: Broad searches return irrelevant results, wasting time. Solution:

  • Use Boolean operators (AND, OR, NOT).
  • Apply filters (e.g., peer-reviewed, date range, subject).

Mistake 5: Not Managing Citations Properly

Problem: Students manually cite sources, leading to errors and lost references. Solution:

  • Use Zotero or Mendeley to automate citations.
  • Double-check citations before submitting work.

Mistake 6: Underutilizing Study Spaces

Problem: Students crush in noisy cafes instead of using library study zones. Solution:

  • Reserve group rooms for collaborative work.
  • Use silent zones for deep focus.

Mistake 7: Not Exploring Special Collections

Problem: Many students don’t know about rare archives and manuscripts. Solution:

  • Visit the Special Collections page to see what’s available.
  • Ask archivists for research guidance.

Answer: Montclair State provides off-campus access to e-resources via single sign-on (SSO). Here’s how to access them:

  • Go to the Montclair State Libraries website.
  • Click on Databases A-Z or E-Journals.
  • Log in with your Montclair username and password.
  • If prompted, select "Montclair State University" as your institution.

Pro Tip: Bookmark the library’s off-campus access page for quick login.

2. Can I request books from other libraries through Interlibrary Loan (ILL)?

Answer: Yes! Interlibrary Loan (ILL) allows you to borrow books and articles from other institutions if Montclair doesn’t have them. Here’s how:

  1. Search OneSearch for your desired item.
  2. If unavailable, click "Request via Interlibrary Loan".
  3. Fill out the request form (takes 3-7 business days).
  4. Receive digital articles via email or physical books via mail.

Note: ILL is free for students, but some rare items may have delays.

3. How do I find peer-reviewed articles for my research?

Answer: To locate peer-reviewed articles, follow these steps:

  1. Use OneSearch and apply the "Peer-Reviewed" filter.
  2. Check subject-specific databases (e.g., JSTOR, ScienceDirect, PubMed).
  3. Use Google Scholar and set Montclair as your library link to access full-text articles.
  4. Ask a librarian for advanced search tips.

Pro Tip: For STEM fields, PubMed (medical) and IEEE Xplore (engineering) are essential databases.
