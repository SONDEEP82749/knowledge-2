Chapter 1: Understanding Montclair State University Libraries – Structure & Services

Montclair State University’s three main libraries—Library North, Library South, and the Music Library—offer a diverse range of resources, from STEM journals to humanities archives, digital collections, and specialized research databases. But how do you navigate them effectively?

1.1 The Three Libraries: What Each Offers

📚 Library North (Main Campus – 100 Library North)

  • Largest academic library on campus, housing general collections (science, social sciences, humanities).
  • Study spaces including group rooms, silent zones, and laptop-friendly areas.
  • Specialized collections:
    • Business & Economics (Bloomberg Terminal, Mergent Online)
    • Education & Psychology (ERIC, PsycINFO)
    • History & Political Science (JSTOR, Project MUSE)
  • 24/7 access via keycard (for current students/faculty).

📖 Library South (Near the Student Center – 100 Library South)

  • Focuses on humanities, arts, and social sciences.
  • Digital humanities lab with 3D printing, scanning, and multimedia tools.
  • Rare books & archives (including Montclair State’s historical documents).
  • Quiet study floors and collaborative workspaces.

🎵 Music Library (Music Building – 100 Music Building)

  • Specialized for music research, with sheet music, sound recordings, and MIDI files.
  • Audio-visual equipment for music students.
  • Access to databases like RILM Abstracts, Naxos Music Library, and IMSLP.

1.2 Key Services You Should Know About

🔍 Research Assistance & Workshops

  • One-on-one research help via Librarian Appointments (bookable via Montclair State Libraries’ website).
  • Workshops on citation, database searching, and academic integrity (check the Events Calendar for schedules).
  • Peer Research Assistants (PRAs)—upperclassmen trained to help with paper topics, APA/MLA formatting, and source evaluation.

📊 Digital & Open Access Resources

  • Montclair State University Digital Commons – A repository of student research, faculty publications, and open-access materials.
  • JSTOR, ProQuest, and EBSCOhost – Millions of academic articles at your fingertips.
  • Interlibrary Loan (ILL) – Borrow books from other universities (takes 3-5 business days).

💻 Technology & Printing

  • Computer labs with Adobe Creative Suite, SPSS, and MATLAB.
  • Free printing & scanning (up to $50/month for students).
  • Wi-Fi access with strong signal coverage across all three libraries.

📚 Special Collections & Archives

  • Montclair State University Archives – Historical documents, yearbooks, and campus memorabilia.
  • Rare Book Collection – First editions, signed manuscripts, and limited-run publications.
  • Digital Collections – Primary sources on New Jersey history, African American studies, and more.

Chapter 2: 10 Actionable Strategies to Master Montclair State University Libraries

Now that you know what’s available, how do you use it effectively? These 10 strategies will help you save time, improve your research, and avoid common mistakes.

Strategy 1: Start with the Library Website – Your First Stop

Problem: Many students go straight to Google instead of using the library’s search tools, leading to irrelevant or low-quality sources.

Solution:

  1. Bookmark the Montclair State Libraries homepage (https://www.montclair.edu/library).
  2. Use the "Search Everything" bar (combines books, articles, and databases in one search).
  3. Refine your search using filters like:
    • Date range (e.g., "2018–Present")
    • Source type (e.g., "Peer-reviewed journals")
    • Subject (e.g., "Climate Change Policy")

Real-World Example: A senior psychology major was researching trauma therapy techniques for her capstone project. Instead of searching Google, she used the library’s PsychINFO database (accessible via the website) and found 15 peer-reviewed studies in three hours—compared to the 5 irrelevant articles she found online.

Strategy 2: Leverage Librarian Appointments for Targeted Help

Problem: Students often struggle with narrowing down topics or finding credible sources but don’t know where to turn.

Solution:

  • Book a 30-minute appointment with a subject librarian (e.g., Business Librarian, STEM Librarian).
  • Ask for:
    • Database recommendations (e.g., Business Source Premier for marketing research).
    • **Help with Boolean operators (AND, OR, NOT) for better search results.
    • Citation guidance (APA, MLA, Chicago).

Real-World Example: *A business student was writing a paper on sustainable supply chains but kept getting too many or too few results. During a librarian appointment, she learned to use quotes ("sustainable supply chains") and limiters (peer-reviewed only). Within two sessions, she found exactly 12 relevant articles—perfect for her 10-page paper.*

Strategy 3: Use Zotero or RefWorks for Citation Management

Problem: Students manually cite sources, leading to errors, lost references, and last-minute formatting headaches.

Solution:

  • Download Zotero (free) or RefWorks (Montclair provides access) to:
    • Automatically save citations from databases.
    • Generate bibliographies in APA, MLA, Chicago, etc.
    • Share libraries with group members (useful for collaborative projects).

Real-World Example: *A grad student in education policy was working on a 20-page thesis with 150+ sources. Instead of copy-pasting citations, she used Zotero to:

  • Drag-and-drop PDFs from JSTOR into her library.
  • Auto-format in-text citations (e.g., (Smith, 2020, p. 45)).
  • Export a full bibliography in one click—saving 10+ hours of work.

Strategy 4: Explore Databases by Subject – Don’t Rely on Google Scholar

Problem: Google Scholar is convenient, but not all sources are peer-reviewed, and Montclair’s paid databases offer better, more relevant results.

Solution:

  • Browse databases by subject (available on the library’s website under "Databases A-Z").
  • Key databases by major:
    • Business: Business Source Premier, IBISWorld, Mergent Online
    • STEM: ScienceDirect, IEEE Xplore, PubMed
    • Humanities: JSTOR, Project MUSE, Oxford Reference
    • Education: ERIC, PsycINFO, Education Research Complete

Real-World Example: *A computer science major needed academic papers on AI ethics. Instead of searching Google Scholar, she used IEEE Xplore (via the library) and found:

  • 18 peer-reviewed papers from top conferences (ICML, AAAI).
  • Full-text access (no paywalls).
  • Citations to older works she couldn’t find elsewhere.

Strategy 5: Use Interlibrary Loan (ILL) for Books Not in Montclair’s Collection

Problem: You find a perfect book in WorldCat or Google Books, but Montclair doesn’t own it.

Solution:

  1. Search WorldCat (via the library’s website).
  2. Request via ILL (takes 3-5 business days).
  3. Check your email for the digital PDF or physical book delivery.

Real-World Example: *A history student was researching Cold War propaganda and found a rare book titled "The Psychology of Persuasion in Soviet Media" in WorldCat. Since Montclair didn’t have it, she requested it via ILL and received a digital copy within 4 days—perfect for her seminar paper.

Strategy 6: Attend Library Workshops & Research Skills Sessions

Problem: Many students don’t know how to evaluate sources or use advanced search techniques.

Solution:

  • Check the library’s Events Calendar for:
    • "How to Find & Evaluate Sources" (critical for avoiding misinformation).
    • "Citation & Plagiarism Workshop" (essential for grading rubrics).
    • "Data Visualization with Excel" (useful for business & STEM projects).
  • Watch recorded workshops on the library’s YouTube channel.

Real-World Example: *A freshman in political science struggled with finding credible news sources for her debate paper. After attending a "Evaluating News vs. Scholarly Sources" workshop, she learned to:

  • Check publisher reputations (e.g., Harvard Business Review vs. random blogs).
  • Look for peer review (most JSTOR articles are peer-reviewed).
  • Use the CRAAP test (Currency, Relevance, Authority, Accuracy, Purpose). This saved her from using biased sources and improved her paper’s credibility.

Strategy 7: Use the Digital Commons for Open-Access Research

Problem: Some students can’t afford journal paywalls, leading to limited research options.

Solution:

  • Montclair State University Digital Commons hosts thousands of open-access papers, including:
    • Student theses & dissertations.
    • Faculty research.
    • Conference proceedings.
  • Search by keyword or browse by department.

Real-World Example: *A grad student in environmental science needed data on urban deforestation in New Jersey. Instead of paying $30 per article, she found:

  • 3 relevant studies in Digital Commons.
  • Free PDFs with raw datasets (no paywall).
  • Citations to older works she couldn’t find elsewhere.

Strategy 8: Optimize Your Study Space – From Silent Zones to Group Rooms

Problem: Students waste time in loud, distracting areas or don’t know where to study efficiently.

Solution:

  • Library North:
    • Silent Study Floor (2nd Floor) – For deep focus (no talking).
    • Collaborative Tables (1st Floor) – For group projects.
    • Laptop & Phone-Free Zones (check the floor plan).
  • Library South:
    • Digital Humanities Lab – For 3D printing, scanning, and multimedia projects.
    • Quiet Study Carrels – Reservable for 2-hour blocks.
  • Music Library:
    • Individual practice rooms (for music students).

Real-World Example: *A marketing student was working on a group presentation but kept getting distracted in the library’s main floor. She booked a group room in Library South (via the library’s reservation system) and:

  • Avoided distractions from passersby.
  • Had a whiteboard for brainstorming.
  • Finished her slides 2 days early instead of pulling an all-nighter.

Strategy 9: Ask for Help Early – Don’t Wait Until the Last Minute

Problem: Students procrastinate and realize too late they need library help.

Solution:

  • Book a research appointment at least a week before your paper is due.
  • Use the "Ask a Librarian" chat (available 24/7 via the website).
  • Visit the Research Help Desk during peak hours (10 AM–2 PM, Mon-Fri).

Real-World Example: *A senior in biochemistry waited until two days before her final exam to find primary research papers on enzyme kinetics. She booked an emergency appointment with the STEM Librarian, who:

  • Taught her how to search PubMed efficiently.
  • Found 15 relevant studies in under an hour.
  • Saved her from failing the exam by providing high-quality sources.

Strategy 10: Explore Special Collections for Unique Research Opportunities

Problem: Most students only use general databases, missing rare and unique resources.

Solution:

  • Montclair State Archives – Campus history, yearbooks, alumni records.
  • Rare Books Collection – First editions, signed manuscripts.
  • Digital Collections – Primary sources on NJ history, African American studies, labor movements.

Real-World Example: *A history major was writing a paper on Montclair’s role in the Civil Rights Movement. Instead of general books, she:

  • Visited the Archives and found letters from 1960s activists.
  • Scanned primary documents for her research paper.
  • Used these sources to stand out in her seminar (most students used textbooks only).
