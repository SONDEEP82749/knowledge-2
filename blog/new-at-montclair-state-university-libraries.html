Chapter 1: Getting Oriented – The Basics of Montclair State University Libraries

Before you can master the libraries, you need to understand their structure, locations, and key features. Montclair State has three main library branches, each serving different academic needs:

1. The Library at the Center for Academic Success (CAS Library)

📍 Location: Center for Academic Success (CAS), 1 Normal Avenue, Montclair, NJ 📌 Best for: General research, undergraduate coursework, and study groups 🔹 Key Features:

  • 24/7 access (via the Library 24/7 program)
  • Computer labs with high-speed Wi-Fi
  • Group study rooms (reservable via the Library Calendar)
  • Printing, scanning, and photocopying (with student ID)

2. The Library at the School of Business (SB Library)

📍 Location: School of Business, 1 Normal Avenue, Montclair, NJ 📌 Best for: Business, economics, finance, and marketing research 🔹 Key Features:

  • Specialized business databases (IBISWorld, Statista, Bloomberg Terminal)
  • Case study collections for real-world business scenarios
  • Quiet study zones ideal for financial analysis

3. The Library at the School of Arts & Sciences (AS Library)

📍 Location: School of Arts & Sciences, 1 Normal Avenue, Montclair, NJ 📌 Best for: Humanities, social sciences, STEM, and interdisciplinary research 🔹 Key Features:

  • Rare books & special collections (including historical manuscripts)
  • Science & engineering databases (ScienceDirect, IEEE Xplore)
  • Digital humanities resources (Primary Source Index, JSTOR)

How to Find What You Need: The Library Catalog & Beyond

The Montclair State University Libraries’ online catalog is your first stop for locating books, e-books, and multimedia. Here’s how to use it effectively:

Step 1: Access the Catalog

  • Go to Montclair State University Libraries Website
  • Click on "Search the Library" (or use the "Find It" button)
  • You can also search directly via WorldCat (a global library database)

Step 2: Advanced Search Techniques

Most students make the mistake of using basic keyword searches, which return irrelevant results. Instead, try these pro tips:

✔ Use Boolean Operators (AND, OR, NOT)

  • Example: "climate change AND policy" (narrows results to both terms)
  • Example: "machine learning NOT artificial intelligence" (excludes AI-only results)

✔ Apply Filters

  • Format: E-books, DVDs, Government Documents
  • Language: English, Spanish, etc.
  • Publication Date: Last 5 years (for current research)

✔ Check "Available Online"

  • Many books are digitally accessible—look for the "Available Online" tag to avoid waiting for physical copies.

✔ Use Subject Headings

  • If a book appears in results, click on its subject tags (e.g., "Environmental Policy") to find related materials.

Real-World Example: Finding a Book on Renewable Energy

A student needs a book on solar energy for a sustainability class. ❌ Bad Search: "solar energy" (returns 500+ results, many unrelated) ✅ Good Search:

  • "solar energy" AND "policy" (narrows to 47 results)
  • Filters: "Available Online" → "Publication Date: 2020–Present"
  • Subject Headings: "Renewable Energy Sources" → Finds "Solar Power in Developing Nations" (2023 e-book)

Common Mistake #1: Ignoring E-Books & Digital Resources

Why it’s bad: Physical books take time to retrieve, and some may be checked out by others. How to fix it:

  • Use the "Available Online" filter in the catalog.
  • Download e-books via OverDrive, EBSCOhost, or ProQuest.
  • Access journals digitally through JSTOR, ScienceDirect, or Google Scholar.

How to Search Like a Pro in JSTOR

Many students struggle with JSTOR’s advanced search. Here’s the right way:

  1. Start with a narrow topic (e.g., "climate change mitigation in Africa" instead of just "climate change").
  2. Use quotation marks for exact phrases: "postcolonial literature"
  3. Apply filters:
    • Subject: Literature, Environmental Studies
    • Publication Date: Last 5 years
    • Content Type: Articles, Book Reviews
  4. Check "Peer-Reviewed" only (unless you need broader sources).
  5. Save & export citations (use Zotero, EndNote, or RefWorks for later use).

Real-World Example: Finding a Peer-Reviewed Article on AI Ethics

A student needs an article on AI bias for a philosophy class. ❌ Bad Search: "AI ethics" (returns 1,200+ results, many non-academic) ✅ Good Search:

  • Database: JSTOR
  • Search: "algorithmic bias" AND "moral philosophy"
  • Filters:
    • Subject: Philosophy, Computer Science
    • Publication Date: 2018–Present
    • Peer-Reviewed: ✅
  • Result: "The Ethics of Algorithmic Decision-Making" (2022, Journal of Applied Philosophy)

Common Mistake #2: Not Using Database-Specific Features

Why it’s bad: Many students treat all databases the same, missing advanced tools. How to fix it:

  • JSTOR: Use "Cited By" to find related research.
  • Statista: Explore "Market Studies" for business projects.
  • IEEE Xplore: Filter by "Conference Papers" for cutting-edge tech research.
  • CQ Researcher: Check "Policy Recommendations" for current events papers.

Chapter 3: Using Library Services & Study Spaces Like a Pro

Montclair State Libraries offer far more than just books—they provide study spaces, printing, expert help, and even career resources. Here’s how to maximize them:

1. Printing, Scanning, & Copying

📌 Where to go: Any library location (CAS, SB, AS) 💰 Costs (as of 2024):

  • Black & White Printing: $0.10 per page
  • Color Printing: $0.25 per page
  • Scanning: Free (save to USB or email)
  • Photocopying: $0.10 per page

✅ Pro Tip:

  • Use the "Print Release" system (avoids wasting ink by printing only what you need).
  • Check your balance via MyMontclair Portal.

2. Study Rooms & Group Workspaces

📍 Locations:

  • CAS Library: 10 group study rooms (reservable via Library Calendar)
  • SB Library: 5 business-focused rooms
  • AS Library: Quiet zones for individual study

✅ How to Reserve:

  1. Go to Library Study Rooms
  2. Select your preferred date/time
  3. Enter your Montclair email & student ID

⚠ Common Mistake #3: Not Reserving Rooms in Advance Why it’s bad: Popular rooms (especially near exam periods) book up fast. How to fix it:

  • Reserve at least 24 hours in advance for peak times.
  • Check availability 1 hour before your session.

3. Research & Writing Help

Montclair Libraries offer free expert assistance to help you:

  • Find sources (librarians can guide you through databases)
  • Cite properly (APA, MLA, Chicago styles)
  • Develop research strategies

📌 How to Access Help:

  • Chat with a Librarian: Ask a Librarian (24/7)
  • Schedule a Research Appointment: Librarian Consultations
  • Attend Workshops: Check Library Events Calendar

4. Interlibrary Loan (ILL) – Get Books You Can’t Find Locally

📌 What it is: Borrow books from other universities if Montclair doesn’t have them. 🕒 Turnaround Time: 3–7 business days 💰 Cost: Free for students!

✅ How to Request:

  1. Search the catalog for your book.
  2. Click "Request via Interlibrary Loan".
  3. Fill out the form with your Montclair email & ID.

⚠ Common Mistake #4: Not Using ILL for Rare or Out-of-Print Books Why it’s bad: Waiting for a book to arrive can delay your work. How to fix it:

  • Check ILL availability before requesting a physical copy.
  • Set reminders for ILL delivery dates.
