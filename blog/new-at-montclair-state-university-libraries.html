Chapter 1: Getting to Know Montclair State University Libraries

Before diving into strategies, it’s essential to understand what makes Montclair State Libraries unique.

1.1 The Library System: More Than Just Books

Montclair State University operates three main campus libraries, each with specialized strengths:

  • The Library at the Center for Academic Success (CAS) – The heart of student services, offering research help, study spaces, and tech support.
  • The Library at the School of Business – A business-focused hub with financial databases, case studies, and industry reports.
  • The Library at the School of Education – Tailored for education research, including early childhood studies, special education, and curriculum development.

Pro Tip: Each library has its own "specialty" collections—don’t assume everything is in one place!

1.2 Key Services You Might Not Know About

Beyond checking out books, Montclair State Libraries offer:

✔ 24/7 Online Access – Full-text databases, e-books, and streaming media available anytime, anywhere. ✔ Research Consultations – One-on-one help from librarians who specialize in your field (yes, they’re experts!). ✔ Interlibrary Loan (ILL) – Need a book not in Montclair’s collection? They’ll borrow it for you from another university. ✔ Study Rooms & Tech Spaces – Book a quiet study room, a group collaboration space, or a tech lab with scanners and printers. ✔ Workshops & Tutorials – From Zotero for citations to advanced database searches, they offer free, hands-on training. ✔ Digital Scholarship Lab – For students working on digital humanities projects, data analysis, or multimedia research.

Real-World Example: A senior psychology major was struggling to find peer-reviewed studies on trauma therapy. She scheduled a research consultation with a librarian, who taught her how to use PsycINFO and Google Scholar filters to narrow down 500+ results to just 10 highly relevant sources. Her final paper earned an A—something she thought was impossible before.

Chapter 2: 8 Actionable Strategies to Master Montclair State Libraries

Now that you know what’s available, let’s dive into how to use it effectively.

Strategy 1: Start with the Library Website—Before You Even Walk In

Problem: Many students wait until the last minute to use the library, only to realize they don’t know where to begin.

Solution: The Montclair State University Libraries website is your first stop. Instead of wandering the stacks aimlessly, use these search filters to find exactly what you need:

  • Subject Guides – Each department (Business, Education, STEM, etc.) has a curated list of recommended databases, journals, and resources.
  • Advanced Search – Beyond simple keyword searches, use Boolean operators (AND, OR, NOT) to refine results.
    • Example: Instead of searching "climate change," try "climate change AND policy AND United States" for more precise results.
  • E-Journal Finder – If you have a specific article citation, plug in the journal title to check if Montclair subscribes.

Common Mistake: Assuming Google Scholar is enough. Why it’s bad: Google Scholar doesn’t always provide full-text access or filter by peer-reviewed sources. Montclair’s library databases do.

How to Fix It:

  • Use Google Scholar’s "Linked to Full Text" filter (under Settings > Library Links), but always cross-check with Montclair’s databases for guaranteed access.

Strategy 2: Use Research Consultations—Before You Get Stuck

Problem: Students often procrastinate and end up wasting hours on irrelevant sources.

Solution: Montclair offers free research consultations with librarians who specialize in your field. These are not just for grad students—undergrads use them too!

How to Book One:

  1. Go to the Library’s Research Help page.
  2. Select your subject area (e.g., "Business," "Psychology," "Computer Science").
  3. Schedule a 15-30 minute appointment via Calendly.

Real-World Example: *A business student was assigned a case study on Amazon’s supply chain. He had no idea where to start, so he booked a consultation. The librarian:

  • Showed him IBISWorld for industry reports.
  • Taught him how to find SWOT analyses on Amazon.
  • Helped him evaluate sources for credibility. By the end of the session, he had a full bibliography—something he couldn’t have done alone in 10 hours.*

Pro Tip: Even if you’re confident, a quick 15-minute check can save you days of work.

Strategy 3: Master the Databases—Beyond Google

Problem: Students default to Google or Wikipedia, which often lead to misinformation or weak sources.

Solution: Montclair provides hundreds of specialized databases, each designed for different fields. Here’s how to pick the right one:

Field of Study Best Databases to Use What They Offer
Business IBISWorld, Business Source Complete Industry reports, company profiles, market analysis
Psychology PsycINFO, Psychology & Behavioral Sciences Collection Peer-reviewed studies, dissertations, therapy techniques
STEM ScienceDirect, IEEE Xplore Research papers, technical reports, engineering case studies
Education ERIC, Education Research Complete Teaching methods, curriculum studies, special education research
Humanities JSTOR, Project MUSE Academic journals, literary criticism, cultural studies

How to Search Effectively:

  • Use subject-specific keywords (e.g., instead of "technology," try "AI in education").
  • Filter by date (e.g., only sources from the last 5 years for up-to-date research).
  • Check the "Peer-Reviewed" box to ensure credibility.

Common Mistake: Assuming all databases are the same. Why it’s bad: A business student searching in PsycINFO will get zero relevant results—they need IBISWorld.

How to Fix It:

  • Ask your professor which databases are most useful for your course.
  • Check the subject guide for your major.

Strategy 4: Learn to Cite Like a Pro (Avoid Plagiarism & Stress)

Problem: Students procrastinate on citations until the night before, leading to last-minute errors and stress.

Solution: Montclair Libraries offer free citation tools to make this painless:

  1. Zotero – A free reference manager that:
    • Automatically captures citations from databases.
    • Organizes sources in folders.
    • Generates citations in APA, MLA, Chicago, etc.
  2. RefWorks – Another cloud-based citation manager with collaboration features.
  3. Purdue OWL (Online Writing Lab) – A free guide to formatting citations correctly.

Real-World Example: *A history student was working on a 15-page paper and kept losing track of sources. She started using Zotero, which:

  • Saved her 10+ hours by auto-filling citations.
  • Prevented plagiarism by tracking every source.
  • Made her bibliography error-free in minutes.

Pro Tip: Start citing as you research—don’t wait until the end!

Common Mistake: Manually copying citations from PDFs. Why it’s bad: Formatting errors (wrong font, missing page numbers) can automatically lower your grade.

How to Fix It:

  • Use Zotero’s "Word Plugin" to auto-format citations as you write.
  • Double-check with Purdue OWL if you’re unsure about a format.

Strategy 5: Use Interlibrary Loan (ILL) for Books You Can’t Find

Problem: You need a specific book, but Montclair doesn’t have it.

Solution: Interlibrary Loan (ILL) lets you borrow books from other universities—for free!

How to Use ILL:

  1. Search for your book in Montclair’s catalog.
  2. If it’s not available, click "Request via Interlibrary Loan."
  3. Fill out the form (usually takes 3-5 business days).
  4. Pick it up at the library or have it emailed to you (PDF for e-books).

Real-World Example: *A philosophy student needed Hegel’s Phenomenology of Spirit for a seminar. Montclair didn’t have it, but through ILL, she got a digital copy in 2 days—no waiting for shipping!

Common Mistake: Assuming ILL takes too long and skipping the book. Why it’s bad: You might miss key arguments in your research.

How to Fix It:

  • Request ILL early—some books take up to 2 weeks.
  • Check if Montclair has an e-book version (some are available instantly).

Strategy 6: Take Advantage of Study Spaces & Tech Labs

Problem: Students crush in noisy cafes or their dorms, leading to distractions and burnout.

Solution: Montclair Libraries offer multiple study environments:

  • Quiet Study Rooms – Book a private space for group or solo work.
  • Collaboration Zones – Whiteboards, laptops, and brainstorming tables.
  • Tech Labs – Scanners, 3D printers, and audio recording equipment.
  • Silent Study Floors – No talking allowed (perfect for deep work).

How to Book a Study Room:

  1. Go to the Library’s Study Rooms page.
  2. Select your preferred date and time.
  3. Arrive 10 minutes early to avoid last-minute cancellations.

Real-World Example: *A group of engineering students was working on a robotics project. They booked a collaboration room with whiteboards and power outlets, which:

  • Saved them 3 hours by avoiding distractions.
  • Helped them visualize designs more clearly.

Pro Tip: If you’re an early bird, reserve a study room for finals week—they fill up fast!

Strategy 7: Attend Workshops & Tutorials (Free & Valuable)

Problem: Students don’t know what workshops are available and miss out on free expert training.

Solution: Montclair Libraries host weekly workshops on:

  • Advanced database searching
  • Citation management (Zotero/RefWorks)
  • Data analysis (Excel, SPSS, R)
  • Digital humanities tools

How to Find Workshops:

  1. Check the Library’s Events Calendar.
  2. Sign up via email (some are first-come, first-served).

Real-World Example: *A biology student was struggling with SPSS for statistics. She attended a free workshop, where she learned:

  • How to run regression analyses correctly.
  • How to interpret p-values without confusion. Her lab report grade jumped from a B to an A+.

Common Mistake: Assuming workshops are "too basic." Why it’s bad: Even advanced students pick up new shortcuts they didn’t know existed.

How to Fix It:

  • Attend at least one workshop per semester—you’ll save hours of trial-and-error.

Strategy 8: Use the Digital Scholarship Lab for Advanced Projects

Problem: Students with digital humanities, data science, or multimedia projects don’t know where to turn.

Solution: The Digital Scholarship Lab offers:

  • Help with data visualization (Tableau, R, Python).
  • 3D printing & scanning for prototypes.
  • Audio/Video editing for research presentations.
  • GIS mapping for geography and urban studies.

Real-World Example: *A grad student in urban planning needed to map gentrification trends in Newark. The Digital Scholarship Lab:

  • Helped her clean and analyze census data.
  • Taught her how to create interactive maps in QGIS. Her thesis visualizations were published in a local newspaper—something she couldn’t have done alone.

How to Access It:

  • Email digitalscholarship@montclair.edu to schedule a consultation.
  • Check the Lab’s hours on the library website.

Chapter 3: Common Mistakes & How to Avoid Them

Even the best students make avoidable errors when using libraries. Here’s how to steer clear of them:

Mistake 1: Not Using Subject Guides

What Happens: Students search randomly in databases, leading to irrelevant or low-quality sources.

How to Fix It:

  • Always start with your department’s subject guide (e.g., "Business Research Guide").
  • Follow the recommended databases—they’re curated by experts.

Mistake 2: Ignoring the "Full Text" Filter

What Happens: You find a great-looking article, but when you click it, you get a "PDF unavailable" message.

How to Fix It:

  • Before clicking, check if the database offers "Full Text" or "PDF" options.
  • If not, use Interlibrary Loan to get a copy.

Mistake 3: Procrastinating on Citation Management

What Happens: You wait until the last minute, leading to disorganized citations and plagiarism risks.

How to Fix It:

  • Install Zotero or RefWorks early and add sources as you research.
  • Set up auto-formatting in your word processor.
