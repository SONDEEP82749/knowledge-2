Chapter 1: Getting Oriented – The Layout & Key Locations You Need to Know

Before diving into research, familiarize yourself with the physical and digital layout of Montclair State’s libraries. Here’s a breakdown of the most important locations and how to navigate them efficiently.

1.1 The Main Libraries: Library North & Library South

Montclair State’s two primary libraries—Library North (home to Library Services, Reference Desk, and most general collections) and Library South (specializing in business, education, and social sciences)—are your first stop for most academic needs.

  • Library North (Main Campus – 1 Normal Ave)

    • Reference Desk (1st Floor): The best place to ask questions—librarians here can help with finding sources, using databases, and understanding citation styles.
    • Circulation Desk (1st Floor): Where you check out books, renew loans, and report lost items.
    • Study Spaces:
      • Group Study Rooms (reservable via My Library Account).
      • Silent Study Zones (ideal for exams and deep focus).
      • Tech Lounge (with printers, scanners, and laptop checkout).
    • Special Collections (2nd Floor): Rare books, archival materials, and historical documents (great for independent research projects).

  • Library South (1 Normal Ave – Near the Student Center)

    • Business & Education Focus: Strong collections in accounting, marketing, education, and psychology.
    • Data & Research Services (2nd Floor): Access to statistical software (SPSS, R), survey tools, and research methodology guides.
    • Multimedia Lab: For audio/video editing, 3D printing, and digital humanities projects.

1.2 The John J. Cali School of Music Library

If you’re a music major, this specialized library (located in Cali Hall) is a must-visit:

  • Sheet music collection (over 50,000+ scores).
  • Audio recordings (classical, jazz, world music).
  • Music technology resources (DAWs, MIDI equipment).
  • Private practice rooms (reservable for individual or ensemble rehearsals).

1.3 The Digital Library & Remote Access

Montclair State’s online resources are just as powerful as the physical ones. Key digital tools include:

  • Montclair State University Library Website: https://library.montclair.edu (your one-stop portal for e-books, databases, and research guides).
  • My Library Account: Track checked-out items, renewals, and fines (accessible via Blackboard or the library portal).
  • LibApps (Library Services App): Available on mobile devices, it lets you:
    • Scan QR codes on book spines to check availability.
    • Receive alerts for new book arrivals in your field.
    • Access 24/7 chat support with librarians.

Pro Tip: Bookmark the library homepage and set up alerts for new publications in your major. Many students miss out on fresh research because they don’t check regularly.

Chapter 2: 10 Actionable Strategies to Master Montclair State University Libraries

Now that you know the layout, let’s dive into practical strategies to maximize your library experience. These tips are backed by librarian best practices and student success stories from Montclair State.

Strategy 1: Start with Research Guides – Your First Stop for Expert Help

Montclair State’s librarians have created over 150 research guides tailored to every major. These guides save hours of trial-and-error by:

  • Listing the best databases for your field.
  • Explaining how to evaluate sources.
  • Providing citation examples (APA, MLA, Chicago, etc.).

How to Use Them:

  1. Go to the library website → Research Guides (under Help).
  2. Search by your major (e.g., "Psychology Research Guide," "Business Management Guide").
  3. Follow the step-by-step instructions on where to find credible sources.

Real-World Example: A senior psychology major struggling with peer-reviewed articles found the Psychology Research Guide, which directed her to PsycINFO—a database with 4.5 million records. She found her thesis topic in 30 minutes instead of wasting days searching Google Scholar.

Common Mistake: Skipping research guides and relying on Google searches, which often lead to biased or low-quality sources.

Strategy 2: Use the Library Catalog Like a Pro (Beyond Basic Searches)

The Montclair State Library Catalog (available on the library homepage) is more powerful than a simple Google search. Here’s how to unlock its full potential:

  • Advanced Search Filters:

    • Keyword vs. Subject Search: A subject search (e.g., "climate change AND policy") narrows results to only relevant academic works.
    • Format Filter: Limit to e-books, videos, or government documents.
    • Date Range: Focus on recent research (last 5 years) for up-to-date information.

  • Interlibrary Loan (ILL) for Books Not at Montclair:

    • If a book isn’t available, request it via ILL (takes 3-7 days).
    • Pro Tip: Check WorldCat (linked from the catalog) to see if other NJ libraries have it.

Real-World Example: A business student needed a rare 1990s economic report not in Montclair’s collection. Using WorldCat, she found it at Rutgers University and requested it via ILL—delivered digitally in 5 days.

Common Mistake: Only searching by title or author, missing subject-based results that could be more relevant.

Strategy 3: Leverage Databases – The Secret Weapon for Academic Success

General Google searches won’t cut it for college research. Montclair State provides premium databases that filter out junk and give you peer-reviewed, credible sources.

Top Databases by Major:

Major Best Databases What They Offer
Business Business Source Premier, ABI/INFORM Industry reports, company profiles
Psychology PsycINFO, PsycARTICLES Peer-reviewed studies, therapy techniques
STEM ScienceDirect, IEEE Xplore Research papers, technical manuals
Education ERIC, Education Full Text Teaching methods, policy analysis
Humanities JSTOR, Project MUSE Academic journals, literary criticism

How to Access Them:

  1. Go to the library website → Databases A-Z.
  2. Click on your major’s top database.
  3. Log in with your Montclair credentials (same as Blackboard).

Real-World Example: A computer science student needed a specific algorithm paper for a project. Instead of searching Google Scholar (which returned 100 irrelevant PDFs), she used IEEE Xplore and found the exact paper in 2 minutes.

Common Mistake: Assuming Google Scholar is enough—it lacks Montclair’s paid subscriptions, meaning you’ll miss high-quality, filtered results.

Strategy 4: Master Citation Management with Zotero or EndNote

Citing sources manually is time-consuming and error-prone. Zotero (free) and EndNote (paid, but available via Montclair) automate citations and build bibliographies in APA, MLA, Chicago, and more.

How to Set Up Zotero (Step-by-Step):

  1. Download Zotero from https://www.zotero.org.
  2. Create an account (syncs across devices).
  3. Install the browser extension (for automatically saving citations).
  4. Add sources from databases or the web.
  5. Generate citations in your Word/Google Docs with one click.

Real-World Example: *A history student was overwhelmed by citations for her 50-source paper. She used Zotero to auto-format APA citations, saving her 10+ hours of manual work.

Common Mistake: Waiting until the last minute to organize citations—start early to avoid plagiarism risks.

Strategy 5: Use the "Ask a Librarian" Service (24/7 Help)

Montclair State’s librarians are your free academic coaches. They can help with:

  • Finding sources for tough topics.
  • Evaluating websites for credibility.
  • Understanding research methods.
  • Citation formatting.

How to Access Help:

  • Live Chat: Available 24/7 via the library website.
  • Email: library@montclair.edu (response within 24 hours).
  • In-Person: Visit the Reference Desk during library hours.

Real-World Example: A nursing student struggled to find evidence-based studies on telehealth in geriatrics. She chatted with a librarian, who directed her to CINAHL Plus and helped refine her search terms—she found 5 key articles in 15 minutes.

Common Mistake: *Assuming librarians are only for "last-resort" help—they can save you hours if used early.

Strategy 6: Explore Special Collections & Archives for Unique Research

Montclair State’s Special Collections (2nd floor, Library North) holds rare books, manuscripts, and historical documents that general research can’t access. Great for:

  • Independent research projects.
  • Thesis/dissertation work.
  • Creative writing inspiration.

What’s Available?

  • Montclair State University Archives: Old yearbooks, newspapers, and alumni records.
  • New Jersey History Collection: Primary sources on NJ’s civil rights movement.
  • Literary Manuscripts: Unpublished works by local authors.

Real-World Example: A graduate student researching 1920s labor strikes in NJ found exclusive union meeting minutes in the Special Collections. Without this, she would have missed a key primary source.

Common Mistake: Assuming only "boring" books are in the library—Special Collections often have surprising, high-value materials.

Strategy 8: Attend Workshops & Events (Free Professional Development)

Montclair State’s libraries host hundreds of workshops each year—most students miss them. Some must-attend events include:

  • Citation Management Workshops (Zotero, EndNote).
  • Data Analysis Bootcamps (SPSS, R, Python).
  • Academic Integrity & Plagiarism Prevention.
  • Grant Writing for Research Projects.

How to Find Workshops:

  1. Check the library calendar (https://library.montclair.edu/events).
  2. Set Google Calendar alerts for workshops in your major.
  3. Attend at least one per semester—they save time and improve grades.

Real-World Example: A grad student struggled with SPSS for her thesis. She attended a free workshop, learned advanced analysis techniques, and completed her data in half the time.

Common Mistake: *Thinking workshops are "optional"—they often teach skills you can’t learn from textbooks.*
