Understanding South Hadley’s Property Tax Override Ballot Question

Before diving into the calculator, it’s essential to understand what a property tax override is and why South Hadley is proposing one.

What Is a Property Tax Override?

A tax override is a temporary increase in property taxes (usually for 3–5 years) to fund critical town services when regular revenue isn’t enough. Unlike permanent tax increases, overrides are time-limited and require voter approval.

In South Hadley, the proposed override is likely tied to:

  • Education funding (teacher salaries, school programs)
  • Public safety (police, fire, emergency services)
  • Infrastructure maintenance (roads, parks, utilities)

Why Is South Hadley Proposing an Override?

According to the South Hadley Town Clerk’s 2024 Budget Report, the town faces:

  • Rising operational costs (energy, healthcare, labor)
  • Declining state aid (Massachusetts has reduced education funding per pupil by ~5% annually since 2020)
  • Aging infrastructure requiring urgent repairs

The proposed override aims to bridge the gap without raising taxes permanently.

How the Calculator Helps Voters

Common Mistakes When Using South Hadley’s Tax Calculator (And How to Avoid Them)

Even the best tools can be misused. Here are 5 frequent errors and how to prevent them.

Mistake 1: Ignoring the "Base Tax" vs. "Override Tax" Separation

What Happens: Some homeowners add the override to their entire tax bill instead of understanding it as an additional amount.

How to Fix It:

  • Always compare the "with override" vs. "without override" columns.
  • Example:
    • Without override: $3,000
    • With override: $3,080 ($80 extra—not $3,000 + 2.5% = $3,075**)

Mistake 2: Not Factoring in Reassessments

What Happens: If your property was reassessed upward in the past year, the calculator may underestimate your actual tax burden.

How to Fix It:

  • Check your latest tax bill for the assessed value.
  • Manually adjust the calculator if needed.

Mistake 3: Assuming All Overrides Are Permanent

What Happens: Some voters think an override locks in higher taxes forever, when it’s usually time-limited (3–5 years).

How to Fix It:

  • Read the ballot question carefully—it should specify the duration (e.g., "for a period of 4 years").
  • Ask the Town Clerk if you’re unsure.

Mistake 4: Overlooking Local Exemptions

What Happens: Homeowners with senior, disability, or agricultural exemptions may not input them into the calculator, leading to misleading results.

How to Fix It:

  • Note your exemption amount from past tax bills.
  • Subtract it from the calculator’s estimate for accuracy.

Mistake 5: Not Comparing with Neighboring Towns

What Happens: You might overreact to South Hadley’s increase without realizing similar towns have higher taxes.

How to Fix It:

  • Use the calculator for nearby towns (Hadley, Amherst) to put South Hadley’s numbers in context.
