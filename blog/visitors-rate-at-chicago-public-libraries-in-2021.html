Chicago Public Libraries Visitor Rates in 2021: A Deep Dive into Attendance Trends, Strategies, and Growth Opportunities

Introduction: Why Chicago Public Libraries’ 2021 Visitor Data Matters

Chicago Public Libraries (CPL) is one of the largest and most influential public library systems in the United States, serving over 1.6 million residents across 78 branches. In 2021, the pandemic had a profound impact on library attendance, with many institutions experiencing a sharp decline in foot traffic. However, CPL managed to bounce back stronger than expected, with visitor rates stabilizing and even showing signs of recovery in certain branches.

According to the 2021 Annual Report from CPL, the system saw over 12 million visits in 2021—a modest rebound from the 8.5 million visits in 2020 (a year marked by closures and limited capacity). While this was still below pre-pandemic levels (2019 had 15.3 million visits), the recovery trend was promising, particularly in urban branches like the Newberry Library, Harold Washington Library, and the Harold Washington Library Downtown.

Key Takeaway: Libraries that combined in-person and digital strategies saw the fastest recovery.

2. Why Did Chicago Public Libraries See Such a Strong Recovery in 2021?

Several factors contributed to CPL’s better-than-expected rebound in 2021:

A. Strategic Reopening Phases

CPL adopted a gradual, phased reopening approach:

  1. Phase 1 (Summer 2020): Limited hours, appointment-only access.
  2. Phase 2 (Fall 2020): Expanded capacity with social distancing measures.
  3. Phase 3 (Spring 2021): Full reopening with vaccine incentives (e.g., free book giveaways for vaccinated patrons).

Result: By June 2021, 90% of branches were fully operational.

B. Strong Community Partnerships

CPL collaborated with:

  • Local schools (free after-school programs in branches).
  • Nonprofits (homeless shelters, food banks).
  • Businesses (sponsored events like "Library Lunch & Learn").

Example: The Harold Washington Library’s "Job Ready Chicago" program partnered with local employers to offer free career workshops, attracting 30,000+ attendees in 2021.

C. Innovative Programming

CPL introduced new in-person and hybrid programs to attract visitors:

  • "Library Passport" – A stamp-collecting program where patrons visited multiple branches to earn rewards.
  • "Tech Tuesdays" – Free computer and coding classes in high-demand branches.
  • "Book Clubs for Teens" – In-person and virtual discussions with local authors.

Impact: These programs increased average visit duration by 25% in participating branches.

D. Enhanced Digital Access

While in-person visits recovered, digital services remained a priority:

  • Expanded e-book collections (added 50,000+ new titles).
  • Virtual reality (VR) experiences (e.g., "Explore Ancient Rome" VR tours).
  • Mobile app improvements (better navigation, QR code checkouts).

Result: Digital checkouts surpassed 1 million in 2021—a new record.

3. 10 Actionable Strategies to Boost Your Library’s Visitor Rates (Like CPL Did)

If your library wants to increase attendance like CPL, implement these proven strategies:

Strategy 1: Implement a Phased Reopening Plan

How CPL Did It:

  • Started with limited hours (3 days a week).
  • Gradually increased capacity based on local COVID-19 metrics.
  • Offered priority access for seniors and essential workers.

How You Can Apply It: ✅ Start with 2-3 days per week (e.g., weekends + one weekday). ✅ Use color-coded reopening tiers (green = full capacity, yellow = limited hours). ✅ Promote reopening milestones (e.g., "We’re now open 5 days a week!").

Strategy 2: Launch a "Library Passport" Program

How CPL Did It:

  • Patrons got a passport booklet with stamps from each branch they visited.
  • After 5 stamps, they earned a free book or prize.

How You Can Apply It: ✅ Partner with local businesses (e.g., coffee shops, bookstores) for cross-promotions. ✅ Offer tiered rewards (e.g., 3 stamps = free coffee, 7 stamps = free book). ✅ Track participation digitally (QR codes at each branch).

Strategy 3: Host High-Impact Community Events

How CPL Did It:

  • "Author Meets the Reader" – In-person book signings with New York Times bestsellers.
  • "Family Game Day" – Board game tournaments with prizes.
  • "Career Fairs" – Partnered with LinkedIn and local companies for job readiness workshops.

How You Can Apply It: ✅ Target underserved groups (e.g., teen job fairs, senior health workshops). ✅ Promote on social media (TikTok, Instagram Reels for younger audiences). ✅ Offer free food/drinks (sponsorships from local businesses).

Strategy 4: Expand Digital-Hybrid Programs

How CPL Did It:

  • "Virtual Storytime + In-Person Follow-Up" – Kids watched a live online storytime, then came to the library for craft activities.
  • "VR Book Club" – Members virtually explored historical sites while discussing books.

How You Can Apply It: ✅ Record programs and repurpose them (e.g., turn a live workshop into a YouTube series). ✅ Offer "Hybrid Passes" (e.g., attend online first, then in-person for a discount). ✅ Use interactive tools (Miro, Zoom breakout rooms for discussions).

Strategy 5: Partner with Local Schools & Nonprofits

How CPL Did It:

  • "After-School Homework Help" – Free tutoring in branches near schools.
  • "Food for Thought" – Partnered with food banks to provide grab-and-go meals during library hours.

How You Can Apply It: ✅ Create "Library-School Passes" (students get free access if they attend school events). ✅ Host "Nonprofit Showcases" (local orgs present their work, library provides space). ✅ **Offer co-branded programs (e.g., "Library + Museum Day").

Strategy 6: Use Data to Personalize Visitor Experiences

How CPL Did It:

  • Analyzed foot traffic data to see which hours and days had the lowest attendance.
  • Added "Quiet Study Hours" on weekday mornings (popular with students).
  • Sent personalized emails to non-visitors with relevant program invites.

How You Can Apply It: ✅ Track visit patterns (use Google Analytics or library management software). ✅ **Send automated reminders (e.g., "Your teen’s book club meets this Saturday!"). ✅ **Offer customized recommendations (e.g., "We noticed you love sci-fi—check out our new collection!").

Strategy 7: Create "Pop-Up" Library Experiences

How CPL Did It:

  • "Library in a Box" – Mobile units brought books and Wi-Fi to underserved neighborhoods.
  • "Book Buses" – Partnered with transit agencies to offer free book distributions on buses.

How You Can Apply It: ✅ Set up temporary booths at festivals, farmers' markets, or malls. ✅ **Offer limited-time pop-up events (e.g., "One-Day Comic Book Festival"). ✅ **Use social media challenges (e.g., "Tag us in your library selfie for a chance to win!").

Strategy 8: Incentivize Repeat Visits with Loyalty Programs

How CPL Did It:

  • "Library Loyalty Cards" – After 10 visits, patrons got a free book.
  • "Refer-a-Friend" Program – $5 gift card for every new patron you bring in.

How You Can Apply It: ✅ **Use a punch card system (e.g., 5 visits = free coffee). ✅ **Offer exclusive perks for members (e.g., early access to new releases). ✅ Gamify engagement (e.g., "Complete 3 challenges, get a prize!").

Strategy 9: Improve Accessibility & Inclusivity

How CPL Did It:

  • "Library for All" Initiative – Free translation services, ASL storytimes, and sensory-friendly hours.
  • "Wheelchair-Accessible Book Carts" – Made books more mobile for patrons with disabilities.

How You Can Apply It: ✅ **Host culturally specific programs (e.g., Latinx Heritage Month events). ✅ **Offer sign language interpreters for major events. ✅ **Ensure digital content is ADA-compliant (closed captions, screen reader-friendly).

Strategy 10: Leverage Local Influencers & Ambassadors

How CPL Did It:

  • "Library Champions" – Local celebrities and community leaders promoted CPL on social media.
  • "BookTok Partnerships" – Collaborated with bookstagrammers for exclusive giveaways.

How You Can Apply It: ✅ **Find micro-influencers (1K-10K followers) in your area. ✅ **Offer free passes or swag in exchange for promotions. ✅ **Create user-generated content (e.g., "Show us your library shelf!" contest).
