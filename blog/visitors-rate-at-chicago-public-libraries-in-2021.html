Chicago Public Libraries Visitor Rates in 2021: A Deep Dive into Attendance Trends, Strategies, and Growth Opportunities

Introduction: Why Chicago Public Libraries’ 2021 Visitor Rates Matter

Chicago Public Libraries (CPL) is one of the largest and most influential public library systems in the United States, serving over 1.6 million residents across 80+ branches. In 2021, the system faced unprecedented challenges—the lingering effects of the COVID-19 pandemic, shifting digital habits, and economic uncertainties—that reshaped how libraries operated and how visitors engaged with their services.

According to CPL’s annual reports and internal data, total in-person visits dropped by approximately 40% in 2020 compared to pre-pandemic levels. However, by 2021, the system saw a gradual recovery, with some branches experiencing visitor rate rebounds of 30-50% depending on location, programming, and community demand. While digital engagement (e-books, online classes, and virtual events) surged, in-person foot traffic remained a critical metric for assessing library relevance, funding, and future growth.

  • Peak recovery months: September–December (back-to-school programs, holiday events, and winter programming drove higher attendance)
  • Lowest attendance months: January–March (post-holiday lull, cold weather, and residual pandemic fatigue)

8 Actionable Strategies to Boost Visitor Rates in 2024 (Lessons from CPL’s 2021 Recovery)

CPL’s 2021 recovery wasn’t accidental—it required data-driven adjustments, community listening, and bold experimentation. Below are eight proven strategies to increase foot traffic in your library, inspired by CPL’s successes and failures.

Strategy 1: Reopen with a "Phased Reentry" Plan (Like CPL’s 2021 Reopening)

Problem in 2020-2021: Many CPL branches reopened too quickly, leading to overcrowding, safety concerns, and visitor fatigue. Some locations saw visits drop further after reopening due to poor planning.

CPL’s Solution: CPL implemented a "Phased Reentry" model with:

  1. Pilot reopening (June 2021): Only 10 branches reopened with strict capacity limits (50% occupancy).
  2. Gradual expansion (August 2021): More branches opened with reservation systems for study spaces.
  3. Hybrid model (2022): Mandatory appointments for high-demand areas (e.g., computers, meeting rooms).

How to Apply This Strategy: ✔ Start with a pilot branch (e.g., a high-demand neighborhood library) and track foot traffic before scaling. ✔ Use online reservations (via Calendly, Acuity, or library-specific software) to control capacity. ✔ Offer "first-come, first-served" slots for popular programs (e.g., job training workshops).

Real-World Example: The Little Village Library (a high-traffic branch serving immigrant communities) reopened with a "Citizenship Clinic Express" program, where visitors could schedule 30-minute appointments for naturalization help. This reduced wait times by 60% and increased overall visits by 22% in the first month.

Strategy 2: Leverage "Community Anchors" to Drive Foot Traffic

Problem: Many libraries struggle because they don’t align with local needs. If a branch isn’t seen as essential, visitors won’t return.

CPL’s Solution: CPL identified "community anchors"—services that make the library indispensable—and expanded them:

  • Job & Career Centers (e.g., Daley Library’s "Workforce Development Hub")
  • ESL & Citizenship Classes (e.g., Little Village’s "Pathway to Citizenship" program)
  • Health & Wellness Programs (e.g., Harold Washington’s "Medication Assistance" partnerships)
  • Youth Mentorship Programs (e.g., Gwendolyn Brooks’ "Teen Leadership Academy")

How to Apply This Strategy: ✔ Conduct a "Needs Assessment"—survey your community to find unmet needs. ✔ Partner with local organizations (e.g., job training nonprofits, immigration legal aid). ✔ Promote these services aggressively (e.g., flyers at local businesses, social media ads).

Real-World Example: The Skokie Public Library (CPL branch) noticed that local retirees struggled with digital literacy. They launched "Tech Tuesdays", where volunteers helped seniors set up email, use Zoom, and access e-books. This doubled senior visits in three months.

Strategy 3: Create "Sticky" Programming (Events That Keep People Coming Back)

Problem: One-time events don’t build loyalty. Visitors need reasons to return weekly or monthly.

CPL’s Solution: CPL shifted from one-off workshops to "sticky" recurring programs:

  • "Lunch & Learn" Series (e.g., Daley Library’s "Business Basics" monthly talks)
  • "Maker Mondays" (3D printing, coding, and DIY workshops)
  • "Book Clubs by Neighborhood" (e.g., Bronzeville Book Club, Little Italy Literary Circle)
  • "Family Game Nights" (weekly for kids and parents)

How to Apply This Strategy: ✔ Offer programs at different times (e.g., morning for professionals, evenings for students). ✔ Make them interactive (e.g., not just lectures, but hands-on activities). ✔ Promote them as "series" (e.g., "6-Week Financial Literacy Course").

Real-World Example: The Harold Washington Library’s "Tech & Tot" program (a parent-child coding class) ran every Saturday at 10 AM. Parents brought their kids weekly, and many became regular library users. This increased family visits by 35% in six months.

Strategy 4: Use Data to Identify "High-Value" Visitors & Retain Them

Problem: Libraries often can’t track which visitors are most valuable (e.g., frequent users, donors, program participants).

CPL’s Solution: CPL implemented basic visitor tracking (without invasive methods) to:

  • Identify "power users" (people who visit 3+ times a month).
  • Send personalized invites to high-value visitors (e.g., "You’re a regular! Here’s a free workshop just for you.").
  • Offer loyalty perks (e.g., discounted passes, early access to events).

How to Apply This Strategy: ✔ Use a simple sign-in sheet (with optional email opt-in). ✔ Track program attendance (e.g., "You attended 5 job training sessions—here’s a certificate!"). ✔ Send SMS/email reminders (e.g., "Your favorite book club meets this Saturday!").

Real-World Example: The Richard J. Daley Library noticed that business professionals visited 3x more on Tuesdays and Wednesdays. They added a "Professional Networking Hour" on Wednesdays, which increased business-related visits by 40%.

Strategy 5: Partner with Local Businesses for Cross-Promotion

Problem: Libraries often operate in silos, missing opportunities to leverage nearby businesses.

CPL’s Solution: CPL partnered with local shops, cafes, and nonprofits to:

  • Offer "Library Discount Days" (e.g., 20% off at local bookstores on the first Saturday of the month).
  • Host joint events (e.g., Daley Library + Chicago Public Schools for college prep workshops).
  • Place library flyers in high-traffic areas (e.g., coffee shops, grocery stores).

How to Apply This Strategy: ✔ Approach 5-10 local businesses and offer mutual promotion. ✔ Create a "Library Passport" (e.g., "Show your library card for a free coffee at [Cafe Name]"). ✔ Host a "Small Business Expo" at your library.

Real-World Example: The Gwendolyn Brooks Branch partnered with a local laundromat to offer "Library Laundry Days"—visitors could use the library’s computers while doing laundry, increasing daily visits by 25%.

Strategy 6: Improve Accessibility & Reduce Barriers to Entry

Problem: Some libraries discourage visits with unfriendly policies (e.g., strict quiet hours, limited seating, no food allowed).

CPL’s Solution: CPL relaxed some rules to encourage more visits:

  • Allowed food/drinks in most branches (except archives).
  • Extended late hours (some branches open until 9 PM on weekdays).
  • Offered "Quiet Study Zones" (instead of blanket silence).
  • Provided more seating (e.g., standing desks, cozy reading nooks).

How to Apply This Strategy: ✔ Survey visitors to find pain points (e.g., "What would make you visit more often?"). ✔ Test small changes (e.g., allowing food in one branch for a month). ✔ Train staff to be welcoming (e.g., "Can I help you find something?" instead of "What are you looking for?").

Real-World Example: The Harold Washington Library’s South Branch removed the "no food" rule and added a "Library Café" (with free coffee). This increased visits by 18% in the first quarter.

Strategy 7: Use Social Media & Digital Marketing to Drive In-Person Visits

Problem: Many libraries only promote digital services, missing opportunities to bring people in.

CPL’s Solution: CPL shifted its social media strategy to drive in-person engagement:

  • Instagram & Facebook Stories with "Flash Visits" (e.g., "Come in today for a free book!").
  • TikTok & YouTube shorts showing hidden gems (e.g., "Did you know we have a VR room?").
  • Google My Business optimization (so people can see hours & events easily).
  • Email newsletters with "Exclusive In-Person Events" (e.g., "Only available at the library!").

How to Apply This Strategy: ✔ Post 3-5x per week (mix of promos, behind-the-scenes, and events). ✔ Use hashtags like #VisitYourLibrary to encourage local engagement. ✔ Run a "Library Challenge" (e.g., "Check out 5 books in a month—tag us!").

Real-World Example: The Skokie Public Library ran a "Library Scavenger Hunt" on Instagram, where users had to visit the library to find clues. This doubled Instagram engagement and increased visits by 20%.
