Chicago Public Libraries Visitor Rates in 2021: Trends, Insights, and Strategies for Growth

Introduction: A Deep Dive into Chicago Public Libraries’ 2021 Visitor Statistics

Chicago Public Libraries (CPL) is one of the largest and most influential public library systems in the United States, serving over 1.6 million residents across 79 branches in 2021. With a rich history dating back to 1873, CPL has always been a cornerstone of education, community engagement, and cultural enrichment. However, like many public institutions, the library faced unprecedented challenges in 2020 and 2021 due to the COVID-19 pandemic.

According to the Chicago Public Library Annual Report (2021), total in-person visits dropped by approximately 60% in 2020 compared to pre-pandemic levels, with a gradual recovery in 2021. By the end of 2021, CPL saw a 30% increase in foot traffic compared to 2020, though it remained about 25% below 2019 benchmarks. Digital engagement surged, with online program participation rising by 400% and e-book checkouts increasing by 150%.

This decline and rebound raise critical questions:

  • How did COVID-19 reshape library usage patterns?
  • Which branches saw the highest recovery in 2021?
  • What strategies can libraries use to attract more visitors post-pandemic?
  • How can data-driven decisions improve engagement?

In this comprehensive guide, we’ll break down Chicago Public Libraries’ 2021 visitor trends, analyze why certain branches performed better than others, and provide actionable strategies for libraries—whether in Chicago or beyond—to boost attendance, digital engagement, and community impact.

Chapter 1: The State of Chicago Public Libraries in 2021 – Key Statistics

1.1 Overall Visitor Trends: A Year of Recovery (But Not Full Restoration)

Before the pandemic, Chicago Public Libraries averaged over 10 million annual visits (2019 data). In 2020, this number plummeted to just 4 million, a 60% decrease. However, 2021 marked a slow but steady recovery:

Metric 2019 (Pre-Pandemic) 2020 (Pandemic Low) 2021 (Recovery Phase) % Change (2021 vs. 2020)
In-Person Visits ~10.2 million ~4.0 million ~5.6 million +40%
Digital Program Views ~1.2 million ~5.5 million ~7.8 million +42%
E-Book Checkouts ~500,000 ~1.2 million ~1.5 million +25%
Program Attendance ~250,000 ~100,000 ~180,000 +80%

Key Takeaway: While in-person visits were still down 45% from 2019, the digital shift was undeniable. Libraries that adapted quickly with virtual programs, expanded e-resources, and improved online accessibility saw higher retention rates in 2021.

1.2 Branch-Specific Performance: Which Libraries Led the Recovery?

Not all CPL branches recovered at the same pace. Some high-traffic urban libraries (like Harold Washington Library Center) saw faster rebounds, while rural and suburban branches struggled more due to limited public transit access and lower digital literacy.

Top 5 Highest-Recovery Branches in 2021

  1. Harold Washington Library Center (Downtown)

    • Pre-pandemic visits (2019): ~2.5 million
    • 2021 visits: ~1.8 million (-28% decline, but still the busiest branch)
    • Why? Central location, high digital adoption, and strong community events (e.g., job fairs, author talks).

  2. Richard J. Daley Library (South Loop)

    • 2019 visits: ~1.2 million
    • 2021 visits: ~900,000 (-25% decline, but strong recovery due to urban workforce)
    • Why? Proximity to corporate offices and student populations from nearby universities.

  3. Edwin D. Dursch Library (West Loop)

    • 2019 visits: ~800,000
    • 2021 visits: ~600,000 (-25% decline, but high digital engagement)
    • Why? Strong adult programming (language classes, tech workshops) and young professional demographic.

  4. South Shore Branch (South Side)

    • 2019 visits: ~450,000
    • 2021 visits: ~350,000 (-22% decline, but high community loyalty)
    • Why? Strong local partnerships with schools and nonprofits, free Wi-Fi initiatives, and bilingual services.

  5. North Branch (Lincoln Park)

    • 2019 visits: ~700,000
    • 2021 visits: ~500,000 (-29% decline, but high digital adoption)
    • Why? Tourist-friendly location, strong youth programs, and expanded e-book collections.

Branches with the Slowest Recovery (2021)

  • South Chicago Branch (-35% decline, limited public transit access)
  • Pullman Branch (-30% decline, older demographic with lower digital comfort)
  • Evergreen Park Branch (-28% decline, suburban commuter challenges)

Why the Disparity?

  • Urban branches recovered faster due to higher foot traffic from workers and students.
  • Suburban/rural branches struggled with lower digital engagement and less flexible hours.
  • Branches with strong community ties (e.g., South Shore) had better retention despite lower numbers.

While in-person visits dropped, digital usage skyrocketed in 2021:

Digital Metric 2020 2021 % Increase
E-Book Checkouts 1.2M 1.5M +25%
Online Program Views 5.5M 7.8M +42%
Digital Media Downloads 800K 1.1M +38%

Key Digital Strategies That Worked: ✅ Expanded e-library collections (more audiobooks, language learning apps). ✅ Live-streamed author readings and workshops (partnering with local bookstores). ✅ Mobile-friendly website redesign (faster load times, better search functionality). ✅ Partnerships with schools for virtual homework help.

Lesson for Other Libraries: If your library doesn’t have a strong digital presence, you’re losing a significant portion of your audience. Even post-pandemic, hybrid models (in-person + digital) are essential.

Chapter 2: Why Visitor Rates Dropped in 2020 – And How CPL Responded

2.1 The Pandemic’s Impact: Fear, Closures, and Behavioral Shifts

The COVID-19 pandemic forced Chicago Public Libraries to close for 18 months (March 2020 – September 2021), leading to:

  1. Immediate Drop in Visits (March–May 2020)

    • 90% decline in foot traffic as shelter-in-place orders took effect.
    • Essential workers (teachers, healthcare staff) still visited, but leisure users disappeared.

  2. Digital Survival Mode (June–December 2020)

    • CPL launched emergency digital programs (free Wi-Fi hotspots, e-book giveaways).
    • Virtual storytimes and adult learning classes became lifelines for families and seniors.

  3. Phased Reopening (2021) – But Not Full Recovery

    • Limited capacity, mask mandates, and reduced hours kept attendance down.
    • Students returning to school (August 2021) boosted youth visits, but adults remained hesitant.

Real-World Example: The Harold Washington Library’s Reopening Strategy When CPL reopened in September 2021, the Harold Washington Library Center implemented:

  • Pre-booking system (reservations for study rooms).
  • Enhanced cleaning protocols (UV sterilization, touchless checkouts).
  • Hybrid events (some in-person, some virtual).

Result: By December 2021, this branch saw a 35% increase in weekday visits compared to 2020.

2.2 Behavioral Shifts: How Library Users Changed Their Habits

The pandemic didn’t just reduce visits—it changed why people used libraries:

Old Habit (Pre-2020) New Habit (Post-2020) Why?
Borrowing physical books More e-books & audiobooks Convenience, no contact
In-person research help Online tutoring & chat support Remote work/study
Weekend leisure reading Weekday work/school visits Hybrid work schedules
Group study sessions Solo or small-group study rooms Social distancing
Attending in-person events Virtual workshops & webinars Safety concerns

Key Insight: Libraries that adapted to these shifts (e.g., more digital resources, flexible hours) retained users better than those that clung to traditional models.

Chapter 3: 10 Actionable Strategies to Boost Visitor Rates (Post-Pandemic)

Now that we’ve analyzed why visits dropped and how CPL recovered, let’s explore practical strategies to increase attendance, engagement, and digital adoption.

Strategy 1: Implement a Hybrid Model (In-Person + Digital)

Problem: Many users still prefer digital convenience, while others need physical access. Solution: Offer both options seamlessly.

How CPL Did It:

  • Expanded e-library (more languages, audiobooks, digital magazines).
  • Live-streamed events (author talks, book clubs).
  • Mobile app updates (better search, reservation system).

Action Step for Your Library: ✔ Audit your digital collection – Are you offering enough audiobooks, language apps, and streaming services? ✔ Train staff on digital tools – Ensure librarians can guide users on e-resources. ✔ Promote hybrid events – Example: A virtual book club with an in-person meetup option.

Real-World Example: The Newberry Library (Chicago) While not part of CPL, the Newberry Library (a research institution) launched "Digital Scholarship" programs post-pandemic, attracting academics and remote researchers who no longer needed to visit in person.

Strategy 2: Expand Community Partnerships for Cross-Promotion

Problem: Many people don’t realize libraries offer free resources. Solution: Partner with local businesses, schools, and nonprofits to promote library services.

How CPL Did It:

  • Collaborated with Chicago Public Schools for free homework help.
  • Worked with local bookstores (e.g., The Book Stall) for author events.
  • Partnered with tech companies (e.g., Google Career Certificates) for free workshops.

Action Step for Your Library: ✔ Identify local partners – Schools, community centers, businesses. ✔ Co-host events – Example: A library + gym partnership for fitness + reading challenges. ✔ Cross-promote services – Example: A local café offers 10% off if patrons show their library card.

Real-World Example: The Public Library of Cincinnati & Hamilton County They partnered with local museums to offer free museum passes with library cards, doubling foot traffic during off-peak hours.

Strategy 3: Offer Flexible Hours & Extended Access

Problem: Many users can’t visit during traditional library hours (9 AM–5 PM). Solution: **Extend hours, offer weekend access, and provide 24/7 digital access.

How CPL Did It:

  • Extended evening hours at Harold Washington Library (open until 9 PM on weekdays).
  • Weekend study sessions for students and remote workers.
  • 24/7 digital access (no time restrictions on e-books).

Action Step for Your Library: ✔ Survey your community – Ask: "What hours would work best for you?" ✔ Test extended hours – Example: Stay-open late on Thursdays for professionals. ✔ Offer "Silent Study Saturdays" – Attract college students and remote workers.

Real-World Example: The Brooklyn Public Library (NYC) They **extended hours at their Central Library to 10 PM on weekdays, seeing a 20% increase in weekday visits from young professionals.

Strategy 4: Create High-Engagement Programs (Not Just Books)

Problem: Many libraries only offer book checkouts, which doesn’t stand out in a digital world. Solution: Host interactive, experiential programs that draw crowds.

How CPL Did It:

  • Virtual escape rooms (for families).
  • Tech workshops (basic computer skills, coding for kids).
  • Language exchange meetups (bilingual communities).
  • Music & art classes (partnering with local artists).

Action Step for Your Library: ✔ Brainstorm "unusual" programs – Example: A "Library Escape Room" night. ✔ Partner with local experts – Example: A chef teaching cooking classes. ✔ Promote on social media – Example: "First Friday of the Month = Free Tech Workshop!"

Real-World Example: The San Francisco Public Library They hosted "SFPL Pop-Ups" – pop-up bookstores in parks, cafes, and markets, doubling attendance for certain events.
