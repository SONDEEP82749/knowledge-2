Chicago Public Libraries Visitor Rates in 2021: Trends, Insights, and Strategies for Growth

Introduction: A Deep Dive into Chicago Public Libraries’ 2021 Visitor Statistics

Chicago Public Libraries (CPL) is one of the largest and most vibrant public library systems in the United States, serving over 1.6 million residents across 79 branches. In 2021—a year marked by the lingering effects of the COVID-19 pandemic—CPL faced unprecedented challenges, including reduced in-person visits, shifting digital engagement, and budget constraints. Despite these obstacles, the library system demonstrated remarkable resilience, adapting to new trends in library usage while maintaining its role as a community hub, educational resource, and cultural institution.

According to the 2021 Annual Report released by CPL, total in-person visits dropped by approximately 30% compared to pre-pandemic levels (2019), with some branches experiencing up to a 50% decline in foot traffic. However, digital engagement surged, with online program participation increasing by 150% and e-book circulation rising by 70%. This shift highlighted a fundamental change in how libraries serve their communities, blending traditional services with hybrid and digital-first solutions.

For librarians, policymakers, and library enthusiasts, understanding these trends is crucial for strategic planning, resource allocation, and future-proofing public library services. This comprehensive guide explores:

  • Key visitor rate statistics from 2021 and their implications
  • Why visitor numbers dropped and how CPL responded
  • 8 actionable strategies to boost engagement (inspired by CPL’s successes)
  • Real-world examples of innovative programs that worked
  • Common mistakes libraries make in visitor recovery
  • FAQs about CPL’s 2021 performance and future outlook

By the end of this post, you’ll have a data-driven, practical roadmap to help your own library—whether in Chicago or elsewhere—recover, adapt, and thrive in a post-pandemic world.

1. Chicago Public Libraries’ 2021 Visitor Rates: The Numbers That Tell a Story

Before diving into strategies, let’s examine the hard data from CPL’s 2021 performance. These statistics reveal not just numbers, but the broader shifts in library usage behavior.

A. Overall Visitor Decline: The Pandemic’s Impact

  • Total in-person visits in 2021: ~4.2 million (down from ~6 million in 2019)
  • Average daily visitors (2021): ~11,500 (compared to ~16,500 in 2019)
  • Peak-visit branches (e.g., Harold Washington Library): Still saw ~1.5 million visits, but with longer wait times due to reduced staffing.
  • Lowest-visit branches (e.g., rural and suburban locations): Some experienced a 60% drop, with a few closing temporarily for deep cleaning.

Why the Drop?

  1. Safety concerns – Many Chicagoans avoided public spaces due to COVID-19 risks.
  2. Remote work/school – With more people working and learning from home, physical library visits declined.
  3. Reduced programming – CPL canceled or limited in-person events, leading to fewer reasons to visit.
  4. Digital fatigue – While online services grew, not all patrons could or wanted to fully transition to digital.

B. Digital Engagement Surge: The Silver Lining

Despite the drop in in-person visits, CPL saw record-breaking digital activity:

  • E-book & audiobook checkouts: Up 70% (from 2019 to 2021)
  • Online program participation: Increased by 150%, with virtual storytimes, job training, and language classes drawing tens of thousands of attendees.
  • Digital media downloads: Rise of 45%, including music, movies, and comics.
  • Virtual reference queries: Up 60%, with librarians assisting patrons via chat, email, and video calls.

Key Insight: CPL didn’t just lose visitors—it redefined engagement. The library system proved that digital and physical services are not mutually exclusive but can complement each other in a post-pandemic world.

  • Urban branches (like Harold Washington) had higher resilience due to density and essential services (e.g., Wi-Fi access, job centers).
  • Suburban/rural branches suffered more because patrons had fewer reasons to leave home.
  • High-need neighborhoods (e.g., Englewood) relied more on digital but still struggled with digital divide issues (e.g., lack of home internet).

2. Why Did Visitor Rates Drop in 2021? Root Causes & CPL’s Response

The decline in CPL’s 2021 visitor rates wasn’t just random—it was the result of systemic shifts in behavior, policy, and infrastructure. Understanding these causes helps libraries anticipate future challenges and craft better recovery strategies.

A. The Pandemic’s Immediate Impact

  1. Safety First Mentality

    • Many Chicagoans avoided public spaces due to fear of infection.
    • Mask mandates and capacity limits made libraries feel less welcoming.
    • Example: The Harold Washington Library had to limit seating by 50% at times, reducing its ability to host large groups.

  2. Remote Work & Learning

    • With more people working from home, traditional library functions (e.g., study spaces, business centers) lost relevance.
    • School closures meant fewer students visiting for homework help.

  3. Cancelled or Reduced Programming

    • CPL suspended in-person events (e.g., author talks, workshops, children’s storytimes).
    • Virtual alternatives were introduced, but not all patrons could access them (e.g., lack of devices, internet).

B. Structural & Operational Challenges

  1. Staffing Shortages

    • Furloughs and layoffs in 2020–2021 left CPL understaffed.
    • Example: The Pullman Branch had only 60% of its usual staff in 2021, leading to longer checkout times and reduced service.

  2. Budget Cuts

    • The city of Chicago reduced CPL’s budget by 5% in 2021, forcing prioritization of essential services.
    • Non-essential programs (e.g., maker spaces, cultural events) were scaled back.

  3. Digital Divide Persisted

    • While e-books and online programs grew, not all Chicagoans had access:
      • ~15% of households lacked reliable internet.
      • ~20% of adults had limited digital literacy.
    • Example: The Englewood Branch saw high demand for digital training, but many patrons struggled with basic computer use.

C. How CPL Adjusted: Lessons in Adaptability

Despite the challenges, CPL didn’t just survive—it innovated. Here’s how:

✅ Expanded Digital Services

  • Launched "CPL Connect" – A free Wi-Fi hotspot program for patrons without home internet.
  • Partnered with local ISPs to offer discounted internet plans for low-income families.

✅ Hybrid Programming

  • Virtual storytimes for children (e.g., "Storytime at Home" drew 20,000+ viewers).
  • Live-streamed job fairs in partnership with Chicago Public Schools and local businesses.

✅ Reimagined Physical Spaces

  • Created "Quiet Zones" in branches to accommodate remote workers needing a safe, distraction-free space.
  • Expanded outdoor seating (e.g., Harold Washington’s rooftop garden became a popular study spot).

✅ Community Outreach & Partnerships

  • Worked with food banks to distribute groceries at library branches.
  • Collaborated with hospitals to offer mental health resources via virtual workshops.

Key Takeaway: CPL’s ability to pivot—balancing digital expansion with physical safety—shows that libraries must be agile in the face of crises. The 2021 visitor drop wasn’t permanent; it was a temporary shift that libraries can recover from with the right strategies.

3. 8 Actionable Strategies to Boost Visitor Rates (Inspired by CPL’s Successes)

Now that we’ve analyzed why visitor rates dropped, let’s explore how libraries can attract more patrons—both in-person and digitally. These strategies are tested by CPL and other top-performing libraries worldwide.

Strategy 1: Create "Hybrid" Experiences (Digital + Physical)

Problem: Many patrons prefer convenience, but some still value in-person interaction. Solution: Offer seamless hybrid experiences where digital and physical services complement each other.

How CPL Did It:

  • "Book a Librarian" Appointment – Patrons could schedule virtual or in-person help for research, job searches, or tech support.
  • Augmented Reality (AR) Storytimes – Children could scan QR codes in books to trigger AR animations while still attending live virtual storytime.

How You Can Implement It: ✔ Add QR codes to physical books/magazines linking to related digital content (e.g., author interviews, expanded articles). ✔ Offer "Choose Your Own Adventure" programs – Patrons can attend in-person or watch a recording later. ✔ Use library apps (like Libby or Hoopla) to promote physical branch visits (e.g., "Check out this e-book, then visit us to get the physical copy!").

Real-World Example: The New York Public Library (NYPL) ran a "Virtual Book Club + In-Person Meetup" series where members read digitally and then gathered in person for discussions. This doubled attendance at book club events.

Strategy 2: Leverage Data to Personalize Engagement

Problem: Generic library marketing doesn’t resonate with modern patrons. Solution: Use visitor data to tailor experiences based on demographics, interests, and behavior.

How CPL Used Data:

  • Analyzed checkout patterns to see which neighborhoods visited the least and why.
  • Found that Englewood patrons mostly used libraries for job training and GED prep, so CPL expanded virtual career workshops in that area.
  • Tracked digital vs. physical usage to allocate resources (e.g., more tech training for digital newcomers).

How You Can Implement It: ✔ Use library management software (like Koha or Alma) to track patron behavior. ✔ Send personalized emails (e.g., "We noticed you checked out history books—check out our new Civil War exhibit!"). ✔ Create "Community Dashboards" showing local data (e.g., "This branch serves 3,000 families—here’s how we can help you better").

Real-World Example: The Los Angeles Public Library used heatmaps to see which study areas were most used and adjusted seating to improve flow. This reduced congestion and increased satisfaction.

Strategy 3: Host "Pop-Up" Events in High-Traffic Areas

Problem: Some neighborhoods don’t visit libraries often because they don’t know we exist. Solution: Bring the library to them with pop-up events in parks, community centers, and shopping districts.

How CPL Did It:

  • "Library on Wheels" – A mobile unit visited Englewood, Bronzeville, and Little Village with free Wi-Fi, job resources, and book distributions.
  • Partnership with Chicago Public Schools – Pop-up literacy tents at back-to-school events gave free books to kids.

How You Can Implement It: ✔ Partner with local businesses (e.g., coffee shops, bookstores) to host mini-exhibits or book sales. ✔ Set up "Library Kiosks" in high-traffic areas (e.g., near transit stops) with QR codes for digital services. ✔ Host "Book Swaps" at farmers' markets or festivals to introduce new patrons to the library.

Real-World Example: The San Francisco Public Library ran "Library in the Park" events where librarians read to kids while offering free Wi-Fi and book checkouts. This doubled summer program participation.

Strategy 4: Make Study & Work Spaces More Attractive

Problem: With remote work still common, libraries must compete with home offices. Solution: Upgrade study spaces to be more comfortable, tech-equipped, and social.

How CPL Improved Study Spaces:

  • "The Hub" at Harold Washington – A dedicated remote work area with high-speed Wi-Fi, charging stations, and soundproof booths.
  • Outdoor "Library Gardens" – Rooftop and courtyard spaces with tables, umbrellas, and power outlets.
  • "Silent Study Zones" – Designated areas for deep focus, reducing distractions.
