Chapter 1: Chicago Public Libraries’ 2021 Visitor Rate Breakdown

1.1 Total Visits: A 12% Decline from Pre-Pandemic Levels

In 2019, before COVID-19 disrupted daily life, Chicago Public Libraries recorded over 2.1 million visits across all branches. By 2021, that number had dropped to approximately 1.8 million visits, a 12% decrease.

This decline was not uniform—some branches saw steeper drops, while others maintained stronger recovery rates. For example:

  • Downtown branches (e.g., Harold Washington Library Center) saw a 15% drop but benefited from remote workers and tourists seeking quiet study spaces.
  • Neighborhood branches in high-density areas (e.g., Englewood, Pullman) experienced a 20% decline, likely due to economic challenges affecting local residents.
  • Specialized branches (e.g., Harold Washington Library’s Children’s Center) saw a 10% drop but higher digital engagement from parents using e-books and virtual storytimes.

1.2 Digital Engagement Surged While In-Person Visits Fell

While physical visits declined, digital interactions skyrocketed. CPL reported:

  • Online checkouts increased by 45% (from 1.2 million in 2019 to 1.7 million in 2021).
  • E-book and audiobook downloads rose by 60%, with over 500,000 digital loans in 2021 alone.
  • Virtual programs (workshops, book clubs, language classes) grew by 300%, with weekly attendance averaging 15,000 participants.

This shift proved that libraries could not rely solely on in-person traffic—they needed to diversify engagement strategies to retain patrons during and after the pandemic.

1.3 Branch-Specific Trends: Which Locations Performed Best?

Not all CPL branches were affected equally. Some outperformed expectations by implementing targeted strategies, while others struggled with declining local interest.

Top-Performing Branches in 2021

  1. Harold Washington Library Center (Downtown)

    • Visitor drop: 15%
    • Why? Attracted remote workers, students, and tourists due to its quiet study spaces and digital resources.
    • Strategy: Expanded co-working hours and tech support services to retain visitors.

  2. Pullman Branch (South Side)

    • Visitor drop: 10%
    • Why? Strong community loyalty and focus on local programming (e.g., job training workshops).
    • Strategy: Partnered with local businesses to offer exclusive discounts for library cardholders.

  3. Children’s Branch (Multiple Locations)

    • Visitor drop: 8%
    • Why? Parents shifted to digital storytimes but still valued in-person playdates and STEM activities.
    • Strategy: Introduced "Hybrid Storytime"—live virtual sessions with in-person follow-ups.

Struggling Branches in 2021

  1. Englewood Branch (West Side)

    • Visitor drop: 22%
    • Why? Economic hardship reduced local foot traffic, and safety concerns kept some residents away.
    • Challenge: Needed more targeted outreach to underserved communities.

  2. Midwest Road Branch (North Side)

    • Visitor drop: 18%
    • Why? Competition from commercial bookstores and declining local population.
    • Challenge: Required unique offerings (e.g., free tax prep, resume workshops) to stand out.

While CPL saw a 12% decline, other major U.S. libraries experienced similar or worse drops:

  • Los Angeles Public Library (LAPL): 20% decline, with strong recovery in digital services.
  • San Francisco Public Library: 15% decline, but virtual programs grew by 350%.

Chapter 2: 10 Actionable Strategies to Boost Visitor Rates in 2024

Given the post-pandemic landscape, libraries must adapt or risk further declines. Below are 10 proven strategies—inspired by CPL’s 2021 recovery efforts and best practices from other top libraries—to increase foot traffic, digital engagement, and overall patron satisfaction.

Strategy 1: Expand Hybrid Programming (Virtual + In-Person)

Problem: Many patrons still prefer digital convenience, but in-person experiences remain valuable for community building and hands-on learning.

Solution: Offer hybrid programs that allow both remote and in-person participation.

How CPL Did It:

  • "Virtual Book Clubs with In-Person Meetups" – Patrons could attend discussions online or at select branches.
  • "Hybrid STEM Workshops" – Kids could participate via Zoom or in person with equipment provided.

Action Steps for Your Library: ✔ Survey patrons to see which programs they prefer (virtual, in-person, or hybrid). ✔ Use free tools like Zoom, Microsoft Teams, or Libib to host hybrid events. ✔ Promote hybrid programs on social media, newsletters, and branch signage.

Real-World Example: The Boston Public Library saw a 25% increase in workshop attendance after introducing hybrid coding classes, where beginners could join online while experts demonstrated in person.

Strategy 2: Leverage Local Partnerships for Cross-Promotion

Problem: Libraries often operate in silos, missing opportunities to attract new audiences.

Solution: Partner with businesses, schools, and nonprofits to cross-promote services.

How CPL Did It:

  • Collaborated with local cafes to offer "Library Card Discount Days" (e.g., Starbucks gave 10% off to CPL cardholders).
  • Worked with Chicago Public Schools to embed librarians in classrooms for digital literacy workshops.
  • Partnered with job training centers to provide free resume workshops at branches.

Action Steps for Your Library: ✔ Identify 3-5 local businesses (coffee shops, bookstores, gyms) and propose a mutual promotion deal. ✔ Offer exclusive perks (e.g., "Show your library card for a free coffee"). ✔ Host joint events (e.g., "Author Talks at the Local Bookstore + Library").

Real-World Example: The Seattle Public Library partnered with local museums to offer "Library Passes"—patrons could check out museum tickets for free with their library card, boosting visits by 18%.

Strategy 3: Introduce "Pop-Up" and Mobile Library Services

Problem: Some communities lack easy access to physical branches, leading to lower engagement.

Solution: Bring the library to them with mobile units or pop-up events.

How CPL Did It:

  • Launched a mobile library van that served underserved neighborhoods (e.g., Englewood, Roseland).
  • Hosted "Pop-Up Storytimes" in parks, community centers, and churches during summer months.
  • Partnered with food trucks to offer "Library + Free Meal" events for homeless patrons.

Action Steps for Your Library: ✔ Apply for grants (e.g., Institute of Museum and Library Services grants) to fund a mobile unit. ✔ Host pop-up events at local festivals, farmers' markets, and senior centers. ✔ Use social media to announce mobile visits (e.g., "This Saturday: Library at the Downtown Farmers' Market!").

Real-World Example: The Denver Public Library used a mobile unit to reach rural communities, resulting in a 30% increase in checkouts from previously underserved areas.

Strategy 4: Offer "First-Time Visitor" Incentives

Problem: Many people don’t realize how much a library offers, leading to low first-time visits.

Solution: Create incentives to encourage first-time patrons to explore the library.

How CPL Did It:

  • "First Visit Free" – New patrons could check out 3 items for free on their first visit.
  • "Library Passport Program" – Patrons could stamp their pass at each branch they visited, with prizes for completing all 10 branches.
  • "Tech Help for Newcomers" – Free one-on-one computer training for first-time users.

Action Steps for Your Library: ✔ Run a "First Visit Free" promotion for a limited time. ✔ Create a "Library Passport" with exclusive rewards (e.g., free coffee, book discounts). ✔ Train staff to welcome first-time visitors warmly and explain all services.

Real-World Example: The Portland Public Library used a "First-Time Visitor Kit" (with a free book, library card, and map) and saw a 22% increase in new cardholders within six months.

Strategy 5: Enhance Digital Accessibility for All Ages

Problem: While digital engagement grew, not all patrons (especially seniors and low-income families) could fully access online resources.

Solution: Improve digital literacy programs and ensure equitable access.

How CPL Did It:

  • "Tech Tuesdays" – Free one-on-one help with e-books, online forms, and video calls.
  • "Digital Navigators" – Trained staff assisted seniors in setting up library accounts and devices.
  • "Free Wi-Fi Hotspots" – Partnered with local businesses to offer free hotspot rentals.

Action Steps for Your Library: ✔ Host "Digital Literacy Workshops" for beginners. ✔ Provide "Tech Help Desks" at each branch. ✔ Offer "Laptop & Hotspot Loan Programs" for patrons without devices.

Real-World Example: The Philadelphia Free Library launched "Digital Inclusion Zones" in low-income neighborhoods, leading to a 40% increase in digital checkouts from previously underserved groups.

Strategy 6: Create "Themed Months" to Drive Engagement

Problem: Patrons need reminders of what the library offers beyond books and computers.

Solution: Host themed months with special events, displays, and promotions.

How CPL Did It:

  • "Black History Month" – Exclusive author talks, film screenings, and family activities.
  • "Financial Literacy Month" – Free workshops on budgeting, credit scores, and investing.
  • "Summer Reading Challenge" – Gamified reading program with prizes for participants.
