Chicago Public Libraries Visitor Rates in 2021: Trends, Insights, and Strategies for Growth

Introduction: A Deep Dive into Chicago Public Libraries’ 2021 Visitor Trends

Chicago Public Libraries (CPL) is one of the largest and most vibrant public library systems in the United States, serving over 1.7 million residents across 79 branches. In 2021—a year marked by the lingering effects of the COVID-19 pandemic—CPL faced unprecedented challenges while also adapting to new digital-first trends.

According to the Chicago Public Library Annual Report (2021), total in-person visits dropped by 30% compared to 2019, reflecting broader national trends where library usage shifted toward online resources, e-books, and virtual programming. However, CPL also saw a 25% increase in digital engagement, with over 5 million downloads of e-books and audiobooks alone.

1. Chicago Public Libraries’ 2021 Visitor Statistics: A Closer Look

Before diving into strategies, let’s examine the hard numbers that defined CPL’s 2021 performance.

A. In-Person Visits: A 30% Decline from Pre-Pandemic Levels

  • 2019 (Pre-Pandemic): CPL reported ~12 million in-person visits across all branches.
  • 2020 (Early Pandemic): Visits plummeted to ~8.5 million (a 30% drop).
  • 2021 (Recovery Phase): Visits slightly rebounded to ~9.2 million, but still 23% below 2019 levels.

Key Observations:

  • Urban branches (e.g., Harold Washington Library, Harold Interlibrary Loan Center) saw the steepest declines due to remote work trends.
  • Suburban and neighborhood libraries (e.g., South Shore Branch, Pullman Branch) maintained higher retention because they served as community hubs for essential services like Wi-Fi, food pantries, and job centers.
  • Children’s and teen programs remained resilient, with preschool storytimes and homework help sessions seeing consistent attendance even during lockdowns.

B. Digital Engagement: A 25% Surge in Online Activity

While in-person visits dropped, CPL’s digital footprint expanded significantly:

  • E-book & audiobook downloads: 5 million+ (up from 3.5 million in 2019).
  • Online course enrollments (via LinkedIn Learning, Coursera): 120,000+ (a 40% increase).
  • Virtual events & webinars: 200,000+ attendees across 1,500+ sessions.
  • Library card registrations: 15,000 new cards issued digitally (a 20% rise in remote sign-ups).

Why the Shift to Digital?

  1. Remote work & learning made physical visits less convenient.
  2. Parents with young children preferred at-home learning resources.
  3. Tech-savvy patrons embraced mobile-friendly library apps (e.g., Libby, Hoopla).
  4. Limited branch capacity during COVID-19 forced libraries to prioritize digital alternatives.

C. Branch-Specific Performance: Which Libraries Thrived?

Not all CPL branches experienced the same decline. Some outperformed expectations by leveraging local community needs:

Branch 2019 Visits 2021 Visits Key Growth Driver
Harold Washington Library 2.5M 1.8M (-28%) Digital literacy programs & pop-up tech clinics
South Shore Branch 350K 420K (+19%) Food pantry & job training partnerships
Pullman Branch 280K 310K (+11%) Bilingual storytime & immigrant support services
Newberry Library (Special Collections) 150K 120K (-20%) Limited in-person access due to rare book restrictions

Takeaway: Libraries that adapted to local needs (food security, language access, career services) retained visitors better than those that relied solely on traditional book lending.

2. Why Did Chicago Public Libraries See Such a Drop in 2021?

Before brainstorming solutions, it’s crucial to understand why visitor rates declined. Several external and internal factors contributed:

A. The Pandemic’s Lingering Impact

  • Social distancing policies limited branch capacity.
  • Mask mandates reduced comfort for some patrons.
  • Fear of illness kept many from returning to crowded spaces.

B. Changing Patron Behavior

  1. Remote Work & Hybrid Models

    • Many Chicagoans no longer needed office spaces, reducing commute-related library visits.
    • Home offices became the norm, making physical library trips less frequent.

  2. The Rise of Digital Alternatives

    • E-books, audiobooks, and streaming services (Netflix, Spotify) reduced reliance on physical collections.
    • Google & Wikipedia made traditional research less necessary for casual users.

  3. Shift in Educational Priorities

    • K-12 schools moved online, reducing after-school library visits.
    • Higher education institutions shifted to digital databases (JSTOR, ProQuest).

  4. Economic Uncertainty

    • Budget cuts led some patrons to prioritize essentials over leisure reading.
    • Job losses reduced discretionary spending on library memberships.

C. CPL’s Own Challenges

  • Limited Staffing: Many librarians were furloughed or reassigned to digital initiatives.
  • Outdated Tech Infrastructure: Some branches lack Wi-Fi upgrades, making them less attractive for remote work.
  • Lack of Clear Digital Strategy: While CPL expanded online offerings, marketing efforts were inconsistent, leading to low awareness among potential users.

Real-World Example: The Chicago Public Library’s "Library Anywhere" initiative (launched in 2020) provided free Wi-Fi hotspots for low-income neighborhoods. However, only 30% of eligible patrons were aware of the program, showing a gap in communication.

3. 10 Actionable Strategies to Boost Visitor Rates (Lessons from CPL & Beyond)

Now that we’ve analyzed the why, let’s explore proven strategies to increase foot traffic and digital engagement—inspired by CPL’s successes and best practices from other top libraries.

Strategy 1: Leverage Data-Driven Branch Planning

Problem: Some CPL branches struggled because they didn’t align services with local needs.

Solution: Use patron data to optimize branch offerings.

How CPL Did It:

  • Analyzed ZIP code-level visit patterns and found that South Side branches had higher retention because they offered job training workshops.
  • Redesigned the South Shore Branch to include a career center with free resume workshops, leading to a 19% visit increase in 2021.

Actionable Steps for Your Library: ✅ Conduct patron surveys (use Google Forms or SurveyMonkey) to ask:

  • "What services would make you visit more often?"
  • "Do you prefer in-person or digital resources?" ✅ Track foot traffic by time of day (e.g., after-school vs. weekend visits) and adjust staffing schedules accordingly. ✅ Partner with local businesses to offer co-branded programs (e.g., a library + gym collaboration for fitness classes).

Real-World Example: The New York Public Library (NYPL) used heatmaps to determine that branch entrances were poorly placed, leading to lower-than-expected traffic. After relocating checkouts near main doors, they saw a 15% increase in first-time visitors.

Strategy 2: Expand Digital Accessibility (Without Sacrificing In-Person Value)

Problem: Many patrons prefer digital convenience, but libraries can’t replace physical spaces entirely.

Solution: Enhance digital offerings while keeping in-person experiences unique.

How CPL Did It:

  • Launched "Library Anywhere", providing free Wi-Fi, hotspots, and laptop rentals for job seekers.
  • Partnered with LinkedIn Learning to offer free professional development courses, attracting working professionals.

Actionable Steps: ✅ Invest in a user-friendly digital platform (e.g., Libby, Hoopla, OverDrive) and promote it aggressively via:

  • Social media ads (Facebook, Instagram, TikTok)
  • Email newsletters with tutorials (e.g., "How to Download an E-Book in 3 Steps") ✅ Offer "Hybrid" programs (e.g., in-person book clubs with virtual attendance options). ✅ Train staff on digital literacy so they can assist patrons with tech issues (e.g., setting up e-readers).

Common Mistake: Assuming all patrons want digital access. Some older adults or low-tech users still prefer physical books and in-person help.

How to Avoid It:

  • Provide both options (e.g., "Ask a Librarian" chat + in-person reference desk).
  • Host "Tech Tuesdays" where librarians demonstrate digital tools in a low-pressure environment.

Strategy 3: Create "Sticky" Community Programs (Not Just Events)

Problem: Many libraries host one-off events that don’t build long-term engagement.

Solution: Develop recurring, high-value programs that keep patrons coming back.

How CPL Did It:

  • "Little Free Libraries" in Neighborhoods – Small book-sharing boxes in high-traffic areas (parks, grocery stores) increased visibility.
  • "Storytime for All Ages" – A weekly program that attracted families, leading to higher youth retention.

Actionable Steps: ✅ Identify "pain points" in your community (e.g., parenting struggles, job hunting, language barriers) and design programs around them. ✅ Partner with local nonprofits to co-host workshops (e.g., a library + food bank collaboration for nutrition classes). ✅ Gamify engagement (e.g., "Read 10 Books, Get a Free Coffee" at a local café).

Real-World Example: The San Francisco Public Library ran "SFPL Reads"—a monthly book club with author talks and themed discussions. This drove consistent attendance and increased digital sign-ups for the program.

Strategy 4: Optimize Branch Layouts for Modern Patrons

Problem: Many libraries still have outdated layouts that don’t accommodate remote work, family visits, or tech needs.

Solution: **Redesign spaces to be multi-functional and patron-friendly.

How CPL Did It:

  • Added "Quiet Zones" with noise-canceling booths for remote workers.
  • Created "Family Lounges" with comfy seating, charging stations, and kids’ activity tables.
  • **Installed touchless checkouts to reduce wait times.

Actionable Steps: ✅ Conduct a "space audit"—ask patrons:

  • "What’s missing in our library?"
  • "Do you prefer open seating or private study carrels?" ✅ Dedicate areas for:
  • Remote work (with power outlets & ergonomic chairs)
  • Parenting & childcare (e.g., play areas, breastfeeding rooms)
  • Tech hubs (with 3D printers, coding stations) ✅ Use modular furniture that can be rearranged for different events.

Common Mistake: Ignoring accessibility needs (e.g., no wheelchair ramps, poor lighting for dyslexic readers).

How to Avoid It:

  • Follow ADA guidelines for physical accessibility.
  • Offer large-print books & audiobooks for visually impaired patrons.

Strategy 5: Partner with Local Businesses for Cross-Promotion

Problem: Libraries often operate in silos, missing opportunities to leverage local partnerships.

Solution: Collaborate with businesses, schools, and nonprofits to expand reach.

How CPL Did It:

  • "Library + Starbucks" Deal – Patrons could show their library card for a free drink at select Starbucks locations.
  • "Job Fair Fridays" – Partnered with Chicago Public Schools to host resume workshops for teens.

Actionable Steps: ✅ Identify complementary businesses (e.g., bookstores, gyms, co-working spaces) and propose co-branded promotions. ✅ Offer discounts or free trials to library patrons (e.g., "Show your CPL card for 10% off at [Local Bookstore]"). ✅ Host "Library + [Business] Pop-Ups" (e.g., a book signing at a café).

Real-World Example: The Los Angeles Public Library partnered with Uber to offer free rides to library events for low-income patrons, boosting attendance by 30%.

Strategy 6: Use Social Media & Content Marketing to Drive Traffic

Problem: Many libraries underutilize social media, leading to low digital engagement.

Solution: **Develop a content strategy that educates, entertains, and drives visits.

How CPL Did It:

  • TikTok & Instagram Reels – Short videos like "5 Hidden Gems in Our Collection" and "How to Find Free Online Courses."
  • YouTube Tutorials – Step-by-step guides on using library databases.
  • Twitter Polls – "What should we host next? A book club or a coding workshop?"

Actionable Steps: ✅ **Post 3-5x per week with a mix of:

  • Educational content (e.g., "How to Research for a College Paper")
  • Behind-the-scenes looks (e.g., "Meet Our Rare Book Curator")
  • Promotions (e.g., "Free Tech Help Every Wednesday!") ✅ Engage with local influencers (e.g., bookstagrammers, parenting bloggers) to promote library events. ✅ Run contests (e.g., "Tag a friend who loves books for a chance to win a free book!").
