Chicago Public Libraries Visitor Rates in 2021: A Deep Dive into Attendance Trends, Strategies for Growth, and Key Takeaways

Introduction: Understanding the Impact of COVID-19 on Chicago Public Library Visitor Rates in 2021

The Chicago Public Library (CPL) system is one of the largest and most influential public library networks in the United States, serving over 1.7 million residents across 80+ branches. Before the COVID-19 pandemic, CPL was experiencing steady growth in visitor rates, with over 12 million visits annually in 2019. However, the global health crisis of 2020 and 2021 disrupted this trajectory, forcing libraries to adapt to new challenges—social distancing, limited capacity, and shifting patron behaviors.

In 2021, CPL saw a significant rebound in attendance, though not yet to pre-pandemic levels. According to the Chicago Public Library Annual Report (2021), total visits reached approximately 8.5 million, a 30% increase from 2020 but still 30% below 2019 figures. This recovery was uneven across branches, with urban hubs like the Newberry Library and Harold Washington Library Center leading in foot traffic, while smaller neighborhood branches struggled to regain lost visitors.

For library professionals, researchers, and policymakers, understanding these trends is crucial. Whether you're analyzing library performance metrics, planning reopening strategies, or exploring digital engagement alternatives, the 2021 visitor data provides valuable insights.

In this comprehensive guide, we’ll break down: ✅ The 2021 visitor rate breakdown by branch type and demographic ✅ Key factors influencing recovery (vaccination rates, remote work trends, digital adoption) ✅ 8 actionable strategies to boost attendance in 2024 and beyond ✅ Real-world examples of successful reopening tactics from CPL and other major libraries ✅ Common mistakes libraries made in 2021—and how to avoid them ✅ FAQs on visitor analytics, digital vs. in-person engagement, and future projections

By the end, you’ll have a data-driven roadmap to improve your library’s visitor rates, whether you're managing a small branch or overseeing a city-wide system like CPL.

Before diving into strategies, let’s examine the hard numbers from CPL’s 2021 performance.

Key Takeaway:

  • 2020 was the worst year on record, with many branches operating at 20-30% capacity.
  • 2021 showed resilience, with urban branches recovering faster than suburban or rural ones.
  • Children and teens made up ~40% of pre-pandemic visits but only ~30% in 2021, suggesting parental hesitation in bringing kids back early.

  • Specialty libraries (e.g., law, music) had niche audiences that were slower to return, possibly due to less urgent need for in-person services.

  • Schools remained hybrid/remote, reducing the need for after-school library visits.

  • Virtual programs (e.g., storytime, homework help) kept some families engaged online.

2. What Caused the 2021 Recovery? Key Factors Behind the 39% Increase

The 39% jump in 2021 wasn’t accidental—it was driven by strategic adjustments by CPL and changing patron behaviors. Here are the top 5 factors:

A. Vaccination Rollouts and Perceived Safety

  • By mid-2021, ~60% of Chicagoans were fully vaccinated, reducing fear of infection.
  • CPL prioritized safety with:
    • Mandatory mask policies (even for vaccinated patrons).
    • Enhanced cleaning protocols (UV sanitizers, touchless checkouts).
    • Capacity limits (e.g., 50% occupancy in some branches).
  • Result: Trust in libraries rebounded, especially among adults and seniors.

B. Hybrid and Digital-First Services

CPL didn’t just wait for people to return—it met them where they were with:

  • Expanded digital lending (over 1 million e-books and audiobooks checked out in 2021).
  • Virtual events (author talks, job fairs, language classes).
  • Mobile app improvements (better reservation systems, contactless checkouts).
  • Result: Digital engagement grew by 45%, ensuring some revenue and service continuity.

C. Government and Corporate Partnerships

CPL secured funding and resources to sustain operations:

  • $10 million in federal CARES Act funding (used for staffing, tech upgrades, and reopening costs).
  • Partnerships with local businesses (e.g., Chicago Public Schools for after-school programs).
  • Corporate sponsorships (e.g., Boeing and Walgreens donated free Wi-Fi hotspots).
  • Result: More programs, better tech, and sustained services even during low attendance.

D. Targeted Reopening Strategies

Not all branches reopened at the same time. CPL used a phased approach:

  1. Phase 1 (Summer 2020): Outdoor reading rooms and drive-thru services.
  2. Phase 2 (Fall 2020): Limited in-person access (appointments only).
  3. Phase 3 (Spring 2021): Gradual capacity increases (starting with 25% occupancy).
  4. Phase 4 (Summer 2021): Near-full capacity (with vaccine verification for some events).
  • Result: Urban branches recovered faster due to higher demand for services.

E. Economic and Social Factors

  • Remote work trends: Many Chicagoans stayed home longer, reducing daily library trips.
  • Inflation and cost of living: Some patrons cut discretionary spending, leading to fewer non-essential visits.
  • Hybrid schooling: Parents with school-aged kids had less time to visit libraries.
  • Result: Adults (especially professionals) drove recovery, while families remained hesitant.

3. 8 Actionable Strategies to Boost Visitor Rates in 2024 (Lessons from CPL’s 2021 Success)

If you’re looking to increase visitor rates in your own library (or plan for post-pandemic recovery), CPL’s 2021 strategies offer proven tactics. Below are 8 high-impact approaches, ranked by effectiveness.

Strategy 1: Implement a Phased Reopening Plan (Like CPL Did)

Why it works: Gradual reopenings reduce panic and allow staff to adjust to new safety protocols.

How CPL Did It:

  • Started with high-demand branches (e.g., Harold Washington Library) first.
  • Used appointment systems to control crowds.
  • Monitored attendance trends and adjusted capacity weekly.

How to Apply This: ✔ Assess your branch’s risk level (urban vs. suburban vs. rural). ✔ Prioritize high-traffic services (e.g., job centers, computer labs). ✔ Use a tiered reopening system (e.g., Phase 1: 25% capacity, Phase 2: 50%, Phase 3: 75%). ✔ Track recovery metrics (e.g., weekly visit counts, event attendance).

Real-World Example: The New York Public Library (NYPL) used a similar phased approach, with Central Branch reopening in stages based on vaccination rates. By summer 2021, NYPL saw a 40% increase in visits compared to 2020.

Strategy 2: Expand Digital-First Services (But Keep In-Person Options)

Why it works: Not everyone will return immediately—some patrons prefer digital convenience.

How CPL Did It:

  • Launched "CPL at Home" (free digital resources, including e-books, music, and language courses).
  • Partnered with OverDrive and Libby to increase e-lending by 50%.
  • Offered virtual events (e.g., book clubs, career workshops).

How to Apply This: ✔ Audit your digital offerings—do you have enough e-books, audiobooks, and streaming services? ✔ Promote digital access via social media, email newsletters, and local partnerships. ✔ Keep in-person services (e.g., computer labs, printing, study spaces) to attract those who need them. ✔ Use data to see which services drive the most engagement (e.g., job training vs. kids’ programs).

Real-World Example: The Los Angeles Public Library (LAPL) saw a 35% increase in digital checkouts in 2021. They expanded their "LAPL Unlimited" program, offering free access to 10,000+ e-books and audiobooks, which kept patrons engaged even when branches were closed.

Strategy 3: Partner with Local Businesses and Nonprofits

Why it works: Collaborations bring in new audiences and funding for programs.

How CPL Did It:

  • Worked with Chicago Public Schools to offer after-school tutoring.
  • Partnered with local tech companies (e.g., Google, Microsoft) for digital literacy workshops.
  • Collaborated with nonprofits (e.g., United Way, YMCA) to host joint events.

How to Apply This: ✔ Identify local businesses that align with your library’s mission (e.g., bookstores, tech firms, job training centers). ✔ Offer co-branded programs (e.g., "Tech Skills for Job Seekers" with a local IT company). ✔ Apply for grants to fund joint initiatives (e.g., workforce development programs). ✔ Promote partnerships in local media and social media to attract new visitors.

Real-World Example: The San Francisco Public Library (SFPL) partnered with local tech hubs to offer free coding classes. This drew in young professionals who otherwise wouldn’t visit the library, boosting attendance by 22% in 2021.

Strategy 4: Offer High-Value In-Person Experiences

Why it works: People return when libraries provide unique, can’t-get-online experiences.

How CPL Did It:

  • Hosted "Library Passport" events (e.g., free museum passes, local attraction discounts).
  • Launched "Storytime in the Park" (outdoor, socially distanced kids’ programs).
  • Offered "Quiet Study Zones" for remote workers.

How to Apply This: ✔ Identify what makes your library special (e.g., rare collections, local history exhibits, maker spaces). ✔ Create exclusive in-person experiences (e.g., workshops, book signings, cultural events). ✔ Promote these events aggressively (e.g., targeted Facebook ads, local radio spots). ✔ Track which events drive the most attendance and double down on them.

Real-World Example: The Boston Public Library (BPL) revived its "First Thursdays" series, where artists, musicians, and authors perform live. This drew crowds back, with some events seeing 200+ attendees—a big jump from pre-pandemic averages.
