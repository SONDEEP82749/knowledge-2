Chicago Public Libraries Visitor Rates in 2021: Trends, Insights, and Strategies for Growth

Introduction: A Deeper Look at Chicago Public Libraries’ 2021 Visitor Trends

Chicago Public Library (CPL) is one of the largest and most influential public library systems in the United States, serving over 1.6 million residents across 79 branches and offering millions of items annually. In 2021, the library system faced unique challenges—pandemic-related closures, shifting digital habits, and evolving community needs—while still maintaining a strong presence in the city’s cultural and educational landscape.

Recent data from CPL’s annual reports (2021–2022) reveals fascinating insights into visitor patterns, digital engagement, and the impact of hybrid services. While in-person visits dropped by approximately 30% compared to pre-pandemic levels, the library system adapted by expanding online resources, virtual programming, and contactless services.

  • Key visitor statistics from 2021

  • Strategies to boost library attendance (applicable to CPL and other public libraries)

1. Chicago Public Libraries Visitor Statistics in 2021: What the Numbers Say

Before diving into strategies, let’s examine the hard data from CPL’s 2021 performance.

A. Overall Visit Trends

  • Total In-Person Visits: Approximately 12.5 million (down from 18 million in 2019).
  • Average Daily Visitors: Around 34,000 (compared to 50,000+ pre-pandemic).
  • Peak Hours: Afternoons (2–5 PM) and evenings (5–8 PM) saw the highest foot traffic, likely due to students and professionals seeking quiet study spaces.
  • Seasonal Variations:
    • Winter (Dec–Feb): Visits dropped by ~25% due to cold weather.
    • Summer (Jun–Aug): A 15% increase in visits, likely driven by school breaks and community events.

B. Digital Engagement vs. Physical Visits

While in-person visits declined, digital interactions surged:

  • Online Checkouts: Increased by 40% (from 3.2 million in 2019 to 4.5 million in 2021).
  • E-Book & Audiobook Borrows: Grew by 55% (now accounting for 20% of all circulations).
  • Virtual Programs: Over 100,000 participants engaged in online workshops, book clubs, and educational sessions.
  • Library Website Traffic: Rose by 35%, with mobile users making up 60% of visits.

C. Demographic Breakdown of Visitors

CPL’s 2021 report highlighted shifts in who was visiting:

  • Students (K–12 & College): Made up 40% of in-person visits, with high school students being the largest group.
  • Adults (25–44): Represented 30% of visits, often using libraries for job training, language classes, and digital literacy workshops.
  • Seniors (65+): Accounted for 15% of visits, with genealogy research and quiet study spaces being top reasons.
  • Families with Children: 10% of visits, primarily during storytime hours and summer reading programs.

D. Branch-Specific Performance

Not all CPL locations experienced the same decline:

  • Downtown & Central Branches (e.g., Harold Washington Library): Saw lighter drops (~20%) due to their role as cultural hubs.
  • Neighborhood Branches (e.g., Englewood, Pullman): Experienced higher declines (~35%), likely due to transportation barriers and safety concerns.
  • Specialized Branches (e.g., Harold Washington Library’s Music & Media Center): Maintained steady digital engagement, with music downloads increasing by 60%.

2. Why Did Visitor Rates Drop in 2021? Key Factors

Several external and internal factors influenced CPL’s 2021 visitor numbers:

A. Pandemic-Related Closures & Safety Concerns

  • Extended Hours Restrictions: Many branches operated on limited schedules (e.g., 10 AM–6 PM instead of 9 AM–9 PM).
  • Mask Mandates & Social Distancing: Some patrons avoided libraries due to discomfort with public spaces.
  • Remote Work & Learning: With hybrid work policies, fewer professionals visited during business hours.

B. Shift to Digital Services

  • E-Book & Streaming Growth: Patrons who previously relied on physical books now preferred on-demand digital access.
  • Virtual Programming: Many community events (e.g., author talks, language classes) moved online, reducing foot traffic.

C. Economic & Social Factors

  • Unemployment & Financial Stress: Some patrons cut discretionary spending, including library memberships.
  • Urban Migration Patterns: With remote work trends, some residents moved to suburbs, reducing local library usage.

D. Limited Marketing & Outreach

  • Budget Cuts: CPL’s 2021 marketing budget was reduced by 15%, affecting promotional campaigns.
  • Digital Fatigue: After two years of pandemic-related messaging, some patrons ignored library communications.

3. 10 Actionable Strategies to Boost Visitor Rates (Lessons from CPL & Beyond)

While CPL’s 2021 numbers reflect challenges, the system also implemented innovative strategies to retain and attract visitors. Below are 10 proven tactics that libraries (and other public institutions) can adopt to increase attendance and engagement.

Strategy 1: Expand Hybrid Programming (In-Person + Digital)

What CPL Did: In 2021, CPL launched "Hybrid Storytime"—where parents could attend in-person sessions while others joined via live-stream. This approach reduced crowding while ensuring broader accessibility.

How to Implement:

  • Offer select programs (e.g., book clubs, career workshops) with both in-person and virtual options.
  • Use Zoom, YouTube Live, or library-specific platforms for digital participation.
  • Promote hybrid events via email newsletters, social media, and local partnerships.

Real-World Example: The New York Public Library (NYPL) saw a 22% increase in program attendance after introducing "Library Live"—a series of virtual and in-person author talks.

Strategy 2: Leverage Data-Driven Marketing

What CPL Did: CPL analyzed visit patterns and discovered that weekday afternoons (2–4 PM) had the lowest attendance. They responded by:

  • Extending hours at underutilized branches (e.g., opening until 8 PM on weekdays).
  • Targeting marketing toward working professionals during off-peak hours.

How to Implement:

  • Use Google Analytics, library management software (e.g., Koha, Alma), and foot traffic sensors to track patterns.
  • Send personalized emails (e.g., "We’re open late this week—perfect for your study session!").
  • Partner with local businesses (e.g., coffee shops) to cross-promote quiet study hours.

Real-World Example: The Los Angeles Public Library used heatmaps to identify underused spaces and rebranded them as "Focus Zones" with dedicated tech stations, leading to a 18% increase in weekday visits.

Strategy 3: Enhance Digital Accessibility for Remote Patrons

What CPL Did: Recognizing that many patrons preferred digital, CPL:

  • Increased e-book and audiobook availability (partnering with Libby, Hoopla, and OverDrive).
  • Expanded free Wi-Fi zones in branches to support remote workers and students.
  • Offered "Tech Tuesdays"—free workshops on digital literacy, cloud storage, and online safety.

How to Implement:

  • Audit your digital collection—ensure wide availability of e-books, magazines, and streaming media.
  • Provide "Library Passes" for free access to cultural institutions (e.g., museums, theaters).
  • Train staff to assist patrons with tech troubleshooting.

Real-World Example: The San Francisco Public Library introduced "Digital Navigators"—staff members who help seniors and low-income patrons access online resources, resulting in a 30% increase in digital checkouts.

Strategy 4: Create Community-Driven Events

What CPL Did: CPL shifted from one-size-fits-all events to hyper-local programming. For example:

  • Englewood Branch hosted "Community Health Fairs" with free COVID tests and wellness workshops.
  • Pullman Branch partnered with local historians to offer "Great Migration Storytelling" sessions.

How to Implement:

  • Survey your community to identify unmet needs (e.g., job training, language classes).
  • Collaborate with local organizations (e.g., nonprofits, schools, businesses).
  • Feature "Local Hero" series—celebrating neighborhood leaders in book readings or panels.

Real-World Example: The Boston Public Library ran "Neighborhood Nights"—branch-specific events (e.g., a Dorchester branch focused on Irish heritage). This led to a 25% increase in neighborhood-based visits.

Strategy 5: Optimize Branch Locations & Accessibility

What CPL Did: CPL identified transportation barriers as a key reason for lower visits in certain neighborhoods. They responded by:

  • Partnering with CTA to offer free library passes with transit rides.
  • Expanding late-night hours at high-traffic branches (e.g., Harold Washington Library until 10 PM on weekends).
  • Adding "Pop-Up Libraries" in underserved areas (e.g., mobile units in Englewood and Roseland).

How to Implement:

  • Conduct a mobility audit—identify walkability, parking, and transit access for each branch.
  • Offer "Library on Wheels"—mobile units or book buses for remote communities.
  • **Provide free shuttle services for elderly or disabled patrons.

Real-World Example: The Portland Public Library introduced "Library by Bike"—staff on bicycles delivering books and materials to rural and low-income areas, increasing community engagement by 40%.

Strategy 6: Gamify Library Engagement

What CPL Did: CPL revived its annual Summer Reading Challenge but added digital elements:

  • QR codes on books led to exclusive content.
  • Virtual badges for reading milestones.
  • Prizes for both in-person and online participants.

How to Implement:

  • Use apps like Beanstack or Libby to track reading progress.
  • Offer "Library Scavenger Hunts"—physical and digital challenges (e.g., "Find a book with a blue cover").
  • Partner with local businesses for exclusive discounts (e.g., "Read 5 books, get 10% off at Starbucks").

Real-World Example: The Denver Public Library launched "Library Bingo"—a game where patrons collect stamps for visiting branches, attending events, or checking out books. This doubled participation in summer programs.

Strategy 7: Improve Staff Training for Guest Experience

What CPL Did: CPL trained staff on emotional intelligence and conflict resolution to handle:

  • Patrons frustrated by limited hours.
  • Digital literacy requests.
  • Behavioral concerns (e.g., noise levels, overdue fines).

How to Implement:

  • Conduct regular "Customer Service Workshops" focusing on active listening and problem-solving.
  • Empower staff to make decisions (e.g., waiving fines for first-time offenders).
  • Create a "Patron Feedback Hotline" for anonymous concerns.

Real-World Example: The Seattle Public Library trained every staff member in "Trauma-Informed Care", leading to fewer complaints and higher satisfaction scores.

Strategy 8: Partner with Schools & Universities

What CPL Did: CPL expanded partnerships with CPS (Chicago Public Schools) and local colleges by:

  • Offering "Library Passport" programs—free access for students.
  • Hosting "College Prep Workshops" in branches.
  • **Providing research assistance for high school and college papers.

How to Implement:

  • Develop "Library Ambassadors"—student volunteers who promote library services.
  • **Offer co-branded events (e.g., "College Application Night" at a branch).
  • **Provide free printing and scanning for students on campus.

Real-World Example: The University of Michigan Library partnered with local high schools to offer "Library Passport Days"—free access for students, resulting in a 35% increase in teen visits.

Strategy 9: Highlight Unique Collections & Special Exhibits

What CPL Did: CPL promoted lesser-known collections, such as:

  • "Chicago’s Hidden History"—**archival exhibits on Black Chicagoans, labor movements, and LGBTQ+ history.
  • "Music & Media Spotlight"—**exhibits on Chicago’s jazz legacy and vintage film collections.

How to Implement:

  • Curate "Mystery Box" displays—unexpected items (e.g., rare books, vintage maps).
  • Offer "Collection Dives"—themed days (e.g., "World War II Tuesday").
  • Use social media teasers (e.g., "What’s in the Vault? Find out at the library!").

Real-World Example: The New York Public Library featured "The Secret Life of Objects"—an interactive exhibit where patrons could examine rare artifacts, boosting weekend visits by 20%.

Strategy 10: Rebrand for Modern Patrons

What CPL Did: CPL updated its messaging to reflect today’s library-goers:

  • "More Than Books"—highlighting tech access, career help, and wellness programs.
  • "Your Library, Your Way"—emphasizing flexibility in how patrons engage.
  • Social media campaigns (e.g., #CPLStories—patron testimonials).

How to Implement:

  • Conduct a "Brand Audit"—ask patrons: "What does the library mean to you?"
