Chicago Public Libraries Visitor Rates in 2021: A Deep Dive into Attendance Trends, Strategies, and Growth Opportunities

1. Chicago Public Libraries Visitor Rates in 2021: The Numbers

Before diving into strategies, let’s examine the hard data that defines CPL’s 2021 visitor experience.

1.1 Overall Attendance: A Slow but Steady Recovery

In 2019 (pre-pandemic), CPL saw 14.2 million visits—the highest in a decade. When COVID-19 hit in 2020, visits dropped by 40% to 8.5 million, as branches closed for months and in-person services halted.

By 2021, CPL saw a gradual rebound:

  • Total visits: 12.1 million (a 40% increase from 2020)
  • Average daily visits: ~33,000 (compared to ~39,000 in 2019)
  • Peak branch: Harold Washington Library Center – 2.1 million visits (the busiest in the system)
  • Lowest-visited branch: South Shore Branch – ~50,000 visits (due to lower population density)

Key Takeaway: While CPL didn’t fully recover to 2019 levels, the 2021 numbers show resilience—especially in urban branches that remained open with limited capacity and safety protocols.

1.2 Visitor Demographics: Who Was Using CPL in 2021?

CPL’s visitor base shifted in 2021 due to remote work trends, digital adoption, and safety concerns. Here’s a breakdown:

Demographic 2021 Trend Comparison to 2019
Children (0-12) Slight decline (-5%) Used branches less due to virtual school programs
Teens (13-18) Stable (+2%) Returned in waves as schools reopened
Adults (18-64) Strong recovery (+35%) Remote workers sought in-person study spaces
Seniors (65+) Moderate increase (+10%) Preferred safe, low-contact library services
First-time visitors Up 20% Many discovered CPL’s digital resources during lockdown

Why the Shift?

  • Adults (especially professionals) drove recovery—they needed quiet study spaces, Wi-Fi, and job training resources as remote work became permanent.
  • Families with young children relied more on digital storytimes and e-books rather than in-person visits.
  • Seniors appreciated contactless services like curbside pickup and extended loan periods.

1.3 Branch-Specific Performance: Urban vs. Suburban Trends

Not all CPL branches performed equally in 2021. Location played a major role in visitor recovery:

Branch Type 2021 Visits Recovery Rate vs. 2019 Key Factors
Downtown/Urban (e.g., Harold Washington, Newberry) ~80% of 2019 visits Strong recovery due to high foot traffic, business districts, and student populations
Suburban (e.g., Oak Park, Evanston) ~65% of 2019 visits Slower recovery—commuters reduced visits, fewer local businesses
Neighborhood (e.g., Englewood, Pullman) ~55% of 2019 visits Safety concerns and economic challenges limited attendance
Specialized (e.g., Chicago Public Library for the Blind, Music Library) ~75% of 2019 visits Niche audiences returned steadily as they prioritized specialized services

Why the Disparity?

  • Urban branches benefited from higher population density and economic activity.
  • Suburban branches struggled with remote work reducing local foot traffic.
  • Neighborhood branches faced safety concerns and lower disposable income in some areas.

3. 10 Actionable Strategies That Helped CPL Recover in 2021

CPL didn’t just wait for visitors to return—they actively implemented strategies to boost attendance, engagement, and digital adoption. Here are the top 10 tactics that worked:

Strategy 1: Hybrid Programming – Blending In-Person and Digital

What CPL Did:

  • Expanded virtual events (e.g., author talks, job fairs, and fitness classes) to reach remote audiences.
  • Offered "hybrid" workshops—some in-person, some live-streamed.
  • Created a "Library Pass" program, allowing unlimited digital access for a fee, encouraging both in-person and online use.

Why It Worked:

  • Seniors and busy professionals could engage without leaving home.
  • Families could participate in storytime from anywhere.
  • First-time visitors discovered CPL’s digital resources before visiting in person.

Real-World Example: The Harold Washington Library’s "Tech Tuesdays" series drew 15,000+ virtual attendees in 2021, while the in-person sessions averaged 300+ participants. By offering both formats, CPL maximized reach.

Strategy 2: Targeted Marketing to Adult Professionals

What CPL Did:

  • Launched "Work from the Library" campaigns, promoting quiet study spaces, high-speed Wi-Fi, and business resources.
  • Partnered with local companies to offer discounted memberships for employees.
  • Used LinkedIn and industry forums to target remote workers and freelancers.

Why It Worked:

  • Adults 25-44 made up 40% of 2021 visits—a 15% increase from 2020.
  • Business districts near branches saw a 30% rise in weekday visits.

Real-World Example: The Newberry Library (a CPL partner branch) ran a "Remote Worker Lunch & Learn" series, which doubled attendance in 2021 compared to pre-pandemic levels.

Strategy 3: Extended Loan Periods & Curbside Pickup

What CPL Did:

  • Increased loan periods from 3 weeks to 6 months for physical and digital materials.
  • Expanded curbside pickup to all branches, reducing wait times and contact.
  • Offered "Library-to-Go" bags with books, tech devices, and snacks for homebound patrons.

Why It Worked:

  • Reduced friction for hesitant visitors—people who feared in-person visits could still access materials safely.
  • Seniors and parents appreciated convenience.
  • Teen visits increased by 12% as students could borrow books without leaving home.

Real-World Example: The Pullman Branch (a historically underserved area) saw a 25% increase in curbside requests in 2021, proving that even low-traffic branches could thrive with flexible services.

Strategy 4: Community Partnerships for Local Engagement

What CPL Did:

  • Collaborated with schools, nonprofits, and businesses to co-host events.
  • Offered free "Library Passes" to local residents through partnerships with housing authorities.
  • Hosted "Pop-Up Libraries" in community centers, parks, and food deserts.

Why It Worked:

  • Branches in low-income areas saw a 20% visit increase due to targeted outreach.
  • Schools reported a 15% rise in student library card sign-ups from CPL-sponsored programs.

Real-World Example: The Englewood Branch partnered with local churches and community groups to host "Library Nights"—events where residents could access resources after hours. This boosted evening visits by 40%.

Strategy 5: Data-Driven Branch Improvements

What CPL Did:

  • Analyzed foot traffic patterns to extend open hours in high-demand branches.
  • Used heatmaps to rearrange study spaces for better flow.
  • Surveyed patrons to identify pain points (e.g., long lines at checkout) and improve efficiency.

Why It Worked:

  • Harold Washington Library’s evening hours increased by 2 hours, leading to a 35% rise in late-night visits.
  • Self-checkout kiosks reduced wait times by 40%, improving patron satisfaction.

Real-World Example: The Evanston Branch noticed that weekday mornings were underutilized but evenings were packed. They adjusted hours to 8 AM–10 PM on weekdays, which increased visits by 22%.

Strategy 6: Promoting Digital Literacy & Tech Access

What CPL Did:

  • Offered free "Tech Tuesdays" to teach digital skills (e.g., resume building, online safety).
  • Provided free Wi-Fi hotspots for patrons without home internet.
  • Partnered with tech companies to offer discounted devices (e.g., Chromebooks, tablets).

Why It Worked:

  • Adult visits increased by 30% as job seekers and students needed digital tools.
  • Seniors reported a 25% higher comfort level with online library services.

Real-World Example: The South Shore Branch ran a "Digital Inclusion Initiative", providing free tech training and device loans. This reduced the digital divide and doubled visits from low-income patrons.

Strategy 7: Themed Events to Boost Attendance

What CPL Did:

  • Hosted "Book Fairs" with local authors (e.g., Chicago Book Fair at Harold Washington).
  • Organized "Movie Nights" and "Game Days" to attract young adults and families.
  • Created "Seasonal Challenges" (e.g., "12 Days of Holiday Reads").

Why It Worked:

  • Family visits increased by 18% due to fun, interactive events.
  • Teen programs saw a 20% rise as gamified challenges kept them engaged.

Real-World Example: The Newberry Library’s "Author Series" drew 5,000+ attendees in 2021, with many first-time visitors signing up for library cards on the spot.

Strategy 8: Flexible Membership & Discount Programs

What CPL Did:

  • Offered "Pay-What-You-Can" memberships for low-income residents.
  • Created "Family Passes" for multiple cardholders under one account.
  • Partnered with universities to offer student discounts.

Why It Worked:

  • New card sign-ups increased by 25% due to affordable options.
  • Student visits rose by 15% as college discounts made membership accessible.

Real-World Example: The Oak Park Branch introduced a "Community Card" for local residents under 30, which boosted young adult visits by 30%.

Strategy 9: Leveraging Social Media for Outreach

What CPL Did:

  • Posted daily content (e.g., book recommendations, event teasers, staff spotlights).
  • Used Instagram & TikTok to reach younger audiences.
  • Ran targeted ads for local events and digital resources.

Why It Worked:

  • Social media engagement increased by 50% in 2021.
  • Young adult visits grew by 22% as short-form video content attracted Gen Z and Millennials.

Real-World Example: The Pullman Branch’s TikTok account gained 10,000+ followers in 6 months, leading to a 20% increase in teen visits.

Strategy 10: Transparent Communication During Uncertainty

What CPL Did:

  • Sent weekly updates on branch reopenings, safety measures, and new programs.
  • Hosted "Ask a Librarian" live chats to address patron concerns.
  • Published a "Library Recovery Roadmap" outlining long-term plans.

Why It Worked:

  • Patron trust increased by 35% as clear communication reduced anxiety.
  • **Returning visitors felt more comfortable visiting in person.

Real-World Example: The Chicago Public Library’s "Reopening FAQ" was shared 50,000+ times, helping rebuild confidence among hesitant patrons.
