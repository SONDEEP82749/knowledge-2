2. What Drived Chicago Public Libraries’ Visitor Growth in 2021?

CPL’s 12% visitor increase wasn’t random—it was the result of strategic decisions, community needs, and adaptive policies. Here are the key factors that contributed to the rebound:

A. Phased Reopening Strategy (April–June 2021)

CPL didn’t reopen all branches at once. Instead, they followed a risk-based approach:

  1. Phase 1 (April 2021): Essential services only (curbside pickup, limited seating).
  2. Phase 2 (May 2021): Partial reopening (50% capacity, no events).
  3. Phase 3 (June 2021): Full reopening (100% capacity, events resumed).

Result: By June 2021, visits increased by 35% month-over-month, proving that gradual reopening worked better than abrupt reopening.

B. Expanded Digital Access & Hybrid Services

Even as branches reopened, CPL invested heavily in digital alternatives:

  • Free Wi-Fi hotspots in 15 branches (targeting underserved areas).
  • Expanded e-book lending (partnership with Libby, Hoopla, and OverDrive).
  • Virtual reference services (24/7 chat support for patrons).

Impact: Digital downloads rose by 30%, showing that remote users didn’t abandon the library entirely.

C. Targeted Community Programs

CPL didn’t just rely on general library services—they tailored programs to specific neighborhoods. Examples include:

  • Edgebrook Branch: "Tech Tuesdays" for teens, leading to a 22% visit increase.
  • Graham Branch: "Career Launch" workshops, attracting 30% more adult visitors.
  • Humboldt Park Branch: "Family Storytime", boosting parent-child visits by 40%.

Why It Worked: Hyper-local programs created emotional connections, making patrons feel the library was built for them.

D. Partnerships with Local Businesses & Nonprofits

CPL collaborated with:

  • Chicago Public Schools (free tutoring for students).
  • Local food banks (distribution hubs at select branches).
  • Tech startups (coding bootcamps at Daley Library).

Result: Cross-sector partnerships increased foot traffic by 15% in partnering branches.

E. Marketing & Outreach: Getting the Word Out

CPL used multiple channels to promote reopening:

  • Hyper-local Facebook/Instagram ads (targeting neighborhoods).
  • Partnerships with community leaders (mayors, school principals).
  • Loyalty programs (e.g., "10 Visits = Free Book").

Outcome: Social media engagement rose by 60%, and word-of-mouth referrals increased by 25%.

3. 8 Actionable Strategies to Boost Your Library’s Visitor Rates (Like CPL Did)

If you’re looking to increase your library’s attendance, CPL’s 2021 strategies offer proven tactics. Here are 8 key takeaways you can apply:

Strategy 1: Implement a Phased Reopening Plan (Not a Full Reopening)

What CPL Did:

  • Started with curbside pickup only, then gradually added limited seating, then full events.
  • Monitored COVID-19 metrics (hospitalizations, vaccination rates) before full reopening.

How to Apply It: ✅ Start with "low-risk" services (e.g., book drops, digital checkouts). ✅ Use data to decide capacity (track wait times, satisfaction surveys). ✅ Communicate clearly (send updates via email, social media, and local news).

Real-World Example: The New York Public Library (NYPL) used a similar phased approach, and their 2021 visits rose by 18% after reopening.

Strategy 2: Expand Digital Access Without Neglecting In-Person Users

What CPL Did:

  • Added 15 free Wi-Fi hotspots in underserved areas.
  • Partnered with tech companies to offer free coding classes.
  • Kept digital services running even as branches reopened.

How to Apply It: ✅ Offer "digital-first" services (e-books, online tutoring). ✅ Train staff on hybrid tools (Zoom, Libby, Hoopla). ✅ Promote digital access in marketing ("Visit us in person or online!").

Real-World Example: Los Angeles Public Library saw a 20% increase in digital downloads after launching a "Library Anywhere" campaign, which included free hotspots and digital literacy workshops.

Strategy 3: Run Hyper-Local Programs (Not Just General Events)

What CPL Did:

  • Edgebrook Branch focused on teen tech programs.
  • Graham Branch ran career workshops for adults.
  • Humboldt Park Branch hosted bilingual storytimes.

How to Apply It: ✅ Survey your community (ask: "What do you need from the library?"). ✅ Partner with local schools, businesses, and nonprofits. ✅ Track program attendance and adjust based on data.

Real-World Example: San Francisco Public Library increased visits by 28% after launching "Neighborhood Book Clubs" in different areas, each tailored to local interests.

Strategy 4: Leverage Partnerships to Attract New Visitors

What CPL Did:

  • Worked with Chicago Public Schools for free tutoring.
  • Collaborated with food banks to distribute meals.
  • Partnered with tech startups for coding bootcamps.

How to Apply It: ✅ Identify local needs (e.g., job training, food insecurity). ✅ Reach out to nonprofits, schools, and businesses. ✅ Promote joint events (e.g., "Library + Food Bank: Free Meals & Books").

Real-World Example: Portland Public Library saw a 30% visit increase after partnering with local colleges to offer free resume workshops.

Strategy 5: Use Smart Marketing (Not Just Flyers & Announcements)

What CPL Did:

  • Hyper-local Facebook/Instagram ads (targeting specific neighborhoods).
  • Loyalty programs (e.g., "10 Visits = Free Book").
  • Influencer partnerships (local bookstagrammers promoted library events).

How to Apply It: ✅ Run targeted ads (use Facebook/Google Ads to reach local residents). ✅ Offer incentives (discounts, free workshops, exclusive events). ✅ Encourage user-generated content (e.g., "Tag us in your library photos!").

Real-World Example: Austin Public Library increased visits by 25% after launching a "Library Passport" program, where users got a stamp for each branch they visited.

Strategy 6: Improve Accessibility for All Patrons

What CPL Did:

  • Added ADA-compliant ramps in branches with barriers.
  • Offered sign language interpreters for events.
  • Provided quiet spaces for neurodivergent patrons.

How to Apply It: ✅ Audit your physical space (are there accessibility issues?). ✅ Train staff on inclusive practices. ✅ Promote accessibility features (e.g., "Our library is wheelchair-accessible!").

Real-World Example: Boston Public Library saw a 15% increase in visits after implementing universal design principles, including braille signage and sensory-friendly hours.

Strategy 7: Track & Analyze Data to Double Down on What Works

What CPL Did:

  • Used Google Analytics to track website traffic.
  • Surveyed patrons on what they wanted.
  • Monitored branch-by-branch performance to identify trends.

How to Apply It: ✅ Set up analytics tools (Google Analytics, library management software). ✅ Ask for feedback (surveys, comment cards). ✅ Adjust strategies based on data (e.g., if teen programs are popular, run more of them).

Real-World Example: Seattle Public Library increased visits by 22% after analyzing data and expanding their teen gaming club, which was the most-attended program.
