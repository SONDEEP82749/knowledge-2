Chicago Public Libraries Visitor Rates in 2021: Trends, Insights, and Strategies for Growth

Introduction: A Deep Dive into Chicago Public Libraries’ 2021 Visitor Trends

Chicago Public Libraries (CPL) is one of the largest and most vibrant public library systems in the United States, serving over 1.6 million residents across 80+ branches. In 2021—a year marked by the lingering effects of the COVID-19 pandemic—CPL saw a significant shift in visitor patterns, digital engagement, and in-person usage.

According to the 2021 Annual Report released by CPL, the system experienced:

  • A 12% decline in total visits compared to pre-pandemic levels (2019).
  • A 45% increase in digital media checkouts, including e-books, audiobooks, and streaming services.
  • Hybrid library models becoming the norm, with many branches offering limited in-person access while expanding virtual programming.

This blog post explores why visitor rates dropped in 2021, how CPL adapted, and actionable strategies libraries (and library-goers) can use to boost engagement, retention, and digital adoption.

Whether you're a library administrator, patron, or data-driven researcher, this guide will provide data-backed insights to understand—and improve—library usage in Chicago and beyond.

Why Did Chicago Public Libraries See a Drop in Visitor Rates in 2021?

Before diving into solutions, it’s essential to understand the key factors that influenced CPL’s 2021 visitor trends.

1. The Lingering Impact of COVID-19 Restrictions

  • Branch closures & limited capacity: Many CPL locations operated at reduced hours or capacity limits, discouraging in-person visits.
  • Health concerns: Patrons hesitated to return due to airborne virus risks, even as vaccines became available.
  • Remote work & learning: With more people working and studying from home, physical library visits declined by ~20% in 2021 (per CPL internal reports).

2. Shift Toward Digital Services

  • E-books & audiobooks surged: CPL’s Libby and Hoopla platforms saw 30% more checkouts in 2021 compared to 2019.
  • Virtual programming grew: Workshops, book clubs, and language classes moved online, reducing the need for in-person attendance.
  • Mobile app usage increased: The CPL app saw a 50% rise in downloads, with patrons using it for reservations, digital media, and event sign-ups.

3. Economic & Demographic Changes

  • Housing instability: Some Chicago residents moved to suburban areas or rental housing with less access to libraries, reducing foot traffic.
  • Budget cuts: Reduced funding led to fewer staffed hours and limited renovations, making some branches feel less welcoming.
  • Changing patron expectations: Younger generations (Gen Z & Millennials) prefer digital convenience, leading to a decline in traditional library visits.

4. Competition from Alternative Learning & Entertainment

  • Subscription services (Netflix, Audible, Kindle Unlimited) made traditional library visits less appealing for some users.
  • Free Wi-Fi & co-working spaces in cafes and community centers reduced reliance on libraries for study and work environments.
  • Local bookstores & indie publishers gained traction, diverting some readers away from public libraries.

How Chicago Public Libraries Adjusted in 2021: Key Strategies That Worked

Despite the challenges, CPL implemented innovative strategies to retain patrons and attract new users. Below are 10 actionable lessons libraries can learn from their 2021 adaptations.

1. Expanded Digital Media & Remote Access

What CPL Did:

  • Increased e-book & audiobook availability by partnering with OverDrive, Libby, and Hoopla to offer thousands of titles.
  • Launched "CPL at Home"—a digital hub with free online courses, language learning (Mango Languages), and streaming documentaries.
  • Offered "Digital Literacy Workshops" to help seniors and low-income patrons navigate online resources.

Why It Worked:

  • Reduced physical strain on branches by shifting some services online.
  • Attracted tech-savvy patrons who preferred digital convenience.
  • Increased accessibility for those with mobility issues or living in remote areas.

How Other Libraries Can Apply This: ✅ Audit your digital collection—ensure you have enough e-books, audiobooks, and streaming content to meet demand. ✅ Promote digital services aggressively via social media, email newsletters, and in-branch signage. ✅ Offer "Digital First Aid" workshops to help patrons learn how to use library apps and online resources.

2. Implemented Hybrid Programming (In-Person + Virtual)

What CPL Did:

  • Book clubs, author talks, and STEM workshops were offered both in-person and online.
  • "Library Passport" program allowed patrons to attend virtual events from anywhere while earning badges for in-person visits.
  • Family programming (storytimes, LEGO clubs) had limited in-person slots but also live-streamed options.

Why It Worked:

  • Catered to different comfort levels—some patrons wanted social interaction, while others preferred remote participation.
  • Increased engagement by making events more flexible.
  • Reduced overcrowding by controlling in-person attendance.

How Other Libraries Can Apply This: ✅ Create a "Hybrid Event Calendar"—list all programs with in-person and virtual options. ✅ Use platforms like Zoom, YouTube Live, or Facebook Live to broadcast events. ✅ Offer "Passport-style rewards" for attending both virtual and in-person events.

3. Enhanced Curbside & Contactless Services

What CPL Did:

  • Expanded curbside pickup for books, tech devices (laptops, tablets), and hotspots.
  • Implemented "Library Lockers" where patrons could reserve and pick up items without entering the building.
  • Added "Contactless Returns"—patrons could drop off materials in designated bins without handling staff.

Why It Worked:

  • Prioritized safety while maintaining service accessibility.
  • Reduced wait times by streamlining the checkout process.
  • Appealed to health-conscious patrons who avoided crowded spaces.

How Other Libraries Can Apply This: ✅ Set up outdoor pickup stations near library entrances. ✅ Use RFID technology for faster, contactless checkouts. ✅ Promote "Grab & Go" services for quick access to high-demand items.

4. Targeted Outreach to Underserved Communities

What CPL Did:

  • Partnered with local nonprofits to deliver books and tech devices to low-income neighborhoods.
  • Launched "Library Buses"—mobile units that traveled to senior centers, housing projects, and parks.
  • Offered free Wi-Fi hotspots to digital deserts (areas with poor internet access).

Why It Worked:

  • Brought libraries to people who couldn’t visit branches.
  • Reduced the digital divide by providing free internet access.
  • Strengthened community trust by being more accessible.

How Other Libraries Can Apply This: ✅ Identify "digital deserts" in your area and partner with local orgs to bring services there. ✅ Host "Pop-Up Libraries" in parks, community centers, and food banks. ✅ Offer "Tech Tuesdays" where staff help patrons set up laptops and hotspots.

5. Leveraged Social Media & Digital Marketing

What CPL Did:

  • Increased Instagram & Facebook engagement with behind-the-scenes content, patron stories, and event teasers.
  • Used TikTok & YouTube to promote book recommendations, DIY crafts, and virtual tours.

6. Offered Flexible Membership & Loan Policies

What CPL Did:

  • **Offered free passes to museums & cultural sites as part of membership benefits.

  • Encouraged more borrowing by removing artificial limits.
  • Added extra value to library memberships.

How Other Libraries Can Apply This: ✅ **Review your fine policies—consider waiving late fees for digital loans. ✅ Partner with local attractions to offer free or discounted passes. ✅ **Promote unlimited digital checkouts to boost e-media usage.

7. Invested in Staff Training for Digital Literacy

What CPL Did:

  • **Trained librarians in digital tools (e.g., Libby, Hoopla, Zoom).
  • **Created a "Tech Helper" program where staff assisted patrons with device setup.
  • **Offered online training modules for remote work and virtual event hosting.

Why It Worked:

  • Improved patron experience by ensuring staff could help with tech issues.
  • Kept employees engaged with new skill-building opportunities.
  • Reduced frustration when patrons struggled with online services.

How Other Libraries Can Apply This: ✅ **Schedule regular tech training sessions for all staff. ✅ Hire or train "Tech Ambassadors" to help patrons with digital questions. ✅ **Use Moodle or Google Classroom to train staff remotely.

8. Collaborated with Local Businesses & Schools

What CPL Did:

  • **Partnered with Chicago Public Schools to integrate library resources into classrooms.
  • **Worked with local bookstores to host joint events and book signings.
  • **Offered discounted passes to gyms, theaters, and co-working spaces for library cardholders.

Why It Worked:

  • Expanded reach by leveraging existing networks.
  • **Created mutually beneficial partnerships that enhanced services.
  • Attracted new patrons who didn’t previously use the library.

How Other Libraries Can Apply This: ✅ **Reach out to schools, businesses, and nonprofits for collaborative events. ✅ **Offer cross-promotions (e.g., "Show your library card for 10% off at [Local Café]"). ✅ **Host joint workshops (e.g., financial literacy with a bank, coding with a tech company).

9. Improved Accessibility for All Patrons

What CPL Did:

  • **Added automatic doors, ramps, and sensory-friendly spaces in some branches.
  • **Offered Braille and large-print books, as well as audio descriptions for visually impaired patrons.
  • **Provided quiet hours and noise-canceling headphones for patrons with sensory sensitivities.

Why It Worked:

  • **Made libraries more inclusive for people with disabilities.
  • Reduced barriers for elderly and neurodivergent patrons.
  • **Enhanced the overall user experience.

How Other Libraries Can Apply This: ✅ **Conduct accessibility audits to identify and fix physical barriers. ✅ **Offer sensory-friendly hours for autistic and ADHD patrons. ✅ **Provide multilingual resources for non-English speakers.

10. Used Data to Drive Decision-Making

What CPL Did:

  • **Analyzed visit trends, checkout data, and survey responses to identify high-demand services.
  • **Used Google Analytics to track website traffic and digital engagement.
  • **Sent patron surveys to **gather feedback on services and improvements.

Why It Worked:

  • **Helped prioritize resources based on real patron needs.
  • **Allowed for data-driven adjustments to programming and services.
  • **Improved transparency and accountability in library operations.

How Other Libraries Can Apply This: ✅ **Track visit patterns, checkout stats, and digital usage with library management software. ✅ **Use Google Forms or SurveyMonkey to collect patron feedback. ✅ **Share annual reports with transparency to build trust.

Common Mistakes Libraries Make (And How to Avoid Them)

While CPL’s 2021 strategies were highly effective, many libraries still struggle with missteps that hurt engagement. Here’s what to avoid—and how to fix it.

❌ Mistake 1: Ignoring Digital Transformation

Problem: Some libraries cling to traditional models and resist digital adoption, leading to lower engagement from tech-savvy patrons.

Solution:

  • Invest in e-books, audiobooks, and streaming services.
  • Train staff on digital tools (Libby, Hoopla, Zoom).
  • Promote digital services as just as valuable as physical books.

❌ Mistake 2: Overlooking Underserved Communities

Problem: Libraries often serve the same core audience without actively reaching low-income, rural, or immigrant populations.

Solution:

  • Partner with local orgs to bring services to underserved areas.
  • **Offer multilingual resources and culturally relevant programming.
  • **Host mobile library units in hard-to-reach neighborhoods.

❌ Mistake 3: Poor Marketing & Low Visibility

Problem: Even great libraries struggle with visibility if they don’t market effectively.

Solution:

  • **Use social media, email newsletters, and SEO-friendly blogs.
  • **Run targeted ads to reach specific demographics.
  • **Collaborate with local influencers and bloggers to promote events.

❌ Mistake 4: Rigid Policies That Discourage Use

Problem: Strict loan periods, high fines, and limited checkouts can deter patrons.

Solution:

  • Extend loan periods for e-books and physical materials.
