Chicago Public Libraries Visitor Rates in 2021: A Deep Dive into Attendance Trends, Strategies, and Growth Opportunities

Introduction: Understanding the 2021 Visitor Trends at Chicago Public Libraries

Chicago Public Libraries (CPL) is one of the largest and most influential public library systems in the United States, serving over 1.6 million residents across 80+ branches. In 2021—a year marked by the lingering effects of the COVID-19 pandemic—CPL faced unprecedented challenges in maintaining visitor engagement while adapting to new safety protocols, digital shifts, and evolving community needs.

According to the 2021 Annual Report from the Chicago Public Library Foundation, total in-person visits dropped by approximately 30% compared to pre-pandemic levels (2019), with some branches experiencing declines as high as 40%. However, digital engagement surged, with online program participation increasing by 150% and e-book checkouts rising by 200%. This dual trend—declining in-person traffic but booming virtual activity—highlighted the need for libraries to rethink their strategies to attract and retain visitors in a post-pandemic world.

B. Branch-Specific Performance: High-Traffic vs. Low-Traffic Locations

Not all CPL branches were affected equally. Some urban hubs (like Harold Washington Library Center and Newberry Library) saw sharper declines, while suburban and community branches (such as Morton East Branch and Pullman Branch) experienced more resilience.

1. Urban Core Branches (High Decline)

  • Harold Washington Library Center (Downtown)

    • 2019 Visits: ~1.2 million
    • 2021 Visits: ~750,000 (-38%)
    • Key Factors:
      • Safety concerns in dense urban areas
      • Competition from digital alternatives (e.g., streaming services, online courses)
      • Reduced foot traffic due to remote work trends

  • Newberry Library (Near the Loop)

    • 2019 Visits: ~450,000
    • 2021 Visits: ~280,000 (-38%)
    • Special Note: This branch saw a rebound in 2022 due to its specialized historical collections, attracting researchers and genealogy enthusiasts.

2. Suburban & Community Branches (Moderate Decline)

  • Morton East Branch (South Side)

    • 2019 Visits: ~220,000
    • 2021 Visits: ~160,000 (-27%)
    • Why It Performed Better:
      • Strong community ties (local events, after-school programs)
      • Lower density allowed for easier social distancing
      • Hybrid model success (in-person + virtual storytimes)

  • Pullman Branch (Southwest Side)

    • 2019 Visits: ~180,000
    • 2021 Visits: ~130,000 (-28%)
    • Unique Strength: Workforce development programs kept it relevant during economic uncertainty.

3. Rural & Outlying Branches (Least Affected)

  • Bensenville Branch (Northwest Suburbs)

    • 2019 Visits: ~110,000
    • 2021 Visits: ~90,000 (-18%)
    • Reason: Limited digital alternatives in rural areas drove higher reliance on in-person services.

  • Waukegan Branch (North Shore)

    • 2019 Visits: ~140,000
    • 2021 Visits: ~110,000 (-21%)
    • Key Driver: Strong adult education programs (GED prep, ESL classes) maintained steady attendance.

C. Demographic Shifts in 2021 Visitors

CPL’s 2021 demographic data revealed shifts in who was visiting the libraries:

  • Children (0-12): -25% drop (due to virtual school and limited in-person programs)
  • Teens (13-18): -30% drop (competition from social media and gaming)
  • Adults (19-64): -20% drop (remote work reducing commute-based visits)
  • Seniors (65+): -10% drop (but highest retention rate in digital services like OverDrive and Hoopla)

Insight: Libraries must re-engage younger demographics with interactive, tech-savvy programs while leveraging the digital strengths of older patrons.

2. Why Did Visitor Rates Drop in 2021? Key Factors Behind the Decline

The 30% drop in in-person visits wasn’t random—it was driven by systemic, behavioral, and economic changes. Understanding these factors is the first step in crafting effective recovery strategies.

A. The Pandemic’s Direct Impact

  1. Health Safety Concerns

    • Many Chicagoans avoided public spaces due to COVID-19 fears, even as restrictions eased.
    • Social distancing policies reduced capacity, leading to longer wait times and frustration.

  2. Shift to Remote Work & Learning

    • Commuters (a major library demographic) worked from home, reducing daily visits.
    • Students relied on online classes, cutting down on in-person research trips.

  3. Closures & Limited Hours

    • Some branches operated on reduced schedules, making them less accessible.
    • Special collections (e.g., archives, maker spaces) were closed or restricted.

B. Digital Fatigue & Alternative Engagement

  • Streaming Services (Netflix, Audible, Spotify) reduced demand for physical media.
  • Free Online Learning (Coursera, Khan Academy) competed with library resources.
  • Social Media & Gaming kept teens and young adults away from traditional library activities.

C. Economic & Social Factors

  • Job Losses & Financial Stress led some patrons to prioritize essential services over library visits.
  • Housing Instability (due to eviction crises) made consistent library use difficult for some communities.
  • Declining Trust in Institutions—some Chicagoans questioned the value of public libraries post-pandemic.

D. Underutilized Branch Locations

  • Branches in high-crime areas saw even steeper declines due to safety perceptions.
  • Branches with outdated facilities struggled to attract modern users.
  • Lack of Marketing—some libraries didn’t effectively promote their new digital offerings.

3. 10 Actionable Strategies to Boost Visitor Rates in 2024 & Beyond

Recovering from a 30% drop in attendance requires bold, data-driven strategies. Below are 10 proven tactics—backed by CPL’s successes and best practices from other top library systems (like New York Public Library and Los Angeles Public Library)—to increase foot traffic, digital engagement, and community loyalty.

Strategy 1: Hybrid Programming – Blending In-Person & Virtual Experiences

Why It Works: CPL’s 2022 Annual Report showed that libraries offering hybrid programs saw a 40% increase in participation compared to purely in-person or virtual-only events.

How to Implement:

  • Example: The Pullman Branch launched "Hybrid Storytime"—where parents could attend live in-person sessions or watch via Zoom if they couldn’t come in.
  • Tech Needed: Zoom, YouTube Live, or library-specific platforms like Libby & Hoopla.
  • Metrics to Track:
    • Attendance growth (both in-person and virtual)
    • Repeat participation rates

Real-World Success:

  • The New York Public Library’s "NYPL at Home" series drew over 500,000 virtual attendees in 2021, while in-person workshops saw a 25% rebound in 2022.

Strategy 2: Targeted Marketing to Underserved Demographics

Why It Works: CPL’s data showed that Latino and Black communities had lower engagement rates in 2021. Culturally relevant marketing can bridge this gap.

How to Implement:

  • Language Accessibility:
    • Offer Spanish-language programs (e.g., bilingual storytimes, ESL classes).
    • Example: The Pilsen Branch saw a 35% increase in visits after launching weekly Spanish story hours.
  • Community Partnerships:
    • Collaborate with local churches, nonprofits, and businesses to co-host events.
    • Example: The Englewood Branch partnered with a local food bank to offer job training workshops, increasing adult visits by 20%.
  • Digital Ads & Social Media:
    • Use Facebook, Instagram, and TikTok to target specific age groups.
    • Example: The Harold Washington Library ran a TikTok challenge (#CPLBookTok) that doubled teen engagement in 2022.

Metrics to Track:

  • Demographic breakdown of attendees
  • Social media engagement rates
  • New patron sign-ups

Strategy 3: Expand Maker & Tech Spaces to Attract Younger Audiences

Why It Works: Gen Z and millennials are less interested in books alone—they want hands-on, tech-driven experiences. CPL’s maker spaces (3D printers, coding labs) saw a 50% increase in teen usage in 2022.

How to Implement:

  • Add High-Demand Tech:
    • VR headsets (for virtual tours, gaming)
    • Podcasting studios (for youth media projects)
    • Robotics kits (for STEM programs)
  • Example: The Morton East Branch introduced a "Tech Tuesdays" series where teens could learn coding, 3D design, and drone piloting. Attendance jumped from 20 to 120 per session.
  • Partner with Local Tech Companies:
    • Example: The Newberry Library collaborated with Google’s Chicago office to offer AI workshops, drawing 200+ attendees.

Metrics to Track:

  • Usage of maker space equipment
  • New skill acquisition surveys
  • Social media buzz (hashtags like #CPLMakerSpace)

Strategy 4: Offer Incentives for Frequent Visitors (Loyalty Programs)

Why It Works: Gyms, coffee shops, and even grocery stores use loyalty programs—why not libraries? CPL’s pilot "Library Passport" program in 2022 increased repeat visits by 30%.

How to Implement:

  • Tiered Rewards System:
    • Bronze (5 visits): Free bookmark
    • Silver (10 visits): Free e-book credit
    • Gold (20 visits): Invitation to a private author event
  • Example: The Pullman Branch launched "Read & Reward", where kids earned badges for reading levels, boosting children’s visits by 25%.
  • Digital Integration:
    • Use QR codes at checkout to track visits automatically.

Metrics to Track:

  • Repeat visit rates
  • Program completion rates
  • Customer satisfaction surveys

Strategy 5: Host High-Impact Community Events (Not Just Book Clubs)

Why It Works: Generic book clubs don’t stand out—unique, community-driven events do. CPL’s 2021 "Chicago Stories" series (featuring local authors and historians) drew 15,000+ attendees across branches.

How to Implement:

  • Themed Months:
    • "Black History Month" with live discussions, film screenings, and guest speakers.
    • "Financial Literacy Week" with banking workshops and tax prep help.
  • Example: The Harold Washington Library hosted "Chicago’s Music Legacy" with live jazz performances and interviews with local musicians, tripling attendance for that month.
  • Pop-Up Events:
    • Example: The Englewood Branch set up a mobile food truck + job fair in the parking lot, attracting 300+ visitors in one day.

Metrics to Track:

  • Event attendance vs. average visits
  • Social media shares & local press coverage
  • Follow-up surveys on event impact
