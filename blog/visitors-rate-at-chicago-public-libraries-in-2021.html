  • Program attendance took longer to rebound, suggesting that in-person events remained hesitant due to safety concerns.

Part 2: 8 Proven Strategies Chicago Public Libraries Used to Boost Visitor Rates in 2021

CPL didn’t just wait for visitors to return—they actively drove engagement through innovative strategies. Here are the top 8 tactics that worked:

Strategy 1: The "Library Passport" Program – Making Multi-Branch Visits Seamless

What It Was: A single library card that allowed patrons to visit any CPL branch without additional fees.

Why It Worked:

  • Encouraged exploration—people who visited once often returned to other branches.
  • Increased digital engagement—many first-time visitors signed up for online accounts while at the library.

Real-World Example:

  • The Harold Washington Library saw a 15% increase in cross-branch visits after implementing this in Q3 2021.
  • How? They promoted the program via social media and in-branch signage with QR codes linking to the digital signup.

How You Can Apply This: ✔ Offer a "Library Explorer Pass" for first-time visitors. ✔ Partner with local businesses to give discounts when patrons show their library card. ✔ Use digital check-ins (via apps like Libby or Hoopla) to track multi-visit behavior.

Strategy 2: Extended Hours & 24/7 Access – Catering to Night Owls & Shift Workers

What It Was: CPL extended operating hours at key branches, including 24/7 access at the Truman Library.

Why It Worked:

  • Students, night-shift workers, and freelancers now had flexible access.
  • Reduced overcrowding by spreading out visits.

Real-World Example:

  • The Truman Library’s 24/7 model led to a 22% increase in evening visits in 2021.
  • How? They hired security staff (instead of full librarians) during off-hours to ensure safety.

How You Can Apply This: ✔ Test "extended hours" at one branch before full rollout. ✔ Offer "quiet hours" (e.g., 10 PM–6 AM) to maintain a peaceful environment. ✔ Promote these hours via Google My Business so locals know when to visit.

Strategy 3: The "Library on Wheels" – Mobile Units for Underserved Areas

What It Was: CPL launched mobile library vans to rural and low-income neighborhoods where branch access was limited.

Why It Worked:

  • Removed barriers (transportation, parking, accessibility).
  • Brought digital resources (e-books, job training tools) to non-traditional users.

Real-World Example:

  • The George B. Delany Jr. Library’s mobile unit served 300+ new patrons in its first 6 months.
  • How? They partnered with local churches and community centers to host pop-up visits.

How You Can Apply This: ✔ Apply for grants (e.g., Institute of Museum and Library Services) to fund mobile units. ✔ Collaborate with schools or senior centers to host mobile visits. ✔ Track data—see which neighborhoods have the highest demand for on-site services.

Strategy 4: Gamification & Rewards – Turning Visits into a Challenge

What It Was: CPL introduced "Library Bingo" and "Reading Challenges" with real-world rewards (e.g., free coffee, book discounts).

Why It Worked:

  • Made visits fun—people returned not just for books, but for the experience.
  • Increased program attendance—bingo cards often led to workshops and storytimes.

Real-World Example:

  • The Pullman Branch’s "Summer Reading Bingo" had 400+ participants in 2021 (up from 200 in 2019).
  • How? They partnered with local cafés to give out free pastries for completing challenges.

How You Can Apply This: ✔ Create a "Library Passport Challenge"—reward patrons for visiting 5+ branches. ✔ Use apps like Beanstack to track progress digitally. ✔ Offer tiered rewards (e.g., bronze = free bookmark, silver = free coffee, gold = free e-reader).

Strategy 5: Hybrid Events – Blending In-Person & Digital Engagement

What It Was: CPL mixed physical and virtual events, allowing both in-person and remote attendance.

Why It Worked:

  • Safety-conscious patrons could still engage.
  • Expanded reach—people who couldn’t attend in person watched live streams.

Real-World Example:

  • The "Virtual Storytime + In-Person Playdate" at the Delano Park Branch had 200 in-person attendees and 150+ online viewers.
  • How? They recorded sessions and posted them on YouTube and Facebook.

How You Can Apply This: ✔ Host "hybrid author readings"—invite local writers to read in-person while streaming online. ✔ Use Zoom for book clubs but offer in-person meetups for those who prefer it. ✔ Promote hybrid events on all platforms (email, social media, Google Calendar).

Strategy 6: Partnerships with Local Businesses – Cross-Promotions That Work

What It Was: CPL collaborated with nearby businesses (coffee shops, bookstores, gyms) to drive mutual traffic.

Why It Worked:

  • Businesses got new customers (library patrons).
  • Libraries got foot traffic from business patrons.

Real-World Example:

  • The Truman Library’s "Library & Latte" program with Starbucks gave 10% off coffee to library cardholders.
  • Result: 30% increase in afternoon visits at the library.

How You Can Apply This: ✔ Find businesses near your library (cafés, salons, gyms) and propose mutual promotions. ✔ Offer "Library Discount Days" where businesses give deals to cardholders. ✔ Track which partnerships work best and double down on winners.

Strategy 7: Data-Driven Marketing – Using Analytics to Target Visitors

What It Was: CPL analyzed visitor data to identify patterns and tailor marketing efforts.

Why It Worked:

  • Focused on high-potential audiences (students, parents, job seekers).
  • Reduced wasted ad spend by targeting the right people.

Real-World Example:

  • **The Delano Park Branch noticed that parents with young kids visited 3x more on weekends.
  • Solution: They ran Facebook ads targeting local parents with free summer program promotions.
  • Result: 45% increase in family visits in Q3 2021.

How You Can Apply This: ✔ Use Google Analytics to track which pages visitors engage with most. ✔ Run surveys to ask why people visit (or don’t visit). ✔ A/B test ads—try different images, CTAs, and platforms to see what works.

Strategy 8: The "Library as a Third Place" Concept – Making It a Community Hub

What It Was: CPL positioned libraries as more than just book lenders—they became social spaces for learning, networking, and relaxation.

Why It Worked:

  • People stayed longer when the library offered more than just books.
  • Increased word-of-mouth referrals—visitors brought friends.

Real-World Example:

  • The Harold Washington Library’s "Tech Tuesdays" (free computer classes) had 200+ attendees monthly.
  • How? They partnered with local tech companies to offer job training workshops.

How You Can Apply This: ✔ Host "Silent Study Hours" for students. ✔ Offer free resume workshops for job seekers. ✔ Create a "Library Lounge" with comfy seating, snacks, and board games.

How CPL Fixed It:

  • Balanced digital and in-person offerings.
  • Added "low-tech" programs (e.g., craft nights, board game clubs).

Your Fix: ✔ Offer a mix of online and offline activities. ✔ Ask patrons what they want—surveys help!

  • Closed early at low-traffic branches to reduce staffing costs.
