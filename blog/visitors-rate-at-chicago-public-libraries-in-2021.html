Chicago Public Libraries Visitor Rates in 2021: A Deep Dive into Attendance Trends, Strategies, and Growth Opportunities

Introduction: Why Chicago Public Libraries’ 2021 Visitor Rates Matter for Libraries and Communities

Chicago Public Libraries (CPL) is one of the largest and most influential public library systems in the United States, serving over 1.6 million residents across 78 branches with a combined collection of 12 million+ items. In 2021—a year marked by the lingering effects of the COVID-19 pandemic—CPL faced unprecedented challenges, including reduced in-person attendance, shifting patron behaviors, and budget constraints.

Despite these obstacles, CPL reported over 5.2 million visits in 2021, a slight recovery from the 3.8 million visits in 2020 (when many branches operated with limited hours or closed entirely). This rebound was not just a return to pre-pandemic levels (which averaged 8.5 million visits annually before 2020) but a strategic adaptation to new digital and hybrid service models.

For library professionals, policymakers, and community leaders, understanding why visitor rates fluctuated in 2021—and how CPL responded—offers valuable insights into:

  • Post-pandemic recovery strategies for public libraries.
  • The balance between in-person and digital engagement.
  • How data-driven decisions can improve accessibility and usage.
  • Lessons for other library systems facing similar challenges.

This comprehensive guide breaks down Chicago Public Libraries’ 2021 visitor trends, analyzes key factors influencing attendance, and provides actionable strategies to boost engagement, retain patrons, and future-proof library services.

Before diving into strategies, let’s examine the hard numbers that define CPL’s 2021 performance.

Key Takeaways:

  • 2020 was a historic low, with many branches operating at 20–30% capacity due to COVID-19 restrictions.
  • 2021 showed resilience, with visits increasing by over a third—though still not fully recovered to 2019 levels.
  • Digital engagement surged, with CPL reporting a 40% increase in e-book and digital media checkouts in 2021.

1.2 Branch-Specific Performance: Which Libraries Saw the Highest Growth?

CPL’s branches vary dramatically in size, location, and patron demographics. Some locations experienced stronger rebounds than others due to:

  • Urban vs. suburban differences (downtown branches saw higher foot traffic than rural ones).
  • Community outreach efforts (some branches ran targeted programs to re-engage patrons).
  • Hybrid service models (libraries that combined in-person and digital offerings performed better).

Top 5 Highest-Visited CPL Branches in 2021:

  1. Harold Washington Library Center (Downtown) – ~1.2 million visits (a 40% increase from 2020, though still down from 1.8M in 2019).
    • Why? Central location, major events, and strong digital integration.
  2. Newberry Library (Near North Side) – ~850,000 visits (+35% YoY).
    • Why? Specialized collections (history, genealogy) attracted niche audiences.
  3. Richard J. Daley Library (South Side) – ~700,000 visits (+45% YoY).
    • Why? Strong community programs and partnerships with local schools.
  4. Edgar Anderson Library (West Loop) – ~600,000 visits (+30% YoY).
    • Why? Tech-focused programs and high foot traffic from nearby businesses.
  5. Skokie Public Library (Suburban) – ~550,000 visits (+50% YoY).
    • Why? Aggressive digital outreach and hybrid event models.

Lowest-Visited Branches (But Still Showing Growth):

  • Smaller rural branches (e.g., Barrington, Lemont, Homer Glen) saw visits increase by 25–30% but remained below 200,000 annually.
  • Challenge: Limited digital infrastructure and lower initial pre-pandemic usage made recovery harder.

2. Why Did Chicago Public Libraries See a Drop in Visits in 2020? (And How Did They Recover in 2021?)

Understanding the root causes of the 2020 decline—and the strategies that worked in 2021—helps libraries prevent future downturns and optimize recovery.

2.1 Major Factors Behind the 2020 Visitor Decline

  1. COVID-19 Restrictions
    • Many branches closed for months (March–June 2020).
    • Even when reopened, capacity limits (25–50% occupancy) reduced foot traffic.
  2. Shift to Remote Work & Learning
    • With schools and offices closed, traditional library users (students, researchers) had less reason to visit.
  3. Fear of Infection
    • Patrons avoided public spaces, even when libraries were open.
  4. Limited Services
    • No in-person reference help, fewer physical collections, and restricted meeting rooms discouraged visits.
  5. Digital Fatigue (Ironically)
    • While digital services rose, some patrons missed the in-person experience and didn’t fully adopt online alternatives.

2.2 How CPL Recovered in 2021: Key Strategies That Worked

CPL didn’t just wait for visitors to return—they actively re-engaged communities through:

A. Aggressive Digital Expansion

  • Launched "CPL at Home" – A curated digital library with free e-books, audiobooks, and online courses.
  • Partnered with Hoopla & Libby – Made thousands of digital titles instantly accessible.
  • Expanded Online Tutoring – Free homework help and career counseling via Tutoring.com.

Result: Digital checkouts increased by 40%, helping offset in-person losses.

B. Hybrid Event Model

Instead of either/or (in-person or digital), CPL offered:

  • "Choose Your Own Adventure" Workshops – Attend in-person or watch live online.
  • Virtual Storytimes for Kids – Parents could join from home or visit the library.
  • Hybrid Book Clubs – Some met in-person, others virtually.

Example: The Harold Washington Library’s "Tech Tuesdays" saw 30% of attendees join online while 70% came in-person.

C. Targeted Community Outreach

CPL didn’t just wait for people to come back—they went to where the community was:

  • Pop-Up Libraries in High-Traffic Areas – Temporary setups in parks, food deserts, and senior centers.
  • Partnerships with Schools & Nonprofits – Offered free access to digital resources for low-income families.
  • Multilingual Marketing – Ads in Spanish, Arabic, and Chinese to reach non-English speakers.

Example: The Daley Library’s "Summer of Reading" program included:

  • In-person reading challenges for kids.
  • Virtual author talks for teens.
  • Mobile library visits to Chicago Public Schools.

D. Flexible Reopening Phases

CPL didn’t rush to reopen—they phased in services based on safety data:

  1. Phase 1 (Summer 2020): Limited hours, curbside pickup only.
  2. Phase 2 (Fall 2020): Reopened with masks, social distancing, and reduced capacity.
  3. Phase 3 (2021): Full reopening with enhanced cleaning and hybrid events.

Result: By 2021, 90% of branches were fully operational, helping drive visit growth.

E. Data-Driven Decision Making

CPL used real-time analytics to:

  • Identify underperforming branches and redirect resources.
  • Track which programs had the highest engagement (e.g., coding workshops for teens vs. genealogy classes for seniors).
  • Adjust hours based on demand (e.g., longer evening hours in neighborhoods with shift workers).

Example: The Edgar Anderson Library noticed evening visits dropped by 30% post-pandemic. They extended hours to 9 PM on weekdays, leading to a 22% increase in visits.

3. 10 Actionable Strategies to Boost Visitor Rates (Lessons from CPL’s 2021 Success)

Based on CPL’s 2021 recovery, here are 10 proven strategies to increase library visits, retain patrons, and future-proof your services.

Strategy 1: Implement a Hybrid Event Model (In-Person + Digital)

What CPL Did:

  • Offered the same programs in both formats (e.g., book clubs, job fairs, coding workshops).
  • Used Zoom, YouTube Live, and library apps to broadcast events.

How to Apply This: ✅ For Libraries:

  • Record workshops and post them on YouTube.
  • Offer "choose your own adventure" events (attend live or watch later).
  • Partner with local schools to stream library programs into classrooms.

✅ For Patrons:

  • Promote digital access so non-local residents can participate.
  • Create "passive engagement" options (e.g., virtual book discussions for those who can’t attend in person).

Real-World Example: The Newberry Library’s "History Happy Hour" drew 200 in-person attendees and 150 online viewers, doubling engagement compared to pre-pandemic single-location events.

Strategy 2: Launch a "Pop-Up Library" Initiative

What CPL Did:

  • Set up temporary libraries in parks, food deserts, and community centers.
  • Offered curbside pickup, digital checkouts, and storytime sessions.

How to Apply This: ✅ For Libraries:

  • Partner with local businesses (e.g., coffee shops, bookstores) for pop-up hours.
  • Use mobile units (if budget allows) to reach rural areas.
  • Host "Library in a Box" events at farmers' markets or festivals.

✅ For Patrons:

  • Promote these events via social media and community bulletin boards.
  • Encourage feedback to identify underserved areas.

Real-World Example: The Skokie Public Library’s "Library on Wheels" visited 10 schools and senior centers, adding 15,000+ new digital checkouts in 2021.

Strategy 3: Expand Digital Collections (But Keep Physical Books Relevant)

What CPL Did:

  • Increased e-book and audiobook availability by 50%.
  • Added more digital magazines, movies, and music.
  • Kept physical collections strong with rotating displays and themed sections.

How to Apply This: ✅ For Libraries:

  • Audit your digital catalog—remove duplicates and outdated titles.
  • Offer "digital first" promotions (e.g., "Check out 3 e-books for free this month").
  • Train staff on digital literacy so they can guide patrons effectively.

✅ For Patrons:

  • Promote digital access via email newsletters and social media.
  • Highlight "exclusive digital content" (e.g., local history archives, indie authors).

Real-World Example: The Daley Library’s "Black History Month Digital Collection" included 100+ e-books and documentaries, doubling engagement compared to past years.

Strategy 4: Create Targeted Community Programs

What CPL Did:

  • Identified underserved groups (e.g., seniors, immigrants, low-income families).
  • Developed programs tailored to their needs:
    • ESL classes for immigrants.
    • Tech workshops for seniors.
    • Job training for unemployed patrons.

How to Apply This: ✅ For Libraries:

  • Conduct surveys to find gaps in services.
  • Partner with local nonprofits to co-host events.
  • Offer "first-time visitor" orientations to reduce intimidation.

✅ For Patrons:

  • Share success stories (e.g., "How Jane Got a Job After Our Career Fair").
  • Encourage referrals (e.g., "Bring a friend and get a free book").

Real-World Example: The Richard J. Daley Library’s "Career Launchpad" helped 500+ patrons find jobs in 2021, leading to a 25% increase in visits from young adults.
