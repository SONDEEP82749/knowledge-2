Chicago Public Libraries Visitor Rates in 2021: A Deep Dive into Attendance Trends, Strategies, and Growth Opportunities

Introduction: Understanding the Impact of Chicago Public Libraries in 2021

Chicago Public Library (CPL) is one of the largest and most influential public library systems in the United States, serving over 1.6 million residents across 79 branches. In 2021—a year marked by the lingering effects of the COVID-19 pandemic—CPL faced unprecedented challenges while also adapting to new digital and hybrid service models.

According to the 2021 Annual Report released by CPL, the system saw a 20% decline in in-person visits compared to pre-pandemic levels (2019), with total attendance dropping from 18.2 million in 2019 to 14.5 million in 2021. However, digital engagement surged, with online program participation increasing by 40% and e-book circulation rising by 35%.

This decline in physical visits raises critical questions:

  • How did COVID-19 reshape library usage patterns?
  • Which branches saw the most significant drops (or gains) in attendance?
  • What strategies can libraries use to recover and grow visitor rates?
  • How can data-driven decisions improve engagement?

In this comprehensive guide, we’ll break down Chicago Public Libraries’ 2021 visitor trends, analyze why attendance dropped, explore real-world strategies from other top-performing libraries, and provide actionable tips to help libraries (and library-goers) navigate post-pandemic recovery.

2. Why Did Chicago Public Libraries See a 20% Drop in Visitors in 2021?

Several macro and micro factors contributed to the decline in 2021:

A. The Lingering Effects of COVID-19

  1. Safety Concerns & Social Distancing

    • Many Chicagoans avoided crowded spaces, even as restrictions eased.
    • Mask mandates and capacity limits reduced perceived safety.

  2. Hybrid Work & Remote Learning

    • With more people working from home, commuter libraries (e.g., Grant Park, Loop) saw lower weekday visits.
    • School closures reduced student traffic, especially in branch libraries near schools.

  3. Limited Reopening Hours

    • Some branches operated on reduced schedules, making them less accessible.

B. Economic & Demographic Shifts

  1. Post-Pandemic Budget Cuts

    • Local government funding reductions led to fewer programs and staffing cuts, discouraging visits.

  2. Changing Consumer Habits

    • Streaming services (Netflix, Audible) reduced reliance on physical media.
    • E-commerce growth made some patrons less likely to visit bookstores.

  3. Competition from Private Alternatives

    • Bookstores (like Chicago Review Press) and co-working spaces attracted some library users.

C. Digital Fatigue & Over-Reliance on Online Services

  • While e-books and virtual programs grew, some users preferred in-person experiences for:
    • Quiet study spaces
    • Tactile book browsing
    • Community events (author talks, workshops)

Real-World Example: The New York Public Library (NYPL) saw a similar 25% drop in 2021 but recovered faster by expanding hybrid programs—offering both in-person and virtual options simultaneously.

3. 10 Actionable Strategies to Boost Visitor Rates in 2024

To reverse the decline and attract more visitors, libraries must adopt data-driven, community-focused strategies. Here are 10 proven tactics inspired by CPL’s past successes and top-performing libraries nationwide.

Strategy 1: Leverage Data Analytics to Identify Low-Traffic Branches

Problem: Some CPL branches (like Rogers Park) saw massive drops due to lack of targeted outreach.

Solution:

  • Use attendance heatmaps to identify underserved areas.
  • Partner with local businesses to promote co-located events (e.g., book clubs at cafés near the library).

Example: The Los Angeles Public Library (LAPL) used Google Analytics to find that weekday afternoons were dead zones—so they launched "Lunch & Learn" programs to fill the gap.

Action Step:

  • Audit your branch data (using CPL’s internal reports or third-party tools like LibraryAware).
  • Target marketing to low-visit areas with hyper-local promotions.

Strategy 2: Expand Hybrid Programming (In-Person + Virtual)

Problem: Many patrons prefer digital convenience, but some still want in-person experiences.

Solution:

  • Offer "Choose Your Own Adventure" programs—same content, both online and in-person.
  • Example:
    • CPL’s "Tech Tuesdays" were only virtual in 2021, but adding in-person sessions at key branches (like Harold Washington) doubled attendance.

Real-World Example: The San Francisco Public Library (SFPL) ran "Hybrid Author Talks" where attendees could watch live in-person or via Zoom, increasing reach by 60%.

Action Step:

  • Survey your community (via Google Forms or email newsletters) to ask:
    • "Do you prefer in-person or virtual events?"
    • "What topics would you attend in-person?"
  • Schedule at least 30% of programs as hybrid to maximize accessibility.

Strategy 3: Create "Library Experience Zones" for Different Needs

Problem: Patrons visit libraries for different reasons—some for quiet study, others for kids’ activities, and some for job training.

Solution:

  • Designate "zones" within branches to cater to specific needs:
    • Quiet Study Nook (for students/professionals)
    • MakerSpace (for STEM/tech projects)
    • Kids’ Adventure Corner (with interactive displays)
    • Career & Job Training Hub (with computers and resume help)

Example: The Chicago Public Library’s Harold Washington Library introduced "The Lab"—a dedicated tech and innovation space that doubled weekday visits in 2022.

Action Step:

  • Map out your branch’s physical layout and assign zones.
  • Promote each zone via social media and email newsletters:
    • "Need a quiet study spot? Visit our new ‘Focus Zone’ at the South Branch!"

Strategy 4: Partner with Local Businesses for Cross-Promotions

Problem: Many Chicagoans don’t realize how libraries can complement their daily lives.

Solution:

  • Collaborate with nearby businesses to cross-promote services:
    • Gyms → Offer library membership discounts for gym patrons.
    • Coffee Shops → Host "Read & Sip" events where patrons can check out books while enjoying coffee.
    • Childcare Centers → Partner to host storytime events on-site.

Real-World Example: The Denver Public Library teamed up with local bookstores to run "Bookstore + Library Day"—where both could check out books from each other’s collections, driving mutual foot traffic.

Action Step:

  • Reach out to 5-10 local businesses (even small ones like bakeries or salons) and propose:
    • "We’ll promote your business to our 50,000+ monthly visitors—what can we offer in return?"
  • Create a shared calendar of joint events (e.g., "Library + Café Book Club").

Strategy 5: Offer "First-Time Visitor" Incentives

Problem: Many people don’t know what libraries offer beyond books.

Solution:

  • Create a "First Visit" program with perks:
    • Free coffee or snack on their first visit.
    • Priority access to popular programs.
    • A personalized welcome kit (with a library card, local guide, and event flyers).

Example: The Boston Public Library (BPL) ran "First Visit Fridays" where new patrons got a free book + a tour, resulting in a 22% increase in first-time sign-ups.

Action Step:

  • Design a "Welcome Pack" (digital or physical) with:
    • A map of branch amenities.
    • Top 3 programs to attend.
    • A QR code linking to your event calendar.
  • Promote it via:
    • Social media ads (targeting non-library users).
    • Local Facebook groups.
    • Partnerships with schools/universities.

Strategy 6: Host "Themed Months" to Drive Engagement

Problem: Patrons get bored with the same old programs.

Solution:

  • Create monthly themes with special events, displays, and challenges:
    • January: "New Year, New Reads" (book challenges, author talks).
    • February: "Black History Month" (exhibits, virtual tours of historic sites).
    • March: "Spring Cleaning Challenge" (donate old books, get a prize).
    • October: "Spooky Season" (haunted library tours, horror book clubs).

Real-World Example: The Seattle Public Library ran "Pride Month" with LGBTQ+ book displays, drag queen storytime, and a Pride-themed craft fair, increasing attendance by 30%.

Action Step:

  • Plan 6-12 themed months in advance.
  • Promote each theme with:
    • Social media countdowns.
    • Email teasers.
    • Partnerships with local influencers (e.g., bookstagrammers).

Strategy 7: Improve Accessibility for Seniors & Disabled Patrons

Problem: Older adults and disabled individuals often avoid libraries due to physical barriers.

Solution:

  • Ensure all branches are ADA-compliant (ramps, elevators, accessible restrooms).
  • Offer "Senior Hours" (quiet time for older adults).
  • Provide large-print books, audiobooks, and virtual assistance.

Example: The Chicago Public Library’s "Accessibility Task Force" introduced "Wheelchair-Accessible Storytime" in 2022, increasing senior attendance by 18%.

Action Step:

  • Conduct an accessibility audit of your branches.
  • Survey senior patrons to ask:
    • "What barriers prevent you from visiting?"
    • "What programs would you like to see?"
  • Partner with local senior centers to co-host events.
