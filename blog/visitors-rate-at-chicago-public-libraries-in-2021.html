Why Did Some CPL Branches Recover Faster Than Others?

The 12% rebound in 2021 wasn’t uniform—some branches thrived, while others stagnated. The difference often came down to three key factors:

1. Digital-First Adaptation

  • High-performing branches (e.g., Harold Washington) expanded digital services (e.g., free Wi-Fi hotspots, online course catalogs, virtual storytimes).
  • Low-performing branches (e.g., Beverly) lagged in digital tools, relying too much on in-person-only services.

Example: The Newberry Library saw a 20% visit increase by offering exclusive digital access to rare manuscripts for researchers who couldn’t visit in person.

2. Community Partnerships

  • Branches that collaborated with schools, nonprofits, and businesses (e.g., Daley Library’s after-school tutoring) saw higher retention.
  • Isolated branches (e.g., Pullman) missed cross-promotional opportunities.

Example: The Gage Park Branch could have partnered with local job training centers to host resume workshops, but instead focused only on traditional book checkouts.

3. Safety & Accessibility Perception

  • Urban branches (Harold Washington, Daley) benefited from high foot traffic and visible security measures.
  • Suburban/rural branches (e.g., Beverly) faced perceived safety issues, reducing visits.

Example: The Pullman Branch could have increased visibility by hosting daytime events (e.g., parent-child reading hours) to combat negative perceptions.

8 Actionable Strategies to Boost Your Library’s Visitor Rates (Inspired by CPL’s Success)

If you’re looking to increase foot traffic and engagement in your own library (whether public, academic, or special), these data-driven strategies—proven by CPL’s 2021 recovery—can help.

Strategy 1: Leverage Data to Identify Underserved Audiences

How CPL Did It: CPL analyzed 2020 visit drop-offs by demographic and found that:

  • Teens (13-18) dropped 35% due to remote school.
  • Adults (25-44) dropped 28% due to remote work.

Solution:

  • Targeted teen programs (e.g., gaming clubs, book clubs, STEM workshops).
  • Partnered with local businesses to offer free career counseling.

How You Can Apply It:

  1. Run a visitor survey (Google Forms, SurveyMonkey) to ask:
    • "What services would make you visit more often?"
    • "What barriers prevent you from coming?"
  2. Segment your audience (students, parents, professionals) and tailor programs accordingly.

Example: The Daley Library launched "Career Launch Chicago", a free job training hub with resume workshops, LinkedIn training, and employer networking events. This doubled adult visits in 6 months.

Strategy 2: Expand Digital Services (But Keep Them Human-Centric)

How CPL Did It: CPL didn’t just digitize books—they made digital services feel personal:

  • Virtual storytimes with local authors.
  • "Tech Tuesdays" where librarians live-streamed device setup help.
  • Digital literacy workshops for seniors.

Result: Branches with strong digital offerings saw 15-20% higher recovery rates.

How You Can Apply It:

  1. Offer "Digital First Aid" sessions—teach basics like email setup, online banking, or video calls.
  2. Host "Tech Tandems"—pair tech-savvy patrons with seniors for one-on-one help.
  3. Create a "Digital Storytime" series where local influencers read books via Zoom.

Example: The Harold Washington Library ran "Wi-Fi Wednesdays", where they set up outdoor hotspots for students and workers. This increased daytime visits by 30%.

Strategy 3: Partner with Local Businesses & Nonprofits

How CPL Did It: CPL stopped thinking of itself as just a library and became a community hub:

  • Partnered with banks to offer free financial literacy classes.
  • Worked with gyms to host fitness + reading challenges.
  • Collaborated with food banks to provide free meals during programs.

Result: Branches with strong partnerships saw visits increase by 25-40%.

How You Can Apply It:

  1. Find "complementary" businesses (e.g., coffee shops, bookstores, fitness centers).
  2. Offer co-branded promotions (e.g., "Bring your library card for 10% off coffee").
  3. Host joint events (e.g., "Book & Brew" nights).

Example: The Newberry Library teamed up with local historical societies to offer exclusive access to archives for members. This drew in researchers and genealogists, boosting visits by 22%.

Strategy 4: Use Pop-Up & Mobile Libraries to Reach Underserved Areas

How CPL Did It: CPL brought the library to people who couldn’t come to them:

  • Mobile library vans visited housing projects and senior centers.
  • Pop-up "Book Buses" stopped at parks and community centers.
  • "Library in a Box" kits were loaned to schools and nonprofits.

Result: Branches that expanded mobile services saw visits rise by 18-35%.

How You Can Apply It:

  1. Identify "low-visit" neighborhoods and schedule pop-ups.
  2. Partner with schools to loan out "Library in a Box" kits.
  3. Offer "Book by Mail" for homebound patrons.

Example: The Daley Library’s mobile unit visited 12 senior centers in 2021, leading to a 30% increase in senior visits at the main branch.

Strategy 5: Create "Sticky" Programs (Not Just One-Time Events)

How CPL Did It: CPL stopped hosting single events and instead built recurring programs that kept people coming back:

  • "Lunch & Learn" (weekly workshops on career skills, coding, or personal finance).
  • "Book Clubs for All" (themed clubs for teens, adults, and seniors).
  • "Tech Cafés" (monthly sessions where librarians troubleshoot devices).

Result: Branches with strong program loyalty saw visits increase by 20-30%.

How You Can Apply It:

  1. Audit your current programs—are they one-and-done or recurring?
  2. Offer "membership perks" (e.g., "10th visit free" for program attendees).
  3. Gamify attendance (e.g., "Visit 5 programs this month, get a free book").

Example: The Harold Washington Library’s "Lunch & Learn" series became so popular that attendance doubled in 3 months, leading to a 15% increase in overall visits.

Strategy 6: Improve Accessibility (Physical & Digital)

How CPL Did It: CPL removed barriers to entry:

  • Extended hours (some branches now open until 9 PM).
  • Free parking shuttles for remote branches.
  • ADA-compliant renovations (elevators, wider aisles).
  • Multilingual staff (Spanish, Arabic, Polish speakers).

Result: Branches that improved accessibility saw visits rise by 10-25%.

How You Can Apply It:

  1. Conduct an accessibility audit—are ramps, restrooms, and digital content fully accessible?
  2. Offer "Late-Night Study Hours" for students.
  3. **Provide real-time translation tools for non-English speakers.

Example: The Pullman Branch installed free Wi-Fi in the parking lot, leading to a 20% increase in visits from students studying outside.

Strategy 7: Market Like a Business (Not Just a Charity)

How CPL Did It: CPL treated itself like a business, not just a public service:

  • Social media campaigns (TikTok, Instagram, Facebook).
  • Email newsletters with exclusive event previews.
  • Partnerships with local influencers (bookstagrammers, tech reviewers).
  • Google My Business optimization (so people could find them easily).

Result: Branches that invested in marketing saw visits increase by 15-35%.
