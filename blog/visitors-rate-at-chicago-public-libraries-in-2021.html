Part 1: Chicago Public Library Visitor Rates in 2021 – The Numbers You Need to Know

Before diving into strategies, let’s examine the hard data that shaped CPL’s 2021 performance.

1. Overall Visitor Trends: From Pandemic Low to Recovery

  • 2019 (Pre-Pandemic): CPL saw 15.2 million visitors across all branches.
  • 2020 (Pandemic Closures): Visits dropped to 8.5 million due to lockdowns, reduced hours, and safety concerns.
  • 2021 (Rebound Year): Visits recovered to 12.1 million, a 42% increase from 2020 but still 20% below 2019 levels.

Key Takeaway: While CPL made progress, full recovery took longer than expected, suggesting that digital engagement alone couldn’t replace in-person visits.

  • Downtown and central branches (e.g., Harold Washington) saw moderate recovery due to business and tourist traffic.
  • Neighborhood libraries in diverse communities (e.g., Little Village, South Branch) exceeded expectations by leveraging local partnerships.
  • Smaller branches struggled more, indicating a digital divide in engagement.

CPL’s 2021 visitor data revealed shifts in who was returning to libraries:

Why the Shift?

  • Adults returned for job training, digital literacy, and quiet study spaces.
  • Teens stayed away due to preference for online gaming and social media.
  • Children had a slower rebound, likely because parents prioritized safety over in-person activities.

Part 2: 10 Proven Strategies Chicago Public Library Used to Boost Visitor Rates in 2021

CPL didn’t just wait for visitors to return—they actively drove engagement through innovative programs, partnerships, and data-driven decisions. Below are the top 10 strategies that worked in 2021, along with how other libraries can adapt them.

Strategy 1: Hybrid Programming – Blending In-Person and Digital Experiences

What CPL Did:

  • Live-streamed author talks but also offered in-person Q&As at select branches.
  • Virtual book clubs with optional in-person meetups for those comfortable returning.
  • "Library Passport" program, where patrons could earn badges for attending both online and in-person events.

Why It Worked:

  • Reduced pressure on in-person attendees by offering alternatives.
  • Attracted digital-native users who might not have visited before.

How Other Libraries Can Apply This: ✔ Offer "flexible attendance" for workshops (e.g., "Come in-person or watch later"). ✔ Create a "hybrid event leaderboard" to encourage participation. ✔ Partner with local schools to bring students who prefer online learning into the library.

Real-World Example: The Newberry Library ran a "Medieval Manuscripts Live" series where scholars presented both online and in-person, drawing 1,200 in-person attendees in 2021—double their pre-pandemic average.

Strategy 2: Targeted Marketing to First-Time Visitors

What CPL Did:

  • Launched a "First Visit" campaign with discounted memberships for new patrons.
  • Used hyper-local ads (e.g., Facebook targeted residents within a 1-mile radius of each branch).
  • Partnered with food delivery apps (Uber Eats, DoorDash) to promote library events in nearby neighborhoods.

Why It Worked:

  • First-time visitors accounted for 30% of 2021 growth—a 15% increase from 2020.
  • Hyper-local ads reduced wasted spend by focusing on high-potential areas.

How Other Libraries Can Apply This: ✔ Run "First Visit" promotions (e.g., "Your first month free"). ✔ Leverage Google My Business to appear in local searches ("libraries near me"). ✔ Collaborate with local businesses to cross-promote (e.g., coffee shops near the library).

Real-World Example: The South Branch of Harold Washington Library saw a 40% increase in first-time visitors after partnering with local churches and community centers to promote bilingual storytime events.

Strategy 3: Expanding Digital Literacy Programs for Adults

What CPL Did:

  • Launched "Tech Tuesdays"—weekly free workshops on resume writing, online banking, and cybersecurity.
  • Partnered with local employers to offer job training certifications (e.g., Google Career Certificates).
  • Created a "Digital Navigators" program, where senior volunteers helped older adults with laptop setup and internet access.

Why It Worked:

  • Adult visitors increased by 5%, with 30% of new attendees citing job training as their reason for visiting.
  • Reduced the "digital divide" by making tech access free and low-pressure.

How Other Libraries Can Apply This: ✔ Host "Tech Cafés" where patrons can drop in for quick help. ✔ Offer "Micro-certifications" (e.g., Excel basics, LinkedIn profile reviews). ✔ Train staff in "digital empathy"—understanding fear of tech among older adults.

Real-World Example: The Evergreen Park Library ran a "Job Ready Chicago" program in partnership with local businesses, leading to 200+ job placements in 2021—directly boosting foot traffic.

Strategy 4: Themed Months & Community Challenges

What CPL Did:

  • "Black History Month" wasn’t just books—it was a citywide scavenger hunt with QR codes leading to hidden historical facts in the library.
  • "Summer Reading Challenge" had physical and digital components, with prizes for both.
  • "Financial Literacy Month" included live debt counseling sessions and budgeting workshops.

Why It Worked:

  • Themed events increased average visit frequency by 20%—patrons came not just once, but multiple times.
  • Community challenges (like scavenger hunts) encouraged word-of-mouth marketing.

How Other Libraries Can Apply This: ✔ Create "library challenges" (e.g., "Visit 5 branches in a month for a free book"). ✔ Partner with local influencers to promote themed months. ✔ Use gamification (e.g., badges for attending 3+ events).

Real-World Example: The Little Village Library used "Latinx Heritage Month" to **host a bilingual poetry slam, which drew 800 attendees—triple their usual event size.

Strategy 5: Extending Hours & Weekend Access

What CPL Did:

  • Extended evening hours at high-demand branches (e.g., Harold Washington Library open until 9 PM on weekdays).
  • Added weekend access at 10 key branches, including 24-hour study rooms in downtown locations.
  • "Late Night Library" events for college students and night-shift workers.

Why It Worked:

  • Evening visitors increased by 25%, with students and professionals making up 40% of late-night attendees.
  • Weekend access reduced weekday congestion, making the library more accessible.

How Other Libraries Can Apply This: ✔ Survey your community to find peak demand times. ✔ Offer "flexible closing hours" (e.g., close early on slow days, stay late on busy ones). ✔ Partner with local businesses to extend hours during off-peak times.

Real-World Example: The Newberry Library introduced "Late Night Research Hours" for graduate students, leading to a 30% increase in weekday evening visits.

Strategy 6: Bilingual & Multicultural Programming

What CPL Did:

  • Doubled bilingual storytime hours (Spanish, Arabic, Polish, and Hmong).
  • Hired community liaisons who speak multiple languages to market events effectively.
  • "Cultural Heritage Months" with food trucks, live music, and language exchange meetups.

Why It Worked:

  • **Branches in diverse neighborhoods (e.g., Little Village, Bronzeville) saw 50%+ growth in 2021.
  • **Patrons who felt "seen" were more likely to return.

How Other Libraries Can Apply This: ✔ **Conduct a language needs assessment in your community. ✔ Hire or volunteer bilingual staff to facilitate events. ✔ Partner with cultural organizations (e.g., ethnic festivals, religious groups).

Real-World Example: The South Branch of Harold Washington Library ran a "Polish Heritage Festival" that drew 1,500 attendees, including many first-time visitors from the neighborhood.

Strategy 7: Data-Driven Branch Improvements

What CPL Did:

  • Analyzed foot traffic heatmaps to rearrange furniture for better flow.
  • Used Wi-Fi analytics to identify underused spaces (e.g., quiet zones vs. collaborative areas).
  • A/B tested event locations—some branches moved storytimes to the back to reduce congestion at the front desk.

Why It Worked:

  • Average visit duration increased by 15%—patrons stayed longer when the space was optimized.
  • Reduced bottlenecks at checkouts and circulation desks.

How Other Libraries Can Apply This: ✔ **Use library management software (e.g., SirsiDynix, Alma) to track patron movement. ✔ **Conduct user experience (UX) surveys to find pain points. ✔ **Experiment with space repurposing (e.g., turning a meeting room into a co-working hub).

Real-World Example: The Bronzeville Branch rearranged its children’s section based on data showing parents struggled to find books, leading to a 20% increase in family visits.

Strategy 8: Partnerships with Local Businesses & Nonprofits

What CPL Did:

  • "Library + Coffee Shop" collaborations—some branches partnered with local cafés to offer discounted drinks for library patrons.
  • Job training with local employers (e.g., Chicago Public Schools, Walmart, and healthcare providers).
  • "Pop-up libraries" in food deserts, where CPL temporarily set up tables in community centers.

Why It Worked:

  • Cross-promotion increased visits by 18% in partnering neighborhoods.
  • Nonprofits brought in new audiences (e.g., homeless shelters, senior centers).

How Other Libraries Can Apply This: ✔ **Find natural partners (e.g., gyms, bookstores, community colleges). ✔ **Offer co-branded memberships (e.g., "Library + Gym Pass").
