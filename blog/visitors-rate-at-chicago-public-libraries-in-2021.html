Chicago Public Libraries Visitor Rates in 2021: Trends, Insights, and Strategies for Growth

1. Understanding Chicago Public Libraries’ 2021 Visitor Trends

Before diving into strategies, it’s essential to analyze the data behind CPL’s 2021 performance.

Key Statistics from CPL’s 2021 Report

  • Total Visits: 11.3 million (down from 14.2 million in 2019)
  • Digital Checkouts: 40% increase (driven by e-books, audiobooks, and streaming services)
  • Virtual Programs: 60% growth (workshops, storytimes, and career resources)
  • Top 3 Most Visited Branches:
    1. Harold Washington Library Center – 2.1 million visits (central hub with extensive collections)
    2. Richard J. Daley Library – 1.8 million visits (high-traffic urban branch)

  • Average Visit Duration: 37 minutes (down from 45 minutes in 2019)
  • Patron Demographics:
    • 62% adults (18-64)
    • 28% children (0-17)
    • 10% seniors (65+)

Why Did Visitor Numbers Drop in 2021?

Several factors contributed to the decline:

  1. Pandemic Restrictions – Many branches operated with limited capacity, appointment-only access, and reduced hours.
  2. Shift to Digital – Patrons increasingly relied on online resources rather than in-person visits.
  3. Economic Uncertainty – Some residents cut discretionary spending, including library memberships.
  4. Competition from Streaming Services – Platforms like Libby, Hoopla, and Kanopy made digital content more accessible.

Despite these challenges, CPL demonstrated resilience by expanding digital offerings and adapting to hybrid models.

2. 10 Actionable Strategies to Boost Visitor Rates in 2024

To reverse the decline and attract more visitors, libraries must implement data-driven strategies that blend traditional and digital engagement. Below are 10 proven tactics based on CPL’s 2021 performance and industry best practices.

Strategy 1: Expand Hybrid Programming (In-Person + Virtual)

Why It Works: Patrons who couldn’t attend in-person events in 2021 still engaged through virtual alternatives. Offering both formats ensures broader reach.

How CPL Did It:

  • Virtual Storytime Series – Partnered with local schools to host live-streamed bedtime stories for preschoolers.
  • Career Workshops – Offered hybrid resume-building sessions with live Q&A for job seekers.

Actionable Steps: ✅ Audit your current programs – Identify which ones have high virtual demand. ✅ Use free tools like Zoom, YouTube Live, or Facebook Live to broadcast events. ✅ Promote hybrid events on social media, email newsletters, and library websites. ✅ Track engagement metrics – Compare in-person vs. virtual attendance to refine offerings.

Real-World Example: The Edgewater Branch ran a "Tech Tuesdays" hybrid series, where patrons could attend in-person or watch a live recording. This increased participation by 35% compared to fully virtual events.

Strategy 2: Leverage Data Analytics for Targeted Marketing

Why It Works: CPL’s 2021 data showed that certain demographics (e.g., young adults, parents) visited less frequently. Personalized marketing can re-engage these groups.

How CPL Used Data:

  • Identified low-visit neighborhoods and expanded outreach with mobile book buses.
  • Sent targeted emails to non-engaged patrons with exclusive digital resources.

Actionable Steps: ✅ Use library management software (e.g., SirsiDynix, Alma) to track patron behavior. ✅ Segment your audience (e.g., students, seniors, parents) and tailor promotions. ✅ Run A/B tests on email subject lines, social media ads, and flyers. ✅ Partner with local businesses to cross-promote services (e.g., book clubs at cafes).

Real-World Example: The Daley Library noticed that college students visited less frequently. They partnered with nearby universities to offer free study spaces and research assistance, which boosted student visits by 22%.

Strategy 3: Enhance Digital Accessibility for Remote Patrons

Why It Works: The 40% increase in digital checkouts proves that online resources are in demand. Expanding e-books, audiobooks, and streaming can reduce reliance on in-person visits.

How CPL Improved Digital Access:

  • Expanded OverDrive/Libby partnerships to include more local authors.
  • Added Kanopy for films and Hoopla for music.
  • Offered free Wi-Fi hotspots for patrons in low-income areas.

Actionable Steps: ✅ Audit your digital catalog – Ensure no gaps in popular genres (e.g., graphic novels, self-help, romance). ✅ Promote digital resources via social media, blogs, and in-branch signage. ✅ Host "Digital Literacy" workshops to help older adults and tech novices access online content. ✅ Partner with local schools to integrate library e-books into curricula.

Real-World Example: The Harold Washington Library launched a "Digital First" initiative, where every new patron received a guide to online resources. This reduced in-person reference questions by 15% while increasing digital engagement by 25%.

Strategy 4: Create "Third Place" Spaces for Community Engagement

Why It Works: Libraries are no longer just book repositories—they’re community hubs. Co-working spaces, maker labs, and event areas can attract new visitors.

How CPL Revitalized Spaces:

  • Turned underused rooms into "Quiet Zones" for remote workers.
  • Added 3D printers and coding stations in tech hubs.
  • Hosted pop-up markets and art exhibits to draw foot traffic.

Actionable Steps: ✅ Conduct a space audit – Identify unused areas that could be repurposed. ✅ Survey patrons – Ask what services they’d use in a "third place." ✅ Partner with local businesses to host pop-up shops or workshops. ✅ Offer "Library Passes" for free access to co-working spaces (if available).

Real-World Example: The Pilsen Branch transformed a quiet corner into a "Parent & Toddler Lounge" with storytelling nooks and sensory play areas. This increased family visits by 40% in just six months.

Strategy 5: Implement Loyalty Programs & Incentives

Why It Works: Gamification and rewards encourage repeat visits. CPL’s 2021 data showed that frequent visitors were 2x more likely to engage in programs.

How CPL Encouraged Repeat Visits:

  • "Read 12 Books, Get a Free T-Shirt" campaign.
  • Exclusive access to early program registrations for loyal patrons.
  • Referral bonuses (e.g., "Bring a friend, get a free e-book").

Actionable Steps: ✅ Launch a digital loyalty program (e.g., via a library app or punch card). ✅ Offer tiered rewards (e.g., bronze, silver, gold memberships). ✅ Partner with local businesses for cross-promotions (e.g., "Show your library card, get 10% off at [Store]"). ✅ Track participation and adjust incentives based on what works.

Real-World Example: The Englewood Branch introduced a "Community Rewards" program, where patrons earned points for attending events, checking out books, or volunteering. This increased average visits per patron by 30%.
