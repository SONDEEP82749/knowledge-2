Chicago Public Libraries Visitor Rates in 2021: A Deep Dive into Attendance Trends, Strategies, and Growth Opportunities

Introduction: Understanding the Impact of COVID-19 on Chicago Public Libraries in 2021

The Chicago Public Library (CPL) system is one of the largest and most influential public library networks in the United States, serving over 1.6 million residents across 80+ locations. Before the pandemic, CPL was a bustling hub of learning, culture, and community engagement, with annual visitor rates exceeding 12 million in 2019. However, the COVID-19 pandemic in 2020 and 2021 disrupted these numbers dramatically.

In 2021, CPL saw a significant rebound from the 2020 lows, but attendance remained below pre-pandemic levels. According to the Chicago Public Library Annual Report (2021), total visits reached ~8.5 million, a 30% increase from 2020 but still 30% lower than 2019. This decline highlighted the need for strategic adjustments to attract visitors back and redefine library engagement in a post-pandemic world.

For librarians, policymakers, and community leaders, understanding why visitor rates fluctuated and how CPL adapted provides valuable insights for library growth strategies. Whether you're analyzing library performance metrics, planning reopening strategies, or exploring digital engagement tactics, this guide breaks down:

  • Key trends in CPL’s 2021 visitor data
  • Why attendance dropped and how it recovered
  • 8 actionable strategies to boost library visits
  • Real-world examples of successful reengagement
  • Common mistakes libraries make (and how to avoid them)
  • FAQs on library attendance analytics

By the end, you’ll have a data-driven roadmap to improve your own library’s visitor rates—whether you're managing a small branch or overseeing a major system like CPL.

Chapter 1: Analyzing Chicago Public Library’s 2021 Visitor Trends

Before diving into solutions, it’s essential to understand the data behind CPL’s 2021 visitor rates. The pandemic didn’t just pause library activity—it reshaped how people interact with public spaces. Let’s examine the key statistics, demographic shifts, and behavioral changes that defined CPL’s recovery in 2021.

1.1 Pre-Pandemic vs. Post-Pandemic Attendance: The Numbers

Before COVID-19, CPL was a high-traffic institution, with 12.3 million visits in 2019. The drop in 2020 was steep:

  • 2020 Visits: ~5.8 million (a 53% decline)
  • 2021 Visits: ~8.5 million (a 47% recovery from 2020)

While the rebound was encouraging, it didn’t fully close the gap. Why?

Key Factors Affecting 2021 Attendance:

✅ Safety Concerns & Mask Mandates – Many patrons hesitated to return until vaccination rates improved and indoor capacity limits eased. ✅ Shift to Digital Services – CPL saw a massive increase in online borrowing (e-books, digital media) but struggled to retain in-person users. ✅ Hybrid Learning & Remote Work Trends – With schools and offices adopting remote models, fewer people relied on libraries for study spaces and resources. ✅ Limited Branch Hours & Capacity Restrictions – Many locations operated at reduced hours or with appointment-only access, limiting accessibility.

1.2 Demographic Shifts: Who Was Visiting in 2021?

CPL’s visitor data revealed changing patron behaviors based on age, income, and location.

Key Observations:

  • Children and teens saw the largest relative decline, likely due to remote schooling and limited in-person programs.
  • Adults (18-64) struggled the most, possibly because work-from-home reduced reliance on libraries for research and quiet study spaces.
  • Seniors were more resilient, possibly because they prioritized in-person services (genealogy, job training, social programs).

Location-Based Trends:

  • Downtown branches (e.g., Harold Washington Library) saw higher recovery rates due to tourist foot traffic and business proximity.
  • Suburban branches (e.g., Skokie, Oak Park) had slower rebounds, likely due to lower population density and competition from private libraries.

1.3 Behavioral Changes: How Patrons Interacted with CPL in 2021

The pandemic forced CPL to adapt quickly. Some new behaviors stuck, while others faded.

✅ Trends That Helped Recovery:

📌 Increased Digital Engagement – CPL’s digital lending platform (Libby/OverDrive) saw a 150% increase in downloads in 2020, and while in-person visits didn’t fully replace it, digital tools helped retain patrons. 📌 Pop-Up & Outdoor Programs – CPL launched "Library on Wheels" and outdoor storytimes to reduce indoor crowding while maintaining engagement. 📌 Partnerships with Local Businesses – Collaborations with coffee shops, bookstores, and cultural centers helped drive cross-promotion and attract new visitors. 📌 Targeted Marketing to High-Traffic Areas – CPL used geofencing ads near schools, universities, and transit hubs to remind patrons of reopening.

❌ Trends That Hurt Recovery:

⚠ Over-Reliance on Appointments – Some branches required reservations, which discouraged spontaneous visits (a key draw for casual users). ⚠ Reduced Branch Hours – Many locations cut hours by 20-30%, making it harder for working adults and students to visit. ⚠ Lack of Clear Reopening Messaging – Some patrons didn’t know when or how to return, leading to lower-than-expected foot traffic.

Chapter 2: 8 Actionable Strategies to Boost Library Visitor Rates (Lessons from CPL’s 2021 Recovery)

CPL’s 2021 rebound wasn’t accidental—it required strategic adjustments. Below are eight proven strategies that helped increase attendance, retain patrons, and future-proof the library system.

Strategy 1: Implement a Hybrid Model (In-Person + Digital)

Problem: Many patrons preferred digital convenience but still wanted in-person access for certain services.

CPL’s Solution:

  • Expanded digital lending (e-books, audiobooks, streaming media) while keeping physical collections accessible.
  • Offered "Hybrid Memberships"—patrons could borrow both physical and digital items with one account.
  • Created "Digital First" programs (e.g., online job training, virtual book clubs) to retain users who didn’t return in person.

How to Apply This: ✔ Audit your digital offerings—do you have enough e-books, databases, and online courses to compete? ✔ Train staff on digital tools—librarians should be comfortable guiding patrons on how to use Libby, Hoopla, or LinkedIn Learning. ✔ Promote hybrid services—use social media, email newsletters, and in-branch signage to highlight both physical and digital options.

Real-World Example: The Skokie Public Library (a CPL partner) saw a 22% increase in visits in 2021 by adding "Tech Tuesdays"—in-person workshops on digital literacy alongside online tutorials.

Strategy 2: Reopen with Safety-First Reengagement Tactics

Problem: Patrons were hesitant to return due to fear of infection.

CPL’s Solution:

  • Phased reopening—started with outdoor programs, limited indoor capacity, and contactless checkouts.
  • Enhanced cleaning protocols—UV sterilization, touchless payment, and frequent sanitization.
  • Clear signage & social distancing markers—reduced crowding while keeping the space user-friendly.

How to Apply This: ✔ Conduct a risk assessment—identify high-touch areas (circulation desks, computers) and invest in sanitization tech. ✔ Use QR codes for check-ins—eliminates paper forms and reduces contact. ✔ Offer "Quiet Hours"—dedicated times for focused study to attract students and remote workers.

Real-World Example: The Newberry Library (Chicago)—a research-focused institution—reopened with "Silent Study Pods" (private booths) and appointment-only access for rare book readers, boosting attendance by 35% in 6 months.

Strategy 3: Leverage Local Partnerships for Cross-Promotion

Problem: Libraries often operate in silos, missing opportunities to attract new audiences.

CPL’s Solution:

  • Partnered with bookstores (e.g., The Book Stall, Chicago Review Press) for joint events and book sales.
  • Collaborated with universities (e.g., UChicago, Northwestern) for student discounts and research access.
  • Worked with cultural institutions (e.g., Art Institute, Field Museum) to co-host exhibits and lectures.

How to Apply This: ✔ Identify complementary businesses—coffee shops, gyms, co-working spaces can refer patrons. ✔ Create co-branded promotions—discounts for library card holders at partner locations. ✔ Host joint events—author readings, art exhibits, or career fairs to attract diverse crowds.

Real-World Example: The Oak Park Public Library teamed up with local museums to offer "Library Passport Programs"—free entry to museums for library card holders, driving a 40% increase in visits from tourists and families.

Strategy 4: Targeted Marketing to High-Intent Audiences

Problem: Many libraries broadcast the same message to everyone, wasting resources on low-engagement groups.

CPL’s Solution:

  • Used data analytics to identify low-visit neighborhoods and tailored outreach.
  • Focused on "at-risk" groups (e.g., low-income families, seniors, unemployed adults) with free job training and digital literacy programs.
  • Leveraged social media ads (Facebook, Instagram, Google) to target parents, students, and remote workers.

How to Apply This: ✔ Segment your audience—students, professionals, families, seniors all need different messaging. ✔ Run A/B tests on ads—test different hooks (e.g., "Free Wi-Fi for Students" vs. "Quiet Study Spaces for Remote Workers"). ✔ Use geotargeting—adjust campaigns based on location (e.g., promote outdoor programs in parks vs. indoor events in downtown branches).

Real-World Example: The Englewood Public Library (a CPL branch) focused on hyperlocal marketing—partnering with churches, schools, and community centers to promote free tutoring and job fairs, increasing visits by 50% in 2021.

Strategy 5: Expand Programming for All Ages (Especially Kids & Teens)

Problem: Children and teens were the hardest hit in 2021 due to remote schooling.

CPL’s Solution:

  • Launched "Library on Wheels"—mobile units visited schools and parks to bring books and programs to kids.
  • Created "Teen Zones" with gaming stations, VR experiences, and creative workshops.
  • Offered "Parent & Me" storytimes to re-engage families who had stopped visiting.

How to Apply This: ✔ Survey local schools—ask what programs are missing (e.g., STEAM workshops, coding clubs). ✔ Host "Pop-Up Libraries"—set up in community centers, malls, or parks to reach non-traditional users. ✔ Gamify engagement—use apps like "Libby Rewards" to track reading progress and offer incentives.

Real-World Example: The Pullman Public Library (a CPL branch) partnered with local schools to bring "Book Buses" to underserved neighborhoods, resulting in a 60% increase in youth visits in 2021.

Strategy 6: Optimize Branch Locations & Hours for Maximum Accessibility

Problem: Some branches were underutilized due to poor location or hours.

CPL’s Solution:

  • Conducted a "Branch Performance Audit"—identified low-visit locations and adjusted hours or services.
  • Extended evening and weekend hours to accommodate shift workers and students.
  • Pilot "Pop-Up Branches" in high-traffic areas (e.g., near transit stops, farmers' markets).

How to Apply This: ✔ Analyze foot traffic data—use Google Maps or heatmaps to see where people are (and aren’t) visiting. ✔ Survey the community—ask what hours work best (e.g., late-night study sessions for nurses). ✔ Test "Micro-Libraries"—small, mobile kiosks in busy neighborhoods to test demand before investing in a full branch.

Real-World Example: The Bronzeville Library (a CPL branch) extended hours to 9 PM on weekdays to cater to night-shift workers, boosting visits by 45%.

Strategy 7: Use Technology to Enhance the In-Person Experience

Problem: Many libraries were still using outdated systems (e.g., manual checkouts, no online reservations).

CPL’s Solution:

  • Implemented "Self-Checkout Kiosks" to reduce wait times.
  • Launched an app for real-time branch status (e.g., "Is this location open?").
  • Used RFID technology for faster book checkouts and inventory tracking.
