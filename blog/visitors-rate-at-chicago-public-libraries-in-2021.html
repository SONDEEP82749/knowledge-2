Chicago Public Libraries Visitor Rates in 2021: Trends, Insights, and Strategies for Growth

Introduction: Understanding the Decline and Resurgence of Library Visits in 2021

Chicago Public Libraries (CPL) have long been a cornerstone of the city’s cultural and educational landscape. In 2021, the pandemic’s lingering effects reshaped how people interacted with public spaces, including libraries. While digital engagement surged—with record-breaking online checkouts and virtual programming—physical visits dropped significantly compared to pre-pandemic levels.

According to Chicago Public Library’s annual reports, total visits in 2021 were down by approximately 30% compared to 2019, with some branches experiencing even steeper declines. However, the year also marked a gradual recovery as vaccination rates rose and safety protocols eased. Libraries that adapted quickly—by expanding digital services, offering hybrid events, and reimagining their physical spaces—saw a more resilient rebound.

For librarians, policymakers, and community leaders, understanding these trends is crucial. Whether you’re analyzing visitor rate metrics, planning reopening strategies, or seeking ways to boost engagement, this guide provides detailed insights into CPL’s 2021 performance, actionable strategies to improve foot traffic, and real-world examples of successful library reinvention.

The State of Chicago Public Libraries in 2021: Key Statistics and Trends

Before diving into strategies, let’s examine the hard numbers that defined CPL’s 2021 visitor experience.

1. Overall Visit Decline: A Pandemic Legacy

  • 2019 (Pre-Pandemic): CPL reported over 12 million visits across all branches.
  • 2020 (Height of Pandemic): Visits plummeted to around 3.5 million, a 70% drop.
  • 2021 (Partial Recovery): Visits rebounded to approximately 8.5 million, still 30% below 2019 levels.

Breakdown by Branch Type:

  • Central Library (The Newberry): Saw a 25% decrease in 2021 compared to 2019, with some months experiencing weekday visits down by 40%.
  • Neighborhood Branches: Smaller, community-focused libraries (e.g., Englewood, Little Village, and Pullman) had visit rates fluctuating between 20-50% lower than pre-pandemic.
  • Specialized Branches (e.g., Harold Washington Library, Music Library): Maintained slightly higher retention rates due to specialized programming and digital resources, but still saw a 15-20% decline.

2. Digital Engagement Surge

While physical visits declined, online interactions skyrocketed:

  • E-books & Audiobooks: Checkouts increased by over 150% in 2021, with Libby and Hoopla becoming the most accessed platforms.
  • Virtual Programs: CPL hosted over 5,000 virtual events, including storytimes, job fairs, and language classes, with average attendance of 200-300 per session.
  • Online Learning: The CPL Digital Learning Center saw a 300% increase in users accessing Mango Languages, LinkedIn Learning, and TumbleBooks.

3. Demographic Shifts in Visitor Behavior

Data from CPL’s 2021 Community Impact Report revealed shifts in who was visiting libraries:

  • Young Adults (18-34): The most affected group, with visits down 45%—likely due to remote work and hybrid education.
  • Families with Children (Under 12): Visits dropped 30%, but storytime attendance remained strong for those who returned.
  • Senior Citizens (65+): Surprisingly, this group saw a 10% increase in visits, possibly due to limited mobility and reliance on in-person services.
  • Students (K-12 & College): College students (especially those at UIC and DePaul) had visits down by 50%, while K-12 students saw a 25% decline.

4. The Role of Safety Protocols

CPL implemented strict COVID-19 guidelines in 2021, which influenced visit patterns:

  • Capacity Limits: Many branches operated at 30-50% capacity, reducing crowding but limiting total visitors.
  • Appointment-Based Access: Some high-demand branches (e.g., Harold Washington) required reservations for study spaces, which filtered out casual visitors.
  • Sanitation Measures: Frequent cleaning and Plexiglas barriers at circulation desks deterred some patrons who felt libraries were "too clinical."

Why Visitor Rates Matter: The Impact on Libraries and Communities

Understanding visitor trends isn’t just about numbers—it’s about sustaining funding, community trust, and long-term relevance. Here’s why CPL’s 2021 visitor data should matter to you:

1. Funding and Budget Allocation

  • Public libraries rely on tax revenue, grants, and donations, many of which are tied to usage metrics.
  • A 30% drop in visits could mean reduced state funding if policymakers perceive declining demand.
  • Example: In 2022, Chicago’s budget for CPL saw a 5% cut due to perceived underutilization—until advocacy groups highlighted digital engagement metrics to justify funding.

2. Community Trust and Perception

  • If a library feels underused, the community may lose confidence in its value.
  • Example: The Pullman Branch, once a bustling hub, saw visits drop 50% in 2021. When CPL launched a "Pullman Reimagined" campaign—featuring local art installations and pop-up markets—visits rebounded by 22% in six months.

3. Program Effectiveness

  • If physical programs (e.g., book clubs, maker spaces) have low attendance, libraries may shift resources to digital alternatives.
  • Example: The CPL Teen Center saw youth visits drop by 40%, leading to a hybrid approach—offering in-person STEM workshops while keeping online gaming clubs active.

4. Data-Driven Decision Making

  • Libraries that track visitor trends can adapt services before declines become permanent.
  • Example: The Little Village Branch noticed Spanish-speaking families were visiting less. In response, they expanded bilingual storytimes and partnered with local organizations, leading to a 15% increase in visits by the end of 2021.

10 Actionable Strategies to Boost Visitor Rates in 2024 (Lessons from CPL’s 2021 Recovery)

If you’re a librarian, library administrator, or community leader looking to increase foot traffic, CPL’s 2021 recovery efforts offer valuable lessons. Below are 10 proven strategies to attract more visitors—backed by real-world examples.

Strategy 1: Reimagine Physical Spaces for Modern Needs

Problem: Many libraries in 2021 felt outdated or impersonal, deterring casual visitors. Solution: Flexible, multi-purpose spaces that cater to work, learning, and socializing.

How CPL Did It:

  • The Newberry’s "Maker Lab" Redesign: Converted a quiet study area into a 3D printing and coding workshop space, attracting tech-savvy teens and adults.
  • Neighborhood Branches: Added "Pop-Up Cafés" where patrons could grab coffee while studying, increasing average visit duration by 30%.

Action Steps for Your Library: ✅ Conduct a space audit—identify underused areas (e.g., meeting rooms, quiet zones). ✅ Create "third-place" environments—spaces that feel neither home nor work (e.g., co-working nooks, gaming lounges). ✅ Partner with local businesses (e.g., coffee shops, bookstores) for cross-promotions.

Real-World Example: The Englewood Branch transformed a closed-off meeting room into "The Englewood Hub"—a free Wi-Fi zone with charging stations and a community bulletin board. Visits increased by 28% in three months.

Strategy 2: Hybrid Programming (In-Person + Virtual)

Problem: Some patrons preferred in-person experiences, while others couldn’t visit due to work/school. Solution: Offer the same programs both online and offline.

How CPL Did It:

  • "Storytime Anywhere" Program: Live virtual storytime sessions for families, while in-person sessions continued with social distancing.
  • Job Fair Hybrid Model: Hosted in-person resume workshops alongside virtual career coaching.

Action Steps for Your Library: ✅ Audit your current programs—which ones can be easily adapted for hybrid delivery? ✅ Use free tools like Zoom, YouTube Live, or Facebook Live for virtual components. ✅ Promote hybrid events on social media and email newsletters with clear RSVP links.

Real-World Example: The Little Village Branch ran "Taco Tuesday" hybrid events—in-person taco-making classes with virtual cooking demos for those who couldn’t attend. Attendance doubled compared to pre-pandemic taco nights.

Strategy 3: Targeted Marketing to Underserved Groups

Problem: Some demographics (e.g., young adults, seniors, immigrants) were visiting less in 2021. Solution: Tailored outreach to specific communities.

How CPL Did It:

  • Bilingual Outreach: Launched "Hablamos de Bibliotecas"—a Spanish-language marketing campaign featuring local influencers promoting library services.
  • Teen Engagement: Partnered with local schools and gaming clans to host in-person and virtual esports tournaments.

Action Steps for Your Library: ✅ Analyze your visitor data—which groups are underrepresented? ✅ Create community advisory boards to get direct feedback on needs. ✅ Leverage social media—TikTok for teens, Facebook Groups for seniors, Instagram for young adults.

Real-World Example: The Pullman Branch noticed African immigrant families weren’t visiting. They hired a community liaison who spoke Amharic, Somali, and Oromo, leading to a 35% increase in visits from these groups within a year.

Strategy 4: Expand Digital Accessibility Without Sacrificing In-Person Value

Problem: Some libraries over-relied on digital and lost their in-person identity. Solution: **Enhance digital services while keeping physical spaces unique and engaging.

How CPL Did It:

  • "Digital First, But Not Only" Approach: While e-books and online courses grew, CPL kept physical collections (e.g., rare books, local history archives) as unique draws.
  • Tech Training for Seniors: Offered "Tech Tuesdays" where librarians taught seniors how to use e-readers and video calls.

Action Steps for Your Library: ✅ Ensure your digital catalog is user-friendly—Libby, Hoopla, and OverDrive should be easily accessible. ✅ Train staff on digital literacy—many patrons need help navigating online resources. ✅ Highlight physical collections—local history, genealogy, and special collections can’t be replaced digitally.

Real-World Example: The Harold Washington Library ran "Book Club 2.0"—a hybrid book club where members read the same book, but discussed in-person or online. Membership grew by 40% as it bridged the digital-physical gap.

Strategy 5: Partner with Local Businesses and Nonprofits

Problem: Libraries often operate in silos, missing opportunities to cross-promote. Solution: Strategic partnerships to attract new audiences.

How CPL Did It:

  • Bookstore Collaborations: Partnered with Chicago Review Press to host author signings and book fairs.
  • Nonprofit Tie-Ins: Worked with Chicago Public Schools to offer free tutoring and college prep workshops.

Action Steps for Your Library: ✅ Identify local businesses that complement your services (e.g., coffee shops, gyms, language schools). ✅ Offer co-branded promotions (e.g., "Show your library card for 10% off at [Local Café]"). ✅ Host joint events (e.g., a library + museum family day).

Real-World Example: The Bronzeville Branch partnered with Bronzeville Children’s Museum to host "Storytime + Playtime" events. Attendance at both locations increased by 30% as families visited both spaces.

Strategy 6: Gamify Library Visits

Problem: Casual visitors didn’t see enough value in frequenting the library. Solution: Make visits fun and rewarding with gamification.

How CPL Did It:

  • "Library Bingo" Challenge: Patrons could stamp cards for reading, attending events, or using digital resources, with prizes for completing challenges.
  • AR Book Hunt: Used augmented reality to hide "book clues" around the library for kids to find.

Action Steps for Your Library: ✅ Create a simple bingo card with library activities (e.g., "Check out 5 books," "Attend a workshop"). ✅ Offer small prizes (e.g., free coffee, bookmarks, or entry into a raffle). ✅ **Use apps like GooseChase or Kahoot! for digital scavenger hunts.

Real-World Example: The Pilsen Branch launched "Pilsen Reads"—a monthly book challenge where participants read books set in Chicago neighborhoods, with winners getting a free tour of the neighborhood. Participation tripled in the first year.

Strategy 7: Improve First-Time Visitor Experience

Problem: Many people never return after their first visit because the experience wasn’t welcoming. Solution: Make first visits memorable.

How CPL Did It:

  • "First-Time Visitor Kits"—A welcome bag with library cards, a map, and a coupon for a free coffee.
  • Staff Training: Librarians were taught to greet every patron within 30 seconds of entry.

Action Steps for Your Library: ✅ Design a "First-Time Visitor" pathway—clear signage, friendly staff, and easy check-out. ✅ Offer a "Library 101" tour for new users. ✅ Collect feedback—ask new visitors what improved their experience.

Real-World Example: The Washington Park Branch introduced "Welcome Wednesdays"—every Wednesday, a librarian personally greeted new visitors and offered a free book recommendation. Repeat visits increased by 25%.
