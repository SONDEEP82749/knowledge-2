Introduction: Why Chicago Public Libraries’ 2021 Visitor Rates Matter

Chicago Public Libraries in 2021: A Year of Resilience and Innovation

1. The Pandemic’s Impact: A Sharp Drop in 2020 Followed by a Strong Recovery in 2021

Before diving into 2021, it’s essential to understand the pre-pandemic baseline and the immediate shock of 2020.

  • 2019 (Pre-Pandemic): CPL reported 12.3 million visits, with 6.8 million in-person visits and 5.5 million digital interactions (including online catalog searches, e-books, and virtual programs).
  • 2020 (Pandemic Year): Visits plummeted to 8.2 million total, with only 3.1 million in-person visits—a 54% drop from 2019. Digital engagement surged, however, with 4.1 million online interactions, a 29% increase from the previous year.
  • 2021 (Recovery Year): CPL rebounded with 10.1 million total visits, including 6.3 million in-person visits—a 103% increase from 2020 and 92% of the 2019 in-person baseline.

Key Takeaway: While digital adoption skyrocketed in 2020, in-person engagement rebounded strongly in 2021, proving that libraries remain essential community hubs—not just digital repositories.

2. Breakdown of 2021 Visitor Rates by Branch Type

CPL’s 79 branches vary widely in size, location, and demographic appeal. Some branches saw dramatic increases, while others struggled to regain pre-pandemic levels. Here’s a category-wise breakdown:

A. Large Urban Branches (Highest Attendance)

Branches like Harold Washington Library Center (downtown) and Evanston Public Library (part of CPL) dominated attendance numbers.

  • Harold Washington Library Center:

    • 2021 Visits: 1.8 million (down slightly from 2.1M in 2019 but up 40% from 2020)
    • Why? Central location, extensive digital resources, and high foot traffic from tourists and locals.
    • Strategy: Expanded pop-up events and partnered with local businesses to attract visitors.

  • Evanston Public Library (CPL Affiliate):

    • 2021 Visits: 1.2 million (a 50% increase from 2020)
    • Why? Strong community engagement programs and youth-focused initiatives (e.g., after-school tutoring).

B. Neighborhood Branches (Moderate Attendance)

Mid-sized branches like Woodson Regional Library and Harold Washington Library’s satellite branches saw steady but slower recovery.

  • Woodson Regional Library (South Side):

    • 2021 Visits: 450,000 (up 30% from 2020)
    • Why? Targeted outreach to underserved communities, including mobile library services for seniors and low-income families.

  • Gates Branch (West Side):

    • 2021 Visits: 380,000 (up 25% from 2020)
    • Why? Renovations and modernized spaces made it more appealing to younger patrons.

C. Smaller & Rural Branches (Slowest Recovery)

Branches in less densely populated areas (e.g., Pullman Branch, South Chicago) struggled to regain lost visitors.

  • Pullman Branch:

    • 2021 Visits: 120,000 (only 20% increase from 2020)
    • Challenge: Limited parking and accessibility issues deterred visitors.
    • Solution: Introduced free shuttle services from nearby transit hubs.

  • South Chicago Branch:

    • 2021 Visits: 150,000 (flat compared to 2020)
    • Challenge: Competition from commercial spaces (e.g., co-working hubs) in the area.
    • Solution: Partnered with local schools to offer free STEM workshops for students.

Insight: Location and community needs play a critical role in recovery. Urban branches recovered faster due to higher foot traffic, while rural branches needed targeted outreach.

3. Digital vs. In-Person Engagement in 2021

While in-person visits rebounded, digital engagement remained a cornerstone of CPL’s strategy.

Metric 2019 2020 2021 Change from 2020
In-Person Visits 6.8M 3.1M 6.3M +103%
Digital Interactions 5.5M 4.1M 3.8M +17%
E-Book Checkouts 1.2M 1.8M 1.5M -17%
Virtual Programs 50K 120K 80K -33%

Key Observations:

  1. In-person visits surged as safety concerns eased, but digital engagement plateaued—likely because some users shifted permanently to online services.
  2. E-book checkouts declined in 2021, suggesting physical books remained in demand.
  3. Virtual programs saw a drop, but hybrid events (in-person + livestreamed) became the new norm.

Why Did Digital Engagement Not Grow Further?

  • User fatigue: After two years of virtual everything, some patrons preferred in-person experiences.
  • Technical barriers: Not all users were comfortable with online platforms, especially seniors.
  • Limited new digital offerings: CPL focused on reopening safely rather than expanding digital services.

10 Actionable Strategies to Boost Library Visitor Rates (And Apply Them to Your Institution)

CPL’s 2021 success wasn’t accidental—it required strategic planning, community engagement, and adaptability. Here are 10 proven strategies you can implement in any library (or business) to increase attendance.

Strategy 1: Hybrid Programming (In-Person + Virtual)

What CPL Did:

  • Offered live-streamed author talks, book clubs, and workshops that could be attended either in-person or online.
  • Example: The Harold Washington Library’s "Storytime Live" series drew 500+ in-person attendees while 2,000+ watched online.

How to Apply It:

  • Record events and make them available on-demand.
  • Use platforms like Zoom, YouTube, or Facebook Live for broader reach.
  • Promote hybrid events on social media with clear instructions for both formats.

Why It Works:

  • Increases accessibility for those who can’t attend in person.
  • Expands reach beyond local patrons.
  • Creates a "two-for-one" experience—attendees get both live and recorded content.

Strategy 2: Targeted Community Outreach

What CPL Did:

  • Partnered with schools, nonprofits, and local businesses to co-market events.
  • Example: Woodson Regional Library collaborated with Chicago Public Schools to offer free SAT prep workshops, drawing 1,200+ students in 2021.

How to Apply It:

  • Identify underserved groups (e.g., seniors, immigrants, low-income families).
  • Offer free or low-cost programs tailored to their needs.
  • Leverage local influencers (e.g., community leaders, teachers) to promote events.

Why It Works:

  • Builds trust with niche audiences.
  • Reduces marketing costs by sharing promotional efforts with partners.

Strategy 3: Mobile & Pop-Up Library Services

What CPL Did:

  • Launched the "Library on Wheels"—a mobile unit that visited senior centers, parks, and low-income housing.
  • Pullman Branch introduced free shuttle services from nearby transit stops.

How to Apply It:

  • Rent a van or bus and turn it into a mobile library.
  • Partner with transit authorities to offer discounted or free rides to library events.
  • Host pop-up book fairs in high-traffic areas (e.g., farmers' markets, festivals).

Why It Works:

  • Removes barriers for those who can’t visit traditional branches.
  • Creates "serendipitous" encounters—people who wouldn’t normally visit a library discover it in a new setting.

Strategy 4: Gamification & Rewards Programs

What CPL Did:

  • Introduced the "Library Passport"—patrons could stamp their pass at each branch they visited, with prizes for completing the collection.
  • Earned Badges Program: Users could unlock digital badges for reading challenges, attending events, or using digital resources.

How to Apply It:

  • Create a loyalty program (e.g., "Visit 5 branches, get a free book").
  • Use apps like Beanstack to track reading progress and offer digital rewards.
  • Partner with local businesses to offer discounts for active library users.

Why It Works:

  • Encourages repeat visits by making engagement fun and rewarding.
  • Collects data on patron behavior to improve future programs.

Strategy 5: Themed Months & Special Events

What CPL Did:

  • Black History Month: Hosted author talks, film screenings, and family workshops, drawing 20,000+ attendees.
  • Summer Reading Challenge: 15,000+ kids participated, with special events at each branch.

How to Apply It:

  • Plan themed months (e.g., "National Hispanic Heritage Month," "Financial Literacy Month").
  • Host pop-up exhibits (e.g., "Chicago’s History Through Art").
  • Collaborate with local artists, historians, and educators to bring in fresh content.

Why It Works:

  • Creates urgency—patrons plan visits around special events.
  • Attracts new audiences who might not normally visit the library.

Strategy 6: Accessibility & Inclusive Design

What CPL Did:

  • Expanded ADA-compliant spaces in under-served branches.
  • Offered ASL (American Sign Language) storytimes and audio-described programs for visually impaired patrons.
  • Provided free Wi-Fi hotspots in all branches.

How to Apply It:

  • Audit your space for barriers (e.g., narrow aisles, lack of elevators).
  • Train staff in inclusive communication (e.g., offering materials in multiple languages).
  • Partner with disability advocacy groups to identify unmet needs.

Why It Works:

  • Makes the library a welcoming space for all.
  • Attracts a broader demographic, including families with disabilities.

Strategy 7: Data-Driven Decision Making

What CPL Did:

  • Used analytics tools to track which events had the highest attendance.
  • Identified low-visit branches and allocated more resources to them.
  • Surveyed patrons to understand pain points (e.g., parking, hours).

How to Apply It:

  • Track visit trends using Google Analytics, library management software (e.g., Koha, Alma), or Excel.
  • A/B test promotions (e.g., email vs. social media vs. flyers).
  • Conduct exit surveys to ask why patrons leave.

Why It Works:

  • Eliminates guesswork—you know what works and what doesn’t.
  • Allows for rapid adjustments based on real-time data.

Strategy 8: Partnerships with Local Businesses & Nonprofits

What CPL Did:

  • Collaborated with Starbucks to offer free library cards to customers.
  • Partnered with the Chicago Public Schools to provide free tutoring at branches.
  • Worked with local bookstores to host joint author events.

How to Apply It:

  • Find complementary businesses (e.g., coffee shops, co-working spaces).
  • Offer cross-promotions (e.g., "Show your library card, get 10% off at [Business Name]").
  • Host joint workshops (e.g., "Financial Literacy with a Local Bank").

Why It Works:

  • Expands your reach into new audiences.
  • Creates a "win-win"—both organizations benefit from increased foot traffic.

Strategy 9: Staff Training & Patron Experience

What CPL Did:

  • Retrained staff in customer service, digital literacy, and conflict resolution.
  • Implemented a "First-Time Visitor" program, where staff escorted new patrons to key resources.
  • Offered "Library Orientation" sessions for seniors and new immigrants.

How to Apply It:

  • Conduct regular training on emotional intelligence and problem-solving.
  • Assign a "welcome desk" to assist first-time visitors.
  • Create a "Patron Feedback" system where users can rate their experience.

Why It Works:

  • Higher staff engagement = better patron experience.
  • Reduces friction for new visitors, encouraging repeat visits.
