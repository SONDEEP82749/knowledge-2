  1. Children & Families (0-18 years old)

    • Digital shift: While in-person visits increased, online storytime views rose by 45% as parents sought hybrid engagement options.

  2. Adults (18-64 years old)

    • ~5.5 million visits (54% of total).
    • Primary reasons for visiting:
      • Job & career resources (LinkedIn Learning, resume workshops).
      • Digital literacy classes (computer training, internet safety).
      • Book clubs and author events (some returned to in-person after 2020 closures).
    • Notable trend: Remote work and learning led to a 15% increase in long-term library card sign-ups among adults.

  3. Senior Citizens (65+ years old)

    • ~1.5 million visits (15% of total).
    • Key services utilized:
      • Genealogy research (increased by 25% as seniors explored family history).
      • Health & wellness programs (meditation sessions, fitness classes).
      • Tech assistance (helping seniors with smartphones and video calls).

2. Why Did Visitor Rates Drop in 2020 and Rebound in 2021?

The pandemic was a game-changer for public libraries. Let’s explore the key factors behind the 2020 decline and the 2021 recovery.

Factors Contributing to the 2020 Decline

  1. Branch Closures & Reduced Hours

    • Peak closures: In March 2020, CPL closed all 80 branches for two weeks. By June, only 20 branches reopened with limited hours.
    • Impact: Visits dropped by 50% in the first month of closures.

  2. Shift to Digital Services

    • E-books & audiobooks surged: Downloads increased by 120% as patrons turned to digital alternatives.
    • Online programs replaced in-person events: Virtual storytimes, book clubs, and career workshops became the norm.

  3. Safety Concerns & Social Distancing

    • Many Chicagoans avoided public spaces due to COVID-19 fears, even after reopenings.
    • Mask mandates and capacity limits reduced comfort levels for some visitors.

  4. Economic Uncertainty

    • With job losses and financial stress, some residents prioritized essential services over library visits.

Why the 2021 Recovery?

  1. Vaccine Rollout & Reopening

    • By spring 2021, CPL had fully reopened all branches with enhanced safety measures (ventilation, contactless checkouts).
    • Result: Visits climbed back to 6.8 million in 2021 (still below 2019 but a 90% recovery).

  2. Hybrid Engagement Model

    • Libraries didn’t abandon digital services—they enhanced them.
    • Example: The Harold Washington Library launched "Library Anywhere"—a platform offering free online courses, career tools, and digital media—which saw 300,000+ users in 2021.

  3. Community Trust & Reassurance

    • CPL’s transparent communication about safety protocols helped rebuild confidence.
    • Programs like "Safe Space Sundays" (mask-free hours for high-risk groups) encouraged return visits.

  4. Federal & Local Funding Boosts

    • American Rescue Plan Act (ARPA) funds allowed CPL to hire more staff, upgrade tech, and expand outreach programs.
    • Result: More one-on-one tech help, job training, and youth programs were available.

3. 8 Actionable Strategies to Boost Visitor Rates in 2024

Now that we’ve analyzed the past, let’s look forward. Here are eight data-backed strategies to increase visitor rates, engagement, and long-term sustainability for libraries like CPL.

Strategy 1: Leverage Data-Driven Branch Planning

Problem: Many libraries assume where demand is highest without hard data. Solution: Use visitor analytics to optimize branch locations and services.

How CPL Did It:

  • Analyzed 2021 foot traffic data to identify underutilized branches in high-density areas.
  • Result: The South Shore Branch (historically strong) saw a 12% increase in visits after expanding after-school tutoring hours based on local school schedules.

Action Steps for Your Library: ✅ Track hourly/daily visit patterns (use library management software like Kohas or Alma). ✅ Survey local schools, businesses, and residents to identify unmet needs. ✅ Adjust operating hours based on peak demand (e.g., extended evening hours for professionals).

Strategy 2: Hybrid Programming (In-Person + Digital)

Problem: Post-pandemic, some patrons prefer digital, while others still want in-person experiences. Solution: Blend both models to maximize reach.

Real-World Example: The New York Public Library (NYPL)

  • NYPL’s "Library Now" initiative offers:
    • In-person workshops (e.g., resume writing, coding classes).
    • Live-streamed events (author talks, book clubs) with on-demand replays.
  • Result: 25% increase in program attendance in 2022 compared to pre-pandemic levels.

Action Steps for Your Library: ✅ Host "hybrid events" (e.g., a book club meeting in-person with a Zoom option). ✅ Record popular programs and post them online for those who can’t attend live. ✅ Partner with local schools to stream library programs into classrooms.

Strategy 3: Targeted Outreach to Underserved Communities

Problem: Some neighborhoods have lower library engagement due to lack of awareness or accessibility. Solution: Tailor outreach to specific demographics.

How CPL Engaged Chicago’s South Side:

  • Identified gaps: The Englewood Branch had only 300,000 visits in 2021 (below the city average).
  • Strategy:
    • Mobile library vans brought books and tech help to neighborhoods without a branch.
    • Partnerships with local churches and community centers to promote library resources.
  • Result: Visits increased by 18% in 12 months.

Action Steps for Your Library: ✅ Conduct community surveys to find barriers to access (transportation, language, tech literacy). ✅ Partner with local organizations (schools, nonprofits, businesses) to co-host events. ✅ **Offer bilingual services if your community has non-English speakers.

Strategy 4: Gamify Library Engagement (Summer Reading & Beyond)

Problem: Passive visitors (those who just browse) don’t fully engage with the library. Solution: Gamification encourages repeat visits and participation.

Example: The Los Angeles Public Library’s "LA Reads" Program

  • 2021 Challenge: "Read 10 Books, Get a Free T-Shirt."
  • Result: 40% increase in summer reading participation and higher retention rates among teens.

Action Steps for Your Library: ✅ Launch a "Library Passport" program—visitors get a stamp for each event attended or book borrowed. ✅ Offer rewards (discounts at local businesses, entry into a raffle). ✅ Use apps like "Libby" or "Hoopla" to track digital reading and tie it to in-person challenges.

Strategy 5: Expand Career & Job Training Resources

Problem: Many visitors don’t realize libraries offer free career tools. Solution: Promote these services aggressively.

How CPL Boosted Job Training Engagement:

  • Added "Career Launch" centers in high-traffic branches (e.g., Harold Washington, Edgar Anderson).
  • Partnered with LinkedIn Learning to offer free courses on resume writing, interview skills, and coding.
  • Result: 35% increase in job training program sign-ups in 2021.

Action Steps for Your Library: ✅ Create a "Career Corner" in your library with computers, career coaches, and job boards. ✅ Host "Resume Review Nights" where staff help patrons one-on-one. ✅ Promote free certifications (e.g., Google Career Certificates, Microsoft Office training).

Strategy 6: Improve First-Time Visitor Experience

Problem: First-time visitors may not return if the onboarding experience is poor. Solution: Make it easy and welcoming.

Example: The Boston Public Library’s "First Visit" Program

  • New visitor guide: A welcome packet with:
    • Library card application (pre-filled with basic info).
    • Map of key areas (children’s section, tech lab, quiet study zones).
    • List of top 5 programs for first-timers.
  • Result: 22% increase in repeat visits within 30 days.

Action Steps for Your Library: ✅ Train staff to greet visitors with a friendly, informative welcome. ✅ Place a "First-Time Visitor" kiosk with quick-start guides. ✅ Offer a "Library 101" tour for new patrons.

Strategy 7: Leverage Social Media & Influencer Partnerships

Problem: Many libraries struggle with digital marketing. Solution: Use social media strategically to attract younger audiences.

How the Seattle Public Library Grew Its Instagram Following

  • Strategy:
    • Behind-the-scenes content (e.g., "Meet Our Librarians" series).
    • TikTok challenges (e.g., "Show Us Your Library Stack").
    • Partnered with local bookstagrammers to promote events.
  • Result: 50% increase in Instagram followers and higher event attendance.
