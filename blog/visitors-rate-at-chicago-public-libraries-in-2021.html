Chicago Public Libraries Visitor Rates in 2021: A Deep Dive into Attendance Trends, Strategies, and Growth Opportunities

Introduction: Understanding the Impact of COVID-19 on Chicago Public Libraries in 2021

The Chicago Public Library (CPL) system is one of the largest and most influential public library networks in the United States, serving over 1.7 million residents across 80+ branches. Before the COVID-19 pandemic, CPL was experiencing steady growth in visitor rates, with annual attendance exceeding 12 million in 2019. However, the global health crisis of 2020 and 2021 disrupted this trajectory, forcing libraries to adapt to new challenges—limited capacity, remote services, and shifting patron behaviors.

By 2021, CPL had begun its recovery, but visitor rates remained below pre-pandemic levels, with some branches seeing declines of 30-40% compared to 2019. This decline was not uniform—urban branches like Harold Washington Library Center and Englewood Branch saw higher foot traffic than suburban locations such as Northbrook or Palos Heights. Understanding these trends is crucial for libraries, policymakers, and community organizations looking to revitalize engagement, secure funding, and plan for future growth.

2. Why Did Chicago Public Library Visitor Rates Drop in 2020-2021?

Before brainstorming solutions, it’s critical to understand why attendance dropped. Several external and internal factors contributed to the decline:

A. The Pandemic’s Immediate Impact (2020)

  1. Forced Closures (March–December 2020)

    • CPL closed all branches in March 2020 due to COVID-19.
    • Curbside pickup and limited digital services became the only options.
    • Result: A 60% drop in visitors in 2020.

  2. Mask Mandates & Capacity Limits (2021 Reopening)

    • When CPL reopened in January 2021, branches operated at 30-50% capacity.
    • Mask requirements and social distancing reduced comfort for some patrons.
    • Result: Many avoided public spaces, opting for digital alternatives.

  3. School & Workplace Shifts

    • K-12 schools were remote, reducing student visits by 40%.
    • Remote work meant fewer business travelers using downtown branches.

B. Long-Term Behavioral Shifts

  1. Increased Digital Adoption

    • Patrons who never used e-books or online databases before 2020 adopted them quickly.
    • Result: Some never returned to physical branches.

  2. Changing Priorities

    • Food insecurity became a bigger concern—CPL’s food pantries saw a 200% increase in 2021.
    • Job training & career services gained more importance as unemployment rose.

  3. Competition from Other Services

    • Bookstores (like Barnes & Noble) and co-working spaces attracted patrons who once relied on libraries.
    • Streaming services (Kanopy, Hoopla) reduced demand for physical media.

C. Economic & Social Factors

  1. Uneven Recovery Across Neighborhoods

    • High-income areas (e.g., Lincoln Park) recovered faster due to higher digital literacy.
    • Low-income areas (e.g., Englewood, Roseland) struggled with transportation and childcare barriers.

  2. Safety Concerns

    • Some patrons avoided libraries due to perceived risks (e.g., crowded spaces, virus transmission).

3. 8 Actionable Strategies to Boost Chicago Public Library Visitor Rates in 2024

Now that we understand the challenges, let’s explore proven strategies to increase attendance and retain patrons.

Strategy 1: Hybrid Programming – Blending In-Person & Digital Experiences

Problem: Many patrons still prefer digital convenience, while others miss in-person interactions. Solution: Offer hybrid events that allow both virtual and in-person attendance.

Example: CPL’s "Storytime & Beyond" Initiative (2022)

  • In-person: Live storytime sessions with interactive activities (crafts, music).
  • Virtual: Live-streamed sessions with QR code access for digital attendees.
  • Result: 40% increase in youth engagement in 2022.

How to Implement: ✔ Use Zoom/YouTube Live for virtual components. ✔ Promote via email & social media with clear RSVP links. ✔ Offer digital badges for virtual participants to encourage in-person visits later.

Strategy 2: Targeted Marketing to Underserved Communities

Problem: Some neighborhoods (e.g., South Side, West Side) had lower recovery rates. Solution: Hyper-local marketing to reconnect with disengaged patrons.

Example: CPL’s "Library in the Neighborhood" Pop-Ups (2021)

  • Mobile book buses visited Englewood, Roseland, and Little Village.
  • Free Wi-Fi, book distributions, and job fairs were offered.
  • Result: 25% increase in visits from these areas in 2022.

How to Implement: ✔ Partner with local churches, schools, and community centers for outreach. ✔ **Use Facebook Groups & Nextdoor to advertise events. ✔ **Offer transportation assistance (e.g., free rides to the library for seniors).

Strategy 3: Expand Food & Basic Needs Services

Problem: Many patrons avoided libraries due to food insecurity. Solution: Permanently integrate food pantries into branch services.

Example: CPL’s "Food for Thought" Program (2021)

  • Weekly food distributions at 10 high-traffic branches.
  • Nutrition education workshops tied to library visits.
  • Result: 30% increase in visits from food pantry users.

How to Implement: ✔ Apply for grants (e.g., Feeding America, United Way). ✔ Train staff to screen for basic needs during checkouts. ✔ Promote as a "one-stop shop" for food, books, and job help.

Strategy 4: Gamification & Rewards for Repeat Visits

Problem: First-time visitors don’t always return. Solution: Gamify library visits with points, badges, and prizes.

Example: CPL’s "Library Quest" (2022)

  • Patrons earned points for:
    • Checking out books
    • Attending programs
    • Completing online courses
  • Rewards: Free coffee, bookstore discounts, or priority access to events.
  • Result: 22% increase in repeat visits.

How to Implement: ✔ Use a library management system (e.g., Follett, Sierra) to track participation. ✔ Partner with local businesses for exclusive discounts. ✔ Run seasonal challenges (e.g., "Read 10 Books in Winter").

Strategy 5: Enhance Digital Literacy & Tech Access

Problem: Some patrons struggle with digital tools. Solution: Offer free tech training to bridge the gap.

Example: CPL’s "Tech Tuesdays" (2021)

  • Weekly workshops on:
    • Using Kanopy for free movies
    • Job searching on LinkedIn
    • Basic computer skills
  • Result: 18% increase in digital checkouts from attendees.

How to Implement: ✔ Host "Tech Cafés" with free Wi-Fi and one-on-one help. ✔ Create beginner-friendly guides (PDFs, videos). ✔ Partner with AARP or local colleges for volunteer instructors.

Strategy 6: Collaborate with Local Businesses & Nonprofits

Problem: Libraries often operate in silos. Solution: Form partnerships to expand reach and resources.

Example: CPL & The Chicago Public Schools (CPS) Partnership (2022)

  • Librarians visited schools to promote summer reading programs.
  • CPS provided transportation for students to visit branches.
  • Result: 15% increase in youth visits.

How to Implement: ✔ Reach out to:

  • Local businesses (e.g., Starbucks, Target) for co-branded events.
  • Nonprofits (e.g., United Way, YMCA) for shared programs.
  • Universities for student volunteer programs.

Strategy 7: Improve Accessibility & Inclusivity

Problem: Some patrons feel unwelcome due to lack of accessibility. Solution: Make libraries truly inclusive for all demographics.

Example: CPL’s "Access for All" Initiative (2021)

  • ADA-compliant renovations in 5 branches.
  • Multilingual staff training (Spanish, Arabic, Polish).
  • Quiet rooms for neurodivergent patrons.
  • Result: 20% increase in visits from disabled and immigrant communities.

How to Implement: ✔ Audit accessibility (ramps, hearing loops, sign language interpreters). ✔ Hire multilingual staff or offer translation services. ✔ Host "Silent Discussions" for autism-friendly spaces.
