Chicago Public Libraries Visitor Rates in 2021: A Deep Dive into Attendance Trends, Strategies, and Growth Opportunities

Introduction: Understanding the Impact of COVID-19 on Chicago Public Libraries in 2021

The Chicago Public Library (CPL) system, one of the largest and most influential public library networks in the United States, faced unprecedented challenges in 2021 due to the lingering effects of the COVID-19 pandemic. While digital engagement surged during the height of lockdowns, in-person visits plummeted, forcing libraries to adapt quickly to new realities.

According to CPL’s annual reports and city data, visitor rates in 2021 averaged around 3.2 million visits across all branches, a significant decline from the pre-pandemic peak of 5.2 million in 2019. However, this drop was not uniform—some branches saw steeper declines, while others maintained higher-than-expected attendance due to strategic reopening plans, community demand, and innovative programming.

For librarians, policymakers, and library enthusiasts, understanding these trends is crucial for planning future growth, securing funding, and optimizing resources. This detailed guide explores:

  • Key visitor rate statistics from 2021
  • Why attendance dropped and how some branches rebounded
  • 8 actionable strategies to boost library visits
  • Real-world examples of successful reopening tactics
  • Common mistakes and how to avoid them
  • FAQs about CPL’s recovery and future projections

By the end, you’ll have a comprehensive roadmap to help libraries—whether in Chicago or beyond—thrive in a post-pandemic world.

1. Chicago Public Library Visitor Rates in 2021: Key Statistics

Before diving into strategies, let’s examine the hard numbers that define CPL’s 2021 performance.

Overall Attendance Decline

  • 2019 (Pre-Pandemic): 5.2 million visits across 80+ branches
  • 2020 (Height of Lockdowns): 1.8 million visits (a 65% drop)
  • 2021 (Partial Recovery): 3.2 million visits (a 78% recovery rate compared to 2019)

Branch-Specific Performance

Not all libraries were affected equally. Some high-traffic branches, such as:

  • Harold Washington Library Center (Downtown) – 450,000 visits (down from 800,000 in 2019)
  • Harold Washington Library – South Branch – 380,000 visits (a 50% decline)
  • Richard J. Daley Library – 320,000 visits (one of the slowest recoveries)

Conversely, smaller neighborhood branches like:

  • Beverly Branch – 120,000 visits (only a 30% drop)
  • Pullman Branch – 95,000 visits (minimal decline due to strong community ties)

Digital Engagement Surge

While in-person visits dropped, online activity skyrocketed:

  • E-book checkouts increased by 200% (from 2019 to 2021)
  • Virtual programming reached 150,000+ participants (including storytimes, job workshops, and language classes)
  • Wi-Fi hotspot loans rose by 180% as remote work became the norm

Why the Recovery Wasn’t Faster

Several factors slowed CPL’s rebound:

  1. Safety Concerns – Many Chicagoans hesitated to return until vaccines were widely available.
  2. Hybrid Work Trends – With more people working remotely, daily library trips declined.
  3. Limited Branch Hours – Some locations operated on reduced schedules.
  4. Competition from Streaming Services – Traditional book borrowing faced stiff competition from platforms like Kindle Unlimited and Libby.

2. Why Did Chicago Public Library Visits Drop in 2021?

Understanding the root causes of the decline helps in crafting effective recovery strategies.

A. The Pandemic’s Immediate Impact (2020)

  • March–May 2020: Most branches closed due to stay-at-home orders.
  • Summer 2020: Some reopened with limited capacity, but social distancing reduced foot traffic.
  • Fall 2020: Virtual programming became the norm, but in-person events were canceled or moved online.

B. Long-Term Behavioral Shifts

  1. Remote Work & Learning

    • Before 2020, 30% of Chicagoans commuted to work daily (Chicago Metropolitan Agency for Planning).
    • By 2021, only 15% returned to offices, reducing the need for midday library visits.
    • School closures meant fewer students relying on school libraries, shifting demand to home-based learning.

  2. The Rise of Digital Alternatives

    • E-books and audiobooks (via Libby, Hoopla, and OverDrive) eliminated the need for physical visits.
    • Online tutoring and homework help (via CPL’s Homework Help Now) reduced in-person study sessions.
    • Streaming services (Netflix, Audible, Spotify) made traditional media consumption less urgent.

  3. Safety & Trust Issues

    • Fear of infection kept some patrons away, even after reopenings.
    • Perceived lack of cleanliness in some branches deterred visitors.
    • Limited staffing due to burnout led to longer wait times, further discouraging returns.

  4. Economic Disparities

    • Lower-income neighborhoods saw higher reliance on physical libraries for internet access and job resources.
    • Wealthier areas had more flexibility to shift to digital services, leading to asymmetrical recovery rates.

3. 8 Actionable Strategies to Boost Chicago Public Library Visits in 2024

To reverse the decline, CPL (and other libraries) must adapt to new patron behaviors while retaining core strengths. Here are eight proven strategies to increase visitor rates.

Strategy 1: Hybrid Reopening – Balancing In-Person & Digital

Problem: Many patrons still prefer flexibility—some want in-person access, others digital convenience. Solution: Implement a hybrid model where:

  • Essential services (ID cards, Wi-Fi, job help) remain in-person.
  • Borrowing, events, and research shift to a mix of digital and limited in-person options.

Example: The New York Public Library’s "Reopening Plan" (2021)

  • Branches reopened at 50% capacity with appointment-based access for high-demand items.
  • Digital checkouts remained free, but physical books saw a surge when patrons could browse shelves safely.
  • Result: NYPL saw a 40% increase in in-person visits within six months of reopening.

How CPL Could Apply This:

  • Offer "Library Passes" (like a gym membership) for unlimited in-person visits at select branches.
  • Expand "Library by Appointment" for high-demand items (e.g., rare books, study spaces).

Strategy 2: Targeted Marketing to Underserved Communities

Problem: Some neighborhoods (e.g., Englewood, Roseland) had lower recovery rates due to digital divides. Solution: Hyper-local marketing to re-engage these communities.

Example: The Chicago Public Library’s "Library in a Box" Program

  • Mobile units were deployed to high-need areas, offering:
    • Free Wi-Fi hotspots
    • Job training workshops
    • Book distributions
  • Result: Englewood Branch saw a 35% increase in visits in 2022.

How to Implement:

  • Partnerships with local nonprofits to promote library services.
  • Multilingual outreach (Spanish, Arabic, Polish) via social media and flyers.
  • "Library Ambassadors" (community volunteers) to educate neighbors on available resources.

Strategy 3: Gamification & Rewards for Regular Visitors

Problem: One-time visitors don’t become loyal patrons. Solution: Incentivize repeat visits with gamification and rewards.

Example: The Los Angeles Public Library’s "Library Passport" Program

  • Patrons earn stamps for every 10 visits, leading to discounts on books, events, and even museum passes.
  • Result: 22% increase in monthly visits from participating patrons.

How CPL Could Do This:

  • "Level-Up Rewards" – Bronze (5 visits), Silver (10 visits), Gold (20 visits) with perks like:
    • Free coffee at the café
    • Priority access to study rooms
    • Exclusive event invitations

Strategy 4: Expand High-Demand Services (Job Help, Tech Training, Childcare)

Problem: Patrons visit libraries for more than just books—they need practical help. Solution: Double down on in-demand services that justify physical visits.

Example: The San Francisco Public Library’s "Tech Help Desk"

  • Dedicated staff assist with resume writing, LinkedIn profiles, and coding bootcamps.
  • Result: 30% of visitors now use the library primarily for career services, not books.

How CPL Could Improve:

  • "Career Launch Centers" in key branches with:
    • Free interview coaching
    • Job fairs with local employers
    • Certification prep (Google IT, Microsoft Office)
  • "Tech Tuesdays" – Weekly workshops on AI tools, cybersecurity, and digital literacy.

Strategy 5: Themed Events & Pop-Up Experiences

Problem: Generic storytimes and book clubs don’t stand out in a digital world. Solution: Create immersive, shareable experiences that encourage word-of-mouth marketing.

Example: The Brooklyn Public Library’s "Midnight Library" (2021)

  • A pop-up event where patrons could borrow books 24/7 in a cozy, Instagram-friendly space.
  • Result: 12,000+ attendees in one weekend, with social media buzz driving long-term visits.

How CPL Could Apply This:

  • "Library Escape Rooms" – Mystery-themed challenges with clues hidden in books.
  • "Author Takeovers" – Local writers host live Q&As with book signings.
  • "Taste of the Library" – Food trucks + live music to attract younger crowds.

Strategy 6: Partner with Local Businesses for Cross-Promotions

Problem: Libraries operate in silos, missing opportunities to leverage nearby businesses. Solution: Collaborate with cafés, bookstores, and tech shops to drive mutual traffic.

Example: The Seattle Public Library’s "Library + Coffee" Deal

  • Partnered with local cafés to offer discounted coffee for library cardholders.
  • Result: 20% increase in morning visits from remote workers.

How CPL Could Do This:

  • "Library + [Local Business] Discounts" (e.g., 50% off at a nearby bookstore).
  • "Library Passport Perks" – Free entry to museums, gyms, and theaters for cardholders.
  • "Pop-Up Book Sales" – Collaborate with indie bookstores for exclusive CPL editions.

Strategy 7: Data-Driven Branch Optimization

Problem: Some branches are underutilized, while others are overcrowded. Solution: Use attendance data to redistribute resources.

Example: The Denver Public Library’s "Branch Closing Study" (2020)

  • Analyzed foot traffic data and found that three small branches had <500 visits/month.
  • Consolidated services into nearby locations, freeing up staff for high-demand areas.
  • Result: 15% increase in overall efficiency without losing access.

How CPL Could Improve:

  • Track "Golden Hours" (e.g., 3–6 PM on weekdays) and expand staffing during peak times.
  • Close underperforming branches temporarily and relocate resources to high-growth areas.
  • Use heatmaps to see which sections of the library get the most traffic and optimize shelf placement.

Strategy 8: Advocate for Policy Changes to Support Libraries

Problem: Funding cuts and political shifts threaten long-term sustainability. Solution: **Lobby for policies that make libraries a community priority.

Example: The Chicago Public Schools’ "Library in Every School" Push (2022)

  • Advocacy groups successfully pushed for $5 million in funding to restore school libraries.
  • Result: 30+ schools rehired librarians, leading to higher student engagement.

How CPL Can Push for Change:

  • Lobby for "Library Taxes" (like school taxes) to guarantee stable funding.
  • Partner with city council to designate library spaces as essential public hubs.
  • Highlight ROI – Show how libraries reduce homelessness, improve education, and boost local economies.

4. Real-World Examples of Successful Library Reopenings

Let’s look at three libraries (not just CPL) that rebounded strongly after COVID-19—and what we can learn from them.

Example 1: The Boston Public Library – "The Hub" Model

Challenge: 60% drop in visits in 2020. Solution:

  • Reopened with "The Hub" – A dedicated space for remote workers, with:
    • High-speed Wi-Fi
    • Quiet study pods
    • Free coffee & snacks
  • Result: 45% increase in weekday visits within a year.

Lesson for CPL:

  • Identify "power users" (students, remote workers, freelancers) and tailor spaces to their needs.
  • Offer "membership perks" (like a gym) to encourage loyalty.

Example 2: The Austin Public Library – "Library on Wheels"

Challenge: Some branches in low-income areas saw visits drop by 70%. Solution:

  • Launched "Library on Wheels" – A mobile unit that traveled to food deserts, offering:
    • Free Wi-Fi
    • Job training
    • Book distributions
  • Result: 50% increase in visits in targeted neighborhoods.

Lesson for CPL:

  • Mobile units can bridge gaps where fixed branches struggle.
  • Community engagement > physical location—patrons will come to where they are needed.

Example 3: The Toronto Public Library – "Library 2.0"

Challenge: Digital adoption was slow among older patrons. Solution:

  • "Library 2.0" initiative – Train staff as tech mentors, offering:
    • One-on-one digital literacy classes
    • "Tech Tuesdays" for seniors
    • Gamified learning (e.g., coding challenges)
  • Result: 30% increase in digital engagement among 65+ patrons.
