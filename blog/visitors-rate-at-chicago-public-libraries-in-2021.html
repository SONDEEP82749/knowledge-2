Chicago Public Libraries Visitor Rates in 2021: Trends, Insights, and Strategies for Growth

According to the 2021 Annual Report from the Chicago Public Library Foundation, total in-person visits dropped by 30% compared to pre-pandemic levels (2019), with digital engagement surging by 45% as patrons shifted to online resources. However, by the end of 2021, CPL saw a slow but steady recovery, with some branches regaining 60-70% of their pre-pandemic foot traffic by December.

1. Chicago Public Libraries in 2021: A Year of Recovery and Reinvention

1.1 Pre-Pandemic vs. Post-Pandemic Visitor Trends (2019-2021)

Before COVID-19, CPL was experiencing steady growth, with annual in-person visits exceeding 10 million in 2019. However, when Illinois entered a stay-at-home order in March 2020, CPL closed all physical locations for nearly 18 months, leading to an immediate 90% drop in visits.

By 2021, CPL implemented a phased reopening strategy, allowing limited in-person access while expanding digital lending, virtual programs, and contactless services. Here’s how visitor numbers evolved:

Metric 2019 (Pre-Pandemic) 2020 (Pandemic Peak) 2021 (Recovery Phase)
In-Person Visits ~10.2 million ~1.2 million (March-June) ~6.8 million (full year)
Digital Checkouts ~5.1 million ~12.3 million ~9.5 million
Program Attendance ~250,000 ~30,000 (virtual) ~180,000 (hybrid)
Branch Utilization 75-85% (peak hours) 10-20% (limited access) 50-70% (recovery phase)

Key Takeaway: While digital engagement saved CPL during lockdowns, in-person visits remained critical for community connection, youth programs, and economic impact.

1.2 Demographic Shifts: Who Was Visiting in 2021?

CPL’s 2021 visitor data revealed three major demographic trends:

  1. Youth & Family Visits (Ages 0-18) Recovered Faster

    • Schools were partially remote, leading to higher parent-child visits (up 40% from 2020).
    • Storytime programs saw a 35% increase in attendance compared to 2020.

  2. Young Adults (18-35) Shifted to Digital

    • This group visited branches 20% less than in 2019 but doubled their digital borrowing (e-books, audiobooks, and online courses).
    • Career centers and tech workshops became critical engagement tools for this demographic.

  3. Senior Visits (55+) Increased in Hybrid Models

    • Older adults preferred in-person access for genealogy research, computer classes, and quiet study spaces.
    • Mobile library vans (which resumed in 2021) helped rural and underserved neighborhoods regain senior engagement.

Why This Matters: Libraries must tailor programs to age-specific needs—while digital accessibility remains essential for younger users.

1.3 Branch Performance: Which Locations Saw the Highest Recovery?

Not all CPL branches recovered at the same rate. High-traffic urban branches (like Harold Washington Library Center and Woodson Regional) saw faster rebounds, while suburban and rural branches struggled more due to limited public transit access.

  • Urban branches benefited from higher foot traffic due to downtown workplaces reopening.

2. Why Did Visitor Rates Drop in 2021? (And How CPL Responded)

2.1 The Pandemic’s Immediate Impact

  • March-June 2020: Zero in-person access → 90% drop in visits.
  • July-December 2020: Limited reopening (10% capacity) → Visits remained at 20% of 2019 levels.
  • 2021: Phased reopening (50-70% capacity) → Gradual recovery, but not full rebound.

2.2 Long-Term Challenges

  1. Safety Concerns – Many patrons avoided crowded spaces even after reopening.
  2. Remote Work Trends – Fewer people commuted downtown, reducing lunch-hour visits.
  3. Digital Fatigue – While e-books and online classes grew, some users preferred in-person experiences.
  4. Budget Cuts – CPL’s 2021 budget was reduced by 5%, limiting marketing and program expansion.

2.3 CPL’s Recovery Strategies (What Worked)

CPL implemented three key strategies to boost attendance in 2021:

✅ Hybrid Programming – Combining in-person and virtual events (e.g., author talks with live-streaming). ✅ Targeted Outreach – Partnering with schools, nonprofits, and local businesses to promote library services. ✅ Flexible Hours & Extended Access – Some branches opened on Sundays and extended evening hours to accommodate shift workers.

Result: By December 2021, 60% of branches had reached 70% of their 2019 visit numbers, with youth programs leading the recovery.

3. 8 Actionable Strategies to Boost Library Visitor Rates (Lessons from CPL 2021)

If you’re looking to increase foot traffic to your library (or improve engagement for CPL), these data-backed strategies can help.

Strategy 1: Leverage Data-Driven Branch Planning

What CPL Did: CPL analyzed 2021 visit patterns and found that weekday mornings (7-9 AM) and evenings (5-7 PM) had the highest attendance. They then:

  • Extended morning hours at Harold Washington Library (now open 6 AM-10 PM on weekdays).
  • Added evening study sessions for students.

How You Can Apply This:

  • Track your library’s visit data (use tools like Google Analytics for Libraries or CPL’s own dashboard).
  • Adjust hours based on peak usage times.
  • Promote extended hours via social media and local newsletters.

Real-World Example: The New York Public Library (NYPL) saw a 25% increase in weekday evening visits after extending hours at Schomburg Center to 8 PM.

Strategy 2: Host High-Impact Community Events

What CPL Did: CPL’s 2021 "Chicago Reads" program (a citywide book club) drew 15,000+ attendees across branches. They also:

  • Partnered with local authors for live book signings.
  • Hosted "Tech Tuesdays" (free computer classes) that doubled attendance in suburban branches.

How You Can Apply This:

  • Identify local interests (history, STEM, arts) and host themed events.
  • Promote events on Facebook, Nextdoor, and local blogs.
  • Offer incentives (e.g., "First 50 attendees get a free book").

Real-World Example: The Los Angeles Public Library’s "LA Reads" program increased youth visits by 30% after launching a monthly author series.

Strategy 3: Improve Digital Accessibility to Retain Users

What CPL Did: Since 45% of 2021 visitors used digital resources, CPL:

  • Expanded e-book collections (partnering with Hoopla and Libby).
  • Added free Wi-Fi hotspots in high-demand branches.
  • Created a "Digital Literacy Hub" with tutorials for seniors.

How You Can Apply This:

  • Ensure your library’s website is mobile-friendly.
  • Offer "Tech Help Desks" for patrons struggling with digital tools.
  • Promote free digital resources via social media ads.

Real-World Example: The San Francisco Public Library saw a 40% increase in digital checkouts after adding a "Digital First Aid" workshop series.

Strategy 4: Partner with Local Businesses & Schools

What CPL Did: CPL collaborated with:

  • Chicago Public Schools to bring libraries into classrooms (via bookmobile visits).
  • Local coffee shops to offer "Library Passes" (e.g., "Show your CPL card for 10% off coffee").
  • Nonprofits to host job fairs and resume workshops.

How You Can Apply This:

  • Reach out to nearby businesses for cross-promotions.
  • Offer free workshops at local community centers.
  • Create a "Library Ambassador" program where trusted locals promote your services.

Real-World Example: The Portland Public Library partnered with local bookstores, leading to a 20% increase in foot traffic during monthly author events.

Strategy 5: Use Social Media & Local SEO to Attract Visitors

What CPL Did: CPL’s 2021 digital marketing push included:

  • Instagram & TikTok challenges (e.g., "Show us your favorite library book!").
  • Google My Business optimization (ensuring branch locations appeared in local searches).
  • Hyper-local Facebook ads targeting neighborhoods with low visit rates.

How You Can Apply This:

  • Post engaging content (e.g., "Did you know? CPL has a rare 1920s Chicago map!").
  • Encourage reviews on Google and Yelp (offer a free book for a 5-star review).
  • Run targeted ads for new programs (e.g., "Free Coding Class for Teens—Sign Up Now!").

Real-World Example: The Denver Public Library increased social media engagement by 50% after launching a "Library Mystery" series on Instagram.

Strategy 6: Offer Incentives for First-Time Visitors

What CPL Did: CPL’s "First Visit Free" program gave new patrons a free book or coffee when they visited a branch for the first time. This boosted first-time visits by 22%.

How You Can Apply This:

  • Create a "Welcome Kit" (e.g., library card + free bookmark).
  • Partner with local businesses for discounts (e.g., "Show your library card for a free smoothie").
  • Run a "Refer-a-Friend" contest (e.g., "Bring a friend, both get a free book").

Real-World Example: The Minneapolis Public Library saw a 15% increase in new cardholders after launching a "Library Passport" program (stamps for each branch visited).

Strategy 7: Focus on Youth & Family Programs

What CPL Did: Since youth visits recovered fastest, CPL:

  • Expanded "Storytime on Wheels" (mobile storytime vans).
  • Added "Lego Clubs" and "Science Saturdays" for kids.
  • Partnered with schools for summer reading challenges.

How You Can Apply This:

  • Host weekly kids’ activities (e.g., crafts, puppet shows, STEM experiments).
  • Offer "Parent & Me" classes for toddlers and caregivers.
  • Create a "Summer Reading Bingo" program with prizes.

Real-World Example: The Houston Public Library increased youth visits by 35% after launching a "Build Your Own Comic" workshop series.
