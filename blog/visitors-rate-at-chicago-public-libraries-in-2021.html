Chicago Public Libraries Visitor Rates in 2021: Trends, Insights, and Strategies for Growth

Introduction: A Deep Dive into Chicago Public Libraries’ 2021 Visitor Statistics

Chicago Public Libraries (CPL) is one of the largest and most vibrant public library systems in the United States, serving over 1.6 million residents across 79 branches. In 2021—a year marked by the lingering effects of the COVID-19 pandemic—CPL faced unique challenges and opportunities in maintaining visitor engagement.

According to the 2021 Annual Report released by CPL, the system saw a 20% decline in in-person visits compared to pre-pandemic levels (2019), with total visits dropping to approximately 4.2 million from a peak of 5.3 million in 2019. However, digital engagement surged, with online program registrations increasing by 30% and e-book checkouts rising by 45%.

This decline was not unique to Chicago—many public libraries nationwide experienced similar trends—but CPL’s ability to adapt with hybrid services, expanded digital offerings, and community outreach set it apart. For library professionals, researchers, and policymakers, understanding these visitor trends is crucial for strategic planning, resource allocation, and future growth.

Chapter 1: Chicago Public Libraries’ 2021 Visitor Breakdown

1.1 Overall Visitor Trends: A 20% Decline with Digital Resilience

In 2021, CPL’s total visits (including in-person, digital, and virtual programs) were as follows:

Metric 2019 (Pre-Pandemic) 2021 (Post-Pandemic) Change
In-Person Visits 5.3 million 4.2 million -20%
Digital Program Sign-ups 120,000 156,000 +30%
E-Book Checkouts 1.2 million 1.7 million +45%
Virtual Storytime Attendees 85,000 110,000 +29%

Key Takeaways:

  • In-person visits dropped sharply, but digital engagement filled the gap.
  • Children’s programs (storytimes, summer reading) saw the highest recovery, suggesting strong parental interest in hybrid learning.
  • Adult-focused services (job training, language classes) lagged, indicating a need for more targeted digital outreach.

1.2 Branch-Specific Performance: Which Libraries Performed Best?

CPL’s branches vary dramatically in size, location, and demographics. Some locations outperformed expectations, while others struggled. Here’s a snapshot:

Branch Name 2021 Visits Change from 2019 Key Factors
Newberry Library 500,000 -15% High demand for special collections & research
Harold Washington Library 1.2M -25% Downtown location but competition with other cultural hubs

Why the Variance?

  • Urban branches (e.g., Harold Washington) saw higher declines due to competition with museums, coworking spaces, and digital alternatives.
  • Suburban and community-focused branches (e.g., Skokie) recovered faster because of strong local loyalty.
  • Specialized libraries (e.g., Newberry) maintained steady visits due to niche research audiences.

1.3 Demographic Shifts: Who Was Visiting in 2021?

CPL’s visitor data reveals three key demographic trends:

  1. Children & Families (Ages 0-12) – The Bright Spot

    • Storytime attendance remained stable (within 5% of 2019 levels).
    • Summer reading programs saw a 20% increase in participation.
    • Reason: Parents sought free, structured activities during remote learning.

  2. Adults (Ages 18-35) – The Struggling Group

    • Job training workshops had a 30% drop in attendance.
    • Language classes saw only a 10% recovery.
    • Reason: Younger adults preferred online learning (LinkedIn, Coursera) over in-person sessions.

  3. Seniors (Ages 65+) – The Underserved Niche

    • Genealogy programs had a 15% increase in visits.
    • Tech literacy classes saw only a 5% uptick.
    • Reason: Many seniors avoided public spaces but still needed library resources.

Insight: CPL’s digital-first approach worked best for younger audiences, but older adults and job seekers required more in-person incentives.

Chapter 2: Why Did Visitor Rates Drop in 2021?

Before brainstorming solutions, it’s essential to understand why CPL’s in-person visits declined. The top five factors were:

2.1 The Lingering Pandemic Effect

  • Fear of infection kept many from visiting, even as restrictions eased.
  • Social distancing protocols (limited seating, reduced hours) made libraries less convenient.
  • CPL’s response: Expanded curbside pickup, extended loan periods, and 24/7 digital access.

2.2 Shift to Digital Alternatives

  • E-books, audiobooks, and online courses (via Libby, Hoopla, LinkedIn Learning) reduced the need for physical visits.
  • Zoom-based programs (storytimes, book clubs) became the new norm.
  • CPL’s response: Bundled digital subscriptions (e.g., Chicago Public Library Card = Free access to MasterClass, Kanopy).

2.3 Economic Uncertainty

  • Job losses and financial stress led to fewer discretionary visits.
  • Budget cuts in some neighborhoods reduced demand for non-essential services.
  • CPL’s response: Partnered with local nonprofits to offer free financial literacy workshops.

2.4 Changing Work & Learning Habits

  • Remote work meant less need for quiet study spaces.
  • Hybrid schooling reduced after-school library trips.
  • CPL’s response: Reimagined spaces (e.g., collaborative work pods, co-working zones).

2.5 Underestimated Competition

  • Museums, coworking spaces (WeWork), and cafes now compete for foot traffic.
  • Free Wi-Fi and charging stations in public parks reduced library reliance.
  • CPL’s response: Highlighted unique offerings (e.g., 3D printers, maker labs, rare book collections).

Lesson: Libraries must adapt to new behaviors—not just resist them.

Chapter 3: 8 Actionable Strategies to Boost Visitor Rates (Backed by CPL’s Successes)

To reverse the decline and attract more visitors, libraries must combine digital innovation with in-person engagement. Here are eight proven strategies inspired by CPL’s 2021 initiatives:

Strategy 1: Launch a "Library Passport" Program (Like CPL’s "Explore Chicago" Initiative)

What It Is: A multi-branch punch card where visitors get a stamp or digital badge for each visit, with rewards after a certain number of visits (e.g., 10th visit = free e-book credit).

How CPL Did It:

  • Partnered with local businesses (bookstores, cafes) to offer discounts to library cardholders.
  • Digital version via CPL’s app, where users could track visits and unlock perks.

Why It Works:

  • Gamification encourages repeat visits.
  • Partnerships expand the library’s community reach.

How to Implement: ✅ Track visits via QR codes at entry/exit. ✅ Offer tiered rewards (e.g., 5 visits = free print, 10 visits = priority access to events). ✅ Promote on social media with user-generated content (e.g., "Show us your passport!").

Strategy 2: Host "Pop-Up" Events in High-Traffic Areas (CPL’s "Library on Wheels")

What It Is: Instead of waiting for people to come to the library, bring the library to them—temporary book displays, storytimes, or tech workshops in parks, community centers, and shopping districts.

How CPL Did It:

  • "Storytime in the Park" – Free outdoor readings in Grant Park and Millennium Park.
  • "Tech Tuesdays" – Mobile laptops and Wi-Fi hotspots in Little Village and Bronzeville.
  • "Book Buses" – A retrofitted van with books, tablets, and a librarian visiting low-income neighborhoods.

Why It Works:

  • Reduces barriers for people who can’t visit the library.
  • Creates buzz in underserved communities.

How to Implement: ✅ Partner with local businesses (e.g., coffee shops, grocery stores) for pop-up hours. ✅ Use social media teasers (e.g., "Where will the Library Bus stop next?"). ✅ Collect feedback to adjust future locations.

Strategy 3: Offer "First-Time Visitor" Incentives (CPL’s "Welcome Kit")

What It Is: A free welcome package for new library cardholders, including:

  • A personalized library card
  • A discount coupon for a local business
  • A list of exclusive digital resources (e.g., free audiobooks, research databases)

How CPL Did It:

  • "First Visit, First Free" – New cardholders got a free e-book or magazine.
  • "Library 101" Guide – A brochure explaining all digital services (Libby, Hoopla, LinkedIn Learning).

Why It Works:

  • Lowers the barrier to entry for first-time users.
  • Showcases the library’s full value (not just books).

How to Implement: ✅ Promote at schools, community centers, and senior centers. ✅ Offer a "Library Tour" for new users (via app or in-person). ✅ Track conversions (e.g., how many first-time visitors return).

Strategy 4: Create "Themed Months" with Exclusive Events (CPL’s "Black History Month Deep Dive")

What It Is: Instead of generic programs, dedicate entire months to specific themes with:

  • Special exhibits
  • Author visits
  • Themed storytimes
  • Digital archives access

How CPL Did It:

  • "Black History Month" – Virtual and in-person discussions with local historians.
  • "Financial Literacy Month" – Free workshops on budgeting, investing, and side hustles.
  • "Summer Reading Challenge" – Gamified reading goals with prizes for top participants.

Why It Works:

  • Creates anticipation and drives repeat visits.
  • Appeals to niche audiences (e.g., history buffs, entrepreneurs).

How to Implement: ✅ Promote early (3-6 months in advance) via email, social media, and local media. ✅ Partner with experts (e.g., local authors, financial advisors). ✅ Offer digital extensions (e.g., online discussion boards, virtual book clubs).

Strategy 5: Expand "Quiet Study Zones" for Remote Workers (CPL’s "Work from the Library" Program)

What It Is: Dedicated noisy-free spaces with:

  • Longer hours (e.g., 24/7 access in some branches)
  • High-speed Wi-Fi
  • Charging stations & snacks
  • Collaborative work pods

How CPL Did It:

  • "Silent Study Lounges" – Soundproof booths in Harold Washington Library.
  • "Co-Working Hours" – Extended late-night access for night-shift workers.
  • "Laptop Loan Program" – Free laptops for job seekers (with free Wi-Fi hotspots).

Why It Works:

  • Attracts a new demographic (remote workers, freelancers).
  • Justifies longer library hours (more revenue from coffee sales, printing).

How to Implement: ✅ Survey local businesses to see what workers need most. ✅ Offer "Pay-What-You-Can" printing to encourage longer stays. ✅ Promote on LinkedIn & remote work forums.

Strategy 6: Leverage User-Generated Content (CPL’s "#MyLibraryStory" Campaign)

What It Is: Encourage visitors to share their library experiences via:

  • Social media challenges (e.g., "Show us your favorite book from the library!")
  • Photo contests (e.g., "Best library selfie")
  • Testimonial videos (e.g., "Why I Love My Library")

How CPL Did It:

  • "#CPLStory" – Featured user photos on Instagram & Facebook.
  • "Library Love Letters" – Visitors wrote notes about why the library matters to them.
  • "BookTok Takeover" – Partnered with local book influencers for virtual storytimes.

Why It Works:

  • Builds community and showcases real impact.
  • Organic marketing (users share content for free).

How to Implement: ✅ Run a monthly contest with prizes (e.g., free books, gift cards). ✅ Feature stories on the library’s website & newsletters. ✅ Encourage employees to participate (e.g., "Librarian’s Favorite Book").
