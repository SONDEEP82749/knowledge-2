Chicago Public Libraries Visitor Rates in 2021: A Deep Dive into Attendance Trends, Strategies, and Growth Opportunities

Introduction: Why Chicago Public Libraries’ 2021 Visitor Rates Matter

Chicago Public Libraries (CPL) is one of the largest and most influential public library systems in the United States, serving over 1.6 million residents across 79 branches with a combined collection of nearly 3 million items. In 2021—a year marked by the lingering effects of the COVID-19 pandemic—CPL faced unprecedented challenges while also seizing opportunities to redefine public engagement.

According to the 2021 Annual Report released by CPL, the system saw over 5.2 million visits across all branches, a 12% increase from 2020 (which had been heavily impacted by lockdowns). While this recovery was significant, it also highlighted the shifting behaviors of library patrons—many of whom had grown accustomed to digital alternatives during the pandemic.

  • Reopening of all branches by mid-2021
  • Expanded hours (some branches extended service to 7 days a week)
  • Hybrid programming (in-person + virtual events)
  • Targeted marketing to underserved communities

    • Branches in Englewood and Roseland (historically lower-visited areas) saw visits rise by 35% due to mobile outreach vans and partnerships with local nonprofits.

2. Why Did Chicago Public Libraries See a Visitor Rate Increase in 2021?

The 12% jump in 2021 wasn’t accidental—it was the result of strategic pivots in response to pandemic disruptions. Here are the top 5 factors that drove the recovery:

2.1 Hybrid Programming: Blending In-Person & Digital Experiences

CPL recognized that patrons wanted both physical and virtual options. In 2021, they introduced:

  • "Library Passport" – A program where patrons could attend one in-person event and get a digital badge for a virtual workshop.
  • Live-streamed author talks with Q&A sessions for those who couldn’t attend in person.
  • Virtual storytimes for toddlers, paired with in-person "Storytime Kits" (books + plush toys) for pickup.

Result: Program attendance rose by 25%, with 30% of participants engaging in both formats.

2.2 Expanded Accessibility: Mobile Libraries & Pop-Up Events

Some neighborhoods lack reliable transportation, so CPL launched:

  • "Bookmobile 2.0" – A rebranded mobile library van that visited 12 underserved ZIP codes weekly.
  • Pop-up libraries in parking lots, community centers, and churches during high-traffic events (e.g., farmers' markets).
  • Partnerships with food banks to offer library resources alongside meals.

Impact: Branches in Englewood and Austin saw visits increase by 40% in targeted areas.

2.3 Targeted Marketing to Re-Engage Patrons

CPL’s digital marketing team used data analytics to identify at-risk patrons (those who hadn’t visited since 2020) and re-engage them with:

  • Personalized email campaigns (e.g., "We miss you! Here’s a free e-book credit.")
  • Social media challenges (e.g., "#ReadWithMe" where patrons shared reading progress).
  • Loyalty rewards (e.g., "Visit 5 times this month, get a free book").

Outcome: Re-engagement rates exceeded 30%, with young adults (18-34) being the most responsive.

2.4 Focus on Community Partnerships

CPL didn’t just wait for patrons to come—they went to them. Key collaborations included:

  • With schools – Offering free tutoring and homework help during after-school hours.
  • With nonprofits – Hosting job fairs and financial literacy workshops in partnership with United Way and Workforce Development Centers.
  • With local businesses – Sponsoring "Library Business Hours" where small shops could use CPL’s free meeting rooms.

Example: The Harold M. Behrman Branch partnered with a local college to offer free SAT prep classes, leading to a 20% increase in teen visits.

2.5 Data-Driven Branch Improvements

CPL analyzed foot traffic patterns and adjusted branch hours based on demand:

  • Extended evening hours at Harold Washington and Daley Branches (now open until 9 PM on weekdays).
  • Added weekend programming at Gwendolyn Brooks (now hosts family game nights every Saturday).
  • Redesigned layouts to reduce congestion (e.g., self-checkout kiosks at Behrman Branch cut wait times by 40%).

Result: Average daily visits per branch increased by 15%.

3. 8 Actionable Strategies to Boost Visitor Rates (Like CPL Did in 2021)

If your library (or any public institution) wants to increase visitor rates, CPL’s 2021 strategies offer a blueprint for success. Here are 8 proven tactics you can implement:

Strategy 1: Launch a Hybrid Event Model (Like CPL’s "Library Passport")

How CPL Did It:

  • Offered one free in-person event + one free virtual event per month for patrons.
  • Used QR codes at events to redirect attendees to live-streamed sessions.

How to Apply It: ✅ Create a "Double Engagement" program where patrons get two entries (one in-person, one digital) for a single fee. ✅ Partner with local businesses to co-host hybrid workshops (e.g., a book club + wine tasting). ✅ Track engagement with unique event codes to measure cross-platform interest.

Expected Outcome:

  • 30%+ increase in event attendance from hybrid offerings.

Strategy 2: Deploy Mobile & Pop-Up Libraries (Like CPL’s Bookmobile 2.0)

How CPL Did It:

  • Reassigned a van to underserved ZIP codes with high poverty rates.
  • Offered "Library in a Box" – A portable setup for events like farmers' markets and festivals.

How to Apply It: ✅ Identify low-visit neighborhoods using census data and foot traffic analytics. ✅ Partner with local orgs (churches, community centers) to host pop-up branches. ✅ Use social media to announce mobile visits (e.g., "Today’s stop: 12 PM at the Englewood Farmers' Market!").

Expected Outcome:

  • 40%+ visit increase in targeted areas within 6 months.

Strategy 3: Implement a Re-Engagement Campaign (Like CPL’s "We Miss You" Emails)

How CPL Did It:

  • Segmented inactive patrons (those who hadn’t visited since 2020).
  • Sent personalized emails with free digital credits and exclusive in-person event invites.

How to Apply It: ✅ Use your library’s database to find lapsed patrons (last visit >6 months ago). ✅ Offer a "Welcome Back" discount (e.g., free book rental for first visit). ✅ Leverage SMS marketing for last-minute event reminders.

Expected Outcome:

  • 25-35% re-engagement rate among targeted patrons.

Strategy 4: Expand Community Partnerships (Like CPL’s School & Business Collaborations)

How CPL Did It:

  • Partnered with Chicago Public Schools to offer free tutoring during after-school hours.
  • Worked with small businesses to rent out meeting rooms at discounted rates.

How to Apply It: ✅ Reach out to local schools to co-host STEM programs or reading challenges. ✅ Offer "Library Business Hours" where entrepreneurs can use free resources. ✅ Host joint events (e.g., a library + museum exhibit night).

Expected Outcome:

  • 20-30% increase in foot traffic from partner-driven programs.

Strategy 5: Optimize Branch Hours Based on Data (Like CPL’s Extended Evening Hours)

How CPL Did It:

  • Analyzed foot traffic data and extended hours at high-demand branches.
  • Added weekend programming at Gwendolyn Brooks to capture family visitors.

How to Apply It: ✅ Use your library’s check-in/out system to identify peak hours. ✅ Survey patrons to ask: "What times work best for you?" ✅ Test extended hours (e.g., late-night study sessions for college students).

Expected Outcome:

  • 15-25% increase in daily visits at optimized branches.

Strategy 6: Introduce Gamification (Like CPL’s "Summer Quest" Reading Challenge)

How CPL Did It:

  • Created a digital reading challenge with badges and rewards.
  • Partnered with local businesses to offer prizes (e.g., free coffee for top readers).

How to Apply It: ✅ Launch a "Read & Win" program with monthly prizes. ✅ Use a points system where every book = 10 points, attending a program = 50 points. ✅ Promote on social media with user-generated content (e.g., patrons sharing their progress).

Expected Outcome:

  • 20-40% increase in book checkouts during challenge periods.

Strategy 7: Improve Accessibility with Tech Upgrades (Like CPL’s Self-Checkout Kiosks)

How CPL Did It:

  • Installed self-checkout machines at high-traffic branches to reduce wait times.
  • Added more public computers with free Wi-Fi for job seekers.

How to Apply It: ✅ Audit your checkout process – Are there long lines? Can you add kiosks or mobile checkouts? ✅ Ensure all digital resources (e-books, databases) are mobile-friendly. ✅ Offer "Tech Help Desks" where staff assist patrons with digital literacy.

Expected Outcome:

  • 30% faster service and higher patron satisfaction.
