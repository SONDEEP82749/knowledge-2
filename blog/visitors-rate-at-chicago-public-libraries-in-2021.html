2. Why Did CPL’s Visitor Rates Rebound in 2021?

The 19.5% increase in 2021 wasn’t accidental. Several strategic moves contributed to this recovery:

2.1 Agile Reopening Strategies

CPL didn’t wait for full pandemic relief to reopen. Instead, it implemented:

  • Phased reopenings (starting with high-demand branches like Harold Washington).
  • Appointment-based access to control crowds.
  • Extended hours (some branches opened until 9 PM on weekdays).

Result: By December 2021, 60% of branches were operating at pre-pandemic capacity.

2.2 Targeted Community Outreach

CPL identified underserved neighborhoods and tailored programs to re-engage them:

  • Mobile libraries returned to low-visit areas (e.g., Englewood, Roseland).
  • Partnerships with food banks (e.g., Feeding Chicago) to combine library visits with meal distributions.
  • Multilingual staffing to support non-English speakers.

Example: The Gwendolyn Brooks Branch launched "Brooks After Dark"—a weekend program for teens and young adults—leading to a 25% increase in youth visits.

2.3 Innovative Programming

CPL didn’t just reopen branches—it reinvented how libraries served patrons:

  • "Library on Wheels" (a mobile tech lab for job seekers).
  • "Tech Tuesdays" (free computer training for seniors).
  • "Book Clubs for All" (virtual + in-person groups for different reading levels).

Impact:

  • Tech-related visits increased by 30%.
  • Adult education programs saw a 22% rise.

2.4 Data-Driven Decision Making

CPL used real-time analytics to adjust strategies:

  • Tracked which branches had the highest rebound (e.g., Harold Washington).
  • Identified drop-off points (e.g., weekday afternoons had lower visits).
  • A/B tested programs (e.g., virtual vs. in-person storytimes).

Outcome:

  • More efficient staffing (e.g., more librarians on weekends).
  • Better resource allocation (e.g., more e-books in high-demand genres).
