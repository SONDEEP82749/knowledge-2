Chicago Public Libraries Visitor Trends in 2021: What the Data Shows

Before diving into strategies, let’s examine the hard numbers from CPL’s 2021 performance.

1. Overall Visitor Decline and Recovery

  • 2019 (Pre-Pandemic): CPL served 1.6 million unique visitors across all branches.
  • 2020 (Pandemic Peak): Visits plummeted to 950,000—a 40% drop.
  • 2021 (Partial Recovery): Visits increased to 1.2 million, but still 25% below 2019 levels.

2. Branch-Specific Performance

Not all libraries recovered at the same rate. Some high-traffic branches, like The Newberry Library (a research hub) and Harold Washington Library Center (downtown flagship), saw faster rebounds due to:

  • Essential services (e.g., Wi-Fi, job centers, community programs)
  • Strategic reopening plans (e.g., limited capacity, appointment-based access)
  • Strong digital integration (online catalogs, virtual events)

In contrast, neighborhood branches in lower-income areas struggled more, with some seeing visits drop by 50%+ in 2021. This disparity highlights equity gaps in library access post-pandemic.

3. Digital vs. Physical Engagement

While in-person visits declined, digital engagement surged:

  • Online catalog searches increased by 30% in 2021.
  • Virtual programs (e.g., storytimes, job workshops) attracted 200,000+ participants.
  • E-book and audiobook loans grew by 15% compared to 2019.

This shift suggests that hybrid models (blending physical and digital) will be key to long-term success.

4. Key Demographics Shifting

  • Students (K-12 & College): Returned in 2021, but homework help services saw a 20% decline due to remote learning persistence.
  • Adults (25-54): Dominated post-pandemic visits, likely seeking job resources, computer access, and quiet study spaces.
  • Seniors (65+): Visited less frequently, with many relying on curbside pickup for books.

5. Why Some Libraries Recovered Faster Than Others

Successful branches in 2021 implemented proactive strategies, such as: ✅ Flexible reopening schedules (e.g., extended hours, weekend access) ✅ Targeted marketing (promoting underutilized services like language classes, tech workshops) ✅ Partnerships with local businesses (e.g., free Wi-Fi for nearby cafes, co-hosted events) ✅ Data-driven decision-making (tracking foot traffic via heatmaps and visitor surveys)

10 Actionable Strategies to Boost Visitor Rates in 2024

Now that we’ve analyzed the 2021 trends, let’s explore proven strategies to increase library visits—whether you’re managing a CPL branch or another public institution.

Strategy 1: Leverage Data Analytics to Identify Low-Traffic Hours

Problem: Many libraries operate on fixed schedules without analyzing when visitors actually arrive.

Solution: Use visitor analytics tools (like Google Analytics for Libraries or branch-specific heatmaps) to track:

  • Peak vs. off-peak hours (e.g., if most visits happen 10 AM–2 PM, consider evening shifts).
  • Seasonal trends (e.g., higher visits in summer due to school breaks).

Real-World Example: The Skokie Public Library (near Chicago) noticed that weekday evenings (5–7 PM) had low attendance despite high demand for quiet study spaces. They adjusted hours, added late-night computer access, and saw a 15% increase in evening visits within three months.

How to Implement:

  1. Install free analytics tools (e.g., LibraryAware, LibInsight).
  2. Survey visitors (via Google Forms or SMS polls) to ask:
    • "What time of day works best for you?"
    • "Are there services you’d use if offered at different hours?"
  3. Test new schedules (e.g., Saturday mornings for families, weekday nights for adults).

Strategy 2: Rebrand Underutilized Spaces

Problem: Many libraries have unused rooms (e.g., meeting spaces, maker labs) that could attract more visitors.

Solution: Repurpose spaces with themed events or specialized services:

  • Teen Zones: Dedicate a space for gaming tournaments, coding clubs, or art workshops.
  • Business Incubators: Partner with local startups to offer free coworking hours.
  • Wellness Corners: Set up meditation spaces, yoga sessions, or mental health resources.

Real-World Example: The **Chicago Public Library’s Graham Library (West Side) transformed its basement into a "Future Center"—a tech hub with 3D printers, VR headsets, and AI workshops. Within a year, weekly visits increased by 30% as tech-savvy users flocked to the space.

How to Implement:

  1. Audit unused spaces—ask staff: "What could this room be used for?"
  2. Host a "Space Redesign Challenge" with local community groups.
  3. Promote new offerings via social media (Instagram, Facebook) and email newsletters.

Strategy 3: Partner with Local Businesses for Cross-Promotion

Problem: Libraries often operate in silos, missing opportunities to collaborate with nearby businesses.

Solution: Form strategic partnerships to:

  • Offer mutual discounts (e.g., "Show your library card for 10% off at Starbucks").
  • Co-host events (e.g., book clubs at local bookstores, fitness classes at gyms).
  • Provide free Wi-Fi access in exchange for business promotions.

Real-World Example: The Newberry Library (Chicago) partnered with The Book Stall (a rare bookshop) to host "Curator’s Corner" events, where local historians gave talks and visitors could browse exclusive collections. This doubled attendance at both locations.

How to Implement:

  1. Identify complementary businesses (e.g., coffee shops, gyms, tech stores).
  2. Propose a 3-month trial of shared promotions.
  3. Track foot traffic before and after to measure success.

Strategy 4: Offer "First-Time Visitor" Incentives

Problem: Many people don’t realize what libraries offer beyond books.

Solution: Create a "First Visit" program with:

  • Free welcome kits (e.g., library card, local guide, discount coupons).
  • Personalized tours (e.g., "Meet the Librarian" sessions).
  • Exclusive perks (e.g., priority access to hotspots, early event registration).

Real-World Example: The **Chicago Public Library’s Englewood Branch launched "First Visit Fridays", where new patrons received:

  • A customized library card holder
  • A free 30-minute tech tutorial
  • Invitation to a community potluck

This increased first-time visits by 45% in six months.

How to Implement:

  1. Design a welcome packet (digital or physical).
  2. Promote via social media with a hashtag challenge (#MyFirstLibraryVisit).
  3. Follow up with a survey to gather feedback.

Strategy 5: Host "Pop-Up" Events in High-Traffic Areas

Problem: Some neighborhoods don’t have a nearby library branch.

Solution: Bring the library to them with:

  • Mobile book buses (like CPL’s "Bookmobile" program).
  • Pop-up reading nooks in parks, community centers, or shopping malls.
  • Flash workshops (e.g., "How to Use a Laptop" at a senior center).

Real-World Example: The **Chicago Public Library’s Mobile Outreach Team set up "Library in a Box" stations at farmers' markets and festivals, offering:

  • Free Wi-Fi
  • Book recommendations
  • Registration for local programs

This reached 5,000+ people who had never visited a CPL branch before.

How to Implement:

  1. Partner with local event organizers.
  2. Set up a portable station (even a pop-up tent with tablets).
  3. Collect contact info to invite them to a full branch visit.

Strategy 6: Gamify Library Engagement

Problem: Passive visitors don’t explore all services.

Solution: Turn library use into a game with:

  • Challenge cards (e.g., "Visit 5 branches, get a free coffee").
  • Points systems (e.g., earn badges for attending workshops).
  • Leaderboards (e.g., "Most Books Checked Out This Month").

Real-World Example: The Skokie Public Library introduced "Library Quest", where users could:

  • Scan QR codes at different branches
  • Complete mini-challenges (e.g., "Find a book by a local author")
  • Win prizes (e.g., gift cards, free passes to museums)

This boosted visits by 22% among teens and young adults.

How to Implement:

  1. Design a simple app or digital dashboard.
  2. Promote via social media with user-generated content.
  3. Offer real rewards (not just bragging rights).

Strategy 7: Improve Accessibility for All Users

Problem: Some groups (e.g., disabled individuals, non-English speakers) avoid libraries due to barriers.

Solution: Make the library more inclusive by:

  • Offering multilingual staff (e.g., Spanish, Arabic, Polish).
  • Providing assistive tech (e.g., screen readers, Braille books).
  • Hosting "Accessibility Awareness Days" with sensory-friendly hours.

Real-World Example: The **Chicago Public Library’s Humboldt Park Branch launched "Library for All" initiatives, including:

  • Weekly ASL storytimes
  • Quiet hours for neurodivergent patrons
  • Free translation services for non-English speakers

This increased visits by 35% in underserved communities.

How to Implement:

  1. Conduct an accessibility audit (ask: "What’s missing for my community?").
  2. Train staff on inclusive practices.
  3. Promote these services in local community groups.

Strategy 8: Use Social Media to Tell Compelling Stories

Problem: Many libraries post the same content repeatedly, leading to low engagement.

Solution: Shift from "broadcasting" to "storytelling" with:

  • Behind-the-scenes content (e.g., "Meet the Librarian Who Curates Rare Books").
  • User-generated content (e.g., "Tag us in your favorite library moment").
  • Trend-jacking (e.g., "Library Edition" of viral challenges).

Real-World Example: The **Chicago Public Library’s Instagram account gained 50,000+ followers by:

  • Posting "Library vs. Pop Culture" memes (e.g., "If Harry Potter Went to CPL").
  • Sharing patron stories (e.g., "How This Student Found Their First Job Here").
  • Running interactive polls (e.g., "What Should We Host Next?").

How to Implement:

  1. Audit your current social media—is it informational or engaging?
  2. Experiment with different formats (Reels, Stories, Threads).
  3. Engage with followers (reply to comments, ask questions).

Strategy 9: Create a "Library Passport" for Local Exploration

Problem: Visitors don’t explore multiple branches, even if they’re close.

Solution: Encourage branch-hopping with:

  • A "Library Passport" (a physical or digital stamp card).
  • Exclusive perks for visiting 3+ branches (e.g., free museum entry, priority event tickets).
  • Themed challenges (e.g., "Visit a Branch Named After a Famous Chicagoan").

Real-World Example: The **Chicago Public Library’s Passport Program allowed patrons to:

  • Get a stamp at each branch they visited
  • Unlock a "Grand Prize" (e.g., a year’s subscription to a local magazine)
  • Enter a raffle for bigger rewards

This doubled cross-branch visits in under a year.

How to Implement:

  1. Design a simple stamp card (digital or paper).
  2. Promote it via email and social media.
  3. Track participation to see which branches benefit most.

Strategy 10: Host "Silent Book Clubs" for Introverts & Busy Professionals

Problem: Some people avoid libraries because they associate them with loud, crowded spaces.
