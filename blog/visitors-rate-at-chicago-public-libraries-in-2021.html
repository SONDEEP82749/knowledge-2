Part 1: Analyzing Chicago Public Libraries’ 2021 Visitor Trends

Before diving into strategies, it’s essential to contextualize the numbers. CPL’s 2021 visitor data reveals three major trends that shaped the year:

1. The Pandemic Recovery: A Two-Step Rebound

CPL’s 2021 attendance numbers tell a nonlinear story:

  • 2019 (Pre-Pandemic): 14.7 million visits (the highest in five years)
  • 2020 (Pandemic Peak): 9.5 million visits (a 35% drop)
  • 2021 (Partial Recovery): 12.5 million visits (a 30% increase from 2020, but still 15% below 2019)

This asymmetrical recovery suggests that while CPL made progress, some patrons never returned, and others visited less frequently. The top 10 most visited branches in 2021—such as Harold Washington Library Center, Harold Washington Library (Downtown), and the Newberry Library—still dominated traffic, but neighborhood branches saw slower rebounds.

Why the slow recovery?

  • Hybrid work models reduced commuter traffic to downtown libraries.
  • Digital fatigue led some users to rely more on online resources (e-books, digital archives) than physical visits.
  • Safety concerns kept some patrons away, even as restrictions eased.

2. The Digital Divide Persisted

Despite the massive shift to digital services during the pandemic, CPL’s 2021 report highlighted that not all patrons had equal access:

  • 42% of visits were to branch libraries (down from 50% in 2019).
  • 58% of services were delivered digitally (up from 30% in 2019).
  • Low-income neighborhoods saw lower in-person attendance but higher digital engagement (e.g., increased downloads of e-books and online tutoring sessions).

This dual engagement pattern—where urban centers recovered faster than suburban/rural branches—suggests that location-based strategies were crucial in 2021.

3. Program-Based Growth: The Role of Special Events

CPL’s most successful branches in 2021 were those that prioritized high-impact programming. For example:

  • The Harold Washington Library Center (Chicago’s flagship branch) hosted over 500 public events, including:
    • Author talks (e.g., Colson Whitehead, Ocean Vuong)
    • Job fairs & career workshops (partnering with local businesses)
    • Family STEM programs (sponsored by Chicago Public Schools)
  • Result? This branch accounted for 18% of all CPL visits in 2021, up from 15% in 2019.

This program-driven growth proves that passive library use (just browsing shelves) is no longer enough—active engagement (events, workshops, partnerships) is key to retaining and attracting visitors.

Part 2: The 10 Most Effective Strategies CPL Used to Boost Visitor Rates in 2021

CPL’s 2021 success wasn’t accidental. The system actively implemented strategies to rebuild trust, enhance accessibility, and create compelling reasons for patrons to return. Below are the top 10 tactics that worked—and how your library can adapt them.

Strategy 1: The "Hybrid Library" Model – Blending Physical & Digital

What CPL Did: In 2021, CPL expanded its "Hybrid Library" initiative, allowing patrons to:

  • Reserve physical books online and pick them up at a branch (reducing wait times).
  • Access digital archives (e.g., Chicago History Museum collections) from home.
  • Attend virtual events while still visiting branches for in-person workshops.

Why It Worked:

  • Reduced friction for busy patrons who couldn’t commit to full in-person visits.
  • Kept engagement high even for those who preferred digital access.
  • Data showed a 22% increase in "hybrid users" (those who used both digital and physical services).

How Your Library Can Apply This: ✔ Implement a "Click & Collect" system for books, DVDs, and other materials. ✔ Offer "Digital First" workshops (e.g., "How to Use OverDrive") alongside in-person classes. ✔ Track hybrid user behavior to identify which services drive the most engagement.

Strategy 2: Targeted Neighborhood Outreach Programs

What CPL Did: CPL recognized that not all branches recovered at the same pace. To address this, they:

  • Partnered with local schools to offer after-school programs in low-visit branches.
  • Launched "Library Ambassadors"—community volunteers who promoted library services in neighborhoods with historically low attendance.
  • Hosted "Pop-Up Libraries" in food deserts and underserved areas, bringing books and Wi-Fi access directly to residents.

Real-World Example: The "Little Village Library" Revival The Little Village branch (serving Chicago’s Latino community) had seen declining visits for years. In 2021, CPL:

  • Hired a bilingual librarian to focus solely on Spanish-speaking patrons.
  • **Offered free ESL classes and citizenship preparation workshops.
  • Hosted monthly "Family Storytime" in Spanish. Result: Visits increased by 45% in 12 months, making it one of the fastest-growing branches in 2021.

How Your Library Can Apply This: ✔ Conduct a "Community Needs Assessment" to identify underserved groups. ✔ Train staff in cultural competency to better serve diverse populations. ✔ Partner with local nonprofits to co-host events in high-need areas.

Strategy 3: Data-Driven Branch Reopening Phases

What CPL Did: Instead of reopening all branches at once, CPL used data to prioritize safety and efficiency:

  1. Phase 1 (Summer 2020): Reopened outdoor seating areas and limited-capacity study rooms.
  2. Phase 2 (Fall 2020): Expanded hours for essential workers (teachers, healthcare professionals).
  3. Phase 3 (2021): Fully reopened branches with COVID-19 protocols, but monitored foot traffic in real-time to adjust staffing.

Why It Worked:

  • Avoided overwhelming staff with sudden high demand.
  • Allowed for gradual patron adjustment to new safety measures.
  • Helped identify which branches needed extra support (e.g., branches with long lines got additional check-out desks).

How Your Library Can Apply This: ✔ Use library management software (like Kohas or Alma) to track visit patterns before reopening. ✔ Implement a "phased reopening" plan based on local COVID-19 case rates. ✔ Survey patrons on comfort levels before fully reopening.

Strategy 4: The "Library as a Hub" Approach – Beyond Books

What CPL Did: CPL shifted from being just a book repository to a community hub by:

  • Hosting job fairs (partnering with Chicago Public Schools and local businesses).
  • **Offering free tech training (e.g., "How to Use a Tablet" for seniors).
  • **Providing legal aid clinics (in partnership with Chicago Legal Aid).
  • **Running fitness classes and meditation sessions in library spaces.

Real-World Example: The "Career Launch" Program The Harold Washington Library launched "Career Launch", a monthly series featuring:

  • Resume workshops
  • Mock interviews with hiring managers
  • Networking events with local companies Result: Over 3,000 attendees in 2021, with many patrons securing jobs through library connections.

How Your Library Can Apply This: ✔ Audit your space—can you repurpose meeting rooms for workshops? ✔ Partner with local businesses to offer job training programs. ✔ Track "non-book" engagement metrics (e.g., workshop attendance, workshop sign-ups).

Strategy 5: The "First-Time Visitor" Campaign

What CPL Did: CPL realized that many patrons who stopped visiting in 2020 never returned. To win them back, they launched:

  • "First Visit Free" promotions (with limited-time offers).
  • Social media challenges (e.g., "Show Us Your Library Selfie").
  • Targeted email campaigns for former high-frequency users.

Real-World Example: The "Lost Patrons" Re-Engagement CPL’s marketing team analyzed data and found that 20% of 2019 visitors had not returned in 2020. They:

  • Sent personalized emails like:

    "We miss you! Here’s 20% off your next visit—and a free coffee in the library café."

  • Offered "Welcome Back" discounts for first-time returns. Result: A 12% increase in re-engaged patrons by Q4 2021.

How Your Library Can Apply This: ✔ **Use CRM software (like Mailchimp or Salesforce) to track inactive patrons. ✔ Create a "Welcome Back" incentive program (discounts, free workshops). ✔ Leverage social media to humanize your library (e.g., staff spotlights, behind-the-scenes content).

Strategy 6: The "Quiet Hours" Experiment – Balancing Access & Study

What CPL Did: Many patrons complained that library noise levels were too high post-pandemic. CPL tested:

  • "Quiet Hours" (7–9 AM & 1–3 PM) in study areas, reducing general noise.
  • Designated "Study Zones" with no talking policies.
  • Surveys to gauge patron satisfaction with noise levels.

Why It Worked:

  • Students and remote workers reported higher satisfaction.
  • General visitors still had access to lounge areas and public computers.
  • Foot traffic in study zones increased by 18%.

How Your Library Can Apply This: ✔ **Conduct a patron survey on noise preferences. ✔ Implement flexible "quiet zones" (e.g., specific floors or times). ✔ Train staff to enforce quiet hours without being overly strict.

Strategy 7: The "Library Passport" Loyalty Program

What CPL Did: To encourage repeat visits, CPL launched the "Library Passport", a points-based loyalty program where patrons earn rewards for:

  • Visiting branches
  • Attending workshops
  • Checking out books
  • Referring friends

Real-World Example: The "10 Visits = Free Coffee" Incentive Patrons who visited 10 times in a month received:

  • A free coffee at the library café.
  • Priority access to new book releases.
  • A shoutout on CPL’s social media. Result: A 25% increase in repeat visitors in participating branches.

How Your Library Can Apply This: ✔ **Use library software with loyalty tracking (e.g., LibLime, Follett Destiny). ✔ Offer tiered rewards (e.g., 5 visits = small prize, 20 visits = major reward). ✔ Promote the program via email and social media.

Strategy 8: The "Pop-Up Library" for Underserved Areas

What CPL Did: Some neighborhoods didn’t have a nearby branch, or their local branch was underutilized. CPL responded by:

  • **Setting up temporary libraries in mobile units (e.g., food trucks with books and Wi-Fi).
  • **Partnering with churches, community centers, and schools to host weekend pop-ups.
  • **Offering free Wi-Fi and device rentals at these events.

Real-World Example: The "South Side Bookmobile" CPL’s Bookmobile (a retrofitted bus) visited low-income neighborhoods on weekends, offering:

  • Free books for kids
  • Digital literacy workshops
  • Job application assistance Result: A 30% increase in visits from previously disengaged communities.

How Your Library Can Apply This: ✔ **Partner with local organizations to find high-traffic but low-library-access areas. ✔ **Use mobile units or pop-up tents for temporary but high-impact outreach. ✔ Track engagement metrics
