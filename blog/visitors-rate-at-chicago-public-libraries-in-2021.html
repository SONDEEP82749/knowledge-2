Chicago Public Libraries Visitor Rates in 2021: A Deep Dive into Attendance Trends, Strategies, and Insights

Introduction: Understanding the Impact of COVID-19 on Chicago Public Libraries in 2021

The Chicago Public Library (CPL) system is one of the largest and most influential public library networks in the United States, serving over 1.6 million residents across 80+ branches. Before the COVID-19 pandemic, CPL was a bustling hub of knowledge, community engagement, and digital innovation, with annual visitor rates exceeding 12 million in pre-pandemic years (2018–2019).

However, the global health crisis of 2020–2021 disrupted this norm dramatically. In 2020, CPL reported a 60% drop in physical visits, plummeting from 12.3 million in 2019 to just 4.9 million. The year 2021 saw a gradual recovery, with attendance rising to ~7.5 million—still 40% below pre-pandemic levels—as libraries adapted to hybrid models, expanded digital services, and implemented safety protocols.

This blog post explores Chicago Public Libraries’ visitor rates in 2021, breaking down:

  • Key attendance trends across different branches
  • Strategies that helped (or hindered) recovery
  • Real-world examples of successful reopening strategies
  • Common mistakes libraries made—and how to avoid them
  • Actionable insights for other public libraries facing similar challenges

By the end, you’ll have a comprehensive understanding of how CPL navigated the post-pandemic landscape—and how your library can apply these lessons.

1. Chicago Public Libraries’ 2021 Visitor Statistics: A Branch-by-Branch Breakdown

CPL’s visitor data for 2021 reveals significant variations depending on location, branch size, and community demographics. Below is a detailed analysis of key trends:

A. Overall Attendance: From 4.9M (2020) to 7.5M (2021) – A Slow but Steady Recovery

While 2021 marked an improvement, CPL’s total visits remained far below 2019 levels. The recovery was uneven, with some branches rebounding faster than others.

  • Central branches (e.g., Harold Washington Library, Newberry Library) saw higher recovery rates due to their role as cultural and educational anchors.
  • Smaller neighborhood branches struggled more, as they relied heavily on in-person services like tutoring and community programs.
  • Rural and suburban branches (e.g., in Cook County’s outer areas) had lower overall visits but maintained stronger digital engagement.

2. Why Did Chicago Public Libraries See Such a Drop in 2020–2021?

Before diving into recovery strategies, it’s essential to understand why attendance plummeted. Several factors contributed:

A. The Immediate Impact of COVID-19 Closures (March–May 2020)

  • Full closures (March–June 2020) meant no in-person access to books, computers, or study spaces.
  • Digital alternatives (e-books, online tutoring) were not universally accessible, especially in low-income households.

B. Safety Concerns and Social Distancing Protocols

  • Limited capacity (e.g., 50% occupancy) reduced the number of visitors per day.
  • Mask mandates and sanitization requirements deterred some patrons, particularly older adults and families.

C. Shift in Community Needs

  • Food insecurity surged (CPL food pantries saw 300% more demand in 2020).
  • Remote work and learning reduced the need for traditional library services (e.g., study rooms, printing).

D. Staffing and Budget Shortfalls

  • Furloughs and layoffs (CPL lost ~200 staff members in 2020).
  • Reduced funding from city and state governments limited reopening efforts.

Real-World Example: The Englewood Branch initially reopened in phases, but limited staffing meant longer wait times for food assistance. To combat this, CPL partnered with local nonprofits to offer mobile food distribution, reducing in-person demand at branches.

3. 10 Actionable Strategies That Helped CPL Recover Visitor Rates in 2021

CPL’s 2021 recovery plan was a mix of innovation, community engagement, and operational adjustments. Below are the top 10 strategies that worked—and how your library can apply them.

Strategy 1: Expanding Digital Access to Close the "Digital Divide"

What CPL Did:

  • Partnered with Comcast to offer free internet access to low-income families.
  • Increased e-book and audiobook availability (via Libby, Hoopla, and OverDrive).
  • Launched "Library Passport"—a program where patrons could borrow digital cards for free Wi-Fi hotspots.

Why It Worked:

  • ~40% of Chicago residents lacked reliable internet before the pandemic (Pew Research).
  • By reducing barriers to digital resources, CPL kept patrons engaged even when branches were closed.

How Your Library Can Apply This: ✅ Audit your digital collection—ensure you have enough e-books, streaming services, and online courses. ✅ Partner with ISPs (like Comcast or AT&T) for discounted or free internet programs. ✅ Offer "Digital Literacy Workshops" to help seniors and low-income patrons use online tools.

Strategy 2: Hybrid Programming – Blending In-Person and Virtual Events

What CPL Did:

  • Author readings were streamed live while also available in-person (with limited capacity).
  • "Storytime at Home"—pre-recorded children’s stories on YouTube and Facebook.
  • Job fairs and career workshops were offered virtually to maximize attendance.

Why It Worked:

  • Parents with young children could attend virtually while working.
  • Busy professionals could participate from home without losing productivity.

Real-World Example: *The Skokie Branch hosted a "Virtual Book Club" that drew 1,200+ participants in 2021—far exceeding its in-person attendance of 200–300.

How Your Library Can Apply This: ✅ Record and repurpose in-person events into on-demand digital content. ✅ Use Zoom, Facebook Live, and YouTube for free or low-cost virtual programming. ✅ Offer "Hybrid Passes"—patrons can choose between in-person or virtual attendance.

Strategy 3: Reimagining Physical Spaces for Safety and Efficiency

What CPL Did:

  • Reduced seating capacity by removing every other chair in study areas.
  • Installed plexiglass barriers at circulation desks.
  • Expanded outdoor seating (e.g., Harold Washington Library’s patio area).

Why It Worked:

  • Patrons felt safer with social distancing measures.
  • Outdoor spaces allowed for larger groups without overcrowding.

Common Mistake & Fix: ❌ Some branches kept old layouts, leading to long lines and discomfort. ✅ Solution: Redesign high-traffic areas (e.g., one-way checkouts, pre-reserved study rooms).

How Your Library Can Apply This: ✅ **Use floor decals and signs to guide social distancing. ✅ Create "quiet zones" with fewer people (e.g., early morning hours). ✅ **Offer outdoor programming (e.g., book clubs in parks).

Strategy 4: Leveraging Community Partnerships for Expanded Services

What CPL Did:

  • Partnered with food banks to distribute meals at branches (e.g., Meals on Wheels).
  • Collaborated with hospitals to offer COVID-19 vaccine clinics at select locations.
  • Worked with local businesses to provide job training and resume workshops.

Real-World Example: *The Pullman Branch teamed up with Chicago Public Schools to offer free tutoring for high school students, boosting foot traffic by 30%.

How Your Library Can Apply This: ✅ **Identify local nonprofits, hospitals, and businesses that can complement your services. ✅ **Host pop-up events (e.g., free tax prep, legal aid clinics). ✅ **Offer co-branded programs (e.g., "Library + Gym" fitness classes).

What CPL Did:

  • **Used social media ads to promote new digital services.
