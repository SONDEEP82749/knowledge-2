Chicago Public Libraries Visitor Rates in 2021: Trends, Insights, and Strategies for Growth

Introduction: A Deeper Look at Chicago Public Libraries’ 2021 Visitor Trends

Chicago Public Libraries (CPL) have long been a cornerstone of the city’s cultural and educational landscape. In 2021, the pandemic’s lingering effects reshaped how people engaged with public libraries—both in-person and digitally. According to CPL’s annual reports, the system saw over 12 million visits across its 80+ branches, a 15% decline from pre-pandemic levels in 2019 but a steady recovery from the sharp drop in 2020.

This shift wasn’t just about numbers—it reflected changing behaviors, evolving service demands, and the need for libraries to adapt. Whether you're a library administrator, urban planner, or data-driven researcher, understanding these trends can help optimize visitor engagement, resource allocation, and community impact.

What does this mean? Libraries are no longer just physical spaces—they’re hybrid hubs where digital and in-person experiences must work in tandem.

2. Why Did Visitor Rates Drop in 2020? (And What Can We Learn?)

The 43% drop in 2020 wasn’t just about COVID-19—it was a perfect storm of factors:

A. Immediate Pandemic Impact

  • Branch closures: Many CPL locations were fully or partially closed for months.
  • Safety concerns: Even when reopened, social distancing, mask mandates, and limited seating reduced capacity.
  • Shift to remote work/learning: With schools and offices closed, non-essential visits declined.

B. Long-Term Behavioral Shifts

  • Increased digital comfort: People got used to online shopping, banking, and education, making physical library trips less urgent.
  • Declining bookstore traffic: Independent bookstores and Amazon took share from traditional lending.
  • Competing entertainment: Streaming services (Netflix, Spotify) reduced demand for in-person media consumption.

C. Economic Factors

  • Lower disposable income: Many Chicagoans cut back on non-essential spending, including library memberships.
  • Unemployment spikes: In 2020, Chicago’s unemployment hit 11.6% (vs. ~8% in 2019), reducing discretionary spending.

Lesson for 2022 and beyond: Libraries must anticipate future disruptions—whether from climate change, economic downturns, or new technologies—and diversify their offerings to stay relevant.

3. 8 Actionable Strategies to Boost Visitor Rates in 2024

Now that we’ve analyzed the past trends, let’s explore proactive strategies to increase visitor rates—both in-person and digitally.

Strategy 1: Leverage Data-Driven Branch Planning

Problem: Some CPL branches underperform due to poor location or outdated services. Solution: Use visitor analytics to optimize branch placement and programming.

How CPL Did It:

  • Analyzed foot traffic data to identify underutilized branches in high-density areas.
  • Redesigned hours for working professionals (e.g., extended evening hours at Daley Library).
  • Partnered with local businesses to offer co-located services (e.g., job training at Englewood Branch).

Actionable Steps for Your Library: ✔ Track heatmaps of high-traffic vs. low-traffic areas. ✔ Survey patrons to ask: "What would make you visit more often?" ✔ Adjust programming based on demographic data (e.g., more parenting workshops in family-heavy branches).

Strategy 2: Enhance Digital Accessibility

Problem: Many patrons prefer digital but struggle with user-friendly platforms. Solution: Improve online resources to retain and attract remote users.

How CPL Succeeded:

  • Expanded e-book collections with more diverse titles (including Spanish-language books).
  • Launched "Library Passport"—a free app for digital checkouts, event RSVPs, and loyalty rewards.
  • Partnered with local tech hubs to offer free Wi-Fi and device training.

Actionable Steps: ✔ Audit your digital catalog—ensure it’s mobile-friendly and easy to navigate. ✔ Offer "Digital Literacy" classes for seniors and low-income users. ✔ Promote "Digital First" services (e.g., online homework help, virtual book clubs).

Strategy 3: Create Hyper-Local Community Programs

Problem: Generic programs don’t resonate with niche communities. Solution: Tailor offerings to specific neighborhoods.

Real-World Example: Little Village Library’s Bilingual Initiatives

  • Recognizing that Little Village (Chicago’s largest Mexican-American neighborhood) had lower engagement, CPL:
    • Hired a Spanish-speaking librarian to lead bilingual storytimes.
    • Hosted "Café Literario"—a book club with Mexican coffee and pastries.
    • Partnered with local schools to offer ESL (English as a Second Language) tutoring.
  • Result: Visits increased by 22% in 2021.

Actionable Steps: ✔ Conduct neighborhood surveys to identify unmet needs. ✔ Collaborate with local businesses (e.g., language schools, cultural centers). ✔ Offer "Pop-Up Programs" in community centers, parks, and churches.

Strategy 4: Gamify Library Engagement

Problem: Patrons forget about library services between visits. Solution: Use gamification to encourage repeat visits.

How CPL Used Gamification:

  • "Summer Reading Challenge"—kids earned prizes for reading a certain number of books.
  • "Library Passport Rewards"—patrons got badges for completing digital tasks (e.g., "10 e-books read").
  • "Scavenger Hunts" in branches with hidden QR codes leading to exclusive content.

Actionable Steps: ✔ Launch a "Library Bingo"—patrons mark off activities (e.g., "Attend a workshop," "Check out a cookbook"). ✔ Use AR (Augmented Reality) for interactive storytimes (e.g., virtual animals appearing on tablets). ✔ Partner with schools for reading competitions with classroom prizes.

Strategy 5: Improve First-Time Visitor Experience

Problem: First-time patrons often don’t return due to poor onboarding. Solution: Make first visits seamless and welcoming.

How CPL Improved Onboarding:

  • "First-Time Visitor Kits"—free notebooks, bookmarks, and a welcome guide.
  • "Library Orientation Tours"—staff-led 10-minute walkthroughs of key services.
  • "Ask a Librarian" Chatbot—24/7 instant help for digital issues.

Actionable Steps: ✔ Train staff to greet visitors with a friendly, personalized welcome. ✔ Offer "New Patron Discounts" (e.g., free first month of digital access). ✔ Create a "First Visit Checklist" (e.g., "Sign up for our newsletter, explore our digital library").

Strategy 6: Expand Partnerships with Local Businesses & Nonprofits

Problem: Libraries often operate in silos, missing cross-sector opportunities. Solution: Collaborate with businesses, schools, and nonprofits to attract new audiences.

Real-World Example: Englewood Branch’s Job Training Hub

  • Partnered with Chicago Public Schools and local employers to offer:
    • Free resume workshops
    • Job fairs with on-site hiring
    • Computer skills training
  • Result: 30% increase in adult visits in 2021.

Actionable Steps: ✔ Reach out to local colleges for student volunteer programs. ✔ Partner with gyms to offer "Library Membership Discounts" for gym members. ✔ Host "Community Market Days" where artisans, food trucks, and service providers set up booths.
