Chicago Public Libraries Visitor Rates in 2021: Trends, Insights, and Strategies for Growth

Introduction: A Deep Dive into Chicago Public Libraries’ 2021 Visitor Statistics

Chicago Public Libraries (CPL) is one of the largest and most vibrant public library systems in the United States, serving over 1.6 million residents across 80+ branches. In 2021—a year marked by the lingering effects of the COVID-19 pandemic—CPL faced unprecedented challenges while also adapting to new digital and hybrid service models.

According to the 2021 Annual Report released by CPL, the system saw a 12% decline in in-person visits compared to pre-pandemic levels (2019), with total visits dropping to approximately 4.2 million from a peak of 4.8 million in 2019. However, digital engagement surged, with online program registrations increasing by 30% and e-book checkouts rising by 25%.

This decline in physical visits raises critical questions:

  • How did COVID-19 reshape library usage patterns?
  • Which branches saw the most significant drops—or unexpected growth?
  • What strategies can libraries use to attract visitors back post-pandemic?
  • How can data-driven decisions help libraries optimize their services?

In this comprehensive guide, we’ll break down the 2021 visitor trends at Chicago Public Libraries, analyze why certain branches performed better than others, and provide actionable strategies to boost attendance, engagement, and community impact.

We’ll also explore real-world examples of successful library initiatives, common mistakes that hurt visitor numbers, and FAQs to help librarians, city planners, and community leaders make informed decisions.

1. The Impact of COVID-19 on Chicago Public Libraries in 2021

The pandemic forced CPL to rethink its operations, leading to:

  • Temporary branch closures (some reopened with limited capacity).
  • Shift to hybrid models (in-person + digital services).
  • Increased demand for remote learning resources (especially for students).
  • A surge in digital media consumption (e-books, audiobooks, online classes).

Why Did Visits Drop?

  1. Health Concerns – Many Chicagoans avoided public spaces due to COVID-19 fears.
  2. Remote Work & Learning – With schools and offices closed, fewer people visited libraries for research or study.
  3. Limited Capacity – CPL enforced social distancing measures, reducing seating and program availability.
  4. Shift to Digital – More people turned to online resources (Kanopy, Hoopla, LinkedIn Learning) instead of physical branches.

Unexpected Growth Areas

Despite the decline, some services thrived:

  • Job & Career Resources – Demand for resume workshops and LinkedIn training increased by 40%.
  • Tech & Digital Literacy Classes – Programs like "Intro to Coding" saw 30% more registrations.
  • Community Outreach Programs – Mobile libraries and pop-up reading events in underserved neighborhoods helped maintain engagement.

2. Branch-by-Branch Analysis: Which Libraries Performed Best?

Not all CPL branches were affected equally. Some recovered faster due to location, programming, and community demand. Below is a breakdown of key branches and their 2021 performance.

A. Harold Washington Library Center (Downtown) – The Anchor Branch

  • 2019 Visits: 1.2M
  • 2021 Visits: 950K (-21%)
  • Why It Struggled:
    • High foot traffic pre-pandemic meant long wait times for seating.
    • Limited outdoor space made social distancing difficult.
    • Competition with downtown offices (many employees worked remotely).
  • Why It Still Performed Well:
    • Strong digital offerings (virtual events, online reference services).
    • Partnerships with local businesses (e.g., free Wi-Fi zones for nearby cafes).
    • Specialized programs (law library, business resources) attracted niche audiences.

Lesson: Even major branches must adapt—diversifying services beyond physical space is key.

B. Edgewater Branch – A Hidden Gem

  • 2019 Visits: 150K
  • 2021 Visits: 180K (+20%)
  • Why It Grew:
    • Strong community ties – Edgewater is a diverse, artsy neighborhood with a loyal patron base.
    • Outdoor programming – The branch expanded patio events (book clubs, live music).
    • Targeted youth programs – "Summer Reading Challenge" saw 50% more participation than 2019.
    • Mobile library partnerships – Collaborated with local schools to bring books to students.

Lesson: Smaller branches can thrive by leveraging local culture and outdoor spaces.

C. Englewood Branch – Fighting the Drop with Community Focus

  • 2019 Visits: 80K
  • 2021 Visits: 65K (-19%)
  • Why It Struggled:
    • High poverty rate in the neighborhood led to lower overall engagement.
    • Limited digital access – Many patrons lacked reliable internet for online resources.
  • Why It Had Hope:
    • Expanded food pantry services – Partnered with local food banks to provide meals.
    • "Tech Tuesdays" – Free computer training helped bridge the digital divide.
    • Mobile library van – Increased accessibility for homebound residents.

Lesson: Libraries in underserved areas must prioritize basic needs (food, tech access) to retain visitors.

D. Newberry Library (Special Collections) – A Niche Success Story

  • 2019 Visits: 45K
  • 2021 Visits: 50K (+11%)
  • Why It Grew:
    • Focused on rare books & historical research – Attracted academics and genealogists.
    • Virtual exhibits – Digitized collections for online viewers.
    • Limited in-person access (due to fragility of materials) forced creative digital engagement.

Lesson: Specialized libraries can thrive by monetizing unique collections (memberships, virtual tours).

3. 10 Actionable Strategies to Boost Visitor Rates in 2024

While 2021 was a tough year, libraries can learn from the past to attract more visitors in 2024. Below are data-backed strategies to increase attendance, engagement, and community impact.

Strategy 1: Leverage Data to Identify Low-Performing Branches

Problem: Some branches see consistent declines—but why? Solution:

  • Analyze attendance trends (peak hours, seasonal drops).
  • Survey patrons (why they visit—or don’t).
  • Benchmark against similar libraries (e.g., NYC Public Library, LA Public Library).

Example: The Chicago Public Library’s 2022 Strategic Plan identified that after-school programs in certain branches had dropped by 30%. The solution? Partnering with local schools to offer extended hours and free tutoring.

Action Step:

  • Use Google Analytics (for digital engagement) and branch visit logs to spot patterns.
  • A/B test different programming schedules (e.g., weekend vs. weekday events).

Strategy 2: Expand Hybrid (In-Person + Digital) Programming

Problem: Many patrons prefer digital convenience, but some still want in-person experiences. Solution:

  • Offer "Choose Your Own Adventure" events (e.g., "Virtual Book Club + In-Person Discussion").
  • Use QR codes in branches to redirect users to online resources.
  • Live-stream popular workshops (e.g., job fairs, coding classes).

Example: The Skokie Public Library (near Chicago) ran a "Hybrid Storytime" program where:

  • Parents could attend in-person (with masks).
  • Others joined via Zoom for live reading sessions.
  • Result: 25% increase in program attendance in 2022.

Action Step:

  • Survey patrons to see which digital vs. in-person options they prefer.
  • Train staff to seamlessly transition between formats.

Strategy 3: Partner with Local Businesses & Nonprofits

Problem: Libraries often operate in silos—collaboration can expand reach. Solution:

  • Cross-promote with cafes, bookstores, and community centers.
  • Host joint events (e.g., "Library Night at a Local Pub").
  • Offer discounts to library cardholders at partner businesses.

Example: The Lincoln Park Branch partnered with The Book Stall (a local indie bookstore) to:

  • Host a "Book Swap & Sip" event (free for library cardholders).
  • Promote each other’s events via social media.
  • Result: 30% increase in foot traffic to both locations.

Action Step:

  • Reach out to 5-10 local businesses and propose mutually beneficial collaborations.
  • Create a "Library Pass" for discounted services (e.g., free Wi-Fi at partner cafes).

Strategy 4: Invest in Outdoor & Pop-Up Programming

Problem: Indoor spaces are limited—outdoor events can attract more visitors. Solution:

  • Set up "Library in the Park" days (free Wi-Fi, book displays, storytime).
  • Host pop-up events at farmers' markets, festivals, and community centers.
  • Use mobile libraries to reach rural or underserved areas.

Example: The Pilsen Branch (South Chicago) ran "Libro al Aire Libre" (Library in the Air) where:

  • Patrons could check out books while enjoying outdoor concerts.
  • Bilingual storytime attracted Spanish-speaking families.
  • Result: 40% increase in summer visits compared to 2021.

Action Step:

  • Apply for grants (e.g., National Endowment for the Arts) to fund outdoor events.
  • Work with local parks departments to secure free event spaces.

Strategy 5: Target Youth & Family Programs

Problem: Children’s program attendance dropped by 20% in 2021. Solution:

  • Make programs more interactive (e.g., Lego-building workshops, science experiments).
  • Partner with schools for after-school clubs.
  • Offer "Family Passes" (discounted rates for multi-generational visits).

Example: The Morgan Park Branch introduced "Tiny Readers Club" where:

  • Preschoolers got a "library passport" for completing reading challenges.
  • Parents received free book bundles for attending.
  • Result: 35% increase in young child visits in 2022.

Action Step:

  • Survey parents to see what types of programs they want.
  • Train librarians to engage kids with hands-on activities.

Strategy 6: Use Social Media & Digital Marketing

Problem: Many libraries still rely on word-of-mouth—social media can amplify reach. Solution:

  • Post behind-the-scenes content (e.g., "Meet Our Librarians" videos).
  • Run targeted ads (e.g., "Free Coding Classes for Teens").
  • Encourage user-generated content (e.g., "Tag Us in Your Library Photos").

Example: The Newberry Library (special collections) used Instagram Reels to:

  • Showcase rare books in fun, short videos.
  • Promote virtual exhibits with interactive polls.
  • Result: 50% increase in digital engagement in 2023.

Action Step:

  • Hire a part-time social media manager (or train a volunteer).
  • Track metrics (clicks, shares, conversions) to optimize posts.

Strategy 7: Offer Specialized Workshops & Career Services

Problem: Adult visits dropped by 20%—but job seekers still need help. Solution:

  • Host "Resume Bootcamps" with local hiring managers.
  • Offer free LinkedIn profile reviews.
  • Partner with universities for career prep workshops.

Example: The Harold Washington Library ran "Career Launch Chicago" where:

  • Job seekers got 1-on-1 resume critiques.
  • Employers hosted virtual hiring fairs.
  • Result: 40% increase in adult visits for career-related programs.

Action Step:

  • Survey local businesses to see what skills they need most.
  • Create a "Career Hub" section in the library with free resources.

Strategy 8: Improve Accessibility & Inclusivity

Problem: Some branches struggle with accessibility—leading to lower engagement. Solution:

  • Offer ASL (American Sign Language) storytime.
  • Provide wheelchair-accessible mobile libraries.
  • Host events in multiple languages (Spanish, Arabic, Polish, etc.).

Example: The Pullman Branch (South Chicago) introduced:

  • "Libro en Español" (Spanish-language programs).
  • Audiobook clubs for visually impaired patrons.
  • Result: 25% increase in diverse patron visits.

Action Step:

  • Conduct an accessibility audit of all branches.
  • Train staff in cultural competency.

Strategy 9: Create Loyalty Programs & Membership Tiers

Problem: One-time visitors don’t return—how to build long-term engagement? Solution:

  • Offer a "Premium Library Card" (extra perks like free concert tickets).
  • Run a "Visit 10 Branches, Get a Free Book" challenge.
  • Partner with local gyms for discounted memberships.

Example: The Skokie Public Library launched "Skokie Reads" where:

  • Patrons who attended 5+ events got a free e-reader.
  • Result: 30% increase in repeat visitors.
