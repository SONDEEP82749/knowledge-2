Common Mistakes That Inflate Parking Costs (And How to Avoid Them)

Many drivers unintentionally pay more for parking than necessary. Here are the biggest mistakes and how to fix them.

Mistake #1: Ignoring Off-Peak Discounts

Problem: Drivers always park during peak hours, even when off-peak spots are cheaper and just as convenient.

Solution: ✔ Use the calculator’s "Off-Peak Finder" to locate discounted lots. ✔ Adjust your schedule (e.g., leave work 15 minutes later to avoid rush-hour fees).

Mistake #2: Not Comparing Street Parking vs. Paid Lots

Problem: Many assume street parking is free, but tickets, meters, and limited availability often make it more expensive than paid lots.

Solution: ✔ Run a "Street vs. Lot" comparison in the calculator. ✔ Factor in time wasted searching for spots (which costs money in gas and lost productivity).

Mistake #3: Overestimating EV Charging Costs at Home

Problem: Some EV owners assume home charging is always cheaper, but utility rates vary, and public charging can be more efficient for short trips.

Solution: ✔ Compare home vs. public charging in the calculator. ✔ **Check for time-of-use rates (cheaper at night).

Mistake #4: Not Factoring in Wear-and-Tear on the Car

Problem: Drivers only consider gas/parking costs, but idling in traffic, tight parking spaces, and frequent stops increase maintenance costs.

Solution: ✔ Use the calculator’s "Total Cost of Ownership" mode to include depreciation and repairs. ✔ Consider carpooling or public transit to reduce wear on your vehicle.

Mistake #5: Forgetting About Tax Incentives for EVs

Problem: Many EV owners don’t know about federal/state tax credits for charging infrastructure, leading to unnecessary home charging costs.

Solution: ✔ Check the calculator’s "EV Incentives" section for applicable credits. ✔ Apply for rebates before committing to a home charging setup.

Mistake #6: Not Planning for Unexpected Parking Needs

Problem: Drivers only budget for normal days, but holidays, events, and construction can triple parking costs.

Solution: ✔ Use the calculator’s "Event Mode" to estimate temporary rate spikes. ✔ Plan ahead (e.g., park farther away or use public transit on high-demand days).
