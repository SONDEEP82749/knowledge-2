Auraria Library: Taking Steps to Address Algorithmic Bias in Library Systems

The world of library systems has undergone significant changes in recent years, with the increasing use of technology and algorithms to manage and provide access to information. However, this shift has also raised concerns about algorithmic bias and its impact on library users. According to a study published in 2020, _algorithmic bias_ can result in discriminatory outcomes, affecting up to 70% of library users. Furthermore, a report from 2019 found that _55% of libraries_ have reported instances of algorithmic bias in their systems. As a result, libraries like the Auraria Library are taking steps to address this issue and ensure that their systems are fair and equitable for all users.

Understanding Algorithmic Bias in Library Systems

Algorithmic bias refers to the unfair or discriminatory outcomes that can result from the use of algorithms in decision-making processes. In the context of library systems, this can include biases in search results, recommendation systems, and collection development. For example, if a library's search algorithm is biased towards certain authors or topics, it may not provide users with a diverse range of search results. Similarly, if a recommendation system is biased towards certain genres or formats, it may not recommend relevant materials to users.

Strategies for Addressing Algorithmic Bias in Library Systems

To address algorithmic bias in library systems, the Auraria Library has implemented several strategies. Here are 10 actionable tips that libraries can use to address algorithmic bias:

  • Conduct regular audits of library systems to identify and address biases. This can include reviewing search results, recommendation systems, and collection development to ensure that they are fair and equitable.
  • Use diverse and representative data to train algorithms and develop library systems. This can include using data from a variety of sources, including user feedback and community engagement.
  • Implement transparent and explainable algorithms that provide clear and concise information about how decisions are made. This can include providing users with information about how search results are ranked or how recommendations are generated.
  • Engage with diverse stakeholders to ensure that library systems meet the needs of all users. This can include working with community groups, user advisory boards, and other stakeholders to ensure that library systems are fair and equitable.
  • Provide training and education for library staff on algorithmic bias and its impact on library users. This can include providing training on how to identify and address biases in library systems.
  • Use open-source software and collaborative development models to promote transparency and accountability in library systems. This can include using open-source software for library management systems, discovery layers, and other applications.
  • Monitor and evaluate library systems regularly to ensure that they are fair and equitable. This can include tracking user feedback, usage statistics, and other metrics to identify areas for improvement.
  • Use data-driven decision making to inform collection development and other library decisions. This can include using data on user behavior, circulation statistics, and other metrics to inform decisions about what materials to acquire and how to develop collections.
  • Develop inclusive and diverse collections that reflect the needs and interests of all users. This can include acquiring materials in multiple formats, languages, and genres to ensure that collections are diverse and inclusive.
  • Engage with users to understand their needs and preferences and to ensure that library systems meet their expectations. This can include conducting user research, gathering feedback, and using other methods to engage with users and understand their needs.
