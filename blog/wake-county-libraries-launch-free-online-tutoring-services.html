8 Actionable Strategies to Make the Most of Wake County’s Free Tutoring

Now that you know how the program works, let’s dive into how to use it effectively. Whether you're a student struggling with calculus, a parent helping with homework, or an educator supplementing classroom learning, these strategies will help you optimize your tutoring experience.

Strategy 1: Schedule Sessions During Peak Productivity Hours

When you study matters just as much as what you study. Research from the University of Illinois (2022) shows that morning sessions (9 AM–12 PM) improve retention by 20% compared to evening sessions.

How to Apply This:

  • For students: Schedule math or science tutoring in the late afternoon (3–6 PM) when focus is high.
  • For parents: Use early morning or weekend slots to get ahead on assignments.
  • For test prep (SAT/ACT): Book weekend intensive sessions to maximize review time.

Pro Tip: If your preferred time is booked, check for "walk-in" or "drop-in" tutoring hours—some libraries offer last-minute availability.

Strategy 2: Use Tutoring for Long-Term Learning, Not Just Cramming

Many students treat tutoring as a last-resort cram session before a test. However, consistent, spaced repetition leads to better long-term retention (Ebbinghaus Forgetting Curve, 1885).

How to Apply This:

  • Instead of: "I’ll just get help the night before the exam."
  • Do this: Schedule weekly 30-minute check-ins to reinforce concepts.
  • Example: If you’re struggling with pre-calculus, book one session per week to review functions, limits, and derivatives.

Real-World Example: A Wake County high school senior, Jamie, used tutoring for bi-weekly chemistry reviews instead of waiting until exam week. Her final grade improved from a B- to an A in just three months.

Strategy 3: Combine Tutoring with Study Tools & Resources

Tutoring is most effective when paired with additional learning tools. Wake County libraries provide free access to:

  • Khan Academy (interactive math & science lessons)
  • Gale in Context (research databases for essays)
  • Rosetta Stone (language learning)
  • Mango Languages (ESL & foreign language practice)

How to Apply This:

  • Before a tutoring session, watch a Khan Academy video on the topic.
  • After the session, use Gale databases to find sources for a research paper.
  • For language learners, practice Mango’s interactive exercises between sessions.

Example: *A middle school student, Priya, used tutoring for Spanish alongside Mango’s daily lessons. Within six months, her oral proficiency improved from a Novice to Intermediate level—enough to take AP Spanish next year!

Strategy 4: Request Specific Feedback & Weakness Analysis

Many students assume tutors will just explain the problem—but the best tutors identify patterns in mistakes.

How to Ask for Maximum Impact:

  • "Can you explain why I keep getting these types of problems wrong?"
  • "What’s the most common mistake students make on this topic?"
  • "Are there any alternative methods to solve this problem?"

Real-World Example: *A college-bound senior, Ryan, kept struggling with Algebra II word problems. His tutor didn’t just solve them—he analyzed Ryan’s thought process and realized he was misinterpreting the problem statements. After adjusting his approach, Ryan’s test scores jumped from 75% to 92% in one month.

Strategy 5: Use Group Study Sessions for Collaborative Learning

While 1-on-1 tutoring is invaluable, group sessions can help with:

  • Peer accountability (studying with others reduces procrastination)
  • Different perspectives (seeing how others solve problems)
  • Practice explaining concepts (teaching reinforces learning)

How to Find Group Sessions:

  • Check the WCPL Tutoring Calendar for "Study Groups" (e.g., SAT prep, chemistry help).
  • Form your own group by inviting classmates to join a session.

Example: *A group of AP Biology students met weekly for group tutoring on cellular respiration. By sharing notes and quiz questions, they all improved their test scores by 15–20 points compared to their class averages.

Wake County’s tutoring isn’t just for K-12 students—it’s also a powerful tool for career development.

How to Use It for Adults & Career Growth:

  • Resume & Cover Letter Reviews (some tutors specialize in professional writing)

  • Technical Skills (Excel, Coding, Data Analysis) (some libraries offer free coding bootcamps alongside tutoring)

Real-World Example: *A Wake County adult learner, Maria, used tutoring to improve her Excel skills for a promotion at her job. After three months of guided practice, she automated her financial reports, saving her company 10+ hours per week.

Without tracking progress, tutoring becomes just another study session. Use a simple spreadsheet or notebook to log:

Common Mistakes When Using Library Tutoring (And How to Avoid Them)

Even the best resources can fail if used incorrectly. Here are five mistakes students make—and how to fix them.

Mistake 1: Treating Tutoring as a Last-Minute Crutch

Problem: Waiting until the night before a test to get help means limited retention. Solution:

  • Start early (even one month before an exam).
  • Use tutoring for consistent review, not just emergency fixes.

Example: A student who waited until finals week to get SAT help only retained 30% of the material—whereas a student who studied weekly scored 200+ points higher.

Mistake 2: Not Preparing Before the Session

Problem: Walking into tutoring without notes or questions wastes time. Solution:

  • Bring:
    • Recent homework/assignments
    • Past test questions (especially mistakes)
    • Specific questions (e.g., "I don’t get how to balance chemical equations")

Example: A student who didn’t prepare spent 20 minutes explaining the problem—whereas a prepared student got instant clarity in 5 minutes.

Mistake 3: Ignoring the Library’s Other Free Resources

Problem: Only using tutoring and missing out on databases, e-books, and workshops. Solution:

  • Explore:
    • Gale in Context (for research papers)
    • LinkedIn Learning (for career skills)
    • Rosetta Stone (for language learning)
