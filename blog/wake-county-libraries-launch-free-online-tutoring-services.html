8 Actionable Strategies to Maximize Your Wake County Free Online Tutoring Experience

Now that you know how the service works, let’s explore how to use it effectively. Many students sign up but don’t get the most out of their sessions—here’s how to optimize your learning.

Strategy 1: Set Clear Goals Before Each Session

Problem: Walking into a tutoring session without a plan leads to wasted time and unmet expectations. Solution:

  • Before logging in, ask yourself:
    • What specific topic am I struggling with?
    • What grade or level is this for?
    • Do I need help with homework, test prep, or conceptual understanding?
  • Example: Instead of saying, "I need help with math," say, "I’m stuck on quadratic equations for my Algebra II test next week."

Pro Tip: Write down 3 key questions you want answered before the session starts.

Strategy 2: Use the "Pre-Session Checklist"

Problem: Many students forget to prepare materials, leading to disorganized sessions. Solution:

  • Gather everything you need before logging in:
    • Textbook or notes (if working on a specific problem).
    • Past assignments (to show the tutor your work).
    • A calculator or graphing tool (if needed for math/science).
    • A quiet workspace (minimize distractions).
  • Example: If you’re struggling with chemistry stoichiometry, bring your lab notes and the problem set you’re working on.

Strategy 3: Leverage Drop-In vs. Scheduled Sessions Wisely

Problem: Some students only use scheduled sessions, missing out on immediate help when needed. Solution:

  • Drop-in sessions (24/7): Best for quick questions (e.g., "How do I solve this system of equations?").
  • Scheduled sessions: Best for deep learning (e.g., "I need a full review of trigonometry before my exam.").
  • Example: If you’re stuck on a biology question at 11 PM, use drop-in tutoring. If you need a full study plan for your AP Chemistry test, book a scheduled session.

Strategy 4: Engage Actively—Don’t Just Listen

Problem: Passive learners don’t retain information as effectively. Solution:

  • Ask follow-up questions (e.g., "Can you explain this another way?").
  • Work through problems together (don’t let the tutor do all the work).
  • Example: Instead of saying, "Explain this," say, "I tried solving this, but I got stuck here—can you help me fix my approach?"

Strategy 5: Use SkillSurfer for Self-Paced Learning

Problem: Some students only rely on live tutors, missing out on interactive practice tools. Solution:

  • SkillSurfer (Brainfuse) offers quizzes, flashcards, and practice exercises in key subjects.
  • How to use it:
    1. Log in to your WCPL tutoring account.
    2. Navigate to SkillSurfer.
    3. Select your subject and grade level.
    4. Take practice tests to identify weak areas.
  • Example: If you’re weak in geometry proofs, use SkillSurfer to practice step-by-step reasoning before your next tutoring session.

Strategy 6: Record Sessions for Review

Problem: Students forget key concepts after the session ends. Solution:

  • Ask the tutor if recording is allowed (most platforms permit it).
  • Review the recording later to reinforce learning.
  • Example: If you’re prepping for the SAT, record your math tutoring session and revisit it before your next practice test.

Strategy 7: Combine Tutoring with Study Groups

Problem: Learning in isolation limits progress. Solution:

  • Form a study group with peers and use tutoring as a supplement.
  • Example: If you’re taking AP Biology, create a weekly study group where:
    • Monday: Review notes together.
    • Wednesday: Use Wake County’s tutoring for weak topics.
    • Friday: Take a practice quiz using SkillSurfer.

Strategy 8: Track Progress & Adjust Strategies

Problem: Without tracking, students don’t see improvement. Solution:

  • Keep a tutoring journal with:
    • Topics covered
    • Problems solved
    • Areas still needing work
  • Example:
    Date Topic Progress Next Steps
    5/10 Algebra Improved solving quadratics Practice word problems
    5/15 Chemistry Still struggling with reactions Book a scheduled session

Common Mistakes to Avoid When Using Wake County’s Free Online Tutoring

Even with the best resources, misusing tutoring services can hinder progress. Here are 5 mistakes students make—and how to fix them.

Mistake 1: Treating Tutoring Like a Crutch (Not a Tool for Learning)

What happens: Students only ask for answers instead of understanding the process. How to fix:

  • Ask "why" and "how"—not just "what."
  • Example:
    • ❌ "What’s the answer to this problem?"
    • ✅ "Why is this the correct approach? Can you walk me through the steps?"

Mistake 2: Skipping Preparation Before Sessions

What happens: Students log in unprepared, wasting tutor time. How to fix:

  • Always bring:
    • Your notes, textbook, or assignment.
    • Specific questions (not just "Help me with math").
  • Example:
    • ❌ "I need help with history."
    • ✅ "I’m stuck on the causes of the American Revolution—can we break down the key events?"

Mistake 3: Not Following Up with Practice

What happens: Students get help once but don’t reinforce learning. How to fix:

  • After each session:
    • Practice similar problems (use SkillSurfer or textbooks).
    • Review notes from the session.
  • Example: If you learn how to balance chemical equations, practice 5 more problems before your next session.

Mistake 4: Ignoring the Writing Lab for Non-Writing Subjects

What happens: Students only use tutoring for math/science but struggle with essays and reports. How to fix:

  • Even if you’re not a writing major, use the Writing Lab for:
    • Resume reviews (for job seekers).
    • Research paper help (for college students).
    • Email/professional communication (for working adults).
  • Example: A business student used the Writing Lab to improve their case study analysis—resulting in an A+.

Mistake 5: Not Using All Available Resources

What happens: Students only use live tutoring but miss out on SkillSurfer, job resources, and more. How to fix:

  • Explore all features:
    • SkillSurfer (practice tests).
    • Job & Career Center (resume help).
    • Language Learning (for non-native speakers).
  • Example: A Spanish student used SkillSurfer’s language tools to practice speaking before tutoring sessions, leading to faster fluency.
