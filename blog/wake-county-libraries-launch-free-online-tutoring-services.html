How to Access Wake County’s Free Online Tutoring Services

Before we explore the strategies and benefits, it’s essential to know how to get started. Wake County Libraries have partnered with Tutoring.com (a leading online tutoring platform) to provide this service. Here’s a step-by-step guide to accessing it:

Step 1: Verify Your Wake County Library Card

  • Do you already have a library card? If yes, you’re one step ahead—most services are available with an active card.
  • Don’t have one? Visit any Wake County Public Library branch (e.g., Raleigh Public Library, Cary Public Library, or Apex Public Library) to register. First-time cardholders can get a free card with just an ID.
  • Online registration? Yes! You can apply for a digital library card via the WCPL website and receive instant access to e-books, databases, and—soon—tutoring services.

Step 2: Log In to the Tutoring Portal

  1. Go to the Wake County Libraries Tutoring Page (link will be live once officially launched).
  2. Sign in with your library card number and PIN (or create an account if you don’t have one).
  3. Once logged in, you’ll see options for:
    • Live Tutoring (Chat & Video)
    • Homework Help
    • Test Prep (SAT, ACT, AP Exams)
    • College & Career Resources

Step 3: Choose Your Subject & Time Slot

  • Browse available tutors by subject (e.g., Algebra, Biology, Essay Writing, Coding).
  • Select a time—tutoring is available daily, including weekends, with no appointment needed for drop-in sessions.
  • Some subjects (like advanced math or foreign languages) may require scheduling in advance for specialized tutors.

Step 4: Start Your Session

  • Live Chat: Instant messaging with a tutor (great for quick questions).
  • Video Tutoring: A face-to-face session via Zoom-like interface (ideal for complex problems).
  • Homework Help: Upload assignments, and tutors will guide you step-by-step.

Step 5: Track Your Progress

  • The platform allows you to save past sessions, review notes, and set goals (e.g., "Improve my SAT score by 100 points in 3 months").

8 Actionable Strategies to Maximize Your Wake County Tutoring Experience

Now that you know how to access the service, let’s discuss how to use it effectively. Many students (and parents) waste valuable tutoring time by not preparing properly. Here’s how to get the most out of every session:

1. Identify Your Weakest Subjects Before Tutoring

Problem: Walking into a tutoring session without a clear focus leads to vague discussions and wasted time. Solution:

  • Take a quick self-assessment (e.g., a 10-question quiz on Khan Academy or a past test).
  • Prioritize topics where you struggle the most (e.g., if you’re bad at word problems in algebra, focus on that first).
  • Example: A high school junior in Wake County used this strategy to boost her math grade from a C to an A in just 6 weeks by targeting her weakest area—exponential functions.

Pro Tip: Use free diagnostic tools like:

  • Khan Academy’s Math Practice
  • IXL Learning (free trial available)

2. Prepare Questions in Advance

Problem: Tutors can’t read minds—if you don’t know exactly what you need help with, the session will be unfocused. Solution:

  • List 3-5 specific questions before logging in (e.g., "How do I solve systems of equations using substitution?").
  • Bring examples from your textbook or past assignments.
  • Example: A Wake County middle schooler who struggled with fractions prepared by writing down 5 fraction problems he couldn’t solve. His tutor explained the concept in 10 minutes instead of 30.

Common Mistake: Showing up and saying, "I don’t get this." → Fix: "I tried solving this problem, but I’m stuck at step 3—can you walk me through it?"

3. Use the "Two-Session Rule" for Complex Topics

Problem: Some concepts (like calculus or advanced chemistry) require multiple explanations to click. Solution:

  • Break learning into two sessions:
    1. First session: Ask for a full explanation of the concept.
    2. Second session (next day): Practice problems with the tutor guiding you.
  • Example: A college-bound Wake County student used this method to master trigonometry by first understanding sine and cosine functions, then practicing angle problems in the second session.

4. Record Sessions for Review

Problem: Forgetting what the tutor explained right after the session is a common issue. Solution:

  • Enable recording (if available) or take notes manually.
  • Revisit notes within 24 hours to reinforce learning.
  • Example: A parent of an elementary schooler in Cary recorded tutoring sessions and played them back while driving, helping her child retain vocabulary words better.

5. Leverage Test Prep Resources

Problem: Many students cram for tests without structured practice. Solution:

  • Use the test prep section for:
    • SAT/ACT practice questions
    • AP Exam strategies
    • College essay feedback
  • Example: A Wake County high school senior used the SAT prep tool to increase her score by 150 points in 2 months by focusing on weak areas identified in practice tests.

6. Set Goals & Track Progress

Problem: Without clear objectives, tutoring feels like a random activity rather than a learning journey. Solution:

  • Define a goal (e.g., "I want to improve my science lab report grade from a B- to an A").
  • Track progress in the platform’s progress dashboard.
  • Example: A homeschooled student in Wake County set a goal to understand 80% of biology concepts by the end of the semester. After 12 tutoring sessions, she achieved 92% comprehension on her final exam.

7. Ask for Alternative Explanations

Problem: If a tutor’s method doesn’t "click," students often give up instead of asking for another approach. Solution:

  • If the first explanation doesn’t make sense, say: "I tried this method, but I’m still confused. Can you explain it another way?"
  • Example: A struggling algebra student in Raleigh kept asking for different approaches until his tutor used visual graphs, which finally helped him understand linear equations.

8. Use Tutoring for Long-Term Study Habits, Not Just Cramming

Problem: Some students only use tutoring before tests, leading to short-term gains but no real learning. Solution:

  • Schedule regular sessions (e.g., every other week) to stay ahead in class.
  • Practice independently between sessions (e.g., 10 problems per day).
  • Example: A Wake County college student used tutoring as a supplement to her weekly study group, leading to consistent A’s in her calculus class.

Common Mistakes to Avoid When Using Online Tutoring

While Wake County’s tutoring service is free and powerful, some users unintentionally sabotage their progress by making these common mistakes:

❌ Mistake 1: Treating Tutoring Like a Crutch (Not a Tool)

What it means: Relying on tutors to do your work instead of learning the process. How to fix it:

  • Ask questions, but do the work yourself first.
  • Example: Instead of saying, "Just solve this for me," say, "I tried this, but I’m stuck here—can you help me understand?"

❌ Mistake 2: Skipping Preparation

What it means: Showing up completely unprepared and expecting instant results. How to fix it:

  • Review notes before the session.
  • Write down questions in advance.
  • Example: A student who didn’t prepare spent 20 minutes explaining the problem before the tutor could help.

❌ Mistake 3: Not Following Up

What it means: Getting help but not practicing what you learned. How to fix it:

  • Do 3-5 practice problems after each session.
  • Review notes within 24 hours.
  • Example: A student who only used tutoring for tests but never practiced saw no long-term improvement.

❌ Mistake 4: Ignoring Weak Areas

What it means: Focusing only on easy topics and avoiding difficult ones. How to fix it:

  • Identify your weakest subjects and prioritize them.
  • Example: A student who avoided chemistry tutoring because it was "too hard" ended up failing the class when the final exam covered weak topics.

❌ Mistake 5: Not Using All Available Resources

What it means: Only using live tutoring and missing test prep, homework help, and study guides. How to fix it:

  • Explore all sections of the tutoring portal.
  • Example: A student who only used live chat but didn’t take practice tests didn’t see maximum improvement.

❌ Mistake 6: Giving Up Too Soon

What it means: Expecting instant mastery and quitting after one or two sessions. How to fix it:

  • Commit to at least 4-6 sessions for noticeable progress.
  • Example: A student who quit after 2 sessions because he "didn’t see results yet" failed to improve in the long run.
