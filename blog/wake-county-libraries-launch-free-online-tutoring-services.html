How Wake County’s Free Online Tutoring Services Work

Wake County Public Libraries, in partnership with Tutoring.com and Homework Help Now, have launched 24/7 virtual tutoring for K-12 and adult learners. Here’s a breakdown of the key features:

1. Eligibility & Accessibility

  • Who can use it? Any Wake County resident with a valid library card (digital or physical).
  • Devices required: A smartphone, tablet, or computer with internet access.
  • No income restrictions—unlike some school-based programs, this is completely free for everyone.

2. Subjects & Grade Levels Covered

The tutoring covers:

  • Core subjects: Math, English, Science, Social Studies
  • Advanced topics: AP Calculus, SAT/ACT prep, college essay writing
  • Adult learning: GED prep, career readiness, and basic computer skills

3. How to Sign Up

  1. Visit the WCPL website (wakelibrary.org) and navigate to the Tutoring Services section.
  2. Create or log in to your library account (if you don’t have one, sign up for free).
  3. Select a tutor from the available schedule (or book a same-day session).
  4. Join via Zoom or a web browser—no downloads required.

4. Key Features of the Program

  • Live, one-on-one tutoring (no pre-recorded lessons).
  • Subject specialists (e.g., a tutor for AP Statistics vs. basic algebra).
  • Homework help & test prep (SAT, ACT, NC Final Exams).
  • Multilingual support (Spanish, Mandarin, and more).
  • No appointment needed for drop-in sessions (though scheduling ensures a tutor is available).

5. Why This Stands Out

Unlike traditional tutoring services (which can cost $30–$100/hour), Wake County’s program is 100% free, making it one of the most affordable and inclusive educational resources in the U.S.

8 Actionable Strategies to Maximize Wake County’s Free Tutoring Services

Now that you know how the program works, let’s dive into how to use it effectively.

1. Schedule Sessions During Peak Productivity Hours

  • Best times for focus: Early mornings (7–9 AM) or late evenings (8–10 PM).
  • Avoid rush hours (3–6 PM) when tutors may be booked.
  • Pro tip: Use the library’s calendar tool to see tutor availability in real time.

2. Prepare a Clear List of Questions Before Each Session

Tutors can only help if you know exactly what you need. Before logging in:

  • Write down specific problems (e.g., "Help me solve this quadratic equation").
  • Bring worksheets or textbook pages (if possible).
  • If struggling with conceptual understanding, ask:
    • "Can you explain this topic like I’m a beginner?"
    • "What are common mistakes students make here?"

3. Use the Program for Long-Term Learning, Not Just Last-Minute Cramming

Many students treat tutoring as a crutch for homework, but the best results come from consistent practice.

  • Example: Instead of waiting until the night before a math test, use tutoring weekly to reinforce weak areas.
  • Action step: Set a bi-weekly tutoring goal (e.g., "I’ll get help with fractions every other Monday").

4. Leverage Multilingual Tutors for Non-Native English Speakers

Wake County’s program offers bilingual support, which is underutilized by many families.

  • How to request a non-English tutor:
    • When scheduling, select "Spanish-speaking tutor" or another preferred language.
    • If unavailable, ask the tutor to explain concepts in simple terms (e.g., "Can you use analogies I’ll understand?").
  • Real-world impact: A 2022 study by the National Center for Education Statistics (NCES) found that non-native English speakers who receive language-adjusted tutoring show 20% higher test scores in core subjects.

5. Combine Tutoring with Free Library Resources

Wake County Libraries don’t just offer tutoring—they provide complementary tools to boost learning:

  • Free e-books & audiobooks (e.g., Strategies & Spelling for ESL learners).
  • Career & college prep guides (for adult learners).
  • Action plan:
    • After a tutoring session, download related study materials from the library’s website.
    • Use library apps (like Libby or Hoopla) for supplementary reading.

6. Use Tutoring for Test Prep (SAT, ACT, NC Final Exams)

Many students overlook tutoring for standardized tests, but it can be a game-changer.

  • How to prepare effectively:
    • Request a test-specific tutor (e.g., "I need help with the SAT Math section").
    • Ask for practice questions and timed drills (many tutors provide these).
    • Example: A Wake County high school senior used 3 months of SAT tutoring and raised their score from 1100 to 1350.
  • Pro tip: Some tutors offer mock tests—take advantage of these to simulate real exam conditions.

7. Encourage Parents & Teachers to Use the Program Too

Tutoring isn’t just for students—parents and educators can benefit from:

  • Parent workshops (e.g., "How to Help Your Child with Algebra").
  • Teacher professional development (some tutors specialize in differentiating instruction).
  • How to get involved:
    • Ask your child’s teacher to recommend tutoring for struggling students.
    • Attend library-hosted tutoring sessions for parents (some libraries offer these).

8. Track Progress & Adjust Strategies Over Time

Like any learning tool, consistency is key. To ensure long-term success:

  • Keep a tutoring journal (note what worked and what didn’t).
  • Set measurable goals (e.g., "I want to improve my science grade from a C to a B in 3 months").
  • Review past sessions—ask tutors for feedback on your progress.
  • Example: A Wake County middle schooler used tutoring every other week and saw her math grade improve from a 72% to 92% in 6 weeks.

Common Mistakes & How to Avoid Them

Even the best programs can fail if not used correctly. Here are 5 mistakes students and parents make—and how to fix them.

Mistake 1: Waiting Until the Last Minute for Help

Problem: Many students only seek tutoring when they’re failing a test, leading to cramming and frustration.

Solution:

  • Schedule regular sessions (e.g., once a week for weak subjects).
  • Use tutoring for prevention, not just treatment.

Mistake 2: Not Preparing Questions Before the Session

Problem: Walking into a tutoring session without clear goals wastes time.

Solution:

  • List 3–5 specific questions before logging in.
  • Bring homework or textbook pages for reference.

Mistake 3: Ignoring Multilingual Options

Problem: Non-native English speakers often skip bilingual tutors, leading to miscommunication.

Solution:

  • Always request a tutor who speaks your preferred language.
  • If unavailable, ask for simplified explanations.

Mistake 4: Treating Tutoring as a Replacement for Study Time

Problem: Some students think tutoring alone will fix their grades, but consistent practice is key.

Solution:

  • Use tutoring as a supplement, not a substitute.
  • Practice problems on your own between sessions.

Mistake 5: Not Tracking Progress

Problem: Without measurable goals, it’s hard to know if tutoring is working.

Solution:

  • Keep a tutoring log (note improvements and areas needing work).
  • Ask tutors for feedback on your progress.
