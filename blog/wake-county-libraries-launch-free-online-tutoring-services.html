8 Actionable Strategies to Make the Most of Wake County’s Free Tutoring

Now that you understand how the service works, let’s explore how to use it effectively. These strategies will help you maximize learning outcomes and ensure you’re getting the most value.

1. Set Clear Learning Goals Before Each Session

Problem: Many students jump into tutoring without a structured plan, leading to wasted time and unclear progress.

Solution:

  • Before your session, ask yourself:
    • What specific topic am I struggling with?
    • What grade-level standard does this align with?
    • Do I need help with homework, test prep, or conceptual understanding?
  • Example: Instead of saying, "I need help with math," specify, "I’m stuck on quadratic equations for my Algebra II test next week."

2. Use the Drop-In vs. Scheduled Session Wisely

Problem: Some students prefer spontaneity, while others benefit from consistent, scheduled practice.

Solution:

  • For urgent homework help → Use drop-in sessions (available during library hours).
  • For long-term test prep (SAT/ACT) → Schedule weekly 30-minute sessions to build consistency.
  • Real-world example: A high school junior preparing for the SAT Math scheduled bi-weekly 45-minute sessions with a tutor who focused on problem-solving strategies rather than just memorization.

3. Leverage Pre-Recorded Lessons for Self-Paced Learning

Problem: Not everyone can attend live sessions due to scheduling conflicts or time zones.

Solution:

  • Watch pre-recorded lessons on topics like:
    • How to solve systems of equations

4. Take Advantage of Study Guides & Practice Quizzes

Problem: Some students rely only on tutors without reinforcing learning through practice.

Solution:

  • After each session, download and complete:
    • Subject-specific practice quizzes (e.g., Algebra 1 End-of-Year Review)

Common Mistakes When Using Wake County’s Free Tutoring (And How to Avoid Them)

While Wake County’s tutoring service is highly effective, some users make avoidable mistakes that reduce its impact. Here’s how to steer clear of pitfalls:

Mistake 1: Not Preparing Before the Session

Problem: Walking into a tutoring session without clear questions leads to wasted time.

How to Avoid It:

  • Before the session, write down:
    • Specific questions (e.g., "How do I solve for x in a system of equations?")
    • Homework problems you’re stuck on
    • Concepts you don’t understand

Mistake 2: Relying Only on Tutors for Homework

Problem: Some students only use tutors for homework but don’t practice independently.

How to Avoid It:

  • After each session, complete at least 5-10 practice problems on your own.
  • Example: If your tutor explains how to balance chemical equations, write out 3 examples without help.

Mistake 3: Ignoring Pre-Recorded Lessons

Problem: Only using live tutoring means missing self-paced learning opportunities.

How to Avoid It:

  • Watch 1-2 pre-recorded lessons per week on topics you’re struggling with.
  • Example: If you’re bad at grammar rules, watch a 10-minute video on subject-verb agreement before your next ELA session.

Mistake 4: Not Following Up with Teachers

Problem: Tutors can’t replace classroom instruction—they supplement it.

How to Avoid It:

  • After tutoring, ask your teacher for extra practice or clarify concepts in class.
  • Example: If your math tutor explains exponents, ask your teacher for a worksheet to reinforce the lesson.

Mistake 5: Giving Up Too Soon

Problem: Some students only attend one session and then stop, expecting instant results.

How to Avoid It:

  • Commit to at least 4-6 sessions for noticeable improvement.
  • Example: A student who only attended one SAT tutoring session saw no improvement, but one who scheduled 12 sessions raised her score by 150 points.

Mistake 6: Not Using All Available Resources

Problem: Some users only use tutoring but miss out on free study guides, quizzes, and e-books.

How to Avoid It:

  • Explore the library’s digital collection for:
    • Subject-specific e-books (e.g., The Princeton Review SAT Guide)
    • Practice tests (e.g., Kaplan ACT Prep)
    • Flashcard apps (like Quizlet, integrated into the platform)
