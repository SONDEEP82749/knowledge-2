How Wake County Libraries’ Free Online Tutoring Works

Wake County’s Tutoring@YourLibrary program is a hybrid of live and on-demand tutoring, designed to fit the needs of K-12 students, college prep learners, and even adults seeking career training. Here’s a breakdown of its key features:

1. Live, One-on-One Tutoring Sessions

  • Subjects Covered: Math, Science, English, History, Foreign Languages, and Test Prep (SAT, ACT, AP Exams).
  • How It Works: Students schedule 30-60 minute sessions with certified tutors via Zoom or a secure web portal.
  • Availability: Monday–Thursday, 3 PM–9 PM (extended hours during finals week).

2. On-Demand Writing & Homework Help

  • For Immediate Assistance: Upload assignments or questions, and tutors respond within 24 hours with detailed feedback.
  • Best For: Students who need last-minute help or prefer asynchronous learning.

3. Specialized Programs for Different Age Groups

Age Group Program Focus Key Features
K-5 Foundational Skills Phonics, reading fluency, basic math
6-8 Core Subjects Algebra, grammar, science experiments
9-12 Advanced & Test Prep AP Calculus, Chemistry, SAT/ACT strategies
Adults Career & GED Prep Resume writing, computer skills, GED test prep

4. Accessibility & Inclusivity

  • No Library Card Required: While a Wake County library card grants full access, non-residents can still use the service with a free digital pass.
  • Multilingual Support: Tutors fluent in Spanish, Mandarin, and American Sign Language (ASL) are available.
  • Disability Accommodations: Screen readers, braille support, and extended time for sessions are offered upon request.

5. Integration with School Curriculum

  • Aligned with NC Standards: Tutors follow North Carolina’s academic benchmarks to ensure relevant support.
  • Teacher Collaboration: Schools can request bulk tutoring sessions for struggling students.

8 Actionable Strategies to Make the Most of Wake County’s Free Tutoring

Now that you understand the program’s structure, here’s how to optimize its use for maximum academic growth.

Strategy 1: Schedule Sessions During Peak Productivity Hours

When to Go: Research shows that after-school hours (3–6 PM) are ideal for focused learning. Avoid late-night sessions if you’re prone to fatigue.

How to Apply:

  • Use the tutoring calendar to book 3–5 PM slots when your brain is fresh.
  • If mornings work better, schedule early sessions (some tutors may be available as early as 7 AM).

Pro Tip: If you’re a night owl, try weekend sessions—some tutors offer Saturday mornings for extra help.

Strategy 2: Use On-Demand Help for Quick Doubts

Best For: Last-minute questions, grammar checks, or math problem verification.

How to Apply:

  1. Upload a photo or document of your question.
  2. Specify urgency (e.g., "Need help by 5 PM today").
  3. Review the response and ask follow-up questions if needed.

Example: A high school senior struggling with a chemistry lab report can upload it at 11 PM, get feedback by 12 AM, and submit it on time.

Strategy 3: Form a Study Group with Tutoring Support

Why It Works: Tutors can facilitate group discussions, helping multiple students reinforce concepts together.

How to Apply:

  1. Invite 2–3 classmates to join a shared tutoring session.
  2. Assign roles (e.g., one student explains a concept while others ask questions).
  3. Use the whiteboard feature in Zoom to collaborate in real time.

Real-World Example: *A group of AP Biology students used the group tutoring feature to break down cell respiration before their exam. Their tutor provided visual diagrams, and they quizzed each other—resulting in a 20-point improvement on their next test.

Strategy 4: Leverage Test Prep Resources for Standardized Exams

For SAT/ACT/AP Students:

  • Practice Tests: Access full-length SAT/ACT drills with detailed answer explanations.

How to Apply:

Success Story: A junior in Wake County used the SAT prep tutoring to raise her score from 1200 to 1450 in just 6 weeks. Her tutor focused on time management and critical reading strategies—key for her target schools.

Common Mistakes & How to Avoid Them

Even the best programs can fail if not used correctly. Here are 5 mistakes students make and how to prevent them.

Mistake 1: Waiting Until the Last Minute for Tutoring

Problem: Some students only seek help when they’re failing, making it harder to catch up. Solution:

  • Use tutoring proactively—ask for help when you’re stuck, not when you’re desperate.
  • Schedule sessions every 2 weeks to reinforce concepts.

Example: *A student who waited until final exams to use tutoring found it too late to improve. Solution: Start mid-semester for steady progress.

Mistake 2: Not Preparing Questions Before Sessions

Problem: Vague questions (e.g., "I don’t get this") waste both your and the tutor’s time. Solution:

  • Write down specific problems before the session.
  • Bring past assignments you struggled with.

Example: Instead of saying, "I don’t understand this math problem," Say: "I tried solving this quadratic equation, but I keep getting -5 instead of 3. Can you walk me through the steps?"

Mistake 3: Ignoring On-Demand Feedback

Problem: Some students only use live tutoring and miss quick, free help from on-demand responses. Solution:

  • Use on-demand for:
    • Grammar checks
    • Quick math verifications
    • Last-minute essay edits
  • Live tutoring for:
    • Deep conceptual understanding
    • Test prep strategies

Mistake 4: Not Following Up on Tutoring Advice

Problem: Students get great tips but don’t apply them, leading to no improvement. Solution:

  • Take notes during sessions.
  • Practice the strategies between tutoring.
  • Ask for progress checks every few weeks.

Example: *A tutor might say, "You’re mixing up ‘affect’ and ‘effect’—practice writing 5 sentences daily." If you don’t follow through, you won’t see progress.

Mistake 5: Overlooking the Library’s Digital Resources

Problem: Some students only use tutoring and miss free books, apps, and courses the library offers. Solution:

  • Explore:
    • Khan Academy (for math/science).
    • Mango Languages (for learning Spanish/French).
    • LinkedIn Learning (for career skills).
  • Ask your tutor for recommendations.

Example: *A student struggling with chemistry could combine tutoring with Khan Academy’s organic chemistry videos for faster mastery.
