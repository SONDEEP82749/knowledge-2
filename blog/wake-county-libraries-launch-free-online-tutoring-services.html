8 Actionable Strategies to Make the Most of Wake County’s Free Tutoring

Not all students (or parents) use tutoring services effectively. Here’s how to optimize your experience:

1. Set Clear Learning Goals Before Each Session

Instead of vague requests like "Help me with math," be specific: ✔ "I need help solving quadratic equations for my upcoming test." ✔ "Can we review grammar rules for my essay?"

Why It Works: Tutors can tailor their approach to your exact needs, making sessions more efficient.

2. Prepare Questions & Materials in Advance

Bringing a list of questions or specific problems ensures you don’t waste time.

  • For math/science, write down problem sets you’re stuck on.
  • For writing, bring drafts you’d like feedback on.

Example: A college student preparing for the SAT used Wake County’s tutoring to practice 10 math problems per session, focusing on weak areas like algebra.

3. Use the "Two-Session Rule" for Complex Topics

If a concept is too difficult in one sitting, break it into two sessions:

  • First session: Understand the basics.
  • Second session: Apply it to practice problems.

Real-World Success: A middle schooler struggling with fractions took two sessions—first to grasp the concept, then to solve word problems. His grades improved from a C to an A in just two weeks.

4. Take Notes During Sessions (But Don’t Just Copy)

Instead of passively listening, actively engage:

  • Summarize key points in your own words.
  • Ask follow-up questions if something is unclear.
  • Review notes after the session to reinforce learning.

Common Mistake to Avoid: ❌ Only listening without taking notes leads to forgetting key concepts.

5. Leverage Post-Session Resources

Wake County provides free digital materials, including:

  • Interactive quizzes
  • Video lessons
  • Study guides

How to Use Them:

  • Revisit weak areas after tutoring.
  • Practice extra problems to build confidence.

6. Schedule Regular Sessions (Consistency > Cramming)

Instead of last-minute cramming, commit to weekly or biweekly sessions to:

  • Build long-term understanding
  • Stay on track with assignments
  • Reduce test anxiety

Example: A Wake County high school junior scheduled two 30-minute sessions per week for AP Chemistry. By the end of the semester, he scored a 5 on the AP Exam—up from a 3 the previous year.

7. Use the "Tutor as a Teacher" Approach

Some students treat tutors like friends, not experts. Instead:

  • Respect their expertise—ask for explanations, not just answers.
  • Follow their study methods (if they suggest flashcards, use them).
  • Avoid interrupting unless absolutely necessary.

Why It Works: Tutors are professionals—treating them with respect ensures better learning outcomes.

8. Track Progress & Adjust Strategies

Keep a simple progress log to track improvements:

Date Topic Covered Before Score After Score Notes
5/10 Algebra Equations 7/10 9/10 Tutored on solving for x

Adjust based on results:

  • If a topic is still hard, request more sessions.
  • If you’re improving, try slightly harder problems.

Common Mistakes When Using Online Tutoring (And How to Avoid Them)

Even the best programs can fail if misused. Here are 5 mistakes students make—and how to fix them:

1. Not Preparing Questions Beforehand

Mistake: Walking into a session with no clear goals. Solution:

  • List 3-5 specific questions before logging in.
  • Bring past assignments you need feedback on.

2. Treating Tutors Like Free Homework Helpers

Mistake: Asking tutors to do homework for you. Solution:

  • Use tutors as guides, not doers.
  • Ask: "How would you solve this?" instead of "Just give me the answer."

3. Skipping Post-Session Review

Mistake: Forgetting to study after tutoring. Solution:

  • Revisit notes within 24 hours.
  • Practice related problems on your own.

4. Only Using Tutoring for Last-Minute Cramming

Mistake: Waiting until the night before a test to seek help. Solution:

  • Start early—consistent practice leads to better retention.
  • Use tutoring for weekly review, not just emergencies.

5. Ignoring Digital Resources

Mistake: Only using tutors but not exploring free materials. Solution:

  • Watch video lessons between sessions.
  • Take online quizzes to test knowledge.
