Common Mistakes & How to Avoid Them

Even the best resources can be misused if not approached correctly. Here are five mistakes students make—and how to fix them.

1. Waiting Until the Last Minute for Help

Mistake: Some students only use tutoring when they’re failing a test. Why It Fails:

  • Last-minute cramming doesn’t build long-term understanding.
  • Tutors may not have time to deeply explain concepts if you’re in a panic.

Solution:

  • Schedule regular sessions (even if you’re "doing fine").
  • Use tutors for "pre-teaching" before a new unit starts.

2. Not Being Specific with Questions

Mistake: Asking vague questions like "Help me with math" instead of "How do I solve systems of equations?" Why It Fails:

  • Tutors can’t pinpoint your exact struggle.
  • You may get general advice instead of targeted help.

Solution:

  • Be precise (e.g., "Explain how to factor quadratics").
  • Show your work (if applicable) so the tutor can identify where you went wrong.

3. Ignoring the Test Prep Resources

Mistake: Focusing only on live tutoring and skipping practice tests, flashcards, and study guides. Why It Fails:

  • Real-world test-taking skills (time management, question strategies) are not covered in live sessions.
  • Passive learning (just reading notes) doesn’t reinforce memory.

Solution:

  • Take at least one practice test per week.
  • Review mistakes with a tutor after each test.
  • Use flashcards for formulas, vocabulary, and key terms.

4. Not Tracking Progress

Mistake: Using tutoring without keeping records of what you’ve learned. Why It Fails:

  • You don’t know what’s working or what needs more practice.
  • Motivation drops if you don’t see improvement.

Solution:

  • Save chat transcripts (if the platform allows it).
  • Keep a study journal noting weak areas and progress.
  • Set monthly goals (e.g., "I’ll improve my reading comprehension score by 20 points").

5. Over-Reliance on Tutors Without Independent Work

Mistake: Only using tutors and not doing homework or practice problems on your own. Why It Fails:

  • Tutors can’t do your work for you—they guide you.
  • Passive learning (just listening) doesn’t reinforce retention.

Solution:

  • Do homework first, then ask tutors to review your answers.
  • Practice problems independently before seeking help.
  • Apply concepts in new ways (e.g., if you learn about fractions in math, use them in real-life cooking recipes).
