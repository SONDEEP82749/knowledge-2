How Wake County Libraries’ Free Online Tutoring Works: A Step-by-Step Guide

Wake County Public Library’s Tutoring Services are part of a broader initiative to democratize education. Unlike traditional tutoring services that can cost $30–$100 per hour, WCPL’s program is completely free, funded by public tax dollars and community partnerships.

Step 1: Accessing the Tutoring Platform

To get started, you’ll need:

  • A valid Wake County Public Library card (available for free with proof of residency)
  • A computer, tablet, or smartphone with internet access
  • A Google account (for logging into the tutoring portal)

Where to find it? The tutoring service is accessible through WCPL’s website under the "Learning & Research" tab. Alternatively, you can bookmark the direct link: 🔗 Wake County Libraries Tutoring Portal

Step 2: Choosing Your Subject & Grade Level

The platform offers tutoring in over 30 subjects, including:

  • Elementary & Middle School: Math, Reading, Science, Writing
  • High School: Algebra, Geometry, Biology, Chemistry, AP Courses
  • College Prep: SAT/ACT Test Prep, Essay Writing, Study Skills
  • Adult Learning: GED Prep, ESL (English as a Second Language), Career Readiness

How to select?

  1. Log in to your account.
  2. Click "Find a Tutor."
  3. Choose your grade level (K-12, College, Adult).
  4. Select your subject and specific topic (e.g., "Advanced Calculus" or "Grammar for ESL").

Step 3: Scheduling a Session

Unlike some tutoring platforms that require pre-booking, Wake County’s service offers two options:

  • Instant Tutoring (Live Chat): Available 24/7 for quick help with homework or concept clarification.
  • Scheduled Sessions: Book a 30–60 minute live video call with a tutor (best for deep-dive learning).

Pro Tip: If you’re preparing for a big exam, scheduling a weekly session with the same tutor helps build consistency.

Step 4: Connecting with a Tutor

Once you’re in a session, you’ll be matched with a certified tutor who specializes in your subject. The platform uses AI-driven matching to ensure: ✔ Subject expertise (e.g., a tutor with a PhD in Chemistry for your organic chem class) ✔ Availability (they’ll connect when you’re free) ✔ Communication style (some tutors prefer structured lessons, others use interactive games)

What to expect in a session?

  • Whiteboard & document sharing (like Zoom + Google Docs)
  • Voice & video chat (for real-time interaction)
  • Recorded sessions (available for review after the call)

Step 5: Maximizing Your Tutoring Experience

The best tutors don’t just answer questions—they teach problem-solving skills. To get the most out of your sessions, follow these 8 actionable strategies:

8 Proven Strategies to Get the Most Out of Wake County’s Free Online Tutoring

Strategy 1: Prepare Before Your Session (The "3-Part Prep" Method)

Problem: Many students walk into tutoring sessions without clear goals, leading to wasted time.

Solution: Use the "3-Part Prep" framework:

  1. List your questions (e.g., "How do I solve quadratic equations?").
  2. Bring past assignments (so the tutor can see where you’re stuck).
  3. Note your learning style (Do you learn better through examples, diagrams, or explanations?).

Example: A high school student struggling with physics might prepare by:

  • Writing down: "I don’t understand Newton’s Third Law—can we work through a problem?"
  • Bringing their last physics worksheet with incorrect answers.
  • Noting: "I learn best with visual diagrams, so please draw force vectors."

Strategy 2: Use the "Two-Tutor Rule" for Complex Topics

Problem: Some subjects (like calculus or advanced chemistry) require multiple perspectives to grasp fully.

Solution: If you’re tackling a difficult concept, ask for two tutors in one session:

  • Tutor #1 explains the theory.
  • Tutor #2 provides real-world applications.

Real-World Example: A college student preparing for organic chemistry scheduled two sessions:

  1. Session 1: A tutor with a PhD in biochemistry explained resonance structures.
  2. Session 2: A lab technician showed how these concepts apply in drug synthesis.

Result: The student scored 92% on their next exam—up from 68% before tutoring.

Strategy 3: Record & Review Sessions (The "Spaced Repetition" Hack)

Problem: Students often forget what they learned in tutoring sessions within days.

Solution: Record every session and review it within 24 hours using spaced repetition:

  1. First review: 1 hour after the session.
  2. Second review: 3 days later.
  3. Third review: 1 week later.

How to implement?

  • Wake County’s platform allows session recordings (stored in your account).
  • Use Anki (a free flashcard app) to create custom study decks from your notes.

Strategy 4: Turn Tutoring into a "Study Buddy" Relationship

Problem: One-off tutoring sessions don’t build long-term confidence.

Solution: Treat your tutor like a study buddy by:

  • Scheduling recurring sessions (e.g., every Sunday for math).
  • Asking for "homework" (e.g., "Can you give me 5 practice problems to solve before next week?").
  • Sharing progress updates (e.g., "I aced my last quiz—here’s how I did it!").

Example: A middle school student with dyslexia worked with the same tutor every Saturday for 3 months. Instead of just answering questions, the tutor:

  • Helped the student break down reading assignments into chunks.
  • Used audiobooks alongside textbooks.
  • Celebrated small wins (e.g., "You read 10 pages without stumbling—great job!").

Result: The student’s reading comprehension improved by 40%.

Strategy 5: Use Tutoring for "Reverse Engineering" Assignments

Problem: Students often don’t know how to approach a problem before diving in.

Solution: Before starting homework, ask your tutor to "reverse engineer" the assignment:

  1. "What’s the first step?" (e.g., "For this essay, start with an outline.")
  2. "What are common mistakes?" (e.g., "Don’t forget to cite sources in MLA format.")
  3. "Can you show me an example?" (e.g., "Walk me through a solved problem.")

Example: A high school senior preparing for the SAT used tutoring to:

  • Break down math word problems into key phrases (e.g., "rate × time = distance").
  • Learn test-taking strategies (e.g., "If stuck, guess and move on").

Result: The student’s SAT score increased by 200 points.

Strategy 6: Leverage Tutoring for "Soft Skills" (Not Just Academics)

Problem: Many students focus only on subject matter, ignoring study habits that lead to long-term success.

Solution: Ask tutors about:

  • Time management (e.g., "How do you fit in 2 hours of study per day?").
  • Note-taking techniques (e.g., "Should I use Cornell notes or mind maps?").
  • Test anxiety reduction (e.g., "How do I stay calm before exams?").

Example: A college freshman with ADHD worked with a tutor to:

  • Use the Pomodoro Technique (25-minute study sessions with breaks).
  • Break assignments into smaller tasks (e.g., "Write 1 paragraph instead of the whole essay").
  • Set phone alarms for focus intervals.

Result: The student’s GPA jumped from 2.3 to 3.7 in one semester.

Strategy 7: Turn Tutoring into a "Peer Teaching" Opportunity

Problem: Some students forget what they learn because they’re passive listeners.

Solution: Teach someone else (even if it’s just your tutor) to reinforce your understanding. Use the "Feynman Technique":

  1. Explain the concept in simple terms.
  2. If you get stuck, go back to the tutor.
  3. Simplify further until you can teach it to a 5-year-old.

Example: A struggling algebra student used this method:

  • Tried to explain solving for x to their tutor.
  • Realized they didn’t understand why the steps worked.
  • After clarification, they created flashcards with real-life examples (e.g., "If you have 10 apples and give 3 to a friend, how many do you have left?").

Result: They mastered the topic and later helped classmates.

Strategy 8: Use Tutoring for "Career Prep" (Even in High School)

Problem: Many students wait until college to think about career readiness.

Solution: Wake County’s tutoring includes career readiness programs, such as:

  • Resume & cover letter reviews (for high school juniors/seniors).

Common Mistakes When Using Online Tutoring (And How to Avoid Them)

While Wake County’s tutoring program is highly effective, some students sabotage their progress by making these common mistakes:

Mistake 1: Treating Tutoring Like a "Last Resort"

Problem: Some students only use tutoring when they’re failing, leading to cramming and stress.

Solution:

  • Use tutoring proactively (e.g., once a week for review).
  • Schedule sessions before an assignment is due (not after failing it).

Example: A student who waited until the night before a test found themselves overwhelmed because they hadn’t reviewed concepts in months.

Mistake 2: Not Setting Clear Goals

Problem: Without a specific goal, tutoring sessions become vague and unproductive.

Solution:

  • Before each session, ask: "What’s the one thing I want to learn today?"
  • Example goals:
    • "I want to understand how to balance chemical equations."
    • "I need help organizing my essay outline."
