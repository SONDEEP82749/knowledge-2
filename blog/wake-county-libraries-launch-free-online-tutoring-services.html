How Wake County’s Free Online Tutoring Services Work

Wake County Public Library’s Tutoring & Learning Center is a one-stop digital hub designed to support students from kindergarten through graduate school. Unlike traditional tutoring services that require in-person visits or expensive subscriptions, WCPL’s platform offers 24/7 access with live tutors, self-paced courses, and interactive tools.

Key Features of the Program

  1. Live, One-on-One Tutoring

    • Students can connect with certified tutors via video chat for real-time help in subjects like math, science, English, and test prep (SAT, ACT, AP exams).
    • Available Monday through Sunday, with extended hours during exam seasons.

  2. Self-Paced Learning Modules

    • Interactive lessons on grammar, algebra, chemistry, and more, with quizzes and progress tracking.
    • Great for students who prefer learning at their own pace.

  3. Homework Help & Writing Assistance

    • Tutors assist with essay writing, research papers, and citation formatting (APA, MLA, Chicago).
    • A plagiarism checker and grammar tools are integrated into the platform.

  4. Test Prep Resources

    • SAT/ACT practice tests, flashcards, and strategy guides are available.
    • AP exam review sessions are scheduled leading up to major testing dates.

  5. Access for All Ages

    • Elementary students get help with reading comprehension and basic math.
    • Adult learners can improve ESL skills, job interview prep, and career certifications.

  6. No Cost, No Membership Required

    • Unlike private tutoring services (which can cost $30–$100 per hour), WCPL’s tutoring is completely free for all Wake County residents.

How to Access the Tutoring Services

  1. Visit the Wake County Library Website

    • Go to Wake County Public Library’s Learning Portal and navigate to the Tutoring & Learning Center.

  2. Create a Free Account

    • If you don’t already have one, register with your Wake County library card number (or get a free digital library card if you don’t have a physical one).

  3. Browse Available Subjects & Tutors

    • Select your grade level, subject, and preferred tutor (or use the random assignment feature).

  4. Schedule a Session

    • Book a live tutoring slot or jump into a self-paced module immediately.

  5. Start Learning!

    • Use the chat, video call, or document-sharing tools to collaborate with your tutor.

8 Actionable Strategies to Make the Most of Wake County’s Free Tutoring

While the service is easy to access, maximizing its benefits requires strategic planning. Here’s how to get the most out of Wake County’s free tutoring:

1. Schedule Tutoring During Peak Productivity Hours

Not everyone learns best at the same time. Wake County’s tutors are available 24/7, but your focus level varies throughout the day.

  • Morning sessions (7–11 AM): Best for math and science—when logical thinking is sharpest.
  • Afternoon sessions (1–5 PM): Ideal for writing and language arts—when creativity flows.
  • Evening sessions (7–11 PM): Great for test prep and last-minute review sessions.

Pro Tip: If you’re a night owl, use the self-paced modules in the evening and live tutoring during the day.

2. Use the "Pre-Session Checklist" to Prepare

Before jumping into a tutoring session, set clear goals to avoid wasting time.

✅ Define your objective:

  • "I need help solving quadratic equations."
  • "I’m stuck on my thesis statement for my history essay."
  • "I want to review AP Biology concepts before the exam."

✅ Gather materials:

  • Have your textbook, notes, or assignment prompt ready.
  • If it’s a writing session, draft an outline first to save time.

✅ Test your tech:

  • Ensure your camera, microphone, and internet connection work before the session.

3. Take Advantage of Self-Paced Learning for Weak Areas

Not every student thrives in live tutoring. If you’re shy, introverted, or prefer learning alone, use the self-paced modules to:

  • Review foundational concepts (e.g., fractions, grammar rules).
  • Practice with interactive quizzes to reinforce learning.
  • Watch video lessons at your own speed.

Example: If you’re struggling with algebra, spend 10 minutes daily on the self-paced algebra course before a live tutoring session.

4. Ask Specific Questions (Not Just "Help Me")

Tutors are highly skilled, but they can’t read your mind. Instead of saying: ❌ "Help me with math."

Try: ✅ "Can you explain how to solve systems of equations using substitution?" ✅ "What’s the best way to structure a 5-paragraph essay?"

Why it works: Specific questions lead to faster, more effective learning.

5. Use the Writing & Research Tools for Academic Success

Many students underestimate the value of writing assistance. Wake County’s tutoring includes:

  • Plagiarism checkers (to ensure original work).
  • Grammar & style suggestions (to improve clarity).
  • Citation generators (for proper MLA/APA formatting).

Real-World Example: A college freshman named Jamie was struggling with a 10-page research paper on climate change. Instead of trying to format citations alone, she used the library’s writing tools to:*

  1. Generate APA citations for her sources.
  2. Get feedback on her thesis statement.
  3. Receive a grammar review before submission.

Result: Jamie earned an A on her paper—something she never thought possible without professional help.

6. Leverage Test Prep Resources for Exam Success

Whether you're prepping for the SAT, ACT, or AP exams, Wake County’s tutoring offers:

  • Full-length practice tests (with answer explanations).
  • Flashcards for key terms (e.g., biology vocabulary, math formulas).
  • Strategy guides (e.g., how to answer multiple-choice questions efficiently).

Strategy:

  • Take a practice test under timed conditions.
  • Review mistakes with a tutor to understand where you went wrong.
  • Focus on weak areas in the next session.

7. Collaborate with Tutors to Build Long-Term Skills

Tutoring isn’t just for one-time help—it’s about building lifelong learning skills.

  • Ask for study strategies (e.g., how to memorize vocabulary, break down complex problems).
  • Request feedback on your study habits (e.g., "Do you think I should review flashcards before bed?").
  • Set long-term goals (e.g., "I want to improve my SAT score by 100 points—how can we get there?").

Example: A high school senior named Priya was aiming for a 36 on her SAT. Instead of just getting help with math problems, she:*

  1. Scheduled weekly tutoring sessions to review math and reading strategies.
  2. Used the flashcard tool to memorize key vocabulary words.
  3. Practiced timed sections with her tutor’s guidance.

Result: Priya scored a 35 on her first practice test—just 1 point away from her goal—and was confident in her test-taking approach.

8. Encourage Parents & Teachers to Use the Resource

Wake County’s tutoring isn’t just for students—parents and teachers can also benefit.

For Parents:

  • Use the writing tools to proofread your child’s essays.
  • Schedule test prep sessions to practice with them (even if you’re not a math whiz, tutors can guide you).

For Teachers:

  • Assign tutoring sessions as homework help for struggling students.
  • Use the self-paced modules to supplement classroom lessons.

Pro Tip: If you’re a parent, consider co-tutoring with your child. Many tutors are patient and encouraging, making it a great way to bond while learning.

Common Mistakes When Using Wake County’s Tutoring (And How to Avoid Them)

While Wake County’s tutoring is incredibly valuable, some users make avoidable mistakes that waste time and reduce effectiveness. Here’s what to watch out for:

Mistake #1: Waiting Until the Last Minute

Problem: Many students only seek tutoring when they’re failing a test or behind on assignments. This leads to rushed, ineffective sessions.

Solution:

  • Schedule regular check-ins (e.g., weekly for math, biweekly for writing).
  • Use self-paced modules to stay ahead of assignments.

Example: A student who waited until the night before a chemistry test to get help struggled because the tutor had to cover too much material too fast.

Mistake #2: Not Setting Clear Goals

Problem: If you don’t define what you want to achieve, the session becomes unfocused.

Solution:

  • Before each session, write down 1–2 specific goals (e.g., "I need help with balancing chemical equations.").
  • Ask the tutor: "What’s the best way to approach this problem?"

Example: A student who just said, "Help me with math," ended up wasting 30 minutes on unrelated topics.

Mistake #3: Ignoring Self-Paced Learning

Problem: Some students only use live tutoring and never engage with the self-paced modules, missing out on structured practice.

Solution:

  • Combine both methods:
    • Use self-paced modules for foundational review.
    • Use live tutoring for deep dives into tricky concepts.

Example: A student who only used live tutoring for calculus but never practiced problems on their own still struggled with test-taking under pressure.

Mistake #4: Not Asking for Feedback on Study Habits

Problem: Tutors know what works in learning, but students don’t always take their advice.

Solution:

  • Ask:
    • "What’s the best way for me to study this topic?"
    • "Should I be using flashcards or practice tests?"
    • "What’s a common mistake I should avoid?"

Example: A student who never reviewed mistakes kept making the same algebra errors in tests.
