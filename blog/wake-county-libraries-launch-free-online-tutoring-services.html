8 Actionable Strategies to Maximize Wake County’s Free Tutoring Services

1. Identify Your Child’s Weakest Subjects Early

Before diving into tutoring, assess where your child needs the most help. Wake County’s tutors specialize in different areas, so prioritize subjects with the lowest grades or test scores.

How to Do It:

  • Review report cards and standardized test results.
  • Ask teachers for specific skill gaps (e.g., algebra word problems, essay structure).
  • Use free diagnostic tools like Khan Academy or IXL to pinpoint weak areas.

Example: A high school junior struggling with calculus derivatives could start with two weekly sessions to build confidence before midterms.

2. Schedule Consistent, Short Sessions

Unlike traditional tutoring, short, frequent sessions (30-45 minutes) are often more effective than long, irregular ones.

Why It Works:

  • Maintains focus (students retain more in shorter bursts).
  • Builds momentum (consistency reinforces learning).
  • Fits busy schedules (parents and students can commit without overloading).

Best Practice:

  • Start with 2-3 sessions per week and adjust based on progress.
  • Use the library’s scheduling tool to block recurring times.

3. Prepare for Sessions Like a Mini-Lesson

Tutors are there to guide, not do the work for you. To get the most out of each session:

  • List 2-3 specific questions (e.g., "How do I solve quadratic equations?").
  • Bring past assignments or practice problems to work through together.
  • Come with an open mind—tutors may introduce new methods (e.g., alternative math strategies).

Example: A middle school student preparing for a science quiz should bring:

  • The chapter review questions
  • Notes from class
  • A specific topic they’re stuck on (e.g., photosynthesis reactions).

4. Leverage Group Tutoring for Peer Learning

While one-on-one tutoring is great for deep dives, group sessions can enhance collaboration and different perspectives.

How to Use Group Tutoring:

  • Join a study group for subjects like history or literature where discussions help.
  • Practice test-taking in a group setting (e.g., SAT prep).
  • Encourage friends to participate—learning together makes it more engaging.

Real-World Example: A group of high school seniors used Wake County’s ACT prep group tutoring to:

  • Quiz each other on math concepts.
  • Share test-taking strategies from past experiences.
  • Reduce stress by working through problems together.

5. Use Tutoring for Test Prep Beyond Just Grades

Tutoring isn’t just for fixing bad grades—it’s a strategic tool for test preparation.

How to Apply It:

  • SAT/ACT Prep: Use tutors to practice timed sections and identify weak areas.
  • AP Exams: Get expert breakdowns of exam formats and question types.
  • College Applications: Refine personal statements with writing tutors.

Example: A student aiming for a high SAT score could:

  • Schedule 4 weekly sessions focusing on math and reading strategies.
  • Practice full-length tests with a tutor reviewing mistakes afterward.

6. Track Progress with the Library’s Resources

Wake County’s tutoring program includes progress tracking tools to measure improvement.

How to Use Them:

  • Review session notes after each tutoring session.
  • Compare pre- and post-tutoring test scores.

Example: A 7th grader improving from a 75% to 90% in math after 8 sessions could:

  • Celebrate the progress with the tutor.
  • Identify the next challenge (e.g., word problems).

7. Combine Tutoring with Free Online Tools

Wake County’s tutoring works best when paired with other free resources.

Recommended Tools:

  • Khan Academy (for math and science practice).
  • Grammarly (for writing tutors to review essays).

8. Advocate for Your Child’s Success

Parents and educators play a critical role in ensuring tutoring is effective.

How to Support Your Child:

  • Encourage consistency—remind them to attend sessions.
  • Ask tutors for feedback—what’s working? What needs improvement?
  • Create a study routine that includes tutoring as a non-negotiable part.

Real-World Success Story: A parent of a struggling 8th grader noticed her daughter’s math grades improve from a D to a B after:

Common Mistakes to Avoid When Using Wake County’s Tutoring Services

While the program is highly beneficial, some users make avoidable errors that reduce its effectiveness.

1. Waiting Until the Last Minute

Mistake: Only seeking tutoring when a test is days away. Why It Fails: Students miss out on long-term skill-building and may still struggle with foundational concepts.

Solution:

  • Start early—even 1-2 sessions per month can prevent major setbacks.
  • Use tutoring for consistent review, not just cramming.

2. Not Being Specific About Needs

Mistake: Showing up to a session with vague questions like "I don’t get math." Why It Fails: Tutors can’t effectively help without clear examples or problems.

Solution:

  • Prepare a list of exact questions (e.g., "How do I factor polynomials?").
  • Bring specific assignments that need review.

3. Skipping Sessions Without Communication

Mistake: Cancelling last-minute without notifying the tutor. Why It Fails: Tutors may overbook or assume the student is disengaged.

Solution:

  • Use the library’s cancellation policy (some allow 24-hour notice).
  • Reschedule instead of dropping—consistency matters.

4. Relying Only on Tutoring, Ignoring Other Resources

Mistake: Thinking tutoring is the only tool needed for success. Why It Fails: Tutoring supplements, not replaces, self-study and practice.

Solution:

  • Combine tutoring with:
    • Practice problems (Khan Academy, IXL).
    • Study groups (for collaborative learning).
    • Flashcards (for memorization-heavy subjects).

5. Not Following Up on Progress

Mistake: Not reviewing grades or test scores after tutoring sessions. Why It Fails: Without measurement, it’s hard to know if the tutoring is working.

Solution:

  • Track improvements in a notebook or spreadsheet.
  • Ask tutors for feedback on what’s improving and what needs more work.
