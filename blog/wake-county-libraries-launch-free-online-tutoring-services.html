How Wake County Libraries’ Free Online Tutoring Services Work

Wake County Public Library’s new tutoring program is designed to be accessible, flexible, and personalized. Unlike traditional tutoring centers that require in-person visits or expensive subscriptions, this service is completely free and available 24/7 via the library’s digital portal.

Key Features of the Program

  1. Subject-Specific Tutoring

    • Math (Algebra, Geometry, Calculus)
    • Science (Biology, Chemistry, Physics)
    • English (Grammar, Essay Writing, Literature)
    • Foreign Languages (Spanish, French, Mandarin)
    • College Prep (SAT/ACT, Essay Editing, Study Skills)

  2. Live & On-Demand Options

    • Live Tutoring Sessions: Scheduled or drop-in sessions with certified educators.
    • On-Demand Videos: Pre-recorded lessons for self-paced learning.
    • AI-Powered Chatbot: Instant help for quick questions (e.g., "How do I solve a quadratic equation?").

  3. Age-Appropriate Learning Paths

    • Elementary (K-5): Focus on foundational skills (reading, basic math).
    • Middle School (6-8): Math, science, and writing reinforcement.
    • High School (9-12): Advanced math, test prep, and college applications.
    • Adult Learners: GED prep, career skills, and language courses.

  4. No Library Card Required?

    • While a Wake County library card gives full access, some services (like limited on-demand videos) may be available to non-residents with a digital pass.

  5. Integration with Popular Learning Tools

    • Works alongside Khan Academy, Duolingo, and Quizlet for a seamless learning experience.

How to Access the Tutoring Services

  1. Visit the Wake County Library Website (www.wakegov.com/library).
  2. Click on "Online Learning" or "Tutoring Resources."
  3. Create an Account (if you don’t have one) using your email or library card number.
  4. Browse Subjects & Schedule a Session (or use on-demand tools).
  5. Start Learning!

8 Actionable Strategies to Make the Most of Wake County’s Free Tutoring

While the service is easy to access, maximizing its benefits requires strategy. Here’s how to use it effectively for academic growth.

1. Identify Your Weakest Subjects First

Before diving into tutoring, assess your strengths and weaknesses using:

  • Practice quizzes (available in the tutoring portal).
  • Past test scores (if applicable).
  • Teacher feedback (for K-12 students).

Example: If you’re struggling with algebra, focus on that before moving to calculus.

2. Schedule Live Sessions Strategically

Live tutoring is most effective when used intentionally. Instead of random drop-ins, try:

  • Weekly 30-minute sessions for consistent progress.
  • Before major tests (e.g., SAT, state exams).
  • For complex problems (e.g., chemistry equations, essay outlines).

Pro Tip: Book sessions in advance to ensure availability, especially during peak times (weekends, holidays).

3. Use On-Demand Videos for Self-Paced Learning

If live tutoring isn’t available, on-demand videos are a great alternative. They’re perfect for:

  • Reviewing concepts before a test.
  • Learning at your own pace (e.g., rewinding difficult explanations).
  • Supplementing classroom learning (e.g., watching a video on exponential growth after school).

Example: A high school student reviewing for the AP Biology exam might watch a 10-minute video on cell respiration before attempting practice questions.

4. Combine Tutoring with Other Free Resources

Wake County’s tutoring isn’t the only free tool available. Pair it with:

  • Khan Academy (for math & science mastery).
  • Duolingo (for language learning).
  • Quizlet (for flashcards and spaced repetition).
  • Grammarly (for writing improvements).

Example: A college student preparing for the SAT might:

  1. Watch a Khan Academy video on algebra.
  2. Use Wake County’s live tutoring to clarify doubts.
  3. Practice with Quizlet flashcards for vocabulary.

5. Set Clear Learning Goals

Vague goals like "I want to get better at math" won’t lead to progress. Instead, define specific, measurable objectives:

  • "I will improve my algebra score from 70% to 90% in 4 weeks."
  • "I will master 10 vocabulary words per week using Duolingo."

How to Track Progress:

  • Keep a learning journal (notebook or digital doc).
  • Take weekly quizzes to measure improvement.

6. Engage Actively During Sessions

Passive participation won’t help—active learning is key. During tutoring sessions:

  • Ask specific questions (e.g., "Can you explain how to balance chemical equations?").
  • Practice problems together (don’t just listen).
  • Take notes on key concepts.

Common Mistake: Zoning out because the tutor is explaining something you already know. Instead, ask follow-up questions to deepen understanding.

7. Use the AI Chatbot for Quick Doubts

The AI-powered chatbot is perfect for:

  • Instant answers (e.g., "What’s the derivative of x²?").
  • Quick grammar checks (e.g., "Is this sentence grammatically correct?").
  • Study tips (e.g., "How should I study for a history test?").

Example: A middle school student stuck on a geometry proof can:

  1. Ask the chatbot: "How do I prove two triangles are congruent?"
  2. Get a step-by-step explanation in seconds.

8. Leverage Parent & Teacher Collaboration

For K-12 students, parents and teachers can work together with the tutoring program:

  • Parents: Schedule sessions based on teacher feedback.
  • Teachers: Recommend specific tutoring topics (e.g., "Focus on fractions this week.").
  • Students: Share progress updates with educators.

Example: A parent notices their child struggles with reading comprehension. They:

  1. Schedule a live tutoring session with the library.
  2. Provide the tutor with past assignments for targeted help.
  3. Follow up with the teacher to reinforce learning.

Common Mistakes to Avoid When Using Wake County’s Tutoring Services

While the program is highly effective, some users make avoidable mistakes that limit its benefits. Here’s how to steer clear of pitfalls:

Mistake 1: Treating Tutoring as a Last Resort

Problem: Some students only use tutoring before a test, leading to cramming and stress.

Solution:

  • Use tutoring consistently, not just when struggling.
  • Schedule regular sessions (e.g., every other week) to prevent gaps in learning.

Mistake 2: Not Setting Clear Goals

Problem: Without specific objectives, tutoring sessions become vague and unproductive.

Solution:

  • Define what success looks like (e.g., "I want to solve 10 algebra problems correctly in 20 minutes.").
  • Track progress with quizzes or a journal.

Mistake 3: Relying Only on Live Tutoring

Problem: Live sessions are limited in availability, and waiting for a tutor can delay learning.

Solution:

  • Combine live tutoring with on-demand videos for flexible learning.
  • Use the AI chatbot for quick, instant help.

Mistake 4: Not Engaging Actively

Problem: Passively listening to a tutor doesn’t reinforce learning.

Solution:

  • Ask questions and participate in problem-solving.
  • Take notes and practice independently after sessions.

Mistake 5: Ignoring Other Free Resources

Problem: Some students only use the library’s tutoring without leveraging additional free tools (Khan Academy, Quizlet, etc.).

Solution:

  • Integrate tutoring with other platforms for a well-rounded study plan.
  • Example: Use Khan Academy for practice + Wake County tutoring for clarification.

Mistake 6: Not Following Up with Teachers

Problem: Tutoring works best when aligned with classroom learning.

Solution:

  • Share progress with teachers to reinforce concepts.
  • Ask teachers for specific topics to focus on in tutoring.
