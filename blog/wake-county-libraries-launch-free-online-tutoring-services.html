How Wake County’s Free Online Tutoring Services Work: A Step-by-Step Guide

Before diving into strategies, let’s break down how the tutoring platform operates so you can navigate it with confidence.

1. Accessing the Tutoring Portal

Wake County’s free tutoring is available through Tutor.com, a leading online education platform partnered with the library system. Here’s how to get started:

  • Visit the Wake County Libraries website (www.wakegov.com/library) and navigate to the "Online Learning & Tutoring" section.
  • Click on "Tutor.com"—this will redirect you to the secure tutoring portal.
  • Log in with your Wake County library card number (if you don’t have one, registration is free and takes less than 5 minutes).
  • Select your subject, grade level, and preferred time slot (tutors are available 24/7 for homework help and specific hours for test prep).

2. Choosing Your Tutor & Session Type

The platform offers three main tutoring modes:

  • Homework Help – Real-time assistance for assignments in math, science, English, history, and more.
  • Test Prep – Specialized coaching for SAT, ACT, AP exams, and college entrance tests.
  • Writing Assistance – Expert feedback on essays, research papers, and resumes.

Pro Tip: If you’re a parent or guardian, you can monitor sessions via the tutor’s dashboard to ensure your child stays on track.

3. Customizing Your Experience

Wake County’s tutoring isn’t one-size-fits-all. You can:

  • Upload documents (PDFs, Word files) for tutors to review.
  • Schedule recurring sessions for consistent progress tracking.
  • Access recorded lessons if you miss a live session.
  • Use the "Ask a Question" chat feature for quick clarifications.

4. Who Can Use This Service?

The tutoring is open to everyone, including: ✔ K-12 students (from elementary to high school) ✔ College students (undergraduate and graduate) ✔ Adult learners (career changers, ESL students, job seekers) ✔ Homeschoolers (structured learning support)

No income restrictions apply—this is truly free for all Wake County residents.

8 Actionable Strategies to Maximize Wake County’s Free Online Tutoring

Now that you know how the service works, let’s explore how to use it effectively to boost your grades, confidence, and academic performance.

Strategy 1: Schedule Regular Sessions for Consistent Progress

Problem: Many students treat tutoring as a last-minute cram session before a test, leading to short-term fixes rather than long-term mastery.

Solution:

  • Block out 30-60 minutes weekly for tutoring, just like a study group.
  • Focus on weak areas first—if algebra is your struggle, dedicate two sessions per week to it.
  • Track your improvement by reviewing past session notes.

Example: *A Wake County high school senior, Jamie, struggled with chemistry. Instead of waiting until exam week, she scheduled biweekly 45-minute sessions with a tutor. By the end of the semester, her grade improved from a C- to an A-, and she even earned a science scholarship for college.*

Strategy 2: Use the "Ask a Question" Feature for Quick Clarifications

Problem: Some students hesitate to ask questions in live sessions because they’re afraid of sounding "dumb" or worried about tutor patience.

Solution:

  • Break questions into smaller parts (e.g., instead of "Help me with this entire essay," ask "Can you explain how to structure the thesis statement?").
  • Use the chat feature for non-urgent questions—it’s less intimidating than voice/video calls.
  • Record your doubts in a notebook and ask them all at once during a session.

Example: *A middle school student, Priya, was stuck on a geometry proof. Instead of panicking, she wrote down her exact confusion and sent it via chat. The tutor drew a step-by-step explanation in the digital whiteboard, and Priya understood the concept instantly.

Common Mistakes When Using Online Tutoring (And How to Avoid Them)

While Wake County’s tutoring is incredibly valuable, some users unintentionally sabotage their progress by making these mistakes:

Mistake 1: Treating Tutoring as a "Last Resort"

Problem: Some students only use tutoring when they’re failing, leading to crash courses rather than long-term improvement.

Solution:

  • Use tutoring proactively—even if you’re doing well, a 15-minute check-in can prevent future struggles.
  • Schedule sessions before exams (not just the night before).

Example of Failure: *A student waited until two weeks before finals to get help with AP U.S. History. Despite tutoring, they still scored below their potential because they missed foundational concepts.

Mistake 2: Not Preparing Questions Beforehand

Problem: Walking into a session without clear questions leads to wasted time and frustration.

Solution:

  • List 3-5 specific questions before each session (e.g., "How do I solve quadratic equations?").
  • Bring your textbook or notes to reference.

Example of Failure: *A student asked, "Help me with my math homework." The tutor couldn’t help efficiently because they didn’t know which problems were confusing.

Mistake 3: Ignoring the Platform’s Additional Tools

Problem: Some users only use live tutoring but miss free resources like flashcards, e-books, and test banks.

Solution:

  • Explore the "Resources" tab for study guides, videos, and practice tests.
  • Use the "My Progress" dashboard to track improvement over time.

Example of Failure: *A student relied only on live sessions but didn’t review flashcards, leading to forgetting concepts quickly.
