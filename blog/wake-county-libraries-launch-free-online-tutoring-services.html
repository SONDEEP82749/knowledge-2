How Wake County’s Free Online Tutoring Services Work

Wake County Public Library’s Tutoring & Homework Help program is designed to be user-friendly, flexible, and accessible for all learners. Here’s a breakdown of how it functions:

1. Platform & Accessibility

The tutoring service is hosted on Tutoring.com, a trusted platform used by over 1,000 school districts nationwide. Students and parents can access it 24/7 via:

  • Desktop computers (Chrome, Firefox, Safari)
  • Mobile devices (iOS and Android)
  • Tablets (iPad, Android tablets)

No library card is required, but users must create a free account using their Wake County Public Library card number (or a valid email for non-residents).

2. Subjects & Grade Levels Covered

The program supports K-12 students across core subjects, including:

  • Math (Algebra, Geometry, Calculus)
  • Science (Biology, Chemistry, Physics)
  • English & Language Arts (Grammar, Writing, Reading Comprehension)
  • Social Studies (History, Government, Economics)
  • Foreign Languages (Spanish, French, Mandarin)
  • Test Prep (SAT, ACT, AP Exams)

For college students, Wake County also partners with local universities to offer peer-to-peer tutoring in advanced subjects like Statistics, Computer Science, and Engineering.

3. Types of Tutoring Available

Wake County’s program offers three main tutoring formats:

Format Description Best For
Live Online Tutoring Real-time, one-on-one sessions with certified tutors Students needing immediate help with homework or concepts
Drop-In Help Short, scheduled sessions (15-30 min) for quick questions Busy students who need last-minute clarification
Writing & Editing Assistance Expert feedback on essays, research papers, and assignments High school and college students working on long-form projects

4. How to Get Started

  1. Visit the Wake County Library Website (www.wakegov.com/library) and navigate to the Tutoring & Homework Help section.
  2. Sign in with your library card or create an account.
  3. Select a subject, grade level, and preferred tutor.
  4. Schedule a session (or use drop-in if available).
  5. Connect via video chat (or text-based chat for non-verbal support).

Pro Tip: Book sessions 1-2 hours before deadlines to ensure tutor availability, especially during peak times like weekend homework assignments or final exam prep.

8 Actionable Strategies to Maximize Wake County’s Free Tutoring Services

Not all students (or parents) use tutoring services effectively. Here’s how to get the most out of Wake County’s program:

1. Schedule Sessions During Peak Productivity Hours

Tutors are most available between 3 PM and 8 PM, but weekday mornings (7-9 AM) are also less crowded. If you’re a night owl, try late-night sessions (9 PM - 12 AM) for subjects like Calculus or Advanced Physics, where fewer students seek help.

Example: A high school senior preparing for the SAT Math section scheduled three 30-minute sessions on Tuesday, Thursday, and Saturday evenings—resulting in a 200-point score increase in just four weeks.

2. Prepare Questions Beforehand for Efficiency

Tutors can only help if you know exactly what you need. Before each session:

  • Review your textbook and highlight confusing concepts.
  • Write down specific questions (e.g., "How do you solve quadratic equations using the quadratic formula?").
  • Bring any past assignments or test questions you struggled with.

Real-World Example: A 9th-grade algebra student kept asking vague questions like "I don’t get this chapter." After preparing a list of 5 specific problems, her tutor could explain each step clearly, and her test score jumped from a C to an A.

3. Use the "Two-Session Rule" for Complex Topics

Some concepts (like calculus derivatives or biological cell processes) require multiple explanations. If you’re still confused after one session:

  • Request the same tutor (if possible) for consistency.
  • Break the topic into smaller parts (e.g., "First, explain limits. Then, show how they relate to derivatives.").

Common Mistake: Assuming one session will "fix" a weak area. Solution: Treat tutoring like repetition practice—reinforce what you learn in class.

4. Leverage Writing & Editing Help for Long-Term Grades

Many students underestimate the impact of strong writing. Wake County’s writing tutors can help with:

  • Thesis statement development
  • Grammar and punctuation fixes
  • Citation formatting (APA, MLA, Chicago)

Example: A college freshman submitted a 5-page research paper to a writing tutor. The tutor identified 12 grammatical errors, suggested better transitions, and strengthened the conclusion. The professor later praised the paper as "one of the best in the class."

5. Take Notes During Sessions (Even Virtually)

Since tutoring is online, it’s easy to zone out. Actively engage by:

  • Typing key takeaways in a separate document.
  • Summarizing concepts in your own words after the session.
  • Recording the session (if allowed) to review later.

Pro Tip: Use Google Docs or OneNote to highlight mistakes and track progress over time.

6. Combine Tutoring with Supplemental Resources

Tutors can’t replace self-study. After each session:

  • Revisit the topic using Khan Academy, Paul’s Online Math Notes, or Crash Course videos.
  • Practice problems from your textbook or PracticeQuiz.com.
  • Join study groups (via Discord or Reddit) to reinforce learning.

Example: A 10th-grade biology student used tutoring to understand cell respiration, then watched a Crash Course video and took a practice quiz—solidifying her understanding before the test.

7. Use Drop-In Sessions for Last-Minute Clarifications

Not every question requires a full 30-minute session. For quick doubts, use:

  • Text-based chat (available 24/7).
  • Drop-in tutoring slots (15-minute sessions).
  • The "Ask a Tutor" forum (for non-urgent questions).

When to Use This:

  • "I don’t understand this chemistry problem—can you explain it?"
  • "How do I cite a website in MLA format?"
  • "What’s the difference between independent and dependent variables?"

8. Follow Up with Tutors for Long-Term Support

Some tutors remember frequent students and can track progress. If you’re working on a long-term project (like a science fair experiment or college essay), ask:

  • "Can we check in weekly to review my progress?"
  • "What are the most common mistakes in this subject?"
  • "Are there any recommended resources outside tutoring?"

Real-World Success Story: A Wake County high school senior worked with the same calculus tutor for three months. The tutor noticed her struggle with limits and recommended Paul’s Online Math Notes. By exam time, she mastered the concept and earned an A on her final.

Common Mistakes & How to Avoid Them

Even with a free, high-quality service, some users waste time or miss opportunities. Here are five mistakes and how to prevent them:

Mistake 1: Waiting Until the Last Minute

Problem: Students often schedule tutoring only when they’re failing a test. Solution:

  • Use tutoring proactively—even if you’re doing well, reinforce weak areas.
  • Set a schedule (e.g., "Every Tuesday at 4 PM for math").

Example: A student who waited until finals week to use tutoring couldn’t get a tutor because all slots were booked.

Mistake 2: Not Engaging Actively During Sessions

Problem: Some students just listen without asking questions or taking notes. Solution:

  • Speak up if you don’t understand.
  • Ask for examples (e.g., "Can you show me another problem like this?").
  • Summarize what you learned at the end.

Mistake 3: Relying Only on Tutoring (No Self-Study)

Problem: Tutors can’t replace practice. If you only use tutoring and never study alone, progress will stall. Solution:

  • Practice problems after each session.
  • Review notes from class.
  • Use free online resources (Khan Academy, Quizlet).

Mistake 4: Not Following Up with Tutors

Problem: Some students only use tutoring once and then forget what they learned. Solution:

  • Check in weekly if working on a long-term project.
  • Ask tutors for recommendations (books, videos, practice tests).
  • Track progress in a notebook or digital doc.

Mistake 5: Ignoring Drop-In & Chat Options

Problem: Students only use scheduled sessions and miss quick help when needed. Solution:

  • Use text chat for fast answers (e.g., "What’s the formula for area of a circle?").
  • Try drop-in sessions if you have a short question.
  • Post in the forum if you need general advice.
