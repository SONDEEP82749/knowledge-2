How Wake County Libraries’ Free Online Tutoring Works

Wake County’s Online Tutoring Hub is a 24/7 digital resource designed to cater to learners from kindergarten to college. Here’s a breakdown of its key features:

1. Who Can Access the Service?

The program is open to all Wake County residents, including:

  • K-12 students (public, private, and homeschooled)
  • College students (community college and university prep)
  • Adult learners (GED, career training, language learning)
  • Parents and educators (for curriculum support)

2. Subjects Covered

The tutoring covers all major academic disciplines, including:

  • Math (algebra, calculus, statistics)
  • Science (biology, chemistry, physics)
  • English & Writing (grammar, essay editing, literature)
  • Foreign Languages (Spanish, French, Mandarin)
  • Test Prep (SAT, ACT, AP exams, NC Final Exams)
  • Career & Technical Education (coding, business, trades)

3. How to Get Started

Accessing the service is simple and free:

  1. Visit the Wake County Library website (www.wakegov.com/library) and navigate to the Tutoring Hub.
  2. Create a free account (requires a Wake County library card—available digitally).
  3. Browse available tutors by subject and time slot.
  4. Schedule a session (instant chat, video call, or document upload for feedback).

4. Technology Requirements

To use the service, you’ll need:

  • A smartphone, tablet, or computer with internet access
  • Google Chrome, Firefox, or Edge (for best compatibility)
  • Microphone and camera (for video sessions)

8 Actionable Strategies to Make the Most of Wake County’s Free Tutoring

1. Schedule Sessions During Peak Productivity Hours

Not all students learn at the same time. Research from the University of Illinois (2022) found that morning sessions (8–10 AM) and late evenings (7–9 PM) yield the highest engagement because fewer distractions exist.

How to apply this:

  • If you’re a night owl, book a 9 PM session for focused study.
  • If you’re an early bird, try a 7 AM tutorial before school starts.
  • Use the library’s calendar tool to find open slots.

2. Prepare a Clear Agenda Before Each Session

Tutors can’t read minds—you must communicate your goals. A 2021 study by Harvard’s Graduate School of Education showed that students who prepared a 3-point agenda saw 30% better results in tutoring sessions.

How to apply this:

  • Example agenda for a math tutor:
    1. Review last week’s algebra homework (focus on quadratic equations).
    2. Practice 5 problems together.
    3. Explain a concept I’m still confused about (e.g., factoring).
  • Send this to your tutor 10 minutes before the session via chat.

3. Use the "Two-Session Rule" for Complex Topics

Some subjects (like calculus or advanced chemistry) require multiple sessions to fully grasp. The Wake County Library’s tutoring team recommends breaking lessons into chunks to avoid overwhelm.

How to apply this:

  • First session: Learn the basic formula (e.g., integration in calculus).
  • Second session: Apply it to 3 practice problems.
  • Third session: Test your understanding with a quiz or real-world example.

4. Leverage the "Document Upload" Feature for Writing Help

Struggling with an essay or research paper? The document upload tool allows tutors to review and suggest edits without live pressure.

How to apply this:

  • Upload a draft of your paper before the session.
  • Ask the tutor to highlight key improvements (structure, grammar, citations).
  • Use their feedback to revise independently before submitting.

5. Join a Study Group via the Library’s Discussion Boards

The Wake County Tutoring Hub includes subject-specific forums where students can:

  • Ask quick questions without booking a full session.
  • Collaborate on group projects.
  • Share study tips from other learners.

How to apply this:

  • Join the Biology Study Group if you’re working on a project.
  • Post: "Who can help me understand mitosis?"
  • Engage with responses before scheduling a 1:1 tutor.

6. Track Progress with the Library’s Analytics Dashboard

The Tutoring Hub includes a progress tracker where you can:

  • See which topics you’ve mastered.
  • Identify weak areas needing more practice.
  • Compare your performance over time.

How to apply this:

  • After 3 months of tutoring, check your math progress report.
  • If you see consistent struggles with fractions, request extra sessions.
  • Use the data to set new goals (e.g., "I’ll improve my SAT math score by 50 points").

7. Combine Tutoring with Free Library Resources

Wake County Libraries offer more than just tutoring—they provide:

  • Online databases (Gale, Britannica School, Khan Academy partnerships)
  • E-books and audiobooks (for supplementary reading)
  • Career tools (resume builders, interview prep)

How to apply this:

  • If you’re studying American history, use the Britannica School database to find primary sources.
  • Pair your English tutoring session with a free e-book on writing styles.
  • For college prep, use the career tools to draft a standout resume.

8. Advocate for Your Needs—Tutors Are There to Help!

Many students hesitate to ask for help, fearing they’ll seem "stupid." But tutors are trained to make learning accessible—you’re not alone!

How to apply this:

  • If you’re stuck on a physics problem, say: "I’ve tried this 3 times, and I’m still not getting it. Can you walk me through it step by step?"
  • If you need extra practice, ask: "Do you have any recommended exercises or videos I can watch?"
  • If you’re overwhelmed by a test, request: "Can we break this down into smaller parts and practice one at a time?"

Common Mistakes to Avoid When Using Wake County’s Tutoring

While the program is incredibly valuable, some users make preventable errors that limit its effectiveness. Here’s how to avoid them:

❌ Mistake 1: Waiting Until the Last Minute

Problem: Many students only book tutoring when they’re failing a test, leaving no time to build foundational skills.

Solution:

  • Schedule regular sessions (e.g., every other week) to prevent cramming.
  • Use the progress tracker to spot trends early.

❌ Mistake 2: Not Communicating Clearly

Problem: Some students don’t explain their struggles well, leading to unhelpful tutoring sessions.

Solution:

  • Be specific—instead of "I don’t get this," say: "I tried solving this problem 3 ways, but I keep getting stuck at step 2. Can you show me another approach?"

❌ Mistake 3: Ignoring the Library’s Additional Resources

Problem: Students only use tutoring and miss free e-books, databases, and study groups.

Solution:

  • Explore the library’s full catalog—many subjects have complementary materials.
  • Join study groups for collaborative learning.

❌ Mistake 4: Assuming Tutors Can Replace Self-Study

Problem: Some students expect tutors to do all the work, leading to no independent practice.

Solution:

  • Treat tutoring as a tool, not a crutch.
  • Practice on your own between sessions (use Khan Academy, Quizlet, or past exams).

❌ Mistake 5: Not Following Up on Feedback

Problem: After a session, some students don’t apply the tutor’s advice, leading to no improvement.

Solution:

  • Write down key takeaways after each session.
  • Revisit old notes to reinforce learning.
