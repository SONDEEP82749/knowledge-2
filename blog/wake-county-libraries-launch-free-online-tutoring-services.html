How Wake County’s Free Online Tutoring Services Work: A Step-by-Step Guide

Wake County Public Library’s free online tutoring is powered by Tutoring.com, a leading educational platform that connects students with certified tutors in various subjects. Here’s how it functions:

1. Eligibility & Access Requirements

  • Who can use it? All Wake County residents (students, adults, and even parents helping children with homework).
  • No age restrictions—from kindergarten to graduate school, everyone is welcome.
  • Requires a valid Wake County library card (or a free digital library account if you don’t have one).
  • Internet access is mandatory (most Wake County residents have reliable broadband).

2. How to Sign Up (Digital & In-Person Options)

Option 1: Online Registration (Fastest Method)

  1. Visit the WCPL website (www.wakegov.com/library).
  2. Click on "Tutoring Services" under the "Learning & Education" tab.
  3. Create or log in to your Wake County library account (if you don’t have one, sign up for a free digital card).
  4. Select your subject (math, science, English, test prep, etc.).
  5. Choose a tutor based on availability and expertise.
  6. Schedule a session (available 24/7 for asynchronous help or live tutoring during peak hours).

Option 2: In-Person Assistance (For Non-Digital Users)

  • Visit any Wake County library branch (e.g., Raleigh, Cary, or Apex locations).
  • Ask a library staff member for help setting up your account.
  • They can also provide hardcopy guides on how to access tutoring remotely.

3. Types of Tutoring Available

Wake County’s program offers three main formats:

Tutoring Type Description Best For
Live Online Tutoring Real-time video chat with a tutor (1-on-1 or group sessions). Urgent homework help, test prep, complex subjects.
Asynchronous Tutoring Submit questions anytime, and tutors respond within 24 hours. Busy schedules, last-minute study questions.
Subject-Specific Help Specialized tutors for AP exams, SAT/ACT prep, college courses, and career certifications. Advanced students, adult learners, career changers.

4. Key Subjects & Specialized Help

Wake County’s tutoring covers:

  • K-12 Core Subjects (math, reading, writing, science)

  • College Courses (algebra, chemistry, psychology, business)
  • Career & Technical Training (coding, accounting, nursing prep)
  • ESL (English as a Second Language) for non-native speakers

5. How to Book a Session (Detailed Walkthrough)

  1. Log in to your WCPL account.
  2. Navigate to the Tutoring Portal (under "Digital Resources").
  3. Browse available tutors by subject and availability.
  4. Select a time slot (some slots fill up fast—book early!).
  5. Join the session via Zoom or a web browser link.
  6. Engage with your tutor—ask questions, submit documents, or work through problems together.

Pro Tip: If you’re new to online tutoring, practice using Zoom or the tutoring platform beforehand to avoid technical issues.

8 Actionable Strategies to Maximize Your Wake County Tutoring Experience

Now that you know how to access the service, let’s dive into how to use it effectively. These strategies will help you get the most out of every tutoring session, whether you're a struggling student or a self-directed learner.

Strategy 1: Identify Your Weakest Subject Early

Before your first session, take a quick self-assessment to pinpoint where you need the most help.

  • Example: If you’re a high school junior, review your last two report cards to see which subjects have the lowest grades.
  • Action Step:
    • List 3-5 topics you struggle with (e.g., "algebra word problems," "grammar rules," "biology cell functions").
    • Prioritize them based on upcoming exams or deadlines.

Why it works: Tutors can tailor sessions to your specific needs rather than wasting time on areas you already understand.

Strategy 2: Prepare Questions & Materials Beforehand

Walking into a tutoring session without preparation is like showing up to a doctor’s appointment without symptoms listed. Tutors can’t help if they don’t know exactly what you need.

  • What to bring:
    • Textbook pages (highlighted sections).
    • Past assignments (with mistakes circled).
    • Practice problems (from worksheets or online quizzes).
    • A list of specific questions (e.g., "How do I solve quadratic equations?").

Real-World Example: A college student in organic chemistry struggled with reaction mechanisms. Instead of just saying, "I don’t get this," he brought:

  • A printed reaction scheme from his textbook.
  • Three practice problems he failed.
  • A note: "Can you explain the arrow-pushing rules?"

Result: The tutor drew step-by-step mechanisms on a virtual whiteboard and gave him two similar problems to practice—leading to an A on his next quiz.

Strategy 3: Use the "5-Minute Rule" for Live Sessions

If you’re new to live tutoring, it’s easy to feel overwhelmed by the real-time interaction. To avoid procrastination or last-minute panic, follow the "5-Minute Rule":

  1. Log in 5 minutes early to test your audio/video.
  2. Write down 1-2 key questions before the session starts.
  3. Start with the easiest question to build confidence.
  4. Take notes during the session (use a digital doc or notebook).

Why it works: This prevents technical issues and ensures you stay focused rather than wasting time.

Strategy 4: Leverage Asynchronous Tutoring for Busy Schedules

Not everyone can commit to live sessions. If you’re a working parent, athlete, or night owl, asynchronous tutoring is your best friend.

  • How to use it effectively:
    • Submit questions at night and get answers by morning.
    • Upload homework with specific errors you need explained.
    • Ask for sample problems to practice before the next class.

Example: A Wake County high school senior used asynchronous tutoring to:

  • Submit his SAT math practice test with 10 wrong answers.
  • Received detailed explanations within 4 hours.
  • Scored 100 points higher on his next practice test.

Strategy 5: Turn Tutoring into a Study Group

If you’re in group tutoring (available for some subjects), maximize collaboration:

  • Share resources (e.g., "I found a great YouTube video on this topic—check it out!").
  • Take turns asking questions to keep the session dynamic.
  • Set a goal (e.g., "Let’s solve 5 problems together before the end of the hour").

Pro Tip: If you’re tutoring alone, pretend you’re teaching someone else—this forces you to explain concepts clearly, reinforcing your own understanding.

Strategy 6: Use Tutoring for Test Prep (Not Just Homework Help)

Many students only use tutoring for last-minute homework, but the real power is in preparing for exams.

  • How to use tutors for test prep:
    • Request full-length practice tests (some tutors provide these).
    • Ask for weak-area drills (e.g., "I keep getting trig questions wrong—give me 10 to practice").
    • Simulate exam conditions (set a timer, work without notes).

Example: A Wake County AP Biology student used tutoring to:

  • Get a full AP practice exam reviewed.
  • Identify his weak spots (e.g., cell respiration).
  • Practice 20 questions daily until he mastered it.
  • Result: Score of 5 on the AP exam (vs. his usual 3).

Example: A Wake County adult learner wanted to become a certified bookkeeper. She:

  • Scheduled a tutoring session to ask accounting-specific questions.
  • Practiced with real financial statements from the library’s business database.
  • Result: Passed her certification exam in 3 months.

Strategy 8: Track Progress & Adjust Strategies

The best learners don’t just take tutoring—they measure results. Use this progress-tracking method:

  1. Before the first session, take a pre-test (find one online or ask the tutor for help).
  2. After each session, note 3 things you learned.
  3. After 4-6 sessions, retake the pre-test (or a similar one).
  4. Adjust your study plan based on what still needs work.

Example: A middle school math student tracked her progress like this:

  • Week 1: Struggled with fractions → Tutored 3x, practiced 20 problems.
  • Week 3: Still mixed up adding vs. multiplying fractions → Asked tutor for visual aids.
  • Week 5: Passed her chapter test with an A.
