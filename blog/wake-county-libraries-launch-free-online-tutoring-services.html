How Wake County Libraries’ Free Online Tutoring Works: A Step-by-Step Guide

Before jumping into strategies, it’s essential to understand how the program operates. Wake County’s free tutoring is powered by partnerships with leading educational platforms, including Tutoring.com, Brainfuse, and Khan Academy, ensuring 24/7 access to expert tutors.

1. Eligibility & Access Requirements

Unlike traditional tutoring services, Wake County’s program is completely free and open to:

  • Wake County residents (proof of residency may be required for some services)
  • Students of all ages (K-12, college, and adult learners)
  • Parents and guardians assisting their children

No income restrictions apply, making it one of the most inclusive educational resources available.

2. How to Sign Up

To access the tutoring services, follow these steps:

  1. Visit the Wake County Public Library Website

    • Go to www.wakegov.com/library and navigate to the "Online Resources" section.

  2. Locate the Tutoring Portal

    • Under "Homework Help & Tutoring", click on "Free Online Tutoring"—this will redirect you to the Tutoring.com or Brainfuse platform.

  3. Create an Account (If Required)

    • Some services (like Brainfuse) require a free library card, which you can obtain in-person or online via the WCPL website.
    • Others (like Khan Academy) allow guest access without an account.

  4. Select Your Subject & Grade Level

    • Choose from math, science, English, history, SAT/ACT prep, and more.
    • Specify your grade level (elementary, middle school, high school, college, or adult).

  5. Schedule a Session or Access Live Tutoring

    • Live tutoring is available daily (Monday-Friday, 3 PM–10 PM) and weekends (12 PM–8 PM).
    • Drop-in help is also available for immediate assistance (no appointment needed).

  6. Start Your Session

    • Use the virtual whiteboard, chat, or voice call to connect with a tutor.
    • Upload homework assignments for real-time feedback.

8 Actionable Strategies to Maximize Wake County’s Free Tutoring Services

Now that you know how the program works, let’s explore practical ways to get the most out of it.

Strategy 1: Schedule Sessions During Peak Hours for Faster Responses

  • Best times for live tutoring: Evenings (5 PM–9 PM) and weekends (12 PM–6 PM) tend to have fewer wait times.
  • Avoid rush hours (3 PM–5 PM) if you need immediate help—tutors are often busy with other students.
  • Use the "Drop-In" feature for instant assistance when scheduling isn’t possible.

Strategy 2: Prepare a Clear List of Questions Before Starting

Tutors can only help as much as you guide them. Before your session: ✔ Write down specific questions (e.g., "How do I solve a quadratic equation?" instead of "Help me with math.") ✔ Upload your textbook or worksheet so the tutor can reference the exact problem. ✔ Mention your learning goals (e.g., "I need help understanding the concept, not just the answer.")

Example: Instead of: "I don’t get this math problem." Try: "I’m stuck on Problem 4 in Chapter 5. Can you walk me through the steps for solving systems of equations using substitution?"

Strategy 3: Use the Virtual Whiteboard Effectively

The interactive whiteboard is one of the most powerful tools in the program. To make the most of it:

  • Draw out your work as you explain your thought process.
  • Ask the tutor to demonstrate a solution step-by-step.
  • Save your session (if available) to review later.

Real-World Example: A high school student struggling with geometry used the whiteboard to sketch a triangle while explaining his confusion about angle sums. The tutor then drew the correct diagram and walked through the proof, making the concept crystal clear.

Strategy 4: Leverage Recorded Lessons for Self-Paced Learning

Not every student can attend a live session. Brainfuse and Khan Academy offer:

  • On-demand video lessons (e.g., "How to Factor Polynomials")
  • Practice quizzes with instant feedback
  • Flashcards for vocabulary and math formulas

Best for:

  • Students who prefer self-study
  • Parents reviewing material before helping their kids
  • Adult learners preparing for the GED

Strategy 5: Take Advantage of Test Prep Resources

Whether you’re preparing for the SAT, ACT, or AP exams, Wake County’s tutoring includes:

  • Full-length practice tests (with answer keys)
  • Subject-specific drills (e.g., "100 Math Problems for the ACT")
  • Strategies for time management (critical for multiple-choice tests)

Pro Tip: Use the "Test Center" in Tutoring.com to simulate real exam conditions and track your progress.

Strategy 6: Form Study Groups with Other Students

Some tutoring platforms (like Brainfuse) allow group sessions. If available:

  • Invite classmates to join a shared study session.
  • Assign roles (e.g., one student explains a concept while others ask questions).
  • Use the whiteboard to collaborate on group projects.

Example: *A group of AP Biology students used a group tutoring session to review cell respiration together. The tutor facilated discussions, and the students quizzed each other to reinforce learning.

Strategy 7: Follow Up with Tutors for Additional Help

One session isn’t always enough. After your first meeting:

  • Ask the tutor for recommended resources (e.g., "What YouTube videos should I watch next?").
  • Request a follow-up session if you’re still struggling.
  • Join a study group (if available) to reinforce concepts.

Common Follow-Up Question: "I understand the concept better now, but I’m still having trouble applying it. Can we do a few practice problems together?"

Strategy 8: Use Tutoring for Long-Term Learning, Not Just Cramming

The best students don’t just ask for answers—they ask for understanding. Instead of: ❌ "Just tell me the answer." Try: ✅ "How does this formula work, and can you show me another example?"

Example: A college student struggling with calculus derivatives didn’t just ask for the derivative of a function—he asked the tutor to explain the "limit definition" and how it connects to real-world motion problems. This deepened his understanding far beyond memorization.

Common Mistakes Students Make (And How to Avoid Them)

Even the best programs can fail if not used correctly. Here are five mistakes students make and how to fix them.

Mistake 1: Waiting Until the Last Minute for Help

Problem: Some students only seek tutoring when they’re failing a test, leading to cramming and stress. Solution:

  • Use tutoring for prevention, not just cure.
  • Schedule regular check-ins (e.g., once a month) to stay on top of concepts.
  • Review weak areas before exams, not after.

Example: A student who waited until finals week to ask for help found that tutors were busy, and they couldn’t get the personalized attention they needed. Solution: Start early—even one session per week helps.

Mistake 2: Not Engaging Actively During Sessions

Problem: Some students passively listen instead of participating, making tutoring sessions less effective. Solution:

  • Speak up with questions—tutors are there to guide you, not just give answers.
  • Show your work (even if wrong) so the tutor can correct you step-by-step.
  • Ask for explanations, not just solutions.

Example: A student who only said, "I don’t get it" received vague answers. Instead, he asked, "Can you walk me through the steps for this problem?"—leading to real understanding.

Mistake 3: Ignoring Recorded Lessons & Practice Quizzes

Problem: Some students only use live tutoring and miss out on self-paced learning. Solution:

  • Combine live help with recorded content for reinforcement.
  • Take practice quizzes to identify gaps before live sessions.
  • Review saved sessions to remember key concepts.

Example: A student who only used live tutoring* found that he forgot what he learned after a week. Solution: He watched the recorded lessons and took quizzes to solidify his knowledge.

Mistake 4: Not Using the Whiteboard Effectively

Problem: Some students don’t take advantage of the interactive tools, making sessions less visual and engaging. Solution:

  • Draw out problems as you explain them.
  • Ask the tutor to demonstrate on the board.
  • Save your session to review later.
