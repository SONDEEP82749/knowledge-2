How Wake County’s Free Online Tutoring Services Work

Wake County Public Library (WCPL) has partnered with Tutor.com and Brainfuse to provide 24/7 access to certified tutors. Here’s a breakdown of how it functions:

1. Eligibility & Access

  • Anyone with a Wake County library card can access the service.
  • No income restrictions—unlike some school-based programs, this is truly free for all.
  • Available on any device (computer, tablet, or smartphone) with an internet connection.

2. Subjects Covered

The tutoring covers:

  • Math (Algebra, Calculus, Statistics)
  • Science (Biology, Chemistry, Physics)
  • English & Writing
  • Test Prep (SAT, ACT, AP Exams)
  • College & Career Readiness

3. How to Get Started

  1. Visit the Wake County Library website (wakelibrary.org).
  2. Log in with your library card (or sign up if you don’t have one).
  3. Navigate to the "Tutoring" section under "Online Resources."
  4. Select a subject and schedule a session (or drop into a live chat).

4. Types of Tutoring Available

Service Description
Live One-on-One Tutoring Real-time video sessions with a certified tutor.
SkillSurfer (Brainfuse) Interactive lessons for self-paced learning.
Homework Help Immediate assistance for assignments.
Test Prep Focused sessions for standardized exams.

5. Why This Stands Out

8 Actionable Strategies to Make the Most of Wake County’s Free Tutoring

Now that you know how the service works, let’s dive into how to use it effectively.

1. Schedule Sessions During Peak Productivity Hours

Students often perform best in the late morning or early evening (between 10 AM–2 PM and 5 PM–8 PM). Avoid last-minute cramming—plan ahead to reduce stress.

Pro Tip: If you’re a night owl, use the 24/7 homework help for urgent questions.

2. Prepare Questions Beforehand

Tutors can’t read minds! Before a session:

  • List specific problems (e.g., "I don’t understand how to solve quadratic equations").
  • Bring relevant materials (textbook pages, past assignments, or notes).
  • Write down your goals (e.g., "I need help with my biology lab report").

Example: Instead of saying, "I’m bad at math," try: "I’m stuck on Problem 12 in Chapter 5—can you walk me through the steps?"

3. Use the Writing Lab for Essay Feedback

The Writing Lab is one of the most underutilized features. Instead of just getting corrections, ask tutors to:

  • Explain grammar rules in a way you understand.
  • Suggest thesis improvements before finalizing an essay.
  • Help with citations (APA, MLA, Chicago styles).

Real-World Example: A high school senior struggling with a college application essay used the Writing Lab to refine her personal statement. After just two sessions, her tutor helped her strengthen her narrative arc, leading to a scholarship acceptance.

4. Leverage SkillSurfer for Self-Paced Learning

If you prefer learning at your own pace, SkillSurfer (Brainfuse’s interactive tool) is perfect. It offers:

  • Video lessons on key concepts.
  • Practice quizzes with instant feedback.
  • Step-by-step guides for complex topics.

How to Use It:

  1. Log in to Brainfuse via the Wake County Library portal.
  2. Select your subject (e.g., Algebra).
  3. Choose a lesson and work through it before a live tutoring session.

5. Take Advantage of Test Prep Resources

Whether you’re prepping for the SAT, ACT, or AP Exams, Wake County’s tutors can:

  • Identify weak areas in your practice tests.
  • Teach test-taking strategies (e.g., time management, elimination techniques).
  • Review key formulas (e.g., chemistry equations, trigonometry identities).

Example: A junior in high school used the SAT prep tutoring to improve her math score by 150 points in just four weeks. Her tutor helped her master the most common question types, which appeared frequently on her practice tests.

6. Use the Service for College & Career Readiness

Even if you’re not in high school, Wake County’s tutoring helps with:

  • Resume writing (tutors can review your LinkedIn profile or cover letter).
  • Interview preparation (practice answering common questions).
  • Career exploration (get advice on majors and job markets).

Pro Tip: If you’re a parent helping a child with college applications, book a session to review their personal statement before submission.

7. Combine Tutoring with Other Library Resources

Wake County Libraries offer more than just tutoring. Pair tutoring sessions with:

  • Online databases (e.g., Gale in Context: High School for research papers).
  • E-books & audiobooks (e.g., Libby for supplementary reading).
  • Career tools (e.g., JobNow for job hunting help).

Example: A college student used tutoring for calculus alongside Khan Academy videos to reinforce concepts. By watching a video first, she could then ask targeted questions in her tutoring session.

8. Follow Up & Reinforce Learning

A single tutoring session won’t magically improve your grades. Follow these steps to retain knowledge:

  • Review notes after each session.
  • Practice similar problems (use textbooks or online exercises).
  • Revisit the tutor if you forget key concepts.

Real-World Success Story: A middle school student struggled with fractions. After three tutoring sessions, she understood the concepts—but her tutor emphasized practicing daily. Within a month, her math test scores improved by 30 points, and she no longer needed extra help.

Common Mistakes & How to Avoid Them

Even the best resources can fail if used incorrectly. Here are mistakes students make and how to prevent them.

❌ Mistake 1: Waiting Until the Last Minute

Problem: Many students only seek tutoring when they’re already failing a test or assignment. Solution:

  • Start early—even if you’re just confused about a concept, address it before it becomes a bigger issue.
  • Use tutoring for prevention, not just cure.

❌ Mistake 2: Not Being Specific with Questions

Problem: Vague questions like "I don’t get this chapter" waste tutor time. Solution:

  • Be precise: "Can you explain how to balance chemical equations?"
  • Show your work (even if it’s wrong) so the tutor can guide you step-by-step.

❌ Mistake 3: Skipping Self-Paced Learning

Problem: Some students rely only on live tutoring and never engage with SkillSurfer or videos. Solution:

  • Combine both methods—watch a lesson, then ask questions in a live session.
  • Practice independently between sessions to reinforce learning.

❌ Mistake 4: Ignoring Writing Feedback

Problem: Students often submit essays without revisions, even after getting feedback. Solution:

  • Take tutors’ suggestions seriously—they’ve seen thousands of papers.
  • Revise at least once before turning in your work.

❌ Mistake 5: Not Using All Available Resources

Problem: Some students only use one tutoring service (e.g., only live sessions) and miss out on SkillSurfer or test prep tools. Solution:

  • Explore every feature—each tool serves a different purpose.
  • Check the library’s website regularly for new additions.
