How Wake County Libraries’ Free Online Tutoring Works

Wake County’s new virtual tutoring platform is designed to be user-friendly, flexible, and accessible—whether you're studying at home, in a library branch, or on the go. Here’s a breakdown of how it functions:

1. Platform & Accessibility

The tutoring service is powered by Tutoring.com, a trusted provider of one-on-one and group tutoring sessions. Students can access it 24/7 through:

  • Wake County Public Library’s official website (www.wakegov.com/library)
  • Mobile app (available for iOS and Android)
  • Library computers at all 48 branches

No library card? Don’t worry—residents can sign up for a free digital card online, granting instant access to all services.

2. Who Can Use It?

The program is open to:

  • K-12 students (grades K-12)
  • College students (for general education subjects)
  • Adult learners (GED prep, career skills, and language learning)
  • Parents and teachers (for homework help and lesson planning)

3. Subjects & Specializations

Tutors are available for:

  • Math (algebra, calculus, statistics)
  • Science (biology, chemistry, physics)
  • English & Writing (grammar, essay writing, literature)
  • Test Prep (SAT, ACT, AP exams)
  • Foreign Languages (Spanish, French, Mandarin)
  • Career & College Readiness (resume writing, interview prep)

4. Types of Tutoring Available

Wake County offers three main tutoring formats:

  1. One-on-One Tutoring – Personalized sessions with a dedicated tutor.
  2. Group Tutoring – Small group sessions (ideal for collaborative learning).
  3. Drop-In Help – Quick questions answered in real time (no appointment needed).

5. How to Book a Session

Booking is simple and fast:

  1. Log in to your Wake County Library account.
  2. Browse available tutors by subject and time slot.
  3. Select a session type (one-on-one, group, or drop-in).
  4. Choose a tutor based on availability and expertise.
  5. Join via video call (Zoom or browser-based) or chat.

Pro Tip: Schedule sessions 1-2 weeks in advance during peak hours (evenings and weekends) to secure your preferred tutor.

8 Actionable Strategies to Maximize Wake County’s Free Tutoring Services

Now that you know how the program works, let’s dive into how to use it effectively to boost academic performance.

1. Identify Your Weakest Subject Early

Before jumping into tutoring, assess where you need the most help. Use:

  • Past test scores (if available)
  • Teacher feedback (ask for specific areas of improvement)
  • Online quizzes (Khan Academy, Quizlet, or ProProfs)

Example: If your math grades are slipping in algebra, schedule a one-on-one session with a math tutor before moving on to calculus.

2. Prepare Before Each Session

Tutors are there to guide you, not do the work for you. Come prepared with: ✔ Specific questions (e.g., "How do I solve quadratic equations?" instead of "Help me with math.") ✔ Relevant materials (textbook pages, past assignments, notes) ✔ A clear goal (e.g., "I want to improve my essay structure")

Real-World Example: A Wake County high school senior, Jamie, struggled with AP Biology but didn’t know where to start. Instead of vague requests, she prepared a list of topics she needed help with—cell respiration, mitosis, and evolutionary theory—and her tutor created a customized study plan for her.

3. Use Group Tutoring for Collaborative Learning

Group sessions are great for:

  • Peer accountability (you’re less likely to procrastinate)
  • Different perspectives (learning from classmates)
  • Cost-effective help (if you’re on a tight budget)

How to Make the Most of It:

  • Participate actively—don’t just listen.
  • Ask follow-up questions if something isn’t clear.
  • Share resources (e.g., "I found this YouTube video that explains it better!")

4. Leverage Drop-In Help for Quick Doubts

Need help right now? Use drop-in tutoring for:

  • Last-minute homework questions
  • Clarifying confusing concepts
  • Quick grammar checks

Best Practices:

  • Be concise—tutors may have multiple students.
  • Follow up with a one-on-one session if needed.

5. Track Progress & Set Goals

Use a simple tracking system to monitor improvement:

  • Week 1: "I’ll understand 50% of my algebra problems."
  • Week 2: "I’ll improve my essay score by 10 points."
  • Week 4: "I’ll ace my next quiz."

Tools to Help:

  • Google Sheets (create a progress tracker)
  • Notion or Trello (for visual goal-setting)
  • Tutor feedback notes (ask for written summaries after sessions)

6. Combine Tutoring with Other Resources

Wake County’s tutoring isn’t the only free resource available. Pair it with:

  • Khan Academy (free video lessons)
  • Duolingo (language learning)
  • Libby & Hoopla (e-books and audiobooks)
  • Wake County’s Digital Learning Hub (interactive exercises)

Example: A middle school student, Priya, used tutoring for science help but also watched Khan Academy’s biology videos before each session to reinforce learning.

7. Ask for Additional Materials & Study Guides

Many tutors provide extra resources like:

  • Cheat sheets
  • Practice problem sets
  • Recommended books & videos

How to Request Them:

  • "Do you have any practice problems for trigonometry?"
  • "Can you recommend a good YouTube channel for English grammar?"

8. Use Tutoring for Test Prep (Not Just Homework)

Tutors can help with:

  • SAT/ACT strategies
  • College essay reviews
  • Flashcard creation for exams

Pro Tip:

  • Schedule test prep sessions 2-3 months before exams (not the night before).
  • Ask for timed practice tests to simulate real exam conditions.

Common Mistakes to Avoid When Using Wake County’s Tutoring Services

While the program is free and powerful, some users make costly mistakes that reduce its effectiveness. Here’s how to avoid them:

1. Not Booking Sessions Early

Mistake: Waiting until the last minute to schedule a tutor. Why It Fails: Popular tutors (especially for SAT/ACT prep) book up weeks in advance. Solution:

  • Check availability 1-2 weeks ahead.
  • Set calendar reminders for recurring sessions.

2. Being Passive During Sessions

Mistake: Just sitting back and letting the tutor do all the work. Why It Fails: Tutoring is interactive—you must engage to learn. Solution:

  • Take notes during sessions.
  • Ask questions if something isn’t clear.
  • Practice problems on your own afterward.

3. Not Using All Available Resources

Mistake: Only using tutoring for homework, ignoring other free tools. Why It Fails: Combining resources (like Khan Academy + tutoring) dramatically improves retention. Solution:

  • Ask tutors for extra materials (cheat sheets, practice tests).
  • Supplement with online courses (Coursera, edX).

4. Skipping Drop-In Help for Quick Fixes

Mistake: Only using one-on-one sessions for everything. Why It Fails: Drop-in help is faster for minor questions. Solution:

  • Use drop-in for quick doubts.
  • Save one-on-one for deep learning.

5. Not Tracking Progress

Mistake: Not measuring improvement over time. Why It Fails: Without clear goals, it’s hard to know if tutoring is working. Solution:

  • Keep a progress journal.
  • Compare test scores before and after tutoring.

6. Over-Reliance on Tutors for Everything

Mistake: Using tutors as a crutch instead of practicing independently. Why It Fails: True learning happens through practice. Solution:

  • Do homework first, then ask for help if stuck.
  • Review notes between sessions.

7. Ignoring Teacher Feedback

Mistake: Only relying on tutors, not communicating with teachers. Why It Fails: Teachers and tutors see different perspectives—combining both helps more. Solution:

  • Share tutor insights with teachers.
  • Ask teachers for additional practice problems.

8. Not Using Tutoring for Career & Life Skills

Mistake: Thinking tutoring is only for school subjects. Why It Fails: Tutors can help with resumes, interviews, and soft skills. Solution:

  • Ask for career advice (even if you’re not in college).
  • Use tutoring for language learning (Spanish, French, etc.).
