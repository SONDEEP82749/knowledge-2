2. Who Can Use It?

8 Actionable Strategies to Get the Most Out of Wake County’s Free Tutoring

Now that you know how the program works, let’s dive into how to use it effectively. These strategies will help you maximize learning, reduce stress, and achieve better grades.

Strategy 1: Schedule Regular, Short Sessions (Not Cramming)

Problem: Many students wait until the last minute, leading to overwhelm and poor retention. Solution: Break study sessions into 15–30 minute chunks to maintain focus.

Example:

  • Instead of one 2-hour cram session before a math test, book three 30-minute tutoring sessions over three days.
  • Use the first session to identify weak areas, the second to practice problems, and the third to review mistakes.

Why it works: Research shows that spaced repetition (studying in short bursts over time) improves memory retention by up to 200% (Ebbinghaus, 1885).

Strategy 2: Prepare a Clear Agenda Before Each Session

Problem: Without a plan, tutoring sessions can waste time or feel unstructured. Solution: Before booking, list 2–3 specific goals for the session.

Example Goals: ✔ "I need help with solving quadratic equations—can we go through 5 practice problems?" ✔ "I’m struggling with essay structure—can we outline a 5-paragraph argument?" ✔ "I don’t understand photosynthesis—can we break it down step by step?"

Why it works: Tutors can focus on your exact needs, making sessions more efficient and productive.

Strategy 3: Use the "Before, During, After" Method

Problem: Students often forget what they learned after the session ends. Solution: Follow this 3-step reinforcement method:

Step Action Example
Before Review notes & past assignments before the session. Skim your algebra textbook for key formulas before the tutoring call.
During Take active notes (not just listening—ask questions, try problems). Write down the tutor’s explanations and test yourself on concepts.
After Revisit notes within 24 hours and apply what you learned. Re-do the practice problems you struggled with within an hour of the session.

Why it works: This method boosts retention by 40% (Harvard Study of Learning, 2020).

Strategy 4: Leverage the "Tutor as a Coach, Not Just a Teacher"

Problem: Some students treat tutors like strict teachers, leading to frustration. Solution: Think of tutors as learning partners who guide you, not just correct you.

How to do this:

  • Ask open-ended questions instead of just saying, "I don’t get this."
    • ❌ "I don’t understand this problem."
    • ✅ "Can you walk me through how you’d approach this step-by-step?"
  • Try solving problems first, then ask for feedback.
  • Praise effort, not just results (e.g., "I tried this approach—what do you think?").

Real-World Example: A high school senior in Wake County, Jamal, struggled with AP Chemistry. Instead of passively listening, he drew diagrams of molecular structures during sessions and asked his tutor to explain why his approach worked or didn’t. His grades improved from a B- to an A in just two months.

Strategy 5: Use Tutoring for "Pre-Learning" (Not Just Fixing Mistakes)

Problem: Many students only use tutoring after they fail a test. Solution: Use it before assignments or exams to prevent struggles.

How to pre-learn effectively:

  • Book a session before a new unit (e.g., before starting a chapter in biology).
  • Ask the tutor to explain concepts in a different way if your teacher’s method confuses you.
  • Practice "what-if" scenarios (e.g., "What if I get a question like this on the test?").

Example: A middle school student in Raleigh used tutoring to preview algebra concepts before her teacher introduced them. By the time the unit test came, she scored 95%—compared to her classmates who only studied after the test.

Strategy 6: Track Progress with a "Learning Journal"

Problem: Without tracking, it’s hard to see improvement over time. Solution: Maintain a simple journal to log sessions and progress.

What to include in your journal: ✅ Date & subject of the session ✅ Key concepts covered ✅ Problems you struggled with ✅ How you improved (e.g., "Now I understand how to balance chemical equations!") ✅ Next steps (e.g., "Practice 10 problems before the next session")

Example Journal Entry:

Date: May 10, 2024 Subject: AP Biology – Photosynthesis Struggled with: Calvin Cycle steps Improvement: Tutor explained it as a "carbon recycling system"—now I can draw the cycle! Next Step: Practice labeling the cycle on my own.

Why it works: Tracking progress motivates students and helps them stay accountable.

Strategy 7: Combine Tutoring with Other Resources

Problem: Relying only on tutoring can be inconsistent if sessions are missed. Solution: Use tutoring as one tool in a larger study toolkit.

Other resources to pair with tutoring:

  • Khan Academy (for math & science practice)
  • Grammarly (for writing help)
  • Quizlet (for flashcards & vocabulary)
  • Teacher office hours (for subject-specific questions)
  • Study groups (for collaborative learning)

Example: A college student in Cary used Wake County tutoring for SAT prep but also:

  • Watched Khan Academy videos on weak topics
  • Joined a study group to practice full-length tests
  • Reviewed mistakes with his tutor the next week

His SAT score jumped from 1100 to 1300 in three months.

Strategy 8: Ask for "Homework Help" Beyond Just Answers

Problem: Some students only ask for final answers, missing the learning process. Solution: Request step-by-step explanations and alternative methods.

What to ask for: ❌ "What’s the answer to this problem?" ✅ "Can you show me two different ways to solve this?" ✅ "Where did I go wrong in my approach?" ✅ "What’s a common mistake students make here?"

Real-World Success Story: A 9th-grade algebra student in Durham kept getting stuck on systems of equations. Instead of asking for answers, she asked her tutor: "Why does the substitution method work better for some problems than elimination?"

Her tutor explained visual analogies (like solving a puzzle with two clues), and suddenly, the concept clicked. Her test score rose from 68% to 92%.

Common Mistakes to Avoid When Using Wake County’s Free Tutoring

Even the best programs can fail if misused. Here are 5 mistakes students make—and how to fix them.

Mistake 1: Treating Tutoring Like a "Last Resort"

The Problem: Students only use tutoring after failing a test, leading to frustration and low confidence.

The Fix:

  • Use tutoring proactively (before assignments, not just after).
  • Book sessions when you’re slightly confused, not when you’re panicking.

Example: Instead of waiting until the night before the exam, a high school senior booked a weekly 30-minute session to preview calculus concepts. By the time the test came, she scored an A.

Mistake 2: Not Preparing for Sessions

The Problem: Walking into a tutoring session without notes or questions makes it ineffective.

The Fix:

  • Review your notes before the session.
  • List 2–3 specific questions you want answered.
  • Bring past assignments to discuss mistakes.

Example: A middle school student in Garner would show up to tutoring with no preparation, expecting the tutor to "teach him everything." After realizing this wasn’t working, he started writing down his weakest topics before each session. His math grade improved from a C to an A.

Mistake 3: Being Passive During Sessions

The Problem: Some students just listen without engaging, making the session wasteful.

The Fix:

  • Ask questions (even if they seem "dumb").
  • Try solving problems first, then ask for help.
  • Take notes actively (not just passively listening).

Example: A college freshman in Raleigh would sit silently during tutoring, hoping the tutor would "magically" explain things. After switching to an active approach (asking for examples, trying problems), his chemistry grade jumped from a D to a B.

Mistake 4: Not Following Up After Sessions

The Problem: Students forget what they learned after the session ends.

The Fix:

  • Review notes within 24 hours.
  • Practice what you learned (even if just for 10 minutes).
  • Book a follow-up session if you’re still struggling.

Example: A high school student in Apex would stop practicing after tutoring sessions. After starting to review notes daily, her history essay score went from 75% to 90%.

Mistake 5: Comparing Yourself to Others

The Problem: Some students get frustrated if their progress seems slower than peers.

The Fix:

  • Focus on your own improvement, not others’.
  • Celebrate small wins (e.g., "I finally understood this concept!").
  • Remember that learning is a journey—progress isn’t always linear.

Example: A struggling reader in Wake Forest felt down because her classmate improved faster. After focusing on her own reading speed and comprehension, she noticed her own steady growth—and eventually passed her state reading test.
