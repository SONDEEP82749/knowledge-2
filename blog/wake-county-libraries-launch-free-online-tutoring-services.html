How Wake County Libraries’ Free Online Tutoring Services Work

Wake County Public Library’s Tutoring & Learning Center is a comprehensive, no-cost resource designed to support students from kindergarten through college. Unlike traditional tutoring services that require subscriptions or high fees, WCPL’s program is fully funded by public tax dollars, making it accessible to every resident.

How to Sign Up: A Step-by-Step Guide

  1. Visit the WCPL Website (www.wakegov.com/library)
  2. Create or Log In to Your Library Card Account
    • If you don’t have a library card, register online (takes ~5 minutes).
  3. Browse Tutoring Options
    • Select your grade level, subject, and preferred tutor type (one-on-one or group).
  4. Schedule Your Session
    • Available daily from 9 AM to 9 PM (some branches offer extended hours).
  5. Join via Zoom or In-Person
    • Receive a unique meeting link via email or text.
    • Alternatively, visit a WCPL branch to use a library computer.

Pro Tip: Book sessions during off-peak hours (early mornings or late evenings) to secure a tutor faster.

8 Actionable Strategies to Maximize Wake County’s Free Tutoring Services

1. Identify Your Weakest Subject & Focus on It

Before jumping into tutoring, assess your academic strengths and weaknesses. Use:

  • Past test scores (if available)
  • Teacher feedback (request a progress report)
  • Online quizzes (Khan Academy, Quizlet, or WCPL’s self-assessment tools)

Example: If you’re struggling with algebra, schedule two weekly sessions with a math tutor instead of spreading sessions across multiple subjects.

2. Set Clear Goals for Each Session

Tutoring is most effective when you have specific objectives. Instead of saying, "I need help with math," try:

  • "I want to master solving quadratic equations by the end of this month."
  • "I need to improve my essay structure for my next English assignment."

Real-World Example: A high school senior in Wake County used tutoring to focus on SAT Math, setting a goal to raise their score by 100 points in 3 months. By tracking progress, they achieved their target and secured a full-ride scholarship to UNC Chapel Hill.

3. Prepare Before Each Session

Tutors are more effective when you come prepared. Before each session:

  • Review past assignments (bring notes, worksheets, or textbook pages).
  • List specific questions (e.g., "How do I balance chemical equations?").
  • Bring any relevant materials (calculators, flashcards, or previous test papers).

Avoid: Walking into a session with no agenda—this wastes both your and the tutor’s time.

4. Use the "5-Minute Rule" for Follow-Up

After each session, summarize key takeaways in 5 minutes. Ask yourself:

  • What concepts did I understand better?
  • What still feels confusing?
  • What practice problems should I work on before the next session?

Example: After a Spanish tutoring session, a middle school student noted:

  • "Learned present tense verbs but still confused reflexive pronouns." They then scheduled a follow-up session to drill those specific pronouns.

5. Leverage Recorded Lessons for Self-Study

Wake County’s tutoring platform includes on-demand video lessons. Use these:

  • Before live sessions to refresh your memory.
  • Between sessions to reinforce learning.
  • For subjects where live tutoring isn’t available (e.g., advanced calculus).

Common Mistake: Only relying on live sessions—recorded lessons help solidify understanding.

6. Join Study Groups for Collaborative Learning

While one-on-one tutoring is valuable, group sessions can enhance learning through:

  • Peer discussion (explaining concepts to others reinforces your knowledge).
  • Shared resources (group members can pool study materials).
  • Accountability (studying with others keeps you on track).

Example: A group of Wake County high school students formed a weekly SAT prep study group using WCPL’s tutoring platform. By comparing notes and taking practice tests together, they all improved their scores by 150+ points.

7. Track Progress & Adjust Strategies

Use a simple spreadsheet or notebook to track:

  • Session dates & topics covered
  • Pre- and post-session quiz scores
  • Areas of improvement

Example Progress Tracker:

Date Subject Goal Pre-Quiz Score Post-Quiz Score Notes
5/15/24 Algebra Quadratic equations 6/10 9/10 Still needs practice with word problems

Adjust your study plan based on trends—if you’re improving in math but struggling in writing, allocate more time to English tutoring.

8. Combine Tutoring with Free Online Resources

Wake County’s tutoring services work best when paired with other free learning tools, such as:

  • Khan Academy (for math and science)
  • Duolingo (for language learning)
  • Grammarly (for writing assistance)
  • Quizlet (for flashcards and vocabulary)

Example: A Wake County college student used WCPL’s chemistry tutoring alongside Khan Academy’s organic chemistry videos to prepare for finals. This hybrid approach helped her earn an A in the course.

Common Mistakes to Avoid When Using Wake County’s Tutoring Services

1. Not Booking Sessions Early Enough

Mistake: Waiting until the last minute to schedule a tutoring session. Why It Fails:

  • Popular tutors (especially for SAT/ACT prep) book up weeks in advance.
  • Some subjects (like advanced calculus) may have limited availability.

Solution:

  • Book sessions at least 1-2 weeks in advance.
  • Set recurring appointments (e.g., every Monday and Thursday).

2. Treating Tutoring Like a Crutch Instead of a Tool

Mistake: Relying solely on tutors without active participation. Why It Fails:

  • Tutors teach concepts, but you must practice to retain knowledge.
  • Passive listening leads to short-term understanding, not long-term mastery.

Solution:

  • Take notes during sessions.
  • Ask questions—don’t just nod along.
  • Apply concepts immediately (e.g., solve practice problems right after the session).

3. Ignoring Recorded Lessons

Mistake: Only using live tutoring and skipping on-demand videos. Why It Fails:

  • Live sessions are limited in time (usually 30-60 minutes).
  • Recorded lessons allow self-paced review without scheduling conflicts.

Solution:

  • Watch 1-2 recorded lessons per week between live sessions.
  • Use them to reinforce what you learned in tutoring.

4. Not Setting Clear Goals

Mistake: Vague requests like, "Help me with math." Why It Fails:

  • Tutors can’t guess what you need help with.
  • Without clear goals, sessions become unfocused.

Solution:

  • Be specific (e.g., "I need help with solving systems of equations.").
  • Track progress to adjust goals as needed.

5. Overlooking Tech Support Options

Mistake: Struggling with the platform and giving up. Why It Fails:

  • Technical issues (like Zoom login problems) can disrupt learning.
  • Some students don’t know how to access resources efficiently.

Solution:

  • Use WCPL’s tech support (available via phone or chat).
  • Visit a library branch for in-person help if needed.
  • Book a "tech troubleshooting" session with a librarian.

6. Not Using All Available Resources

Mistake: Only using tutoring and ignoring free books, e-books, and study groups. Why It Fails:

  • Libraries offer more than just tutoring—they provide:
    • Free e-books (for research papers).
    • Study spaces (quiet areas for focused work).
    • Workshops (on resume writing, interview skills, etc.).

Solution:

  • Explore WCPL’s full digital library (OverDrive, Hoopla, Kanopy).
  • Attend library workshops (check the calendar for events).
