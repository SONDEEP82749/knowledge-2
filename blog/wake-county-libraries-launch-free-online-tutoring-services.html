8 Actionable Strategies to Make the Most of Wake County’s Free Tutoring

Not all students (or parents) know how to optimize their tutoring experience. Here are proven strategies to ensure you get the most out of Wake County’s services:

1. Choose the Right Tutor for Your Needs

Not all tutors are created equal. When scheduling:

  • For math: Look for tutors with engineering or STEM backgrounds.
  • For writing: Prefer tutors who have published academic papers or taught writing courses.
  • For test prep: Select tutors with experience in standardized testing (e.g., SAT/ACT coaches).

Pro Tip: Check tutor reviews (if available) and ask about their teaching style before booking.

2. Prepare a Clear Agenda Before Each Session

Tutors can’t read minds—set clear goals for each session. Example:

  • "I need help with solving quadratic equations—can we focus on the quadratic formula?"
  • "I’m stuck on my thesis statement—can we brainstorm together?"

Avoid vague requests like "Just help me with my homework"—this wastes time.

3. Use the Homework Help Hotline for Quick Fixes

If you have a last-minute question (e.g., "How do I solve this integral?"), use the 24/7 text/email support instead of scheduling a full session.

Example: "I’m stuck on Problem 5 in my algebra textbook—can you explain the steps?" → The tutor will respond within hours with a clear breakdown.

4. Record Sessions for Review

Many tutors allow session recording (with permission). Use this to:

  • Revisit difficult concepts later.
  • Share notes with parents or study groups.

Warning: Always ask before recording—some tutors may have privacy policies against it.

5. Join Group Study Sessions for Accountability

Group tutoring (e.g., SAT prep groups) helps with:

  • Collaborative problem-solving.
  • Reduced anxiety (studying with peers is less intimidating).
  • Cost efficiency (one tutor for multiple students).

Where to find them? Check the library’s events calendar for scheduled group sessions.

6. Leverage Self-Paced Learning Modules

For subjects where you need flexible practice, use:

  • Interactive math drills (e.g., Khan Academy-style problems).
  • Writing workshops (for essay structure and grammar).
  • Flashcard decks (for vocabulary and formulas).

Best for: Students who prefer self-study over live sessions.

7. Follow Up with Tutors Between Sessions

If you’re working on a long-term project (e.g., a research paper), check in regularly with your tutor to:

  • Get feedback on drafts.
  • Adjust your study plan based on progress.

Example: "I finished my first draft—can we review it before my next session?"

8. Combine Tutoring with Library Resources

Wake County Libraries offer more than just tutoring. Pair your sessions with:

  • Free e-books & textbooks (via OverDrive or Libby).
  • Online courses (Coursera, edX—some are library-funded).
  • Printable study guides (available at all branches).

Example Workflow:

  1. Book a math tutor for algebra help.
  2. Borrow a calculus textbook from the library.
  3. Use the homework hotline for quick verification.

Common Mistakes to Avoid When Using Library Tutoring Services

Even the best tools can fail if used incorrectly. Here are major pitfalls and how to avoid them:

1. Waiting Until the Last Minute to Book a Tutor

Mistake: Only scheduling a tutor when an exam is days away. Solution:

  • Book sessions at least 1-2 weeks in advance (popular tutors fill up fast).
  • Set a study schedule (e.g., "I’ll tutor every Tuesday and Thursday").

2. Not Communicating Your Goals Clearly

Mistake: Saying, "Help me with math" without specifying the topic. Solution:

  • Be specific: "I need help with solving systems of equations using substitution."
  • Bring your textbook or worksheet to the session.

3. Skipping Self-Study Between Sessions

Mistake: Relying solely on tutors without practicing on your own. Solution:

  • Do homework independently before sessions.
  • Use library resources (e-books, flashcards) for extra practice.

4. Ignoring the Homework Hotline

Mistake: Only using live tutoring and missing quick help options. Solution:

  • Use the hotline for urgent questions (e.g., "How do I factor this polynomial?").
  • Check responses within 24 hours—some tutors may take longer.

5. Not Following Up on Progress

Mistake: Booking a tutor once and never revisiting the topic. Solution:

  • Review notes after each session.
  • Ask for additional resources if you’re still struggling.

6. Assuming All Tutors Are Equal

Mistake: Choosing any available tutor without checking their expertise. Solution:

  • Look for tutors with relevant degrees or experience.
  • Ask about teaching methods (some prefer lecture-style, others interactive).

7. Overlooking Group Study Options

Mistake: Only using 1-on-1 tutoring when group sessions could be better. Solution:

  • Group sessions are great for test prep and collaborative learning.
  • Less intimidating for shy students.

8. Not Using All Available Library Resources

Mistake: Only using tutoring and ignoring other free tools. Solution:

  • Combine tutoring with:
    • E-books & textbooks (via Libby/OverDrive).
    • Online courses (Coursera, Khan Academy).
    • Printable study guides (available at branches).
