8 Actionable Strategies to Maximize Your Tutoring Experience

Not all tutoring sessions are created equal. To get the most out of Wake County’s free tutoring, follow these proven strategies:

1. Choose the Right Time for Tutoring

  • Morning sessions (7–9 AM): Best for high school students who need help before school.
  • Afternoon sessions (2–5 PM): Ideal for college students balancing work and studies.
  • Evening sessions (6–10 PM): Perfect for working professionals or parents helping kids with homework.

Real-World Example: A Wake County high school senior, Jamie, struggled with AP Chemistry. By scheduling weekly 8 PM sessions, she improved her grade from a C to an A- in just two months.

2. Prepare a Clear List of Questions Before the Session

Tutors can only help if you know exactly what you need. Before each session: ✅ List 3–5 specific problems (e.g., "Help me solve this quadratic equation") ✅ Bring past assignments (if available) ✅ Write down weak areas (e.g., "I don’t understand exponential growth in economics")

Common Mistake: Walking into a session without preparation leads to wasted time and frustration.

3. Use the "Two-Session Rule" for Complex Topics

If a concept (like calculus derivatives or grammar rules) is confusing, break it into two sessions:

  • First session: Ask for an explanation of the core concept.
  • Second session: Practice applied problems together.

Example: A college student, Ryan, was stuck on linear algebra matrices. His first session covered theory, and the second session focused on real-world applications, making it click.

4. Record Sessions for Later Review

Many tutoring platforms allow session recording. If you’re comfortable, enable this feature to:

  • Review mistakes you made
  • Re-listen to explanations at your own pace
  • Share notes with teachers for consistency

Warning: Only record if the tutor permits it—some may have privacy policies against it.

5. Leverage Free Supplemental Resources

Wake County’s tutoring platform includes additional tools, such as:

  • Interactive math games (for elementary students)
  • Vocabulary builders (for ESL learners)
  • College prep guides (SAT/ACT strategies)
  • Career exploration tools (resume templates, interview prep)

Strategy: Spend 10–15 minutes per week on these resources to reinforce learning.

6. Ask for "Homework Check" Sessions

Instead of just getting answers, request a "homework check" where the tutor: ✔ Reviews your work step-by-step ✔ Identifies common errors ✔ Explains corrections in detail

Example: A middle school student, Priya, would submit her science lab reports for review. The tutor didn’t just grade them—she explained how to structure arguments better, improving her writing skills.

7. Use Tutoring for Test Prep, Not Just Homework

Many students only use tutoring for assignments, missing out on test preparation. Wake County’s services include:

  • SAT/ACT practice tests (with score breakdowns)
  • College essay editing
  • Standardized test strategies

Action Step: If you’re preparing for a big exam, block 2–3 tutoring sessions specifically for test prep.

8. Follow Up with Your Teacher

After a tutoring session, share key takeaways with your teacher to ensure consistency. For example:

  • "Today, I learned about parallel circuits—can we practice more problems in class?"
  • "The tutor helped me understand grammar rules—I’ll apply them in my next essay."

Why This Works: Teachers can reinforce what the tutor taught, making learning stick longer.

Common Mistakes to Avoid (And How to Fix Them)

Even with free, high-quality tutoring, some students sabotage their progress. Here are biggest mistakes and how to avoid them:

❌ Mistake 1: Treating Tutoring Like a Crutch (Not Learning Independently)

Problem: Some students only rely on tutors and don’t practice on their own. Solution:

  • Practice problems between sessions
  • Use free online tools (Khan Academy, Mathway)
  • Ask tutors for study strategies, not just answers

Example: A student who only asked for answers but never practiced failed to retain concepts.

❌ Mistake 2: Skipping Weak Areas

Problem: Students focus only on easy topics and avoid their biggest struggles. Solution:

  • Rank your weakest subjects and prioritize them
  • Tell the tutor: "Help me with my hardest topic first"
  • Use the library’s skill-building tools for extra practice

Example: A student who ignored algebra but focused on easy science topics still failed the test.

❌ Mistake 3: Not Using All Available Resources

Problem: Many students only use live tutoring and miss free supplemental materials. Solution:

  • Explore the library’s full resource list
  • Try interactive games (great for younger students)
  • Download e-books on your subject

Example: A student who only used tutoring but didn’t check out science e-books missed out on visual learning aids.

❌ Mistake 4: Poor Time Management

Problem: Students schedule too many sessions and get overwhelmed. Solution:

  • Limit to 2–3 sessions per week (quality over quantity)
  • Space out sessions (e.g., Monday & Thursday)
  • Use weekends for review

Example: A student who had 5 sessions in one week became burned out and forgot what they learned.

❌ Mistake 5: Not Following Up with Teachers

Problem: Students get help from tutors but don’t connect it to classwork. Solution:

  • Share key concepts with your teacher
  • Ask for extra practice in class
  • Use tutoring as a supplement, not a replacement

Example: A student who got help on algebra but never applied it in class still failed the test.
