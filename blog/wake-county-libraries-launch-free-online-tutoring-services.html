How Wake County’s Free Online Tutoring Services Work

Wake County Public Library’s Tutoring & Learning Center is a completely free resource designed to assist students from kindergarten through college. Unlike traditional tutoring services that charge $20–$50 per hour, this program offers unlimited access to qualified tutors in math, science, English, and test prep.

Key Features of the Program

  1. 24/7 Access – Students can log in anytime, making it ideal for late-night study sessions or busy schedules.
  2. Live, One-on-One Sessions – Unlike pre-recorded videos, students get real-time interaction with tutors.
  3. Subject-Specific Expertise – Tutors specialize in AP exams, SAT/ACT prep, college applications, and even career counseling.
  4. No Membership Required – All Wake County residents (and some out-of-county users) can access it without a library card.
  5. Mobile-Friendly Platform – Works seamlessly on phones, tablets, and laptops, ensuring accessibility anywhere.

Who Can Benefit?

  • K-12 Students struggling with algebra, chemistry, or essay writing
  • College Students needing help with statistics, literature, or research papers
  • Adult Learners preparing for GED tests or career certifications
  • Parents who want to support their children’s education without hiring private tutors

How to Get Started

  1. Visit the WCPL Website (www.wakegov.com/library)
  2. Click on "Tutoring & Learning Center" under the "Services" tab
  3. Create a free account (if you don’t have one)
  4. Browse available tutors by subject and time slot
  5. Schedule a session—instantly or in advance

8 Actionable Strategies to Make the Most of Wake County’s Free Tutoring

While the program is easy to access, maximizing its benefits requires strategic planning. Here’s how to get the most out of your tutoring sessions:

1. Identify Your Weakest Subjects Early

Before diving into tutoring, assess your strengths and weaknesses using:

  • Past test scores (if available)
  • Homework errors (track recurring mistakes)
  • Teacher feedback (ask for specific areas needing improvement)

Example: If you consistently struggle with word problems in algebra, focus on math tutors first.

2. Schedule Consistent, Short Sessions

Instead of cramming for one long session, break study time into 30–45 minute blocks for better retention.

  • Weekdays: 2–3 sessions (e.g., Monday, Wednesday, Friday)
  • Weekends: 1–2 review sessions

Why? Short, frequent sessions improve focus and reinforce learning better than marathon study marathons.

3. Prepare Questions Beforehand

Tutors are busy professionals, so coming unprepared wastes time. Before each session: ✔ List 3–5 specific questions (e.g., "How do I solve quadratic equations?") ✔ Bring relevant materials (textbook pages, past assignments) ✔ Write down notes during the session for later review

Pro Tip: Use a notebook or digital doc (Google Docs, OneNote) to organize questions before logging in.

4. Use the "Two-Session Rule" for Complex Topics

Some subjects (like calculus or advanced physics) require multiple sessions to grasp fully. Break them down:

  • Session 1: Learn the basic concept (e.g., derivatives in calculus)
  • Session 2: Apply it to practice problems
  • Session 3: Review common mistakes and take a practice quiz

5. Leverage Test Prep Resources

Wake County’s tutoring includes SAT/ACT prep, but many students don’t know how to use it effectively. Try this:

  • Take a full-length practice test (use official College Board/ACT materials)
  • Identify weak sections (math, reading, writing)
  • Book 1–2 tutoring sessions per week focusing on those areas

Example: If your SAT math score is low, schedule math tutors 3x/week and reading tutors 2x/week.

6. Combine Tutoring with Supplemental Materials

Tutors provide guidance, but self-study is key. Pair sessions with:

  • Khan Academy (free video lessons)
  • Chegg Study (step-by-step solutions)
  • Library e-books (e.g., "The Princeton Review SAT Prep")

Why? This reinforces learning outside tutoring hours.

7. Track Progress with a Study Journal

Keep a weekly journal to monitor improvement:

Date Subject Questions Asked New Concepts Learned Homework Applied
10/15 Algebra Factoring quadratics FOIL method 5/5 problems correct

Benefit: You’ll see progress over time and adjust study strategies accordingly.

8. Ask for Alternative Explanations

If a tutor’s method doesn’t click, politely ask:

  • "Can you explain this another way?"
  • "Are there real-world examples I can relate to?"
  • "What’s a common mistake students make here?"

Why? Different learning styles (visual, auditory, kinesthetic) respond better to varied explanations.

Common Mistakes & How to Avoid Them

While Wake County’s tutoring is incredibly valuable, some users waste time or miss opportunities due to poor habits. Here’s how to avoid the most common pitfalls:

Mistake 1: Treating Tutoring Like a Crutch (Not a Tool for Learning)

❌ Do: Rely only on tutors without self-study. ✅ Fix: Use tutors to clarify doubts, then practice independently.

Example: If your tutor explains how to balance chemical equations, do 10 practice problems afterward.

Mistake 2: Booking Last-Minute Sessions

❌ Do: Wait until the night before a test to book a tutor. ✅ Fix: Schedule weeks in advance to build consistency.

Why? Tutors have limited slots, and last-minute requests may not get priority.

Mistake 3: Not Following Up on Homework

❌ Do: Get help in tutoring but ignore homework. ✅ Fix: Apply tutor lessons to assigned problems immediately.

Example: If your tutor teaches how to write a thesis statement, use it right away in your next essay.

Mistake 4: Comparing Yourself to Others

❌ Do: Feel discouraged if others seem to learn faster. ✅ Fix: Focus on your own progress, not others’.

Reminder: Everyone learns at different speeds—consistency matters more than speed.

Mistake 5: Skipping Review Sessions

❌ Do: Only book new lessons, never revisit old topics. ✅ Fix: Schedule monthly review sessions to reinforce weak areas.

Why? Forgetting is the #1 reason students fail—regular reviews prevent this.

Answer: The program offers expert help in:

  • Test Prep: SAT, ACT, AP Exams, GED

Bonus: Some tutors specialize in foreign languages (Spanish, French) and career counseling.

3. How do I find a tutor for a specific subject?

Answer:

  1. Log in to the Tutoring & Learning Center.
  2. Click "Browse Tutors" and filter by subject.
  3. Check availability (some tutors have fixed hours).
  4. Book a session—you can reschedule or cancel up to 24 hours before.

Tip: If your first-choice tutor is booked, try alternative times or different tutors in the same subject.

4. Can I use tutoring for college-level courses?

Answer: Yes! Wake County’s program supports:

5. What if I don’t have a computer or internet?
