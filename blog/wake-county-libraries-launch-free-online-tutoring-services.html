Strategy 1: Identify Your Weakest Subject & Stick to a Plan

Before jumping into tutoring, assess where you need the most help. Are you struggling with algebra word problems or grammar rules? Wake County’s tutoring platform allows you to track your progress over time, so start by:

Strategy 2: Use the "Two-Session Rule" for Maximum Retention

Research shows that spaced repetition (reviewing material over time) improves memory retention by up to 80% (Ebbinghaus Forgetting Curve). Instead of cramming in one session, try:

  • Session 1: Get a basic explanation of the concept (e.g., "How do I solve a system of equations?").
  • Session 2 (2–3 days later): Revisit the topic with practice problems to reinforce learning.
  • Session 3 (1 week later): Test yourself on the material without notes.

Example: A Wake County middle school student, Mia, was struggling with fractions. She scheduled:

  • First session: Her tutor explained adding and subtracting fractions with visual aids.
  • Second session (3 days later): They worked on real-world fraction problems (e.g., splitting pizza).
  • Third session (1 week later): Mia took a timed quiz and aced it—her confidence soared!

Common Mistakes to Avoid When Using Wake County’s Tutoring Services

While these services are incredibly valuable, some users make critical errors that prevent them from seeing results. Here’s how to avoid the most common pitfalls:

Mistake 1: Not Setting Clear Goals

Problem: Some students book tutoring sessions without knowing what they want to achieve. Solution:

  • Before each session, write down 1–2 specific goals (e.g., "I want to understand how to solve quadratic equations").
  • Ask your tutor: "What should I focus on today?"

Mistake 2: Skipping Preparation

Problem: Walking into a session without notes or questions leads to wasted time. Solution:

  • Review your textbook before the session.
  • List your toughest problems to discuss.
  • Bring any past assignments you’re stuck on.

Mistake 3: Relying Only on Tutors (Not Doing the Work)

Problem: Some students expect tutors to do their homework for them. Solution:

  • Take notes during sessions.
  • Practice problems on your own between sessions.
  • Ask for explanations, not just answers.

Mistake 4: Ignoring the Platform’s Additional Resources

Problem: Many users only use live tutoring and miss SkillSurfer, writing labs, and study groups. Solution:

  • Explore all available tools (e.g., interactive quizzes, essay reviews).
  • Join study groups for collaborative learning.
  • Use the progress tracker to stay motivated.

Mistake 5: Giving Up Too Soon

Problem: Some students only try tutoring for a week and then quit when they don’t see instant results. Solution:

  • Consistency is key—most students see improvement in 4–6 weeks.
  • Celebrate small wins to stay motivated.
  • Adjust your approach if a strategy isn’t working.

Mistake 6: Not Using the Recording Feature

Problem: Many students don’t record sessions, so they forget key concepts. Solution:

  • Enable recording (if available) to review later.
