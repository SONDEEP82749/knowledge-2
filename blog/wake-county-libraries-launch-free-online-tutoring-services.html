8 Actionable Strategies to Maximize Wake County’s Free Online Tutoring

Not all students get the full benefit from online tutoring—some miss key opportunities due to poor preparation or lack of engagement. Here’s how to get the most out of Wake County’s free tutoring services:

1. Set Clear Goals Before Each Session

Before logging in, define what you want to achieve:

  • "I need help solving quadratic equations for my Algebra II test."
  • "I want to improve my essay structure for my English paper."
  • "I’m struggling with organic chemistry—can we focus on reaction mechanisms?"

Why it works: Tutors can tailor their approach to your specific needs rather than wasting time on unrelated topics.

2. Prepare Your Materials in Advance

Bringing the right resources to each session ensures efficient use of time:

  • For Math/Science: Write down problems you’re stuck on, bring textbook pages, or upload scanned notes.
  • For Writing: Have your rough draft ready for feedback, not just the final version.
  • For Test Prep: List weak areas (e.g., "I keep getting word problems wrong in the SAT Math section").

Real-World Example: A Wake County high school senior, Jamie, struggled with AP Biology but only brought his test questions to tutoring. His tutor realized he didn’t understand cell respiration basics, so they spent the first half of the session reviewing concepts—leading to a 20-point improvement on his next exam.

3. Engage Actively During Sessions

Online tutoring is not a passive experience—the more you participate, the more you’ll learn.

  • Ask specific questions (e.g., "Why does this chemical reaction happen this way?" instead of "Can you help me with this?").
  • Explain your thought process (tutors can identify misconceptions faster if you verbalize your approach).
  • Take notes during the session (review them later to reinforce learning).

Common Mistake: Students who only listen without contributing often don’t retain information as effectively.

4. Use the Drop-In Option for Quick Questions

If you’re short on time but need help with a specific problem, the drop-in tutoring room is perfect for:

5. Request Progress Reports & Follow-Up Sessions

Common Mistakes When Using Free Online Tutoring (And How to Avoid Them)

While Wake County’s tutoring program is highly effective, some users waste opportunities due to poor habits. Here’s what to avoid:

1. Treating Tutoring Like a Last Resort

Mistake: Only using tutoring when you’re failing a test instead of proactively for improvement. Solution:

  • Use tutoring before you fall behind (e.g., weekly check-ins for strong subjects).
  • Schedule sessions during midterms to prevent cramming.

Example: *A student who waited until the final week of a unit to get help often missed key concepts and struggled to catch up.

2. Not Following Up on Tutoring Recommendations

Mistake: Ignoring practice problems or study materials suggested by tutors. Solution:

  • Save all tutor notes in a dedicated folder (digital or physical).
  • Revisit weak areas in 1-2 weeks to reinforce learning.

Example: One student was told to practice exponential growth problems but never did. When the same issue appeared on a test, she still scored poorly because she didn’t apply the feedback.

3. Over-Reliance on Tutors Without Independent Work

Mistake: Expecting tutors to do all the work while the student passively listens. Solution:

  • Take notes during sessions.
  • Attempt problems on your own before asking for help.
  • Review material after each session (watch videos, read textbooks).

Example: *A student who only asked for answers but didn’t explain his thought process never truly understood the material, leading to long-term retention issues.

4. Skipping Drop-In Sessions for Urgent Help

Mistake: Waiting for scheduled sessions when you need immediate help with homework. Solution:

  • Use drop-in tutoring for quick questions (e.g., "How do I solve this quadratic equation?").
  • Schedule a follow-up session to dive deeper.

Example: *A student who had a math test the next day but didn’t use drop-in help failed to understand a key concept and scored poorly.
