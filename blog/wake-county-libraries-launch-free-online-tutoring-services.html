How Wake County’s Free Online Tutoring Works: A Step-by-Step Breakdown

Before we explore the tips and tricks for getting the most out of this service, it’s crucial to understand how the program operates.

1. Who Can Access the Service?

Wake County’s free tutoring is available to:

  • K-12 students (public, private, and homeschooled)
  • College students (undergraduate and some graduate levels)
  • Adult learners (GED prep, career training, language learning)
  • Parents and educators (for homework help and lesson planning)

No library card? Don’t worry—many services allow temporary access with a valid Wake County address.

2. What Subjects Are Covered?

The program offers tutoring in:

  • Math (algebra, geometry, calculus, statistics)
  • Science (biology, chemistry, physics)
  • English & Language Arts (reading comprehension, writing, grammar)
  • History & Social Studies (U.S. history, world cultures)
  • Foreign Languages (Spanish, French, Mandarin)
  • Test Prep (SAT, ACT, AP exams, college entrance tests)
  • College-Level Courses (business, computer science, psychology)

3. How to Sign Up: A Simple Process

  1. Visit the Wake County Libraries Website (www.wakegov.com/library)
  2. Click on "Online Tutoring" (usually under "Digital Resources" or "Learning Tools")
  3. Choose a platform (most use Tutoring.com, Brainfuse, or Homework Help Now)
  4. Create an account (some require a Wake County library card)
  5. Select your subject and grade level
  6. Schedule a session (or drop into a live chat)

Pro Tip: Some services offer walk-in tutoring (no appointment needed) during peak hours (evenings and weekends).

4. Key Platforms Used by Wake County Libraries

Wake County partners with three major tutoring platforms:

  • Tutoring.com – Offers 24/7 live tutors for K-12 and college students.
  • Brainfuse – Provides homework help, skill-building labs, and live writing assistance.
  • Homework Help Now – A real-time chat service with subject experts.

Each platform has unique features, so we’ll compare them later in this guide.

8 Actionable Strategies to Get the Most Out of Wake County’s Free Tutoring

Now that you know how to access the service, let’s explore how to use it effectively. Many students (and even parents) underutilize these resources because they don’t know the best practices for maximizing their time.

Strategy 1: Schedule Sessions During Peak Hours for Faster Responses

Tutors are not robots—they’re real people with limited availability. If you’re struggling with a complex math problem, don’t wait until 3 AM to ask for help.

Best times to connect:

  • Weekdays (5 PM – 9 PM) – Many students are busy during the day.
  • Weekends (10 AM – 6 PM) – Tutors are often available for last-minute help.
  • Evenings (7 PM – 10 PM) – Great for college students balancing work and school.

Real-World Example: A Wake County high school senior, Jamie, was stuck on a calculus derivative problem for hours. She scheduled a 7 PM session on Brainfuse and got a response within 10 minutes—enough time to understand the concept before her 8 PM study group.

Strategy 2: Prepare Your Questions Before Logging In

Tutors can’t read your mind—the more specific your question, the faster you’ll get help.

Do this before your session: ✔ Write down the exact problem (e.g., "How do I solve for x in 3x + 5 = 20?") ✔ Attach a screenshot or diagram (if applicable) ✔ Explain what you’ve tried so far (e.g., "I divided both sides by 3 but got stuck at x = 5/3") ✔ Mention deadlines (e.g., "I need this for a quiz tomorrow")

Common Mistake: Asking vague questions like "I don’t get this math problem." Instead, try: "I tried solving 2x² + 5x - 3 = 0 using the quadratic formula but got x = [-5 ± √(25 + 24)]/4. Did I make a mistake?"

Strategy 3: Use the "Skill Building" Features for Long-Term Learning

Most platforms (like Brainfuse) offer interactive lessons that go beyond just answering questions.

How to use them:

  • Math Skills Lab – Practice algebra, geometry, and statistics with step-by-step guidance.
  • Writing Lab – Get feedback on essays, resumes, and research papers from professional editors.
  • Career Center – Take resume reviews, interview prep, and job search quizzes.

Real-World Example: *A college freshman, Priya, used the Writing Lab to refine her sociology research paper. The tutor helped her improve her thesis statement and citation format, which boosted her grade from a B- to an A-.

Strategy 4: Leverage Recorded Lessons for On-Demand Help

Not every student can get a live tutor at their preferred time. That’s where recorded lessons come in.

Where to find them:

  • Tutoring.com’s "On-Demand" Library – Pre-recorded videos on key concepts (e.g., "How to Balance Chemical Equations").
  • Khan Academy Integration – Some Wake County libraries link to Khan Academy’s free video lessons.

Best for:

  • Reviewing before a test
  • Filling gaps in self-study
  • Learning at your own pace

Strategy 5: Join Study Groups for Collaborative Learning

Some platforms (like Tutoring.com) allow you to join virtual study groups with peers.

How it works:

  1. Find a group based on your subject (e.g., "AP Biology Study Group").
  2. Participate in discussions and ask questions.
  3. Share resources (like YouTube videos or practice problems).

Why it’s effective:

  • Accountability – You’re more likely to stay on track when others are counting on you.
  • Different Perspectives – Peers may explain concepts in ways your tutor didn’t.
  • Networking – You might meet future study partners or college classmates.

Real-World Example: *A group of Wake County homeschoolers formed a chemistry study group on Tutoring.com. They took turns explaining reaction mechanisms, and by the end of the month, their test scores improved by 20%.

Strategy 6: Use the "Flashcard Maker" for Active Recall

Spaced repetition is one of the most effective learning techniques, and many tutoring platforms have flashcard tools.

How to use them:

  1. Create flashcards for key terms, formulas, or vocabulary.
  2. Set up daily reviews (e.g., "Review 20 flashcards every morning").
  3. Share your deck with study partners.

Best for:

  • Memorizing history dates
  • Learning foreign language vocabulary
  • Practicing math formulas

Strategy 7: Take Advantage of Test Prep Resources

Whether you’re prepping for the SAT, ACT, or AP exams, Wake County’s tutoring services have specialized help.

What’s available:

  • Practice tests with timed conditions
  • Subject-specific drills (e.g., "30 Math Problems in 45 Minutes")
  • Strategy guides (e.g., "How to Answer Reading Comprehension Questions")

Real-World Example: A junior in high school, Carlos, used Tutoring.com’s SAT prep tools to increase his score from 1100 to 1350 in just 6 weeks. He credited the daily practice questions and timed drills for his improvement.

Strategy 8: Follow Up with Tutors for Long-Term Support

A single tutoring session won’t fix all your learning gaps. The best students build relationships with their tutors.

How to do this:

  • Ask for additional resources (e.g., "Can you recommend a YouTube video on this topic?")
  • Request follow-up sessions if you’re still struggling.
  • Provide feedback on what worked (or didn’t work) in your session.

Real-World Example: *A college student, Emily, struggled with statistical analysis for her psychology research paper. She scheduled three sessions with the same tutor, who:

  1. Explained the concept in her first session.
  2. Gave her a practice dataset to analyze in the second.
  3. Reviewed her work and suggested improvements in the third.

By the end, she understood the material deeply and earned an A on her paper.

Common Mistakes to Avoid When Using Wake County’s Free Tutoring

Even the best resources can backfire if you don’t use them correctly. Here are five mistakes students make—and how to fix them.

Mistake 1: Waiting Until the Last Minute

Problem: Many students cram and then panic when they can’t understand the material.

Solution:

  • Start early—even 15 minutes of daily review helps.
  • Use the "Skill Building" tools to prep before tests.
  • Set reminders for weekly check-ins with tutors.

Example: A student who waited until two days before his chemistry test to ask for help got frustrated when the tutor couldn’t cover everything in one session. If he had started a week earlier, he could have built a stronger foundation.

Mistake 2: Not Engaging Actively in Study Groups

Problem: Some students join study groups but don’t participate, making it a wasted opportunity.

Solution:

  • Ask questions—don’t just listen.
  • Share your own insights (even if they’re wrong).
  • Take notes during discussions.

Example: A student who only listened in a history study group didn’t retain much. When he started contributing, he remembered key facts better and scored higher on quizzes.

Mistake 3: Ignoring Recorded Lessons

Problem: Live tutors are great, but recorded lessons are just as valuable for self-paced learning.

Solution:

  • Watch videos when you’re too tired for live sessions.
  • Bookmark useful lessons for later review.
  • Combine live help with recorded content for deeper understanding.
