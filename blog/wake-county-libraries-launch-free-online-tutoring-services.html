8 Actionable Strategies to Make the Most of Wake County’s Free Tutoring

Now that you know how the program works, let’s explore proven strategies to maximize your tutoring experience and achieve better grades.

Strategy 1: Schedule Sessions During Peak Productivity Hours

Not all hours are equal when it comes to learning. Research from the University of Illinois (2022) shows that students retain information best between 10 AM and 2 PM—a time when focus and cognitive function peak.

How to apply this:

  • Avoid late-night cramming—schedule tutoring early in the morning or mid-afternoon.
  • If you’re a night owl, use the "Pomodoro Technique" (25 minutes of study + 5-minute break) to stay sharp.

Real-World Example: Jasmine, a 10th-grade student in Raleigh, struggled with algebra but noticed she understood concepts better when tutoring was scheduled for 11 AM on weekends. After adjusting her schedule, her test scores improved by 20% in just two months.

Strategy 2: Prepare Questions Beforehand for Efficient Sessions

Tutors are not unlimited resources—they have time constraints. Coming prepared ensures you get the most out of every session.

How to prepare: ✔ List 3-5 specific questions (e.g., "How do I solve quadratic equations?" instead of "Help me with math.") ✔ Bring past assignments or textbook problems you’re stuck on. ✔ Review notes before the session to clarify what you don’t understand.

Common Mistake: Wasting time by asking vague questions like "I don’t get this chapter." Instead, say, "I’m stuck on problem 12—can you walk me through it?"*

Strategy 3: Use the "Feynman Technique" to Reinforce Learning

Richard Feynman, a Nobel Prize-winning physicist, developed a simple but powerful learning method:

  1. Explain the concept in simple terms as if teaching a child.
  2. Identify gaps in your understanding.
  3. Review and simplify again until you can teach it clearly.

How to apply this with tutors:

  • After the session, try explaining the concept back to your tutor.
  • If you struggle, ask: "Can you help me break this down even simpler?"

Real-World Example: Michael, a college freshman, used this method during his SAT prep tutoring. Instead of just asking for answers, he would explain each math problem aloud. His tutor noticed his conceptual understanding improved, and his SAT score rose by 150 points.

Strategy 4: Leverage Recorded Sessions & Resources

Many tutoring platforms (like Khan Academy and Brainfuse) offer recorded lessons, flashcards, and practice quizzes. Don’t just rely on live tutors—use these tools too!

How to use them effectively:

  • Watch recorded lessons before a live session to identify weak areas.
  • Take practice quizzes to test your knowledge.
  • Bookmark helpful videos for later review.

Example: The Wake County Libraries’ partnership with Khan Academy provides free video tutorials for every subject. A high school senior used these alongside tutoring and reduced her study time by 30% while improving her grades.

Strategy 5: Join Study Groups & Collaborative Learning

Some tutoring platforms (like Tutor.com) allow you to join group study sessions. Collaborative learning helps reinforce concepts through peer discussion.

How to participate:

  • Look for "Study Groups" or "Peer Tutoring" options.
  • Ask questions in a group setting to see different perspectives.
  • Teach others—explaining concepts to peers helps solidify your own understanding.

Real-World Example: A group of Wake County high school students formed a weekly study group using the library’s tutoring platform. They rotated teaching each other and saw collective grade improvements of 15-20% across subjects.

Strategy 6: Set Clear Goals Before Each Session

Vague goals lead to vague results. Define what you want to achieve in each tutoring session.

Example Goals: ✅ "Today, I’ll master long division." ✅ "I’ll practice 5 SAT math problems with my tutor." ✅ "I’ll understand the causes of the Civil War by the end of this session."

How to track progress:

  • Keep a study journal to note what you learned and what’s still unclear.
  • Review goals after each session and adjust future plans.

Strategy 7: Use the "Spaced Repetition" Method

Forgetting is natural, but spaced repetition (reviewing material over time) boosts long-term retention.

How to apply this:

  • Review notes 1 day after tutoring, then 3 days later, then 1 week later.
  • Use flashcards (Anki, Quizlet) to reinforce key concepts.
  • Revisit past tutoring sessions to reinforce weak areas.

Study Backing: A 2021 study in Memory & Cognition found that spaced repetition improves recall by 200% compared to cramming.

Strategy 8: Combine Tutoring with Active Learning Techniques

Passive listening doesn’t work. Active learning (engaging with the material) doubles retention.

Active Learning Strategies: ✔ Summarize concepts in your own words after each session. ✔ Teach someone else (even if it’s just explaining to a pet or mirror). ✔ Apply knowledge to real-world problems (e.g., "How would I use this math in budgeting?"). ✔ Use mnemonics or visual aids to remember complex topics.

Example: *A Wake County middle schooler used color-coded flashcards to memorize U.S. state capitals while reviewing history tutoring notes. Her test score jumped from a 78% to a 92%.*

Common Mistakes to Avoid When Using Online Tutoring

Even the best tools can fail you if misused. Here are five mistakes students make and how to fix them.

Mistake 1: Treating Tutoring Like a Crutch (Not a Tool for Growth)

What’s wrong? Some students only ask for answers without trying to understand the process.

How to fix it:

  • Ask "why" and "how" instead of just "what."
  • Attempt problems first before asking for help.
  • Use tutors as coaches, not just answer machines.

Example: ❌ "What’s the answer to this chemistry problem?" ✅ "I tried solving this, but I’m stuck at step 3—can you help me see where I went wrong?"

Mistake 2: Skipping Preparation Before Sessions

What’s wrong? Walking into a tutoring session without notes or questions wastes both your and the tutor’s time.

How to fix it:

  • Review past assignments before the session.
  • List specific questions you want answered.
  • Bring any materials (textbooks, worksheets) you’re working on.

Mistake 3: Ignoring Recorded Lessons & Resources

What’s wrong? Some students only use live tutors and miss out on free supplementary materials.

How to fix it:

  • Watch recorded lessons before live sessions.
  • Use practice quizzes to test your knowledge.
  • Bookmark helpful videos for later review.

Mistake 4: Not Following Up After Sessions

What’s wrong? If you don’t reinforce learning, you’ll forget what you learned in the next day.

How to fix it:

  • Review notes within 24 hours.
  • Practice related problems on your own.
  • Revisit weak areas in future sessions.

Mistake 5: Comparing Yourself to Others

What’s wrong? Every student learns at different speeds. Comparing progress leads to frustration and burnout.

How to fix it:

  • Focus on your own improvement, not others’.
  • Celebrate small wins (e.g., "I finally understood this concept!").
  • Adjust your pace—some topics take longer to master.
