How Wake County’s Free Online Tutoring Services Work

Wake County Public Libraries have partnered with leading educational platforms to offer real-time tutoring, homework help, and skill-building sessions—all completely free for residents. Here’s a breakdown of how it functions:

1. Platforms Available

Wake County libraries provide access to three major tutoring platforms:

  • Tutor.com – Offers live, one-on-one tutoring in subjects like math, science, English, and test prep (SAT, ACT, AP exams).
  • Brainfuse – Provides 24/7 homework help, skill-building workshops, and live writing labs.
  • Rosetta Stone – Focuses on language learning for non-native English speakers and adults seeking career advancement.

2. Who Can Access These Services?

  • K-12 students (public, private, and homeschooled)
  • College students (undergraduate and graduate)
  • Adult learners (career training, GED prep, ESL)
  • Parents and educators (for research and teaching assistance)

3. How to Get Started

  1. Visit the Wake County Libraries website (www.wakegov.com/library) and log in with your library card.
  2. Browse the "Online Learning" section under "Digital Resources."
  3. Select a platform (Tutor.com, Brainfuse, or Rosetta Stone).
  4. Schedule a session or access on-demand help (available at different times based on the platform).
  5. Connect via webcam or chat—no special software required!

4. Key Features of the Service

✅ Live, human tutors (not AI-only—real educators available) ✅ No appointment needed (walk-in help available during peak hours) ✅ Subject-specific focus (math, writing, science, languages, etc.) ✅ Test prep assistance (SAT, ACT, GRE, TOEFL) ✅ Adult career training (resume help, interview prep, Excel tutorials)

8 Actionable Strategies to Make the Most of Wake County’s Free Tutoring

Now that you know how the service works, let’s dive into how to use it effectively. These strategies will help you maximize your learning experience and achieve better grades with minimal effort.

1. Schedule Sessions During Peak Hours for Faster Access

Tutors are not always available 24/7, so timing is crucial.

  • Tutor.com has the most availability (Monday–Thursday, 2 PM–10 PM; Friday, 2 PM–6 PM; Saturday, 10 AM–6 PM).
  • Brainfuse offers 24/7 homework help, but live tutoring is best between 3 PM–9 PM on weekdays.
  • Rosetta Stone is self-paced, but live coaching is available Monday–Friday, 9 AM–5 PM.

Pro Tip: If you need help urgently, use Brainfuse’s "Homework Help" chat—it’s available all day, every day.

2. Prepare a Clear List of Questions Before Each Session

Tutors are there to help, but they can’t read your mind. Come prepared with:

  • Specific problems (e.g., "I don’t understand how to solve quadratic equations.")
  • Past assignments (if you’re stuck on a worksheet).
  • Test questions (if you’re reviewing for an exam).

Example: Instead of: "I’m bad at math." Try: "I’m stuck on problem #4 in my algebra homework—can you walk me through it step by step?"

3. Use the "SkillSurfer" Tool in Brainfuse for Self-Paced Learning

Brainfuse’s SkillSurfer is a database of lessons on topics like:

  • Basic math (addition, fractions)
  • Advanced algebra (polynomials, logarithms)
  • Writing skills (grammar, essay structure)
  • Career readiness (resume writing, Excel basics)

How to use it:

  1. Log in to Brainfuse.
  2. Click "SkillSurfer" under the "Learn" tab.
  3. Search for your topic and watch interactive videos.
  4. Take quizzes to test your knowledge.

This is great for filling gaps before a live tutoring session.

4. Leverage Tutor.com’s "Drop-In" Sessions for Immediate Help

If you can’t schedule ahead, use Tutor.com’s "Drop-In" tutoring:

  • Available 24/7 (but live tutors are most active during peak hours).
  • No appointment needed—just log in and find an available tutor.
  • Best for:
    • Last-minute homework questions
    • Quick explanations of concepts
    • Test prep reviews

When to use it: ✔ Before a quiz (to clarify doubts) ✔ After struggling with a problem (to get real-time feedback)

5. Take Advantage of Rosetta Stone’s Live Coaching for Language Learners

If you’re learning Spanish, Mandarin, French, or English as a Second Language (ESL), Rosetta Stone’s live coaching can help:

  • Practice speaking with a native tutor.
  • Get feedback on pronunciation.
  • Learn cultural nuances (not just vocabulary).

How to access it:

  1. Log in to Rosetta Stone.
  2. Go to "Live Coaching" under the "Help" tab.
  3. Schedule a 15–30 minute session with a tutor.

6. Use the "Writing Lab" in Brainfuse for Essay and Research Help

Struggling with college essays, research papers, or even high school assignments? Brainfuse’s Writing Lab offers:

  • Live editing help (grammar, structure, flow).
  • Plagiarism checks (via Turnitin integration).
  • Topic brainstorming (if you’re stuck on a thesis).

Example Use Case: A high school senior working on a AP English Lit essay can:

  1. Upload their draft.
  2. Get real-time feedback from a tutor.
  3. Revise before submission.

7. Combine Tutoring with Other Library Resources

Wake County libraries offer more than just tutoring. To supercharge your learning, combine tutoring with:

  • Digital databases (Gale, ProQuest for research).
  • Mango Languages (for additional language practice).
  • Khan Academy integration (for math and science).
  • Hoopla & Libby (for e-books and audiobooks on your topic).

Example Workflow:

  1. Find a research topic in Gale Opposing Viewpoints.
  2. Use Brainfuse’s Writing Lab to structure your argument.
  3. Get live tutoring to refine your essay before submission.

8. Track Your Progress and Set Goals

Just like a fitness tracker, you should monitor your academic progress. Here’s how:

  • Keep a tutoring journal (note what you learned in each session).
  • Set weekly goals (e.g., "Improve my SAT math score by 20 points in 4 weeks").
  • Review past sessions to identify weak areas.

Example: A college student struggling with biology could:

  • Schedule 2 tutoring sessions per week.
  • Take practice quizzes in Brainfuse SkillSurfer.
  • Review notes after each session to reinforce learning.

Common Mistakes to Avoid When Using Free Tutoring

While Wake County’s tutoring services are incredibly valuable, some students don’t use them effectively—leading to wasted time and missed opportunities. Here are the biggest mistakes and how to avoid them.

Mistake 1: Waiting Until the Last Minute for Help

Problem: Many students only seek tutoring when they’re failing a test or behind on homework. Result: Tutors can’t undo months of missed learning—they can only help you catch up.

Solution:

  • Use tutoring proactively (e.g., once a week for review).
  • Identify weak areas early (take practice quizzes in Brainfuse SkillSurfer).
  • Set a schedule (e.g., "Every Tuesday at 4 PM, I’ll review math.").

Mistake 2: Not Being Specific with Questions

Problem: Some students vaguely say, "I don’t get this chapter" without explaining what exactly they’re stuck on. Result: Tutors waste time guessing what you need help with.

Solution:

  • Be precise (e.g., "I’m confused about how to balance chemical equations—can you show me step 1?").
  • Bring your textbook or worksheet to the session.
  • Use the "chat" feature first to clarify before jumping into a live session.

Mistake 3: Relying Only on Tutoring Without Self-Study

Problem: A few students think tutoring is a "magic fix" and don’t do their own work. Result: They still fail because they don’t retain information without practice.

Solution:

  • Combine tutoring with self-study (e.g., watch Khan Academy videos before a session).
  • Take notes during tutoring and review them later.
  • Practice problems on your own between sessions.

Mistake 4: Ignoring the Library’s Other Resources

Problem: Some students only use tutoring and miss out on free books, databases, and courses. Result: They limit their learning potential.

Solution:

  • Explore all digital resources (Gale, ProQuest, Mango Languages).
  • Check out e-books related to your subject.
  • Attend virtual workshops (many libraries host free career and skill-building events).

Mistake 5: Not Following Up After a Session

Problem: A tutor explains something, but the student doesn’t reinforce the learning. Result: They forget what they learned by the next class.

Solution:

  • Summarize key points in your own words.
  • Do a quick practice problem right after the session.
  • Revisit the topic in a few days (spaced repetition helps memory).

Mistake 6: Assuming All Tutors Are Equally Skilled

Problem: Some tutors are more experienced than others—not all are experts in every subject. Result: You might get incorrect advice if you don’t choose the right tutor.
