8 Actionable Strategies to Make the Most of Wake County’s Free Tutoring Services

Now that you understand the how and why, let’s dive into practical steps to ensure you (or your students) get the maximum benefit from these services.

1. Choose the Right Time for Tutoring

Not all students thrive in the same time slots. Experiment with:

  • Early mornings (for students who wake up early and need a head start).
  • After school (to reinforce daily lessons).
  • Weekends (for catching up on missed concepts).

Pro Tip: If you’re a parent, track your child’s energy levels—some kids focus better in the late afternoon, while others need an evening session.

2. Set Clear Goals Before Each Session

Vague requests like "Help me with math" lead to unfocused tutoring. Instead:

  • Break down problems (e.g., "I’m struggling with quadratic equations—can we solve 3 practice problems together?").
  • Ask for specific feedback (e.g., "Where am I going wrong with word problems?").
  • Set a timer (e.g., "Let’s focus on 20 minutes of algebra before moving to geometry").

3. Use the Self-Paced Modules for Weak Areas

Not every student needs live tutoring. The self-paced learning modules are perfect for:

  • Reviewing past lessons (e.g., revisiting fractions before moving to decimals).
  • Practicing weak topics (e.g., grammar exercises for ESL students).
  • Preparing for tests (e.g., SAT math drills).

Example: A high school senior struggling with biology cell functions could spend 30 minutes on interactive quizzes before a live session to clarify doubts.

4. Leverage Test Prep Resources for College Admissions

The SAT/ACT prep tools are a hidden gem for students aiming for scholarships. Strategies include:

  • Taking full-length practice tests under timed conditions.
  • Reviewing wrong answers with a tutor to understand mistakes.
  • Using the essay rubric to refine writing skills.

Real-World Example: A Wake County student who used these resources raised their SAT score from 1100 to 1350 in three months, securing a full-ride scholarship.

5. For Parents: Guide Your Child’s Tutoring Experience

Parents play a critical role in ensuring tutoring is effective. Try:

  • Preparing questions in advance (e.g., "What’s the hardest part of this chapter?").
  • Reviewing notes afterward (e.g., "Can you explain what we learned today?").
  • Encouraging consistency (e.g., "Let’s book two sessions this week").

Common Mistake: Assuming the tutor will teach everything. Parents should supplement with textbooks or online resources.

Common Mistakes & How to Avoid Them

Even the best resources can fail if misused. Here are five pitfalls and how to steer clear:

1. Waiting Until the Last Minute for Tutoring

Mistake: Some students only seek help before a test, leading to cramming and stress. Solution:

  • Start early—even one session per week makes a difference.
  • Use self-paced modules for consistent review.

2. Not Setting Clear Goals Before a Session

Mistake: Vague requests like "Help me with science" lead to wasted time. Solution:

  • Be specific (e.g., "Explain photosynthesis step-by-step").
  • Bring questions prepared (e.g., "What’s the difference between mitosis and meiosis?").

3. Skipping Self-Paced Learning

Mistake: Relying only on live tutors can be expensive in time. Solution:

  • Use modules for weak areas before live sessions.
  • Review notes afterward to reinforce learning.

4. Ignoring Tutor Feedback

Mistake: Students who don’t apply tutor advice see little improvement. Solution:

  • Take notes during sessions.
  • Practice what the tutor suggests before the next meeting.

5. Not Exploring All Available Resources

Mistake: Some students only use tutoring and miss test prep, career tools, or ESL help. Solution:

  • Check the full resource list on the Wake County library website.
  • Ask the librarian for recommendations.
