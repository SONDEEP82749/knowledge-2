Common Mistakes to Avoid When Using Wake County’s Tutoring Services

While the service is free and powerful, some users make avoidable mistakes that reduce its effectiveness. Here’s how to steer clear of pitfalls:

Mistake 1: Not Booking Sessions in Advance

Why it’s bad: Tutors are limited in number, and last-minute requests often get no-shows.

How to fix it:

  • Schedule at least 24 hours ahead.
  • Set recurring appointments for consistent help.

Mistake 2: Being Too Vague with Questions

Why it’s bad: Tutors can’t help if they don’t understand your exact struggle.

How to fix it:

  • Be specific (e.g., "Help me solve this chemistry problem: 2H₂ + O₂ → 2H₂O").
  • Show your work if you’re stuck mid-problem.

Mistake 3: Relying Only on Live Tutors (Ignoring 24/7 Tools)

Why it’s bad: Live tutors are not always available, and AI tools can fill gaps.

How to fix it:

  • Use 24/7 tools for quick answers.
  • Book live sessions for deep explanations.

Mistake 4: Not Setting Academic Goals

Why it’s bad: Without clear targets, tutoring feels directionless.

How to fix it:

  • Define a goal (e.g., "Improve my history essay grade").
  • Ask your tutor for a study plan.

Mistake 5: Not Reviewing Notes After Sessions

Why it’s bad: If you don’t reinforce learning, you’ll forget what you learned.

How to fix it:

  • Take notes during sessions.
  • Review them later (use flashcards or practice problems).

Mistake 6: Using Tutoring Only for Last-Minute Help

Why it’s bad: Cramming leads to short-term memory, not long-term learning.

How to fix it:

  • Use tutoring proactively (e.g., weekly check-ins instead of only before tests).

Mistake 7: Not Encouraging Siblings or Friends to Use It

Why it’s bad: Group learning can be more effective than solo sessions.

How to fix it:

  • Study together and take turns asking questions.
  • Compare notes to reinforce learning.
