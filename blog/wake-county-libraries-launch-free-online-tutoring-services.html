How Wake County’s Free Online Tutoring Services Work

Wake County Public Library’s Tutoring & Learning Center is a completely free resource designed to help students from kindergarten through college with subjects like:

  • Math (Algebra, Calculus, Statistics)
  • Science (Biology, Chemistry, Physics)
  • English & Writing (Grammar, Essay Writing, Literature)
  • Foreign Languages (Spanish, French, Mandarin)
  • Test Prep (SAT, ACT, AP Exams, College Entrance Tests)

Key Features of the Program

  1. Live Virtual Tutoring Sessions

    • Students can connect with certified tutors via Zoom or Google Meet in real time.
    • Sessions are scheduled in advance (weekdays and weekends) or drop-in for urgent help.

  2. Self-Paced Learning Modules

    • Pre-recorded video lessons, interactive quizzes, and step-by-step problem-solving guides are available 24/7.

  3. Homework Help & Project Assistance

    • Tutors provide detailed feedback on assignments and help students understand complex concepts rather than just providing answers.

  4. Specialized Programs for Different Age Groups

    • Elementary & Middle School: Focus on reading comprehension, basic math, and study skills.
    • High School: Emphasis on AP prep, college applications, and advanced STEM subjects.
    • College Students: Assistance with writing research papers, statistics, and career readiness.

  5. Accessibility & Inclusivity

    • Multilingual tutors available for non-English speakers.
    • ADHD and dyslexia-friendly learning strategies for students with special needs.

  6. Partnerships with Local Schools & Universities

    • Wake County collaborates with UNC Chapel Hill, NC State, and local high schools to provide highly qualified tutors.

How to Access the Services

  1. Visit the WCPL Website (www.wakegov.com/library) and navigate to the Tutoring & Learning Center section.
  2. Create a free library account (if you don’t have one).
  3. Browse available tutors by subject and schedule a session.
  4. Log in on the day of the appointment and join via the provided link.

8 Actionable Strategies to Maximize Wake County’s Free Tutoring Services

While the program is free and accessible, not all students (or parents) know how to get the most out of it. Here are eight proven strategies to ensure success:

1. Schedule Regular, Consistent Sessions

Problem: Many students only seek tutoring when they’re desperate before a test, leading to last-minute cramming. Solution:

  • Block 1-2 tutoring sessions per week (e.g., every Monday and Thursday) to build consistent progress.
  • For high school students, aim for biweekly sessions to reinforce concepts before exams.
  • Example: A Wake County high school senior used tutoring every other week to improve her SAT Math score from 550 to 720 in three months.

2. Prepare Before Each Session

Problem: Walking into a tutoring session without specific questions or materials wastes time. Solution:

  • List 2-3 key topics you need help with (e.g., "I don’t understand how to solve quadratic equations").
  • Bring your textbook, notes, or past assignments so the tutor can reference them.
  • Example: A middle school student struggling with fractions prepared by writing down three problems he couldn’t solve—this allowed the tutor to target his weakest areas immediately.

3. Use the Self-Paced Modules for Review

Problem: Some students rely only on live tutoring and miss out on reinforcement through practice. Solution:

  • After each live session, complete the corresponding self-paced modules to reinforce learning.
  • Example: A college student preparing for Calculus II used both live tutoring and pre-recorded lessons to master integration techniques, leading to an A on his final exam.

4. Ask for Study Strategies, Not Just Answers

Problem: Students often ask, "What’s the answer?" instead of "How do I solve this?" Solution:

  • Focus on understanding the process rather than memorizing solutions.
  • Example: A 7th grader struggling with geometry proofs asked his tutor, "How do I structure a proof?" Instead of just getting the answer, he learned logical steps, which helped him solve similar problems independently.

5. Leverage Drop-In Sessions for Urgent Help

Problem: Some students forget to schedule sessions and need help last-minute. Solution:

  • Check the WCPL website for drop-in availability (usually 1-2 hours per day).
  • Example: A high school student forgot to schedule a chemistry tutoring session but was able to get immediate help during a drop-in slot, preventing a last-minute panic before a quiz.

6. Track Progress & Set Goals

Problem: Without tracking progress, students don’t see improvement and may lose motivation. Solution:

  • Keep a notebook of topics covered, scores on practice problems, and self-assessments.
  • Set monthly goals (e.g., "Improve my algebra score from 70% to 90%").
  • Example: A Wake County parent tracked her daughter’s reading comprehension scores and saw a 20% improvement in three months after consistent tutoring.

7. Use Tutoring for Test Prep, Not Just Homework

Problem: Many students use tutoring only for homework, missing out on test preparation benefits. Solution:

  • Schedule extra sessions 2-4 weeks before major exams (SAT, ACT, AP tests).
  • Example: A junior in high school used weekly SAT prep tutoring and raised his score from 1100 to 1350 in six months.

8. Encourage Parents to Stay Involved (Without Overwhelming the Student)

Problem: Parents sometimes hover too much, making the student dependent on external help. Solution:

  • Ask the tutor for strategies you can use at home (e.g., "How can I help my child with fractions?").
  • Example: A parent of a 5th grader learned simple math tricks from the tutor and reinforced them at home, leading to better retention.

Common Mistakes to Avoid When Using Wake County’s Tutoring Services

While the program is highly effective, some users make preventable mistakes that reduce its impact. Here’s what to avoid:

1. Not Scheduling Sessions Far Enough in Advance

Mistake: Waiting until the last week before a test to book a tutor. Solution:

  • Book at least 2-4 weeks in advance for test prep.
  • Example: A student who waited until two days before the AP Bio exam found no available slots and had to cram alone.

2. Treating Tutoring Like a Crutch Instead of a Tool

Mistake: Relying only on the tutor without putting in effort. Solution:

  • Do homework before the session and ask for explanations, not answers.
  • Example: A student who just copied answers from the tutor failed to understand concepts and still struggled on tests.

3. Ignoring Self-Paced Learning Modules

Mistake: Only using live tutoring and skipping pre-recorded lessons. Solution:

  • Combine both for maximum retention.
  • Example: A student who only used live sessions found it hard to remember what was taught without reinforcement through videos.

4. Not Setting Clear Goals

Mistake: Going to tutoring without a plan (e.g., "I just need help"). Solution:

  • Define specific goals (e.g., "I want to improve my algebra score by 20%").
  • Example: A student who didn’t track progress didn’t realize they were improving until the final exam.

5. Being Passive During Sessions

Mistake: Not asking questions or letting the tutor do all the work. Solution:

  • Engage actively—ask for examples, explanations, and practice problems.
  • Example: A student who just listened instead of participating didn’t internalize the concepts.

6. Not Using Tutoring for Long-Term Learning

Mistake: Only using tutoring when behind instead of for continuous improvement. Solution:

  • Use it proactively—even if you’re doing well, tutoring can take you to the next level.
  • Example: A student who only used tutoring when failing missed out on mastering advanced topics.

7. Overlooking the Library’s Additional Resources

Mistake: Thinking tutoring is the only help available. Solution:

  • Explore other WCPL resources, such as:
    • Online databases (e.g., Gale, Britannica School)
    • Free coding and tech courses (via Codecademy)
    • Book clubs and writing workshops
  • Example: A student who only used tutoring didn’t discover free coding classes until a parent suggested checking the library’s full catalog.

8. Not Communicating with Tutors About Weak Areas

Mistake: Not telling the tutor what specific topics you struggle with. Solution:

  • Be honest about your weaknesses so the tutor can tailor sessions.
  • Example: A student who didn’t mention their fear of word problems kept getting frustrated until the tutor adjusted the approach.
