How Wake County’s Free Online Tutoring Services Work

Wake County Public Library’s tutoring program is designed to be user-friendly, flexible, and accessible to anyone with an internet connection. Here’s a breakdown of its key features:

1. Platform & Accessibility

The tutoring services are hosted on Tutoring.com, a trusted platform used by thousands of libraries nationwide. Students can access it 24/7 via:

  • Desktop computers at any Wake County library branch
  • Mobile devices (iOS/Android) through the library’s app or website
  • Laptops/tablets borrowed from the library’s Tech Connect program

2. Subject & Grade Coverage

The program supports K-12 students across core subjects, including:

  • Math (Algebra, Geometry, Calculus)
  • Science (Biology, Chemistry, Physics)
  • English/Language Arts (Grammar, Essay Writing, Literature)
  • History & Social Studies
  • Foreign Languages (Spanish, French, Mandarin)
  • Test Prep (SAT, ACT, AP Exams)

For higher education students, college-level tutoring is available in subjects like Statistics, Programming (Python, Java), and Economics.

3. Types of Tutoring Available

Wake County offers three main tutoring formats:

  • Live One-on-One Tutoring – Real-time, personalized sessions with certified tutors.
  • Drop-In Help – Students can ask quick questions without a scheduled appointment.
  • Homework Help – Tutors assist with specific assignments, breaking down complex problems.

4. How to Sign Up

Getting started is simple:

  1. Visit the WCPL website (www.wakegov.com/library) and click on "Online Tutoring."
  2. Create a free account using your library card (or sign up for a digital card if you don’t have one).
  3. Browse available tutors by subject and grade level.
  4. Schedule a session or join a drop-in chat.

No appointment is required for drop-in help, making it ideal for last-minute study sessions.

5. Who Can Use It?

  • Wake County residents (students, parents, teachers)
  • Non-residents (some libraries offer reciprocal access)
  • Adult learners (GED prep, career training, language learning)

8 Actionable Strategies to Maximize Wake County’s Free Online Tutoring

While the service is free and easy to access, not everyone uses it to its full potential. Here are 8 proven strategies to get the most out of Wake County’s tutoring program:

1. Schedule Sessions During Peak Productivity Hours

Tutors are available 24/7, but some times are more effective than others.

  • Best times for focus: Early mornings (6–9 AM) or late evenings (8–11 PM) when distractions are minimal.
  • Avoid rush hours (3–6 PM) when tutors may be busier with other students.

Pro Tip: Use the "Book Now" button to secure a tutor during off-peak hours for undivided attention.

2. Prepare a Clear List of Questions Before Each Session

Tutors can only help if you know exactly what you need. Before logging in:

  • List 3–5 specific problems you’re struggling with.
  • Bring your textbook or notes (if working on paper).
  • Write down past attempts at solving the problem—this helps tutors identify where you’re stuck.

Example: ❌ "I don’t get this math problem." ✅ "I tried solving for x in this quadratic equation, but I keep getting -3 instead of the answer key’s 5. Can you walk me through the steps?"

3. Use Drop-In Help for Quick Doubts (Not Deep Dives)

Drop-in tutoring is perfect for short, urgent questions but not ideal for in-depth learning.

  • Good for: Quick grammar checks, basic math operations, vocabulary definitions.
  • Not ideal for: Complex calculus proofs or essay revisions (schedule a one-on-one instead).

When to use Drop-In:

  • "What’s the past tense of ‘go’?"
  • "How do I solve 12x + 5 = 29?"

4. Record Sessions for Future Reference

Many students forget key concepts after a tutoring session. Enable recording (if available) to:

  • Review mistakes you made.
  • Replay explanations at your own pace.
  • Share notes with friends or parents.

How to Record (if supported):

  1. Check if your tutor offers recording (some platforms allow this).
  2. Ask politely: "Is it possible to record this session for my notes?"
  3. Save the recording in a dedicated study folder on your device.

5. Combine Tutoring with Study Groups

Online tutoring works best when paired with active learning.

  • Form a study group with classmates and use tutoring for group problem-solving.
  • Take turns asking questions to reinforce understanding.
  • Use the "Whiteboard" feature (if available) to collaborate in real time.

Example: Three friends struggling with AP Biology can:

  1. Schedule a group tutoring session.
  2. Take turns explaining concepts to each other.
  3. Use the tutor to clarify doubts when stuck.

6. Leverage Tutoring for Test Prep (SAT, ACT, AP Exams)

Wake County’s tutoring is especially valuable for standardized tests, where small mistakes can cost big points.

  • Practice full-length sections with a tutor’s guidance.
  • Review common errors (e.g., misplaced negative signs in math).
  • Simulate test conditions by timing yourself with a tutor’s feedback.

SAT/ACT Strategy:

  • Week 1: Focus on math word problems with a tutor.
  • Week 2: Work on reading comprehension with essay feedback.
  • Week 3: Take a full practice test with a tutor reviewing mistakes.

7. Use Tutoring for Career & Adult Learning

Wake County’s program isn’t just for students—adults can benefit too.

  • GED prep: Tutors can help with math and writing sections.
  • Career training: Learn Excel, Python, or project management basics.
  • Language learning: Get conversational practice in Spanish, French, etc.

Example: A 30-year-old nurse wants to transition into IT support. She can use tutoring to:

  • Learn basic Python scripting for automation.
  • Practice troubleshooting network issues with a tutor.
  • Prepare for certification exams like CompTIA A+.

8. Follow Up with Independent Practice

Tutoring is a tool, not a crutch. After each session:

  • Reattempt the problem without help.
  • Explain the concept aloud to reinforce memory.
  • Create flashcards for key formulas or vocabulary.

Example: After a chemistry tutor explains molarity calculations, the student should:

  1. Solve 3–5 practice problems alone.
  2. Teach the concept to a friend (or record a voice memo).
  3. Review mistakes in the next session.

Common Mistakes to Avoid When Using Online Tutoring

While Wake County’s program is highly effective, some users make preventable mistakes that limit its benefits. Here’s how to avoid them:

Mistake 1: Treating Tutoring Like a Substitute for Study

What to Avoid:

  • Relying solely on tutors without putting in effort.
  • Skipping practice after sessions.

Solution:

  • Treat tutoring as a supplement, not a replacement for independent work.
  • Follow up with practice problems (use Khan Academy or textbook exercises).

Mistake 2: Not Being Specific with Questions

What to Avoid:

  • Vague questions like:
    • "I don’t get this chapter."
    • "This is too hard."

Solution:

  • Be precise:
    • "Can you explain how to balance chemical equations?"
    • "I tried solving this problem but got stuck at step 3—what did I miss?"

Mistake 3: Ignoring Drop-In Help for Urgent Needs

What to Avoid:

  • Waiting until the last minute to ask for help.
  • Assuming one-on-one is always necessary (Drop-In is faster for quick fixes).

Solution:

  • Use Drop-In for:
    • Grammar checks
    • Basic math operations
    • Vocabulary definitions
  • Schedule one-on-one for:
    • Complex problems
    • Essay revisions
    • Test prep strategies

Mistake 4: Not Recording or Taking Notes

What to Avoid:

  • Assuming you’ll remember everything after the session.
  • Not reviewing mistakes later.

Solution:

  • Enable recording (if available).
  • Take handwritten or digital notes during sessions.
  • Revisit notes before exams.

Mistake 5: Overloading Tutors with Too Many Questions

What to Avoid:

  • Asking 10 questions in one session without focusing.
  • Dominating the session and not letting the tutor guide you.

Solution:

  • Prioritize 2–3 key questions per session.
  • Let the tutor lead—they may suggest a different approach.
  • Schedule multiple short sessions if needed.

Mistake 6: Not Using Tutoring for Long-Term Learning

What to Avoid:

  • Only using tutoring for homework help, not concept mastery.
  • Not applying lessons to new problems.

Solution:

  • Ask tutors for practice problems beyond your homework.
  • Explain concepts aloud after sessions to reinforce memory.
  • Use tutoring to prepare for future challenges (e.g., college-level math).

Mistake 7: Forgetting About the Library’s Other Resources

What to Avoid:

  • Only using tutoring and ignoring other library tools.
  • Not exploring free e-books, databases, or study guides.

Solution:

  • Combine tutoring with:
    • Khan Academy (for math/science)
    • Gale in Context (for research papers)
    • Libby/OverDrive (for e-books)
  • Ask librarians for recommendations on study aids.
