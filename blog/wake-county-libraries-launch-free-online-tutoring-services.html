8 Actionable Strategies to Get the Most Out of Wake County’s Free Tutoring

Now that you know how the service works, let’s dive into practical tips to ensure you (or your child) maximizes its potential.

Strategy 1: Schedule Consistent Tutoring Sessions

Problem: Many students wait until the last minute before exams or assignments, leading to rushed, ineffective sessions. Solution:

  • Block 30–60 minutes weekly for tutoring, even if you’re not struggling.
  • Use the "Pomodoro Technique" (25 minutes of focused work + 5-minute tutoring review).
  • Example: A high school senior in AP Biology scheduled biweekly 45-minute sessions to stay ahead of complex topics like cell respiration and DNA replication.

Strategy 2: Prepare Questions Beforehand

Problem: Tutors can’t read minds—if you don’t know exactly what you need help with, the session will be inefficient. Solution:

  • List 2–3 specific questions before logging in (e.g., "How do I solve quadratic equations?" instead of "I don’t get math.").
  • Bring homework problems and highlight where you’re stuck.
  • Real-world example: A middle schooler struggling with fractions prepared by writing down:

    "Why does 1/2 + 1/4 equal 3/4 instead of 2/6? Can you explain it like I’m 10?"

Strategy 3: Use the Writing Lab for Long-Term Improvement

Problem: Many students only use tutors for last-minute fixes on essays, missing out on long-term writing skills. Solution:

  • Submit drafts early (even if incomplete) for structured feedback.
  • Ask for:
    • Thesis clarity
    • Grammar & punctuation fixes
    • Argument strength (for persuasive essays)
  • Example: A college freshman in English 101 submitted three drafts before the final paper, improving his GPA from a C+ to an A- in just one semester.

Strategy 4: Leverage Flashcards for Retention

Problem: Passive tutoring (just asking questions) doesn’t reinforce memory. Solution:

  • Use Tutor.com’s flashcard tool to create custom study sets.
  • Example topics:
    • Chemistry: Balancing chemical equations
    • History: Key dates in the American Revolution
    • Math: Trigonometric identities
  • A student in AP Chemistry used flashcards to memorize periodic trends, boosting her test score from a 78% to 92%.

Strategy 5: Record Sessions for Review

Problem: Some students forget key explanations after the session ends. Solution:

  • Enable the "record session" feature (available for logged-in users).
  • Review recordings later to reinforce concepts.
  • Example: A community college student taking statistics recorded his tutor’s explanation of standard deviation, allowing him to understand the formula better on his own.

Strategy 6: Use Tutors for Test Prep (Not Just Homework)

Problem: Many students only seek help when behind on assignments, missing out on proactive test prep. Solution:

  • Book a tutor 1–2 weeks before exams to:
    • Practice sample questions
    • Identify weak areas
    • Get test-taking strategies
  • Example: A high school junior preparing for the SAT used tutors to master algebra word problems, increasing his math score by 150 points.

Strategy 7: Involve Parents in the Process (For Younger Students)

Problem: Parents often don’t know how to guide their kids in online tutoring. Solution:

  • Sit in on sessions (if comfortable) to understand the tutor’s approach.
  • Ask the tutor:
    • "What’s the best way to explain this to a 7th grader?"
    • "Are there online resources you recommend?"
  • Example: A parent of a 5th grader struggling with long division attended a session and learned visual aids (like base-10 blocks) that made the concept click for her child.

Strategy 8: Combine Tutoring with Library Resources

Problem: Some students rely only on tutors without using additional library tools. Solution:

  • Pair tutoring with:
    • Online databases (e.g., Gale in Context: Opposing Viewpoints for research papers)
    • E-books & audiobooks (for reading practice)
    • Career guides (for college applications)
  • Example: A high school senior used tutors for college essay editing while accessing Wake County’s college prep guides to narrow down her school list.
