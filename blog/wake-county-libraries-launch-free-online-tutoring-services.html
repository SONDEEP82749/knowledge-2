How Wake County’s Free Online Tutoring Services Work: A Step-by-Step Guide

Wake County Libraries have partnered with leading tutoring platforms to provide a seamless, user-friendly experience. Here’s how it works:

1. Accessing the Tutoring Portal

To begin, you must:

  • Have a valid Wake County Public Library card (or apply for one online).
  • Visit the Wake County Libraries Tutoring Page (or use the mobile app).
  • Log in with your library card number and PIN.

Pro Tip: If you don’t have a library card, digital passes are available for residents who can’t visit in person.

2. Choosing Your Subject & Grade Level

Once logged in, you’ll see a dashboard with options to select:

  • Subject (Math, Science, English, History, etc.)
  • Grade Level (Elementary, Middle School, High School, College Prep)
  • Specialized Help (SAT/ACT prep, college essay writing, career counseling)

Example: A 10th-grade student struggling with algebra can filter for "High School Math" and see available tutors specializing in pre-calculus and geometry.

3. Scheduling a Session (Live or On-Demand)

Wake County offers two tutoring formats:

  • Live 1-on-1 Tutoring (real-time video chat with a tutor)
  • On-Demand Video Lessons (pre-recorded tutorials for self-paced learning)

Live Tutoring:

  • Available Monday–Friday, 3 PM–9 PM (with extended hours on weekends).
  • Sessions last 30–60 minutes (no long-term commitments).
  • Tutors are certified educators with expertise in their fields.

On-Demand:

  • Accessible 24/7 via the library’s digital platform.
  • Includes interactive quizzes and progress tracking.

4. Using the Virtual Whiteboard & Tools

During live sessions, tutors provide:

  • Real-time problem-solving (math, science, writing assignments).
  • Interactive whiteboard for visual learners.
  • Homework help & test prep (with practice questions).

Example: A college-bound student preparing for the SAT can use the whiteboard to work through math word problems while the tutor explains strategies.

5. Tracking Progress & Getting Reports

After each session, you’ll receive:

  • Session summaries (what was covered, areas for improvement).
  • Progress reports (if using multiple sessions).
  • Recommendations for further study (books, online resources).

Pro Tip: Parents can monitor their child’s progress via the parent portal, ensuring accountability.

8 Actionable Strategies to Get the Most Out of Wake County’s Free Tutoring

Now that you know how the service works, let’s explore how to use it effectively.

1. Set Clear Goals Before Each Session

Don’t just show up—define what you want to achieve.

  • Example: Instead of saying, "I need help with math," try:
    • "I want to master quadratic equations before my next test."
    • "I need to improve my essay structure for my college application."

Why it works: Tutors can tailor their approach to your specific needs rather than wasting time on unrelated topics.

2. Prepare Your Materials in Advance

Bringing the right resources ensures efficient use of time.

  • For math/science: Have your textbook, notebook, and recent assignments.
  • For writing: Bring drafts of essays or research papers.
  • For test prep: List specific questions you’re struggling with.

Example: A high school senior preparing for the ACT should:

  • List 5 math problems they keep getting wrong.
  • Bring their practice test scores to discuss weak areas.

3. Use the On-Demand Library for Self-Paced Learning

Not every student needs live help—on-demand videos are perfect for:

  • Reviewing concepts before a test.
  • Filling in knowledge gaps independently.
  • Revisiting tough topics after a live session.

Pro Tip: Bookmark key on-demand lessons (e.g., "How to Solve Systems of Equations") for later reference.

4. Ask Specific Questions (Not Just "Help Me")

Vague requests lead to unfocused sessions. Instead of: ❌ "Can you help me with math?" Try: ✅ "I don’t understand how to solve for x in this inequality. Can you walk me through it step-by-step?"

Why it works: Tutors can pinpoint exactly where you’re stuck and provide targeted guidance.

5. Take Notes & Review After Each Session

Your brain doesn’t retain everything in one sitting—active recall is key.

  • Summarize key takeaways in your own words.
  • Revisit notes before the next session to reinforce learning.
  • Apply new skills immediately (e.g., solve a similar problem on your own).

Example: After a science tutoring session on cell biology, a student should:

  • Write a 1-paragraph summary of mitochondria’s role.
  • Draw a diagram of cell organelles to visualize concepts.

6. Leverage Parent/Guardian Portals (If Applicable)

Parents can:

  • Track session history (what was covered, tutor notes).
  • Receive progress reports (grades, areas needing improvement).
  • Schedule recurring sessions for consistent support.

Example: A parent of an elementary student can:

  • Check if their child is mastering reading comprehension.
  • Request additional writing practice if needed.

7. Combine Tutoring with Other Library Resources

Wake County Libraries offer more than just tutoring—combine it with:

  • Free online courses (Coursera, Khan Academy partnerships).
  • E-books & audiobooks (for supplementary reading).
  • Career counseling (for adults seeking job training).

Example: A working adult taking college courses can:

  • Use tutoring for math homework.
  • Borrow textbooks via OverDrive.
  • Attend free career workshops on resume writing.

8. Give Feedback to Improve the Service

Your input helps shape future improvements.

  • After a session, rate the tutor’s effectiveness.
  • Suggest new subjects or time slots if needed.
  • Report technical issues (e.g., poor video quality).

Why it matters: Libraries adjust services based on user feedback, ensuring they meet real needs.

Common Mistakes to Avoid When Using Wake County’s Tutoring

While the service is free and highly effective, some users make preventable mistakes that reduce its impact.

Mistake 1: Not Preparing Before the Session

What happens? Tutors spend time explaining basics you already know. How to fix it:

  • Review past assignments before the session.
  • List specific questions you want answered.

Mistake 2: Treating Tutoring Like a Crutch (Not a Tool for Learning)

What happens? You rely on the tutor to do your work instead of understanding it. How to fix it:

  • Try solving problems first, then ask for help.
  • Ask "why" not just "how" to deepen understanding.

Mistake 3: Skipping On-Demand Resources

What happens? You miss out on self-paced learning that fits your schedule. How to fix it:

  • Use on-demand videos for reviewing tough topics.
  • Bookmark key lessons for later reference.

Mistake 4: Not Tracking Progress

What happens? You don’t see improvement over time. How to fix it:

  • Keep a study journal of what you learned.
  • Review progress reports after each session.

Mistake 5: Ignoring Parent/Guardian Portals (If Applicable)

What happens? Parents miss updates on their child’s progress. How to fix it:

  • Log in to the parent portal weekly.
  • Set reminders for recurring sessions.
