How Wake County’s Free Online Tutoring Works: A Step-by-Step Breakdown

Wake County’s Tutor.com platform is designed to be user-friendly, with minimal setup required. Here’s how it functions:

1. Accessing the Service

  • Website: Wake County Libraries Tutor.com Portal
  • Mobile App: Available on iOS and Android (search for "Tutor.com" in app stores)
  • Library Card Required: You must have an active Wake County Public Library card (free for residents).

Pro Tip: If you don’t have a library card, sign up online in just 5 minutes via the WCPL website.

2. Choosing a Subject & Grade Level

The platform supports:

  • Elementary (K-5): Reading, writing, basic math

Tutors provide: ✔ Real-time problem-solving (e.g., solving a quadratic equation together) ✔ Voice and text chat for clear communication ✔ File sharing (uploading PDFs, documents, or scanned work)

Common Mistakes When Using Online Tutoring (And How to Avoid Them)

1. Not Being Specific About Your Question

Mistake: "I don’t get this math problem." Problem: Tutors can’t guess what you need help with. Solution:

  • Be precise: "I’m stuck on question 12—it’s about solving for x in a linear equation."
  • Show your work: Upload a photo or screenshot of the problem.

2. Waiting Until the Last Minute

Mistake: "I’ll just ask for help when I’m failing." Problem: Cramming leads to more confusion and lower retention. Solution:

  • Use tutoring proactively—ask questions before you fall behind.
  • Schedule regular sessions (e.g., every other week for weak subjects).

3. Not Using All Available Resources

Mistake: "I only use the live chat—nothing else." Problem: Tutoring is more effective when combined with library databases, practice quizzes, and self-study. Solution:

  • Combine live tutoring with:
    • Practice tests (from the library’s Gale databases)
    • Interactive lessons (e.g., Khan Academy links provided by tutors)
    • Study groups (via the Tutor.com forum)

4. Expecting Tutors to Do Your Work

Mistake: "Just tell me the answer—I’ll copy it." Problem: Cheating leads to long-term failure—tutors won’t help with plagiarism. Solution:

  • Ask for explanations, not just answers.
  • Practice problems together before moving on.
  • Understand the concept before moving to the next question.

5. Not Following Up After Sessions

Mistake: "I got help once and never used it again." Problem: One session isn’t enough—learning is cumulative. Solution:

  • Review notes after each session.
  • Practice similar problems on your own.
  • Schedule follow-ups to check progress.

6. Ignoring the Forum for General Advice

Mistake: "I’ll just ask tutors everything—I don’t need the forum." Problem: The community forum has valuable insights from other students. Solution:

  • Ask for study tips (e.g., "What’s the best way to memorize vocabulary?").
  • Share your own advice (helps build a supportive learning community).

7. Not Using the Virtual Whiteboard Effectively

Mistake: "I’ll just describe the problem—I don’t need the whiteboard." Problem: Visual learning helps—tutors can draw graphs, show steps, or explain diagrams. Solution:

  • Use the whiteboard for:
    • Math problems (e.g., plotting functions)
    • Science experiments (e.g., labeling diagrams)
    • Writing essays (e.g., outlining arguments)
