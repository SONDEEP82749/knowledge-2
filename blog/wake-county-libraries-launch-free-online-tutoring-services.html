8 Actionable Strategies to Make the Most of Wake County’s Free Tutoring Services

Now that you understand the scope and benefits of the program, let’s dive into practical ways to maximize its potential.

1. Schedule Tutoring Sessions Strategically

Problem: Many students wait until the last minute, leading to rushed sessions. Solution:

  • Break down assignments into smaller chunks and schedule short, focused sessions (30-45 minutes).
  • For long-term subjects (like calculus or chemistry), commit to weekly sessions to build consistency.
  • Example: A high school senior preparing for the SAT might schedule two 1-hour sessions per week—one for math and one for reading comprehension.

2. Prepare Questions Beforehand

Problem: Tutors can’t read minds—if you don’t know what you need help with, the session will be unproductive. Solution:

  • List specific questions before logging in (e.g., "How do I solve quadratic equations?" or "What are the key themes in To Kill a Mockingbird?").
  • Bring past assignments or test questions to discuss.
  • Example: A middle school student struggling with fractions might ask:
    • "How do I convert 3/4 to a decimal?"
    • "What’s the easiest way to compare fractions like 2/5 and 3/7?"

3. Use the "Two-Tutor Rule" for Complex Topics

Problem: Some subjects (like advanced physics or coding) require multiple perspectives to grasp fully. Solution:

  • If one tutor’s explanation isn’t clicking, ask for a different expert.
  • Example: A college student learning Python programming might first ask about loops, then switch to a tutor specializing in data structures for deeper understanding.

4. Combine Tutoring with Library Resources

Problem: Tutoring alone isn’t enough—students need supplemental materials. Solution:

  • Pair sessions with library databases like:
    • Gale in Context: High School (for research papers)
    • LinkedIn Learning (for career skills)
    • Khan Academy videos (for visual learners)
  • Example: A student writing a history essay could:
    1. Tutor session to refine thesis and structure
    2. Use Gale databases to find credible sources
    3. Library e-books for additional context

5. Leverage the "Tutor as a Study Partner" Approach

Problem: Some students don’t just need answers—they need strategies. Solution:

  • Instead of asking for direct solutions, ask:
    • "How would you approach this problem?"
    • "What’s the best way to study for this test?"
  • Example: A student struggling with essay writing might ask:
    • "What’s a strong hook for a persuasive essay?"
    • "How do I organize my arguments logically?"

6. Track Progress & Set Goals

Problem: Without tracking, students may not see long-term improvement. Solution:

  • Keep a journal of:
    • Topics covered
    • Mistakes made
    • Progress over weeks
  • Set SMART goals (Specific, Measurable, Achievable, Relevant, Time-bound).
  • Example: A student aiming to raise their math grade from a C to a B might:
    • Week 1: Focus on algebra basics
    • Week 2: Practice word problems
    • Week 3: Take a practice test and review mistakes

7. Use Tutoring for Career & Soft Skills

Problem: Many students see tutoring as only for schoolwork. Solution:

  • Career tutors can help with:
    • Resume writing
    • Interview preparation
    • Networking strategies
  • Example: A recent graduate applying for tech jobs might:
    • Get resume feedback from a career tutor
    • Practice behavioral interview questions
    • Learn how to research companies before interviews

8. Encourage Peer Learning & Study Groups

Problem: Tutoring can feel isolating if done alone. Solution:

  • Form study groups using the library’s virtual meeting tools.
  • Example: A group of AP Biology students could:
    • Schedule weekly tutoring sessions together
    • Take practice exams and discuss answers
    • Use library e-books for extra reading

Common Mistakes to Avoid When Using Online Tutoring

While Wake County’s tutoring program is highly effective, some users make avoidable mistakes that hinder progress. Here’s how to steer clear of them:

Mistake 1: Treating Tutoring as a Last Resort

Problem: Some students only use tutoring when they’re failing, leading to crash courses that don’t build long-term skills. Solution:

  • Use tutoring proactively—even for moderate struggles.
  • Example: If you’re confused about a concept, ask for help before it snowballs into a bigger issue.

Mistake 2: Not Being Specific with Questions

Problem: Vague questions like "Help me with math" lead to wasted time. Solution:

  • Be precise: "How do I solve systems of equations using substitution?"
  • Example: Instead of "I don’t get this essay," try "What’s a strong thesis statement for a persuasive essay on climate change?"

Mistake 3: Skipping Homework Before Tutoring

Problem: Tutors can’t help if you haven’t attempted the work first. Solution:

  • Do your best before the session, then ask where you went wrong.
  • Example: If you’re stuck on a science experiment, show your notes and attempts before asking for help.

Mistake 4: Ignoring Supplemental Resources

Problem: Relying only on tutors without using library databases, videos, or practice tests limits growth. Solution:

  • Combine tutoring with other tools (e.g., Khan Academy, LinkedIn Learning).
  • Example: If learning Python, use tutoring for concepts and library coding exercises for practice.

Mistake 5: Not Following Up After Sessions

Problem: Some students forget what they learned in tutoring sessions. Solution:

  • Review notes after each session.
  • Practice similar problems on your own.
  • Example: If your tutor explains how to balance chemical equations, do 5 practice problems before the next session.

Mistake 6: Overlooking Career & Soft Skills Tutoring

Problem: Many students only use tutoring for schoolwork, missing out on career growth opportunities. Solution:

  • Book career tutoring for resumes, interviews, and job searches.
  • Example: If applying for marketing jobs, use tutoring to practice LinkedIn outreach and data analysis.

Mistake 7: Not Tracking Progress

Problem: Without measuring improvement, it’s hard to stay motivated. Solution:

  • Keep a log of:
    • Topics mastered
    • Test scores before/after tutoring
    • Skills gained
  • Example: Track how many math problems you solve correctly each week.
