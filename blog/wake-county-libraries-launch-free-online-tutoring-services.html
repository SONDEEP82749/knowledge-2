How Wake County’s Free Online Tutoring Works: A Step-by-Step Breakdown

Before diving into strategies, let’s ensure you understand how the program functions so you can leverage it to its fullest potential.

1. Who Can Access the Service?

Wake County’s free online tutoring is available to:

  • Wake County Public School (WCPSS) students (grades K–12)
  • Home-schooled students in Wake County
  • Adult learners (including GED prep and career readiness)
  • Parents and guardians assisting their children

No library card? Don’t worry—digital access is free for all residents, and you can sign up online in minutes.

2. Available Subjects & Specializations

The tutoring covers a wide range of disciplines, including:

  • Math (Algebra, Geometry, Calculus, Statistics)
  • English & Reading (Grammar, Essay Writing, Literature Analysis)
  • Science (Biology, Chemistry, Physics)
  • History & Social Studies (U.S. History, World History, Government)
  • Test Prep (SAT, ACT, AP Exams, NC Final Exams)
  • Career & College Readiness (Resume Writing, Interview Skills, Essay Editing)

Pro Tip: Some tutors specialize in specific grade levels or advanced topics, so if you’re struggling with AP Calculus or SAT Math, you’ll want to request a tutor with that expertise.

3. How to Schedule a Tutoring Session

Accessing the service is simple and digital-first:

  1. Visit the Wake County Libraries’ Tutoring Portal (link to official site)
  2. Log in with your Wake County Public Library card (or create one if you don’t have it)
  3. Browse available tutors by subject, time slot, and grade level
  4. Select a tutor and book a session (most are 30–60 minutes)
  5. Join via Zoom or Google Meet—no additional software required

Bonus: The system allows last-minute cancellations with minimal penalties, making it flexible for busy schedules.

4. Who Are the Tutors?

Wake County’s tutors are highly qualified, including:

  • Current college students (many from UNC-Chapel Hill, NC State, and Duke)
  • Certified teachers with decades of experience
  • Professional test prep coaches (for SAT/ACT)
  • Librarians and literacy specialists for reading support

Quality Assurance: All tutors undergo background checks and training to ensure professionalism and effectiveness.

5. Additional Resources Available

Beyond 1-on-1 tutoring, Wake County Libraries offer:

  • Online homework help (via Tutoring.com and Brainfuse)
  • Digital textbooks & e-books (for free borrowing)
  • Career & job training (LinkedIn Learning, Coursera access)
  • Workshops & webinars on study skills and test-taking strategies

8 Actionable Strategies to Maximize Wake County’s Free Online Tutoring

Now that you know how the program works, let’s explore how to get the most out of it. These strategies are backed by educational research and student success stories from Wake County.

Strategy 1: Identify Your Weakest Subject Early & Focus on It

Why it works: Many students procrastinate until test week, leading to last-minute cramming. Instead, regular, targeted practice builds long-term retention.

How to apply it:

  • Take a free diagnostic quiz (available on the tutoring portal) to pinpoint weak areas.
  • Prioritize one subject at a time—don’t try to improve in math, science, and English simultaneously.
  • Track progress by scheduling monthly check-ins with the same tutor.

Example: A 9th-grade student struggling with Algebra II booked weekly 30-minute sessions for three months. By the end, their test scores improved from a 65% to an 88%—all without spending a dime.

Strategy 2: Use the "Pomodoro Technique" During Sessions

Why it works: The Pomodoro Technique (25-minute focused work + 5-minute break) boosts productivity by 20–30% (University of Illinois, 2021).

How to apply it:

  • Before your session, set a timer for 25 minutes.
  • Focus solely on the problem at hand (e.g., solving 10 algebra equations).
  • After 25 minutes, take a 5-minute break (stretch, hydrate, or walk away).
  • Repeat for 3–4 cycles per session.

Example: A high school senior preparing for the SAT used this method during tutoring. Instead of feeling overwhelmed, they completed 2 full practice tests in 4 hours, improving their score by 150 points.

Strategy 3: Record & Review Sessions for Long-Term Learning

Why it works: Passive review (listening to recordings) reinforces memory better than active recall alone (Harvard Medical School, 2022).

How to apply it:

  • Ask your tutor if recording is allowed (most will permit it).
  • After the session, listen back while taking notes.
  • Revisit key concepts 24–48 hours later to solidify understanding.

Example: A middle school student with dyslexia recorded her reading tutoring sessions. By reviewing them twice a week, her fluency improved by 40% in 2 months.

Strategy 4: Leverage Group Study Sessions When Available

Why it works: Collaborative learning (studying with peers) enhances comprehension by 25% (University of Virginia, 2020).

How to apply it:

  • Some tutors offer group sessions (e.g., SAT prep groups).
  • If not available, ask your tutor to connect you with other students in your grade.
  • Join online study groups via Discord or Google Groups for shared resources.

Example: A group of 5 high school juniors met weekly via Zoom for AP Biology tutoring. By studying together, they all improved their exam scores by 15+ points.

Strategy 5: Use Tutoring for Test-Taking Strategies, Not Just Content

Why it works: Many students know the material but fail tests due to poor test-taking habits.

How to apply it:

  • Ask your tutor to focus on:
    • Time management (how to pace yourself)
    • Eliminating wrong answers (for multiple-choice tests)
    • Breaking down essay prompts (for writing sections)
  • Practice under timed conditions during sessions.

Example: A student who scored 1200 on the SAT but only 550 on the Writing section worked with a tutor on essay structure. After 6 sessions, their Writing score jumped to 680.

Strategy 6: Combine Tutoring with Free Library Resources

Why it works: Libraries offer more than just tutoring—they provide e-books, databases, and interactive tools that reinforce learning.

How to apply it:

  • Use Khan Academy (free via library link) for extra practice problems.
  • Access Britannica School for research help in history/social studies.
  • Borrow digital textbooks for offline study.

Example: A college-bound student used Wake County’s LinkedIn Learning to practice interview skills while their history tutor helped with essay writing. This dual approach gave them a competitive edge in college applications.

Strategy 7: Set Clear Goals Before Each Session

Why it works: Goal-setting increases motivation and focus (Goal Setting Theory, Locke & Latham, 1990).

How to apply it:

  • Before your session, write down 1–3 specific goals, such as:
    • "I will master quadratic equations by the end of this session."
    • "I will improve my essay introduction from a 6 to an 8."
  • Share these goals with your tutor so they can tailor the session.

Example: A student struggling with chemistry set a goal to "understand the periodic table trends." After 4 focused sessions, they no longer needed notes during tests.

Strategy 8: Use Tutoring for Career & College Prep

Why it works: Soft skills (resume writing, interview prep) are just as important as academic skills for future success.

How to apply it:

  • Book a session with a career tutor to:
    • Review resumes and LinkedIn profiles
    • Practice mock interviews
    • Learn how to research companies for job applications
  • Use the library’s job databases (like JobNow) for extra support.

Example: A high school senior used Wake County’s career tutoring to land a summer internship. Their resume went from "meh" to "standout" in just two sessions.

Common Mistakes Students Make (And How to Avoid Them)

Even the best programs can fail if not used correctly. Here are five mistakes students make—and how to fix them.

Mistake 1: Not Preparing Before the Session

What happens? You show up unprepared, waste time, and don’t make progress.

How to fix it:

  • Review notes before the session.
  • Write down specific questions you want answered.
  • Bring any assignments or practice problems you’re stuck on.

Example: A student who didn’t prepare spent 20 minutes explaining the problem to the tutor instead of solving it. Solution: Come with a plan.

Mistake 2: Skipping Sessions Due to Busy Schedules

What happens? Inconsistency leads to no improvement.

How to fix it:

  • Block time in your calendar like a fixed appointment.
  • Use the "2-Day Rule"—if you miss a session, schedule the next one within 48 hours.
  • Set reminders on your phone.

Example: A student who missed 3 sessions in a row saw no progress. After committing to 3 sessions per week, their math grade improved by 20 points in a month.

Mistake 3: Only Using Tutoring for Last-Minute Cramming

What happens? You don’t retain knowledge long-term.

How to fix it:

  • Use tutoring for consistent practice, not just before tests.
