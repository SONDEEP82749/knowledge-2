How Wake County’s Free Online Tutoring Services Work: A Step-by-Step Guide

Wake County’s Tutoring & Learning Center is a completely free, 24/7 accessible platform designed to cater to students from preschool to college. Unlike traditional tutoring services that require appointments or hefty fees, this program offers flexibility, expertise, and instant support.

Step 1: Accessing the Platform

To begin, users must:

  1. Visit the Wake County Public Library website (www.wakegov.com/library) and navigate to the "Tutoring & Learning Center" section.
  2. Create a free account using a valid email address (students under 13 may need parental consent).
  3. Select a subject (math, science, English, history, SAT/ACT prep, etc.) and grade level (K-12, college, or adult learners).

Step 2: Choosing a Tutoring Mode

The platform offers three primary modes:

  • Live One-on-One Tutoring – Real-time sessions with certified educators (available Monday–Friday, 10 AM–8 PM).
  • Group Study Sessions – Collaborative sessions for subjects like AP Biology or Algebra II (great for peer learning).
  • Self-Paced Learning Modules – Pre-recorded lessons, quizzes, and interactive exercises (available 24/7).

Step 3: Scheduling & Customizing Sessions

  • Live tutoring requires advance booking (up to 7 days in advance) via the calendar system.
  • Group sessions are drop-in friendly—no appointment needed, but early arrival is recommended.
  • Self-paced learners can jump in anytime, making it ideal for last-minute homework help or exam prep.

Step 4: Maximizing the Experience

To get the most out of the service, users should: ✔ Set clear goals before each session (e.g., "I need help with quadratic equations"). ✔ Prepare questions in advance to avoid wasting time. ✔ Engage actively—tutors are there to guide, not just provide answers. ✔ Use the chat feature for quick clarifications outside live sessions.

The best approach is hybrid learning—using self-paced modules to build foundational knowledge and live tutors for deep dives.

Example: An 8th-grade student watched the Algebra I self-paced videos to understand linear equations, then used a live tutor to work through real-world word problems, making the concept click much faster.

  • Start with self-paced lessons to grasp basics.
  • Follow up with live tutoring for complex problems.

Common Mistakes When Using Wake County’s Tutoring Services (And How to Avoid Them)

Even the best resources can be misused if users don’t approach them strategically. Here are five frequent mistakes and how to prevent them.

Mistake 1: Treating Tutoring as a Last Resort

Problem: Some students only use tutoring when they’re failing, leading to cramming and frustration. Solution:

  • Use tutoring proactively—even if you’re doing well, prep for upcoming tests.
  • Set a schedule (e.g., "I’ll use tutoring every Sunday for review").

Mistake 2: Not Engaging Actively in Group Sessions

Problem: Students sit passively during group tutoring, missing out on collaborative learning. Solution:

  • Ask questions—tutors encourage participation!
  • Contribute answers—even if you’re unsure, guessing helps reinforce learning.

Mistake 3: Ignoring Self-Paced Modules

Problem: Some students only use live tutoring, missing out on structured, on-demand learning. Solution:

  • Combine both—use self-paced for basics, live tutoring for deep dives.
  • Take quizzes to track progress.

Mistake 4: Not Following Up on Advice

Problem: Students get help but don’t apply it, leading to no improvement. Solution:

  • Practice what you learn—do extra problems after each session.
  • Review notes from tutoring sessions.

Mistake 5: Over-Reliance on Tutors for Answers

Problem: Some students expect tutors to do their work, rather than learning independently. Solution:

  • Treat tutors as guides, not answer machines.
  • Try solving problems first, then ask for help if stuck.
