8 Actionable Strategies to Make the Most of Wake County’s Free Tutoring

Now that you know how the program works, let’s explore how to use it effectively. Whether you're a student, parent, or educator, these strategies will help you optimize your tutoring experience.

Strategy 1: Schedule Sessions During Peak Productivity Hours

Not all hours are equal when it comes to focus. Research from the National Sleep Foundation (2023) shows that afternoon sessions (4–7 PM) tend to be more productive for students due to lower fatigue levels.

How to apply this:

  • If you’re a high school student, try scheduling 5–7 PM sessions when energy levels are high.
  • If you’re a college student, late-night sessions (8–10 PM) may work better for cramming before exams.

Strategy 2: Use the "Pre-Session Checklist" to Set Clear Goals

Many students walk into tutoring sessions without a clear plan, leading to wasted time. Before your first session, ask yourself:

  • What specific topic am I struggling with? (e.g., "I don’t understand quadratic equations.")
  • What’s my learning objective? (e.g., "I want to solve 10 problems correctly in 30 minutes.")
  • What resources do I already have? (e.g., textbook, past quiz questions).

Pro Tip: Write these down and share them with your tutor at the start of the session.

Strategy 3: Leverage the "Whiteboard Feature" for Visual Learners

Some students grasp concepts better when they see them written out. Wake County’s tutoring platform includes a shared whiteboard where:

  • You can draw graphs, diagrams, or equations in real time.
  • Your tutor can highlight mistakes and explain corrections visually.

Example: If you’re struggling with geometry proofs, your tutor can draw the figure step-by-step while explaining the logic.

Strategy 4: Record Sessions for Review (When Allowed)

Not all tutors permit recording, but Wake County’s policy allows it (with tutor consent). Recording sessions helps with:

  • Revisiting difficult concepts later.
  • Reviewing weak areas before the next session.

How to request a recording:

  • Simply ask at the start: "Would it be possible to record this session for my notes?"
  • Most tutors will agree if it’s for personal study purposes.

Strategy 5: Combine Tutoring with Supplemental Materials

Relying only on tutoring sessions won’t maximize learning. Pair them with:

  • Khan Academy videos (free for math/science).
  • Grammarly or Hemingway Editor (for writing help).
  • Past exam papers (available on Wake County’s Online Learning Center).

Example: If your tutor helps with AP Biology, supplement with Khan Academy’s AP Bio playlist to reinforce concepts.

Strategy 6: Use the "Homework Help" Feature for Immediate Feedback

Wake County’s tutoring platform offers 24/7 homework help, meaning you can get instant feedback even outside scheduled hours. This is perfect for:

  • Last-minute questions before a deadline.
  • Double-checking answers before submitting assignments.

How to use it:

  1. Upload your homework question via the Tutor.com app.
  2. Get a response within minutes (not hours like traditional email tutoring).

Strategy 7: Join Study Groups via the Library’s Online Community

Wake County’s library doesn’t just offer one-on-one tutoring—it also hosts virtual study groups. These are great for:

  • Collaborative learning (e.g., group SAT prep).
  • Accountability (studying with peers keeps you motivated).
  • Access to guest experts (e.g., former teachers running workshops).

How to find them:

  • Check the "Events & Programs" section on WCPL’s website.
  • Join the Wake County Library Facebook Group for announcements.

Strategy 8: Follow Up with Tutors for Long-Term Support

Some students stop after one session, but the best learners build a relationship with their tutor. Here’s how:

  • Send a thank-you email after each session.
  • Ask for recommendations on study methods.
  • Request follow-up sessions if you’re still struggling.

Example: A Wake County high school senior used the same tutor for three months to prepare for the SAT, improving her score from 1100 to 1350.

Common Mistakes When Using Free Online Tutoring (And How to Avoid Them)

While Wake County’s tutoring program is highly effective, some users make critical mistakes that waste time and money (even though it’s free). Here’s what to avoid:

Mistake 1: Not Setting Clear Goals Before the Session

Problem: Walking into a tutoring session without a plan leads to vague discussions that don’t help. Solution:

  • Before the session, write down:
    • What topic do I need help with?
    • What’s my learning objective?
    • How will I measure success?

Example: Instead of saying, "Help me with math," say, "I need to understand how to solve systems of equations in 30 minutes."

Mistake 2: Skipping the "Homework Help" Feature

Problem: Some students only use scheduled sessions and miss out on 24/7 support. Solution:

  • Upload homework questions outside tutoring hours for instant feedback.
  • Use this for quick checks before submitting assignments.

Mistake 3: Not Recording Sessions (When Allowed)

Problem: Forgetting to save notes or recordings means losing progress. Solution:

  • Always ask: "Can I record this session?"
  • Take handwritten notes during the session.

Mistake 4: Relying Only on Tutoring (No Supplemental Study)

Problem: Tutoring alone won’t replace self-study. Solution:

  • Combine tutoring with:
    • Khan Academy videos (for math/science).
    • Grammarly (for writing).
    • Past exam papers (for practice).

Mistake 5: Not Following Up with Tutors

Problem: Some students stop after one session, leaving unanswered questions. Solution:

  • Send a thank-you email after each session.
  • Ask for recommendations on study methods.
  • Request follow-up sessions if needed.

Mistake 6: Ignoring the Library’s Other Resources

Problem: Tutoring is just one tool—Wake County’s library offers hundreds of free resources. Solution:

  • Check out:
    • Online databases (e.g., Gale in Context for research papers).
    • Digital books & audiobooks (via Libby or Hoopla).
    • Virtual workshops (e.g., resume writing for college apps).
