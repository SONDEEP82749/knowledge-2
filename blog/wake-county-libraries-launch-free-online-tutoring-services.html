How Wake County’s Free Online Tutoring Services Work

Wake County Public Library’s new virtual tutoring hub is designed to be intuitive, flexible, and tailored to individual learning needs. Here’s a breakdown of how it functions:

1. Accessible 24/7 via the Library’s Website

Unlike traditional tutoring centers with fixed hours, Wake County’s platform is open 24/7, allowing students to seek help at any time—whether it’s late at night before a test or early in the morning for homework.

2. Subject-Specific Tutors for All Grade Levels

The program covers:

  • Elementary (K-5): Reading, math, and basic science
  • Middle School (6-8): Algebra, English grammar, and STEM subjects
  • High School (9-12): Advanced math, chemistry, history, and AP courses
  • College Prep & Adult Learners: SAT/ACT prep, GED assistance, and career readiness

3. Multiple Tutoring Formats

Students can choose from:

  • Live one-on-one sessions (via video chat)
  • Group study sessions (for collaborative learning)
  • Self-paced learning modules (for independent review)
  • Homework help hotline (for quick, text-based assistance)

4. No Cost—Just a Library Card

The best part? It’s entirely free for Wake County residents with a valid library card. No hidden fees, no subscriptions—just high-quality academic support.

5. Integration with Popular Learning Tools

The platform connects with:

  • Khan Academy (for math and science)
  • Duolingo (for language learners)
  • Quizlet (for flashcard-based studying)
  • Google Classroom (for seamless schoolwork integration)

6. Bilingual Support Available

Recognizing Wake County’s diverse population, the library offers Spanish-speaking tutors and resources, ensuring language is never a barrier to education.

8 Actionable Strategies to Maximize Wake County’s Free Online Tutoring

Now that you understand how the program works, let’s dive into practical ways to make the most of it.

1. Schedule Regular Tutoring Sessions (Not Just for Crunch Time)

Problem: Many students only seek tutoring when they’re desperate before a test, leading to last-minute confusion. Solution: Treat tutoring like a study habit. Schedule weekly 30-minute sessions to reinforce concepts before they become difficult.

Example: A high school junior in AP Chemistry uses tutoring once a week to review reaction mechanisms, rather than waiting until the final exam. This approach builds confidence and consistency.

2. Use the Self-Paced Modules for Weak Areas

Problem: Some students struggle with specific topics (e.g., fractions in math, grammar in English) but don’t know where to start. Solution: The library’s self-paced learning modules allow students to revisit concepts at their own speed.

Example: An 8th grader who struggles with algebraic equations spends 15 minutes daily on the library’s interactive math module. Within a month, their test scores improve by 20%.

3. Leverage Group Study Sessions for Accountability

Problem: Studying alone can be isolating and unproductive. Solution: Join virtual group study sessions where tutors facilitate discussions and problem-solving.

Example: A group of 9th-grade biology students meets weekly via the library’s platform to quiz each other on cell functions. The tutor provides real-time corrections, making the session far more effective than solo studying.

4. Combine Tutoring with Homework Help Hotline for Quick Checks

Problem: Some students get stuck on small, specific questions but don’t want to book a full session. Solution: Use the text-based homework help hotline for instant answers while saving live sessions for deeper explanations.

Example: A middle schooler working on a history essay texts the hotline: “What’s the difference between primary and secondary sources?” Within minutes, they receive a clear, concise response—saving them from hours of confusion.

5. Request Subject-Specific Tutors for Advanced Courses

Problem: General tutors may not specialize in AP Physics or Calculus. Solution: Ask for subject-expert tutors who can provide advanced explanations.

Example: A student taking AP Calculus BC books a math specialist who breaks down integration techniques in a way that makes complex problems easy to follow.

6. Use the Platform for College & Career Prep

Problem: Many students don’t realize libraries offer college readiness resources. Solution: Take advantage of SAT/ACT prep tutors and resume-building workshops.

Example: A high school senior uses the ACT tutoring service to improve their score by 100 points in just 8 weeks, securing a scholarship for college.

7. Encourage Parents to Monitor Progress Together

Problem: Parents often don’t know how to guide their child’s learning. Solution: Parents can join tutoring sessions (if allowed) or review progress reports to stay informed.

Example: A parent who doesn’t understand algebra attends a family tutoring session with their child. By the end, they learn alongside their teen, strengthening their bond and improving academic support at home.

8. Explore Bilingual Resources for Non-English Speakers

Problem: Language barriers can limit learning opportunities. Solution: Wake County’s Spanish-speaking tutors ensure every student can access help.

Example: A Spanish-speaking 7th grader struggles with English literature. A bilingual tutor explains symbolism in The Catcher in the Rye in both languages, making the lesson far more accessible.

Common Mistakes to Avoid When Using Wake County’s Tutoring

While the program is highly effective, some users make avoidable mistakes that reduce its impact. Here’s how to steer clear of them:

❌ Mistake 1: Waiting Until the Last Minute

Problem: Cramming before a test leads to short-term memorization rather than long-term understanding. Solution: Use tutoring consistently, not just when stressed.

❌ Mistake 2: Not Communicating Needs Clearly

Problem: If a tutor doesn’t know exactly where you’re stuck, they can’t help effectively. Solution: Be specific—say, “I don’t understand how to solve quadratic equations—can you explain the quadratic formula?”

❌ Mistake 3: Ignoring Self-Paced Modules

Problem: Some students only use live tutoring, missing out on interactive practice. Solution: Combine both—live sessions for doubt clarification and modules for reinforcement.

❌ Mistake 4: Not Using All Available Resources

Problem: The library offers more than just tutoring—e-books, online courses, and career tools go underutilized. Solution: Explore everything—for example, if studying for the SAT, use both tutoring and Khan Academy’s free prep courses.

❌ Mistake 5: Assuming Tutors Are Only for Struggling Students

Problem: Some students think tutoring is only for those who are behind. Solution: Tutors can also challenge advanced students with college-level problems or research assistance.

❌ Mistake 6: Not Tracking Progress

Problem: Without progress tracking, it’s hard to see what’s working. Solution: Ask for progress reports or keep a study journal to track improvements.

❌ Mistake 7: Over-Reliance on Tutors Without Independent Work

Problem: Some students depend entirely on tutors without practicing on their own. Solution: Use tutoring as a guide, but practice problems independently to reinforce learning.
