How Wake County’s Free Online Tutoring Services Work: A Step-by-Step Guide

Wake County Libraries’ free online tutoring is designed to be user-friendly, flexible, and accessible—whether you're a high school student struggling with calculus or a college freshman needing help with essay writing. Here’s how it works:

1. Eligibility & Access Requirements

Unlike traditional tutoring services that require expensive subscriptions, Wake County’s program is open to:

  • Wake County residents (proof of residency may be required for library card access)
  • Students enrolled in Wake County Public Schools (WCPSS)
  • Home-schooled students in the county
  • Adult learners (GED prep, career training, language learning)

No income restrictions apply, making it one of the most inclusive free tutoring programs in the U.S.

2. How to Sign Up

To access the tutoring services, follow these steps:

  1. Get a Wake County Library Card

    • Visit WCPL’s website or any Wake County library branch.
    • Bring proof of residency (utility bill, lease, or government ID).
    • Digital library cards are also available for remote access.

  2. Explore the Online Tutoring Platform

    • Log in to Wake County Libraries’ Online Resources.
    • Navigate to the "Tutoring Services" section (often under "Homework Help" or "Learning Support").

  3. Choose Your Subject & Time Slot

    • Available subjects include:
      • Math (Algebra, Geometry, Calculus)
      • Science (Biology, Chemistry, Physics)
      • English & Writing (Grammar, Essay Writing, SAT/ACT Prep)
      • Foreign Languages (Spanish, French, Mandarin)
      • Test Prep (SAT, ACT, AP Exams)
    • Live tutoring sessions are scheduled via Zoom or a dedicated tutoring portal, while on-demand help is available 24/7 via chat.

  4. Book a Session or Use On-Demand Help

    • Scheduled Tutoring: Select a tutor and time slot (typically Monday–Thursday, 3 PM–8 PM).
    • Drop-In Tutoring: Walk-in sessions (if available) for immediate help.
    • Asynchronous Help: Upload assignments for recorded feedback within 24–48 hours.

3. Who Are the Tutors?

Wake County’s tutoring program partners with:

  • Certified teachers from WCPSS
  • College students (undergraduate/graduate) with strong academic records
  • Subject-matter experts (e.g., engineers for physics, writers for literature)
  • ESL specialists for non-native English speakers

All tutors undergo background checks and receive ongoing training to ensure high-quality support.

4. Devices & Internet Requirements

Since tutoring is fully online, you’ll need:

  • A computer, tablet, or smartphone with stable internet (Wi-Fi or mobile data).
  • Headphones (recommended for better audio quality).
  • Basic tech skills (most libraries offer digital literacy workshops for assistance).

Pro Tip: If internet access is an issue, some Wake County libraries provide free Wi-Fi hotspots or laptop rentals for in-person use.

Common Mistakes When Using Wake County’s Tutoring (And How to Avoid Them)

Even the best resources can be wasted if not used correctly. Here are 5 mistakes students make and how to fix them:

Mistake 1: Treating Tutoring as a Last Resort

Problem: Some students only use tutoring when they’re failing, leading to crash courses that don’t build long-term understanding. Solution:

  • Use tutoring proactively (e.g., after every quiz to reinforce weak areas).
  • Set a "check-in" schedule (e.g., "I’ll tutor every other week to stay on track").

Mistake 2: Not Asking Specific Questions

Problem: Vague questions like "I don’t get this" lead to generic answers that don’t help. Solution:

  • Be precise: "I tried solving this problem using [method], but I got stuck at [step]. Can you show me another way?"
  • Show your work (even if wrong) so tutors can guide you effectively.

Mistake 3: Ignoring On-Demand Resources

Problem: Some students only use live tutoring and miss 24/7 chat help. Solution:

  • Bookmark the chat link for quick questions.
  • Use it for:
    • Grammar checks (before submitting essays).
    • Math problem breakdowns (e.g., "How do I factor this polynomial?").
    • Test prep tips (e.g., "What’s the best way to study for the SAT?").

Mistake 4: Not Following Up on Feedback

Problem: Tutors provide great explanations, but students don’t practice what they learned. Solution:

  • Revisit concepts in your study notes.
  • Do practice problems on your own before the next session.
  • Ask tutors for additional resources (videos, worksheets).

Mistake 5: Assuming Tutors Can Do All the Work

Problem: Some students expect tutors to do their homework instead of learning the process. Solution:

  • Treat tutors as guides, not homework helpers.
  • Ask: "Can you explain how to solve this type of problem?" instead of "Just do it for me."
  • Take notes during sessions to reinforce learning.
