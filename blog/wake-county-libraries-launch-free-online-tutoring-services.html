8 Actionable Strategies to Make the Most of Wake County’s Free Tutoring

Now that you know how the service works, let’s dive into how to use it effectively to boost your grades and confidence.

1. Identify Your Weakest Subject & Prioritize Help

Before jumping into tutoring, assess where you need the most improvement. Use:

  • Past test scores (quizzes, exams)
  • Homework completion rates (which subjects take the longest?)
  • Teacher feedback (if available)

Example: If algebra is your struggle, schedule multiple sessions with a math tutor before moving to calculus.

2. Schedule Consistent Tutoring Sessions

Consistency is key—just like gym memberships, tutoring works best when done regularly. Aim for:

  • 1-2 sessions per week for struggling subjects
  • Weekly check-ins for maintenance (e.g., reviewing concepts before a test)

Pro Tip: Use the library’s calendar tool to block time for tutoring, just like you would a study session.

3. Prepare Questions Before Each Session

Tutors are busy professionals, so coming prepared ensures you get the most value. Before each session:

  • List 3-5 specific questions (e.g., "How do I solve quadratic equations?")
  • Bring homework problems you’re stuck on
  • Review notes from class to clarify what you don’t understand

Avoid: Vague questions like "I don’t get this chapter." Instead, say: "I’m confused about the difference between independent and dependent variables in experiments."

4. Use the Chat Feature for Quick Doubts

Not every question requires a full session. The 24/7 chat support is perfect for:

  • Clarifying definitions (e.g., "What is photosynthesis?")
  • Checking answers (e.g., "Is this math problem solved correctly?")
  • Getting study tips (e.g., "How should I organize my notes for a history essay?")

Example: A high school student struggling with AP Biology used the chat to confirm cell respiration steps before a quiz—boosting their score from a C to an A.

5. Record Sessions for Later Review

Many tutors allow session recording (check with them first). If available:

  • Save recordings of key concepts
  • Replay difficult explanations at your own pace
  • Use them as study aids before exams

Warning: Always ask permission before recording—some tutors may have privacy policies against it.

6. Combine Tutoring with Other Library Resources

Wake County Libraries offer more than just tutoring. Pair your sessions with:

  • Online textbooks (via OverDrive or Gale Databases)
  • Interactive learning tools (like Khan Academy or Mathletics)
  • Study guides & flashcards (available in the Digital Collection)

Example: A college student preparing for Calculus II used: ✅ Tutoring for problem-solving help ✅ Khan Academy videos for concept reviews ✅ Past exam questions from the library’s database

7. Join Study Groups via the Library’s Platform

Some libraries host virtual study groups where you can:

  • Collaborate with peers on group projects
  • Practice exams in a structured setting
  • Get peer feedback on essays or presentations

How to find them: Check the "Events" tab on the Wake County Library website for upcoming study sessions.

8. Follow Up with the Tutor for Long-Term Growth

A great tutor doesn’t just help with one problem—they should guide your learning journey. After each session:

  • Ask for additional resources (books, websites, practice problems)
  • Request a study plan for your subject
  • Check in weekly to track progress

Example: A middle school student struggling with fractions had a tutor create a 30-day study plan with:

  • Daily fraction drills
  • Weekly quiz reviews
  • Real-world application problems (e.g., cooking measurements)

By the end of the month, their math grade improved by 20 points.

Common Mistakes to Avoid When Using Wake County’s Tutoring

While the service is free and powerful, some users make avoidable mistakes that limit its effectiveness. Here’s how to steer clear of pitfalls:

Mistake 1: Waiting Until the Last Minute for Help

Problem: Many students only seek tutoring when an exam is days away, making it hard to catch up. Solution:

  • Start early—even 1-2 sessions per month can prevent last-minute cramming.
  • Use the chat feature for weekly check-ins to stay on track.

Mistake 2: Not Being Specific with Questions

Problem: Vague questions like "I don’t get this chapter" waste tutor time and leave you confused. Solution:

  • Break questions into parts (e.g., "Can you explain the difference between mitosis and meiosis?")
  • Show work (if it’s math/science) so the tutor can pinpoint errors.

Mistake 3: Ignoring the Library’s Other Resources

Problem: Some users only use tutoring and miss out on free textbooks, e-books, and study guides. Solution:

  • Explore the "Digital Collection" for supplementary materials.
  • Check out audiobooks if reading is difficult.

Mistake 4: Not Following Up with Tutors

Problem: A single session won’t fix long-term learning gaps. Solution:

  • Ask for a study plan after the first session.
  • Schedule follow-ups to reinforce concepts.

Mistake 5: Assuming Tutoring Replaces Self-Study

Problem: Some students depend entirely on tutors instead of practicing independently. Solution:

  • Use tutoring as a supplement, not a replacement for homework and practice.
  • Apply what you learn in real-world scenarios (e.g., cooking for math fractions).

Mistake 6: Not Using the Mobile App for Convenience

Problem: Some users only access tutoring via desktop, missing mobile-friendly features. Solution:

  • Download the library app for:
    • On-the-go scheduling
    • Push notifications for upcoming sessions
    • Offline access to study materials

Mistake 7: Overlooking Career & Technical Tutoring

Problem: Many focus only on academic subjects, missing career-ready skills. Solution:

  • Explore tutoring for:
    • Coding (Python, JavaScript)
    • Graphic Design (Photoshop, Illustrator)
    • Business & Finance basics
