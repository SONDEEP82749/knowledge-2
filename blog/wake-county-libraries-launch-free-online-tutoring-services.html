How Wake County Libraries’ Free Online Tutoring Services Work: A Step-by-Step Guide

Wake County Public Library has partnered with leading tutoring platforms to offer real-time and on-demand academic support. Here’s how it functions:

1. Accessing the Tutoring Portal

Students (and parents) can access the tutoring services through:

  • WCPL’s official website (www.wakegov.com/library)
  • The "Online Resources" tab under "Education & Learning"
  • Mobile app (available for iOS and Android)

Once logged in, users can browse available tutors by subject, grade level, or even specific topics (e.g., algebra, essay writing, SAT prep).

2. Choosing a Tutoring Session

The platform offers three main types of tutoring:

  • Live One-on-One Tutoring – Real-time video calls with certified tutors (available Monday–Friday, 1–9 PM and Saturday, 10 AM–5 PM).
  • Group Study Sessions – Small group discussions led by tutors (great for collaborative learning).
  • 24/7 Homework Help – AI-assisted tools for quick answers (useful for last-minute questions).

3. Selecting a Tutor & Scheduling

  • Users can filter tutors by expertise (e.g., AP Biology, Spanish, coding).
  • No appointment needed for drop-in sessions, but scheduled sessions are recommended for consistency.
  • Parent/guardian approval is required for minors under 13.

4. Participating in the Session

  • Video chat via Zoom or a built-in WCPL tutoring platform.
  • Shared whiteboard for math problems, diagrams, or writing exercises.
  • File uploads (students can submit assignments for review).

5. Tracking Progress & Resources

  • Session recordings (for review) are available for logged-in users.
  • Additional learning materials (videos, worksheets, practice tests) are provided post-session.
  • Progress reports can be generated for parents and teachers.

Common Mistakes When Using Wake County’s Free Tutoring (And How to Avoid Them)

Even with such a valuable resource, some students and parents make avoidable mistakes. Here’s how to steer clear of pitfalls:

Mistake 1: Waiting Until the Last Minute

Problem: Some students only seek tutoring when they’re failing, making it harder to catch up.

Solution:

  • Start early—even one session per month helps.
  • Example: A student who waited until finals week to use tutoring still failed his chemistry test, while a peer who started in September passed with an A.

Mistake 2: Not Setting Clear Goals

Problem: Without a focused plan, tutoring sessions become unproductive.

Solution:

  • Before each session, write down:
    • "Today, I want to master [specific topic]."
    • "I need help with [specific problem]."
  • Example: A student who didn’t set goals left sessions feeling lost, while one who tracked progress saw noticeable improvement.

Mistake 3: Ignoring Additional WCPL Resources

Problem: Some students only use tutoring and miss out on free books, courses, and tools.

Solution:

  • Explore WCPL’s full education portal, including:
    • Mango Languages (for ESL learners)
    • Lynda.com (for career skills)
    • Brainfuse (for homework help)
  • Example: A student who combined tutoring with WCPL’s coding courses landed a summer internship in tech.

Mistake 4: Not Reviewing Session Recordings

Problem: Students forget key lessons after the session ends.

Solution:

  • Take notes during the session.
  • Watch recordings to reinforce learning.
  • Example: A student who reviewed his tutoring videos scored higher on quizzes than those who didn’t.

Mistake 5: Over-Reliance on Tutors for Everything

Problem: Some students expect tutors to do all the work, rather than applying concepts independently.

Solution:

  • Use tutors as a guide, not a crutch.
  • Practice problems on your own between sessions.
  • Example: A student who only relied on tutors struggled with real-world applications, while one who practiced daily mastered the material faster.

Mistake 6: Not Communicating with Tutors

Problem: Some students don’t ask questions or don’t provide enough context, making sessions less effective.

Solution:

  • Be specific about what you need help with.
  • Example: A student who said, "Help me with math" got vague answers, while one who said, "I don’t understand how to solve systems of equations" got detailed, targeted help.
