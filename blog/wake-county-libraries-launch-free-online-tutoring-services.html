Tutors can’t read minds! Come to each session with a structured plan to maximize time. For example:

Common Mistakes to Avoid When Using Wake County’s Tutoring

Even the best resources can fail if used incorrectly. Here are five mistakes students make—and how to fix them.

Mistake 1: Waiting Until the Last Minute to Seek Help

Problem: Many students only ask for tutoring when they’re already failing a test or assignment. Solution:

  • Start early. If you’re struggling with a concept, book a session within a week of the next quiz.
  • Use the "On-Demand" library for immediate help on small questions.

Example: *A student who waited until the night before a math test to get help spent 3 hours just trying to understand the material—whereas a student who started a week early could have mastered it in 30 minutes.

Mistake 2: Not Being Specific About What You Need Help With

Problem: Vague questions like "I don’t get this math problem" lead to wasted time. Solution:

  • Be precise. Instead of "I’m bad at writing," say:
    • "I need help structuring a 5-paragraph essay."
    • "Can you review my thesis statement?"
  • Share your work (if possible) so the tutor can give targeted feedback.

Mistake 3: Ignoring Self-Paced Resources

Problem: Some students only use live tutoring, missing out on free, always-available lessons. Solution:

  • Combine both. Use self-paced videos to learn basics, then live tutoring to clarify doubts.
  • Bookmark helpful videos for later review.

Mistake 4: Not Following Up on Tutoring Notes

Problem: Tutors often provide written summaries or homework tips, but students don’t review them. Solution:

  • Save tutor notes in a Google Doc or notebook.
  • Revisit them before tests to reinforce learning.

Mistake 5: Assuming Tutors Are Only for "Hard" Subjects

Problem: Some students only use tutoring for struggling subjects, missing out on proactive learning. Solution:

  • Use tutoring for all subjects, even if you’re excelling.
    • Example: "Can you help me speed up my essay writing?"
    • Example: "I want to learn Python coding—can you recommend resources?"
