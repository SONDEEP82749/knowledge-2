How Wake County’s Free Online Tutoring Services Work

Wake County’s Tutoring@YourLibrary program is a cutting-edge digital initiative designed to bridge the learning gap. Here’s a breakdown of its key features:

1. Platform & Accessibility

The tutoring services are available 24/7 through the Wake County Libraries’ website, with no need for a library card in most cases. Users can access sessions via:

  • Desktop computers (Chrome, Firefox, Edge)
  • Mobile devices (iOS/Android)
  • Tablets (with stable internet)

The platform integrates with Google Classroom, Microsoft Teams, and other LMS (Learning Management Systems), making it seamless for schools to integrate tutoring into their curricula.

2. Subject Coverage & Expert Tutors

Wake County’s tutors are certified educators and subject-matter specialists with experience in:

  • Elementary & Middle School: Reading, writing, basic math
  • High School: Algebra, geometry, biology, chemistry, U.S. history
  • College Prep: SAT/ACT test prep, essay writing, study skills
  • ESL (English as a Second Language): Conversational English, grammar, vocabulary

3. Session Types & Flexibility

Students can choose from:

  • One-on-one tutoring (personalized attention)
  • Group sessions (collaborative learning)
  • Drop-in help (no appointment needed)
  • Scheduled appointments (for consistent study plans)

4. No Cost, No Hidden Fees

Unlike traditional tutoring services (which can cost $20–$50 per hour), Wake County’s program is completely free for all residents. Funding comes from public library grants, community partnerships, and local government support.

5. Additional Resources

Beyond live tutoring, the platform offers:

  • Interactive practice quizzes
  • Video lessons (for self-paced learning)
  • Homework help databases (like Gale, Brainfuse, and Rosetta Stone)
  • Career counseling (for high school and college students)

8 Actionable Strategies to Make the Most of Wake County’s Free Tutoring

Now that you know how the program works, let’s dive into how to use it effectively. These strategies will help students, parents, and educators optimize their learning experience.

1. Schedule Consistent Tutoring Sessions

Problem: Many students wait until the last minute for help, leading to rushed, ineffective sessions. Solution: Treat tutoring like a fixed appointment. For example:

  • Elementary students: 30-minute sessions 2–3 times per week (e.g., Monday & Thursday after school).
  • High schoolers: Weekly 1-hour sessions for test prep (e.g., SAT math every Saturday).
  • College-bound students: Bi-weekly 1.5-hour sessions for essay writing and critical thinking.

Pro Tip: Use a Google Calendar reminder to stay on track.

2. Prepare Questions Beforehand

Problem: Tutors can’t read minds—if you don’t know what you need help with, the session will be unproductive. Solution: Before logging in, list 3–5 specific questions (e.g.,):

  • "I don’t understand how to balance chemical equations—can you walk me through an example?"
  • "How do I structure a 5-paragraph essay for my English class?"
  • "What are the best strategies for memorizing vocabulary for the SAT?"

Pro Tip: If you’re stuck on a specific problem, screenshot it and share it with the tutor before the session starts.

3. Use the "Pre-Session Quiz" Feature

Wake County’s platform includes diagnostic quizzes that assess your knowledge before a tutoring session. This helps tutors tailor their approach to your weakest areas.

How to Use It:

  1. Take the pre-session quiz (usually 5–10 questions).
  2. Review the results with your tutor.
  3. Focus the session on your lowest-scoring topics.

4. Combine Tutoring with Self-Study

Problem: Relying solely on tutors means you’re not developing independent problem-solving skills. Solution: Use tutoring as a supplement, not a replacement, for self-study. For example:

  • Math: After a tutoring session, practice 5 similar problems on Khan Academy.
  • English: Write an essay, then get feedback from a tutor before revising.
  • Science: Watch a YouTube lecture on the topic before the session.

Pro Tip: Keep a study journal to track progress between sessions.

5. Leverage Group Sessions for Collaboration

While one-on-one tutoring is great for personalized help, group sessions can be more engaging for social learners. Benefits include: ✔ Peer learning (students explain concepts to each other) ✔ Accountability (group deadlines keep everyone on track) ✔ Cost-effective (more students get help in one session)

Best For:

  • Study groups (e.g., 3–4 friends working on the same assignment)
  • Test prep (e.g., SAT/ACT review groups)
  • Language learning (ESL students practicing together)

6. Use the "Homework Help" Database

Not every question requires a live tutor. Wake County’s homework help databases (like Brainfuse) offer:

  • Step-by-step solutions for math problems
  • Grammar checks for writing assignments
  • 24/7 chat support for quick answers

How to Access It:

  1. Log in to the Wake County Libraries website.
  2. Navigate to "Homework Help" under the "Resources" tab.
  3. Search for your topic or upload a file for instant feedback.

7. Set Clear Goals for Each Session

Without specific objectives, tutoring sessions can feel vague and unproductive. Instead of saying: ❌ "Help me with math." Try: ✅ "Today, I want to master solving quadratic equations—can you explain the quadratic formula and give me 3 practice problems?"

Goal-Setting Framework:

  1. Identify the topic (e.g., "exponents in algebra").
  2. Define the skill (e.g., "simplify expressions").
  3. Set a measurable outcome (e.g., "I want to solve 5 problems correctly").

8. Follow Up & Reinforce Learning

Problem: Knowledge fades if not reinforced. Solution: After each session, review the material and apply it in new ways:

  • Math: Teach the concept to a sibling or friend.
  • Science: Create a mind map of key terms.
  • English: Rewrite a paragraph in different styles.

Pro Tip: Use spaced repetition (e.g., review notes 24 hours later, then 1 week later).

Common Mistakes to Avoid When Using Online Tutoring

While Wake County’s program is highly effective, some users make avoidable mistakes that reduce its impact. Here’s how to steer clear of pitfalls:

Mistake 1: Treating Tutoring as a Last Resort

Problem: Waiting until the night before an assignment means tutors can’t provide deep, meaningful help. Solution: Use tutoring proactively, not reactively. For example:

  • Weekly check-ins for consistent progress.
  • Early intervention for struggling topics.

Mistake 2: Not Engaging Actively in Sessions

Problem: Some students passively listen instead of participating, making sessions ineffective. Solution:

  • Ask questions (even if they seem "dumb").
  • Take notes during sessions.
  • Practice problems immediately after learning.

Mistake 3: Ignoring Additional Resources

Problem: Only using live tutoring means missing out on self-paced learning tools. Solution: Combine tutoring with:

  • Video lessons (e.g., Khan Academy)
  • Interactive quizzes (e.g., Quizlet)
  • Homework help databases

Mistake 4: Not Setting Realistic Expectations

Problem: Some students expect overnight mastery of difficult concepts. Solution:

  • Break learning into small steps (e.g., master one topic before moving to the next).
  • Celebrate small wins (e.g., "I finally understand exponents!").
  • Accept that progress takes time.

Mistake 5: Skipping Homework & Practice

Problem: Relying only on tutors without self-study leads to weak retention. Solution:

  • Apply concepts immediately after tutoring.
  • Review notes daily (even for 10 minutes).
  • Use spaced repetition (e.g., Anki flashcards).
