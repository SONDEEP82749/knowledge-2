How Wake County Libraries’ Free Online Tutoring Works: A Step-by-Step Breakdown

Wake County Public Library’s new tutoring initiative is powered by Tutoring.com, a leading online education platform trusted by schools nationwide. Here’s how it functions:

1. Accessibility: Available Anywhere, Anytime

Unlike traditional tutoring centers with fixed hours, WCPL’s service is open 24/7, allowing students to get help at 3 AM if needed. All you need is:

  • A Wake County library card (or a digital pass if you don’t have one)
  • A device with internet access (computer, tablet, or smartphone)
  • A quiet, distraction-free space (though some students even use headphones in noisy environments)

2. Subject Coverage: From Algebra to AP Exams

The tutoring service supports multiple grade levels and subjects, including:

  • Elementary (Grades K-5): Reading, writing, basic math
  • Middle School (Grades 6-8): Algebra, science, history, ELA
  • High School (Grades 9-12): Advanced math, chemistry, AP exams, SAT/ACT prep
  • Adult Learners: GED, career skills, language learning

3. Live vs. On-Demand Tutoring: Flexibility for Every Learner

Students can choose between:

  • Live, one-on-one tutoring (real-time chat with a certified tutor)
  • On-demand writing assistance (for essays and research papers)
  • Skill-building modules (interactive lessons for self-paced learning)

4. How to Sign Up: A Simple 3-Step Process

  1. Get a Wake County Library Card (if you don’t have one)

    • Visit WCPL’s website or call 919-856-4700 to register.
    • Digital passes are available for remote access—no physical card needed.

  2. Access the Tutoring Portal

    • Go to Tutoring.com and select "Wake County Public Library" as your institution.

  3. Book a Session or Start Learning

    • Choose a subject, grade level, and tutor availability.
    • For live tutoring, select a time slot (or use the "Now" button for immediate help).
    • For on-demand writing, upload an assignment, and a tutor will review it within 24 hours.

8 Actionable Strategies to Maximize Wake County’s Free Online Tutoring

Now that you know how the service works, let’s dive into how to use it effectively to get the best results.

Strategy 1: Schedule Tutoring During Peak Productivity Hours

Not all students learn best at night. Research shows that early risers (morning people) perform better in the morning, while night owls (evening people) should schedule sessions later in the day (National Sleep Foundation, 2022).

Action Step:

  • If you’re a morning person, book tutoring right after school (3–5 PM).
  • If you’re a night owl, use the service after dinner (7–9 PM) when your brain is freshest.

Strategy 2: Use the "Skill Builder" for Self-Paced Learning

The Skill Builder section allows students to practice problems independently before live tutoring. This is especially useful for:

  • Math students struggling with algebra or calculus
  • Science students reviewing biology or chemistry concepts
  • Test-takers preparing for the SAT, ACT, or AP exams

Action Step:

  • Spend 10–15 minutes in Skill Builder before a live session to identify weak areas.
  • Example: If you’re bad at exponential growth in math, do 5 practice problems first, then ask a tutor to explain the concept.

Strategy 3: Ask Specific Questions (Not "Help Me with My Homework")

Tutors are not homework-doers—they’re educators. Vague questions like "Help me with my math" lead to frustrating, unproductive sessions.

Do This Instead: ✅ "I don’t understand how to solve quadratic equations—can you walk me through the quadratic formula?" ✅ "I keep getting stuck on balancing chemical equations—can you show me step 1?"

Common Mistake: Asking for answers without explanation. Fix: Always ask for step-by-step breakdowns.

Strategy 4: Record Sessions for Later Review

Many students forget what they learned during a tutoring session. Solution? Use the session recording feature to:

  • Replay confusing parts later
  • Share notes with parents or teachers
  • Study for upcoming tests

Action Step:

  • Before ending a session, ask the tutor to save the recording.
  • Listen back within 24 hours to reinforce learning.

Strategy 5: Use On-Demand Writing Help for Essays & Research Papers

Struggling with AP English, college applications, or research papers? The on-demand writing service is a game-changer because:

  • Tutors review and edit your work (not just grade it).
  • They provide feedback on structure, grammar, and argument strength.
  • Turnaround time is fast (usually 24 hours).

Action Step:

  • Upload a draft of your essay and request:
    • "Can you help me strengthen my thesis statement?"
    • "Are my sources credible? How can I improve my citations?"

Strategy 6: Form a Study Group with Tutoring

Some students learn better in group settings. WCPL’s tutoring platform allows you to:

  • Invite classmates to join a session (if they have their own library cards).
  • Discuss problems together before seeking tutor help.
  • Take turns explaining concepts (teaching reinforces learning).

Action Step:

  • Find 2–3 friends who also need help in the same subject.
  • Schedule a group tutoring session and take turns asking questions.

Strategy 7: Prepare a "Tutoring Cheat Sheet" Before Sessions

Tutors can’t read your mind—if you don’t know what you don’t know, the session will be wasted.

Action Step: Before booking, write down:

  1. The exact topic you’re struggling with.
  2. Specific examples of where you’re stuck (e.g., "I don’t get how to find the slope of a line").
  3. Your goal for the session (e.g., "I want to pass my next quiz").

Strategy 8: Combine Tutoring with Other Library Resources

Wake County Libraries offer more than just tutoring. To maximize your learning, pair tutoring with:

  • Free online courses (Coursera, Khan Academy via WCPL).
  • E-books & audiobooks (for supplementary reading).
  • Career & college prep tools (if you’re an adult learner).

Action Step:

  • After a tutoring session, search WCPL’s digital catalog for:

Common Mistakes When Using Online Tutoring (And How to Avoid Them)

Even with a free, high-quality service, some students waste time due to bad habits. Here’s what not to do:

Mistake 1: Treating Tutoring Like a Homework Helper

What to Avoid:

  • Saying: "Just do my homework for me."
  • Expecting tutors to do all the work while you relax.

How to Fix It:

  • Be an active participant—ask questions, take notes, and try problems yourself first.
  • Treat tutors like coaches, not answer machines.

Mistake 2: Not Following Up After Sessions

What to Avoid:

  • Ending a session and never reviewing notes.
  • Forgetting what the tutor taught you the next day.

How to Fix It:

  • Revisit notes within 24 hours.
  • Practice similar problems to reinforce learning.

Mistake 3: Using Tutoring Only When Struggling (Not for Proactive Learning)

What to Avoid:

  • Only calling a tutor when you’re failing a test.
  • Not using tutoring for preparation (e.g., before a big quiz).

How to Fix It:

  • Use tutoring for:
    • Weekly review (e.g., "Help me understand last week’s lesson").
    • Test prep (e.g., "Walk me through SAT math strategies").

Mistake 4: Ignoring the "Skill Builder" Section

What to Avoid:

  • Skipping interactive practice problems before live tutoring.
  • Only relying on tutors to explain concepts without self-practice.

How to Fix It:

  • Do 5–10 practice problems before each session.
  • Identify patterns in your mistakes (e.g., "I keep getting trigonometry wrong").

Mistake 5: Not Asking for Clarification When Confused

What to Avoid:

  • Nodding along without understanding what the tutor says.
  • Saying "I get it" when you don’t.

How to Fix It:

  • Pause and ask: "Can you explain that differently?"
  • Use examples: "Can you show me how this applies to a real-world problem?"

Mistake 6: Overloading Tutoring Sessions

What to Avoid:

  • Trying to cover 3 different topics in one 30-minute session.
  • Multitasking (e.g., checking social media while tutoring).

How to Fix It:

  • Focus on 1–2 key topics per session.
  • Turn off distractions (phone, TV, siblings).
