Actionable Strategies for Success

To get the most out of the Wake County libraries' online tutoring services, students can follow these 10 actionable strategies:

  1. Set clear goals: Before starting an online tutoring session, define what you want to achieve and communicate your goals to your tutor.
  2. Prepare in advance: Review the material you will be covering during the session and come prepared with questions and topics you want to discuss.
  3. Establish a routine: Schedule regular online tutoring sessions to ensure consistent progress and reinforcement of new skills.
  4. Ask questions: Don't be afraid to ask questions or seek clarification on concepts you don't understand.
  5. Practice actively: Engage with the material by practicing problems, completing assignments, and applying what you have learned to real-world scenarios.
  6. Seek feedback: Request feedback from your tutor on your progress and use it to adjust your learning strategy.
  7. Stay organized: Keep track of your schedule, assignments, and progress using a planner, calendar, or task list.
  8. Communicate with your tutor: Share your concerns, questions, and feedback with your tutor to ensure you are getting the most out of your online tutoring experience.
  9. Review and reflect: Regularly review what you have learned and reflect on what works best for you, making adjustments as needed.
  10. Celebrate progress: Acknowledge and celebrate your achievements, no matter how small, to stay motivated and encouraged.

Common Mistakes and How to Avoid Them

While online tutoring services can be incredibly beneficial, there are some common mistakes that students should be aware of and try to avoid. These include:

  • Not being prepared: Failing to review material, come up with questions, or establish clear goals can hinder the effectiveness of an online tutoring session.
  • Not communicating with the tutor: Failing to ask questions, provide feedback, or share concerns can make it difficult for the tutor to provide the best possible support.
  • Not staying organized: Failing to keep track of assignments, schedules, and progress can lead to missed deadlines, lost opportunities, and decreased motivation.
  • Not being consistent: Failing to establish a regular routine or schedule can make it difficult to make progress and reinforce new skills.
