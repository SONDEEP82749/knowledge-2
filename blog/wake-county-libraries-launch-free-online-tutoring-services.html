8 Actionable Strategies to Make the Most of Wake County’s Free Tutoring

Now that you know how the service works, let’s dive into practical ways to optimize your learning experience.

1. Schedule Consistent Tutoring Sessions

Problem: Many students wait until the last minute, leading to rushed, ineffective sessions. Solution:

  • Block 30-60 minutes weekly for tutoring, just like a fixed study schedule.
  • Example: If you have a math test on Friday, book a session on Monday or Tuesday to reinforce concepts.
  • Pro Tip: Use a Google Calendar reminder to stay on track.

2. Prepare Questions Beforehand

Problem: Tutors can’t read minds—if you don’t know what you need help with, the session will be unproductive. Solution:

  • List 3-5 specific questions before logging in (e.g., "How do I solve quadratic equations?").
  • Bring your textbook or notes to reference.
  • Example: Instead of asking, "Help me with my math homework," say, "I’m stuck on Problem 12—can you explain how to factor this?"

3. Use the Writing & Test Prep Features for College Applications

Problem: Many students struggle with college essays and standardized tests but don’t know where to get help. Solution:

  • For Essays:
    • Upload a draft and ask tutors to review structure, thesis clarity, and grammar.
    • Request alternative phrasing for weak sentences.
  • For SAT/ACT:
    • Practice timed sections with tutors to improve speed and accuracy.
    • Example: A student in Wake County used the test prep feature to raise their SAT score by 150 points in 3 months.

4. Leverage Multilingual Tutors for Non-Native English Speakers

Problem: Language barriers can make tutoring less effective. Solution:

  • Example: A Wake County high school student who spoke little English used the bilingual tutor to master AP Biology vocabulary, leading to an A on her final exam.

5. Combine Tutoring with Study Groups

Problem: Tutoring alone may not be enough for collaborative learning. Solution:

  • Form a study group with peers and use tutoring for weekly check-ins.
  • Example: A group of Wake County students met biweekly via Zoom, tackled problems together, and then booked a group tutoring session to clarify doubts.

6. Track Progress with a Learning Journal

Problem: Without tracking, it’s hard to measure improvement. Solution:

Common Mistakes & How to Avoid Them

Even with a free, high-quality service, some users make avoidable mistakes. Here’s how to steer clear of pitfalls:

❌ Mistake 1: Waiting Until the Last Minute

Why it’s bad: Cramming before a test leads to misunderstood concepts and poor retention. Solution:

  • Start early—even 1-2 weeks before a test is better than the night before.
  • Example: A Wake County student who waited until two days before her AP Bio exam scored a 3, while a peer who started tutoring a month early scored a 5.

❌ Mistake 2: Not Being Specific with Questions

Why it’s bad: Vague questions like "Help me with math" waste both your and the tutor’s time. Solution:

  • Be precise: "How do I solve systems of equations using substitution?"
  • Show your work so the tutor can pinpoint exactly where you’re stuck.

❌ Mistake 3: Skipping Homework Before Tutoring

Why it’s bad: Tutors can’t teach you from scratch—they help you understand what you’ve already tried. Solution:

  • Attempt the problem first, then ask for help if stuck.
  • Example: A student who didn’t try before tutoring session lost 30 minutes explaining the basics.

❌ Mistake 4: Ignoring the Writing & Test Prep Features

Why it’s bad: Many students only use tutoring for homework, missing out on higher-impact services. Solution:

  • Use test prep for:
    • SAT/ACT practice (timed sections).
    • College essay reviews (before deadlines).
  • Example: A Wake County student who only used tutoring for math missed out on SAT score improvements until she started using test prep.

❌ Mistake 5: Not Following Up on Feedback

Why it’s bad: Tutors provide actionable advice, but if you don’t apply it, progress stalls. Solution:

  • Take notes during sessions.
  • Revisit weak areas in the next session.
  • Example: A student who didn’t revisit grammar mistakes saw no improvement in her writing.

❌ Mistake 6: Assuming Tutors Can Replace Self-Study

Why it’s bad: Tutoring supplements learning, not replaces it. Solution:

  • Study independently between sessions.
  • Use tutoring to clarify doubts, not as a crutch.
  • Example: A student who only relied on tutors for all learning struggled with independent problem-solving.
