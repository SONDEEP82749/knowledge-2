How Wake County’s Free Online Tutoring Services Work: A Step-by-Step Breakdown

Wake County Public Library’s new tutoring platform is designed to be user-friendly, flexible, and accessible—no matter your schedule or subject level. Here’s how it functions:

1. Platform Access & Registration

The tutoring service is fully online, accessible via the Wake County Libraries’ website or through partnered learning apps like Tutoring.com and Brainfuse.

  • No library card? Don’t worry—digital access is free for all residents.
  • Registration is quick: Simply visit Wake County Libraries’ Tutoring Page and follow the prompts.
  • Multi-language support: Tutors are available in Spanish, Mandarin, and English to accommodate diverse learners.

2. Subject & Grade-Level Options

Wake County’s program covers K-12 and college prep subjects, including:

  • Math (Algebra, Geometry, Calculus)
  • Science (Biology, Chemistry, Physics)
  • English & Writing (Grammar, Essay Writing, SAT Prep)
  • Foreign Languages (Spanish, French, Mandarin)
  • Test Prep (SAT, ACT, AP Exams)

3. Live vs. On-Demand Tutoring

Students can choose between:

  • Live, real-time tutoring (synchronous sessions)
  • On-demand writing assistance (asynchronous feedback)
  • 24/7 homework help (via chat or video)

4. How to Book a Session

  1. Log in to your account on the tutoring portal.
  2. Select your subject, grade level, and preferred tutor (if available).
  3. Choose a time slot (or request a last-minute session).
  4. Join via video call (Zoom or built-in platform) or text chat.

5. Additional Features

  • Recorded sessions (for review later)
  • Customized study plans (for long-term improvement)
  • Parent dashboards (to track progress)

8 Actionable Strategies to Make the Most of Wake County’s Free Tutoring

Now that you know how the program works, let’s explore how to use it effectively. These proven strategies will help you (or your child) get the most out of every tutoring session.

1. Schedule Sessions During Peak Focus Hours

Not all times are equal when it comes to learning. Studies show that morning sessions (9 AM–12 PM) and late afternoon (3 PM–6 PM) tend to be more productive because the brain is fresh and alert.

Pro Tip:

  • If you’re a night owl, try early morning sessions (6–8 AM) when tutors are less busy.
  • Avoid right after school (1–3 PM) if possible—students often need a mental break before diving into structured learning.

2. Prepare a Clear List of Questions Before Each Session

Tutors can’t read your mind—you must come prepared. Before each session, write down: ✔ Specific problems you’re struggling with (e.g., "I don’t understand how to solve quadratic equations.") ✔ Past assignments you’d like reviewed ✔ Test questions you got wrong and want explained

Example: Instead of saying, "I’m bad at math," Say, "I keep getting stuck on word problems involving ratios. Can you walk me through one?"

3. Use the "Two-Session Rule" for Complex Topics

Some subjects (like calculus or advanced chemistry) require multiple sessions to fully grasp. Instead of trying to cram everything in one hour, break it down:

  • Session 1: Learn the concept (e.g., derivatives in calculus).
  • Session 2: Practice applying it (e.g., solving derivative problems).
  • Session 3: Review common mistakes and take a practice test.

4. Record Sessions for Later Review

Many tutoring platforms allow session recording. If you’re allowed to record, do it! Reviewing your own session helps reinforce learning.

How to Use Recordings Effectively:

  • Pause and rewind confusing parts.
  • Take notes on key takeaways.
  • Revisit old recordings before exams.

5. Leverage On-Demand Writing Assistance for Essays

If you’re struggling with essay writing, Wake County’s on-demand writing lab is a game-changer. Instead of waiting for a live tutor, you can:

  • Upload a draft.
  • Get detailed feedback within 24 hours.
  • Revise based on grammar, structure, and argument strength.

Example: A high school senior submitted a 5-paragraph essay on climate change. The tutor highlighted: ✔ Weak thesis statement (needed to be more specific). ✔ Repetitive arguments (suggested adding counterarguments). ✔ Grammar errors (run-on sentences, missing commas).

After revisions, her grade improved from a C+ to an A-.

6. Set Specific Goals for Each Session

Vague goals like "I want to get better at math" won’t cut it. Instead, define measurable objectives:

Goal How to Track Progress
"I’ll master long division by the next session." Practice 10 problems before the session.
"I’ll improve my SAT reading score by 50 points." Take a practice test before and after tutoring.
"I’ll understand the periodic table by heart." Quiz myself daily using flashcards.

7. Use Tutoring for Test Prep, Not Just Homework Help

Many students only use tutoring for homework, but the best way to boost grades is to prepare for tests. Wake County’s tutors can help with:

  • SAT/ACT strategies (time management, question types).
  • Flashcard creation for memorization-heavy subjects (history, science).
  • Mock exams under timed conditions.

Real-World Example: A junior in Wake County used tutoring exclusively for SAT prep. Instead of just answering questions, she:

  • Identified weak areas (e.g., math word problems).
  • Practiced under real test conditions (4-hour sessions).
  • Reviewed mistakes with her tutor to avoid repeating them.

Result? Her SAT score jumped from 1100 to 1350—well above her target college admissions requirement.

8. Combine Tutoring with Supplemental Resources

Tutoring is powerful, but not the only tool you should use. Pair it with:

  • Khan Academy (free video lessons).
  • Quizlet (flashcards for memorization).
  • Grammarly (for writing feedback).
  • Mathway (step-by-step problem-solving).

Example Workflow:

  1. Watch a Khan Academy video on linear equations.
  2. Practice problems on Quizlet.
  3. Book a tutoring session to clarify doubts.
  4. Use Mathway to verify answers.

Common Mistakes to Avoid When Using Wake County’s Free Tutoring

While the program is free and highly beneficial, some students waste opportunities by making simple but costly mistakes. Here’s what to avoid at all costs:

1. Showing Up Without Preparation

Mistake: Walking into a session completely unprepared and expecting the tutor to teach from scratch. Why It Fails:

  • Tutors don’t have unlimited time—they help with specific problems, not general knowledge.
  • You’ll waste valuable session time if you don’t know what you need help with.

Solution:

  • Come with a list of questions.
  • Review past assignments before the session.
  • Try solving problems first—then ask for help if stuck.

2. Only Using Tutoring for Last-Minute Cramming

Mistake: Waiting until the night before a test to book a session. Why It Fails:

  • Tutors can’t magically make you an expert in one session.
  • Long-term learning requires consistent practice.

Solution:

  • Schedule regular sessions (e.g., biweekly for math, weekly for writing).
  • Use tutoring for test prep (not just homework).

3. Ignoring the "Why" Behind the Answer

Mistake: Asking for answers only, without understanding how they’re derived. Why It Fails:

  • You’ll forget the solution the next time you see a similar problem.
  • Tutors waste time explaining if you don’t engage with the process.

Solution:

  • Ask "why" and "how"—not just "what’s the answer?"
  • Take notes on the method, not just the result.

4. Not Following Up on Feedback

Mistake: Getting detailed feedback from a tutor but not applying it. Why It Fails:

  • If you don’t revise your work, you’ll keep making the same mistakes.
  • Tutors can’t force you to improve—you must take action.

Solution:

  • Implement changes based on tutor feedback.
  • Revisit old assignments to see progress.
  • Ask for a follow-up session if needed.

5. Over-Reliance on Tutoring Without Self-Study

Mistake: Only using tutoring and not doing independent work. Why It Fails:

  • Tutors can’t do your homework for you.
  • Passive learning (just listening) doesn’t build true understanding.

Solution:

  • Balance tutoring with self-study (e.g., 1 session + 2 hours of practice).
  • Use supplemental resources (Khan Academy, textbooks).
  • Test yourself before and after sessions.

6. Not Utilizing All Available Resources

Mistake: Only using live tutoring and ignoring on-demand features. Why It Fails:

  • Live sessions are limited—you can’t always get them when you need them.
  • On-demand writing help is invaluable for essays and reports.

Solution:

  • Use chat/email support for quick questions.
  • Upload assignments for detailed feedback.
  • Explore recorded lessons for self-paced learning.

7. Giving Up Too Soon

Mistake: Quitting after one or two sessions if progress isn’t immediate. Why It Fails:

  • Learning is a marathon, not a sprint.
  • Some concepts take time to click.

Solution:

  • Set realistic expectations.
  • Track progress over weeks/months.
  • Celebrate small wins (e.g., "I finally understood exponents!").
