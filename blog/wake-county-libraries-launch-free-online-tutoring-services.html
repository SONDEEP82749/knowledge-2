Unlike traditional tutoring services that require appointments or subscriptions, this program offers instant access to certified tutors in math, science, English, history, and more.

Wake County’s tutors are specialized in:

How to Get Started in 5 Simple Steps

  1. Get a Wake County Library Card

    • If you don’t already have one, register online at Wake County Public Libraries.
    • Residents can get a card for free with proof of address (utility bill, lease, etc.).
    • Non-residents may need to pay a small fee (~$50/year).

  2. Visit the Tutoring Portal

    • Go to Tutor.com – Wake County (or search "Wake County Library Tutoring").
    • Log in with your library card number and PIN.

  3. Select Your Subject & Grade Level

    • Choose from predefined categories (e.g., "High School Math," "College Essay Help").
    • If your topic isn’t listed, select "Other" and describe your need.

  4. Connect with a Tutor

    • A tutor will appear in your chat window within minutes.
    • You can upload documents (homework, test questions) or type questions in real time.

  5. Start Learning!

    • Use the whiteboard tool to work through problems together.
    • Ask follow-up questions until you fully understand the concept.

8 Actionable Strategies to Make the Most of Wake County’s Free Tutoring

While the service is easy to use, maximizing its benefits requires strategic preparation and engagement. Here’s how to get the most out of every tutoring session:

1. Prepare Questions in Advance (Avoid Last-Minute Struggles)

Problem: Walking into a tutoring session with vague questions ("I don’t get this math problem") wastes time. Solution:

  • Write down specific questions before logging in.
  • Highlight confusing parts in your textbook or worksheet.
  • Example:
    • ❌ "I don’t understand algebra."
    • ✅ "I’m stuck on solving for x in 3x + 5 = 20. Can you walk me through the steps?"

Pro Tip: If you’re working on a multi-step problem, break it into smaller questions (e.g., "First, how do I isolate the variable?").

2. Use the Whiteboard Tool Effectively (It’s More Than Just Chat)

Problem: Some students treat tutoring like a one-way lecture instead of an interactive session. Solution:

  • Type or draw your work on the whiteboard so the tutor can see your thought process.
  • Example:
    • Instead of saying, "I don’t get this," write out your attempt and ask, "Where did I go wrong?"
  • Tutors can also draw diagrams, graphs, or equations to explain concepts better.

3. Request Practice Problems (Don’t Just Ask for Answers)

Problem: Students often ask for final answers without understanding the process. Solution:

  • Ask for step-by-step explanations rather than just solutions.
  • Example:
    • ❌ "What’s the answer to this?"
    • ✅ "Can you show me how to solve this quadratic equation?"
  • Request additional practice problems to reinforce learning.

4. Schedule Consistent Sessions (Repetition Builds Confidence)

Problem: Many students use tutoring only when stuck, leading to knowledge gaps. Solution:

  • Set a weekly schedule (e.g., "Every Tuesday at 4 PM for 30 minutes").
  • Review past sessions to identify recurring weak areas.
  • Example:
    • If you struggle with word problems in math, request extra practice during sessions.

5. Leverage Test Prep Resources (Ace Standardized Exams)

Problem: Students often crash last-minute for SAT/ACT prep without structured help. Solution:

  • Use the tutoring service for:
    • Practice tests (full-length or section-specific).
    • Strategy tips (e.g., how to eliminate wrong answers on the SAT).
    • Essay review (tutors can help refine arguments).
  • Example:
    • "I’m taking the SAT next week. Can we go over the math section and focus on algebra?"

6. Ask for Study Tips Beyond Tutoring (Long-Term Success)

Problem: Tutoring is short-term help, but students need long-term strategies. Solution:

  • Request study techniques from tutors, such as:
    • Active recall (testing yourself without notes).
    • Spaced repetition (reviewing material over days/weeks).
    • Time management (how to break down big assignments).
  • Example:
    • "I have a big history exam next week. What’s the best way to study?"

7. Use the Service for Non-Academic Needs (Career & Soft Skills)

Problem: Many students overlook tutoring for non-school subjects. Solution:

  • Tutors can help with:
    • Resume & cover letter writing (for job applications).
    • Interview preparation (practice answering common questions).
    • ESL support (if English is a second language).
    • Career readiness (how to research jobs, use LinkedIn).
  • Example:
    • "I’m applying for college next year. Can you help me write a strong personal statement?"

8. Follow Up & Apply What You Learned (Don’t Just Rely on Tutors)

Problem: Some students stop practicing after a tutoring session. Solution:

  • Immediately apply what you learned in homework or practice problems.
  • Review notes from the session later in the day.
  • Example:
    • If your tutor explained how to balance chemical equations, practice 3-5 problems on your own before the next session.

Common Mistakes to Avoid (And How to Fix Them)

Even the best programs can fail if used incorrectly. Here are five mistakes students make—and how to avoid them:

1. Waiting Until the Last Minute for Help

Mistake: Some students only seek tutoring when they’re failing. Solution:

  • Use tutoring for prevention, not just emergency fixes.
  • Example:
    • Instead of waiting until test week, ask for help on a weekly basis to stay ahead.

2. Not Engaging Actively in Sessions

Mistake: Passively listening instead of participating. Solution:

  • Take notes during sessions.
  • Ask follow-up questions if something isn’t clear.
  • Example:
    • If the tutor explains a concept, try solving a similar problem before asking for help.

3. Relying Only on Tutors (Not Doing Independent Work)

Mistake: Thinking tutors will do the work for you. Solution:

  • Tutors are guides, not replacements for effort.
  • Example:
    • After a session, practice 5-10 problems on your own before the next one.

4. Ignoring the Whiteboard & Chat Tools

Mistake: Only typing questions without showing work. Solution:

  • Use the whiteboard to write out your attempts.
  • Example:
    • Instead of saying, "I don’t get this," show your work and ask, "Where did I go wrong?"

5. Not Following Up with Teachers

Mistake: Getting help from tutors but not applying it in class. Solution:

  • Discuss tutoring insights with your teacher.
  • Example:
    • If your tutor explains how to solve word problems, ask your teacher for similar practice questions.
