How Wake County Libraries’ Free Online Tutoring Works

Wake County’s Tutoring Services are part of a broader initiative to democratize education by removing financial and logistical barriers. Here’s a breakdown of how it functions:

1. Platform & Accessibility

The tutoring is delivered through Tutoring.com, a leading online tutoring platform, integrated seamlessly into the Wake County Library’s digital portal. Students and parents can access it 24/7 via:

  • Library website (www.wakegov.com/library)
  • Mobile app (available for iOS and Android)
  • Browser-based login (no app required)

2. Who Can Use It?

The program is open to all Wake County residents, including:

  • K-12 students (grades K-12)
  • High school seniors preparing for college entrance exams (SAT, ACT)
  • Adult learners seeking GED or career training support
  • Parents and guardians looking for homework help

3. Subjects & Specializations

Tutors are available for:

  • Math (algebra, calculus, statistics)
  • Science (biology, chemistry, physics)
  • English & Reading (grammar, essay writing, literature analysis)
  • Test Prep (SAT, ACT, AP exams)
  • Foreign Languages (Spanish, French, Mandarin)
  • College & Career Readiness (resume writing, interview prep)

4. How to Book a Session

The process is simple and user-friendly:

  1. Create an account (if you don’t have one) via the library’s website.
  2. Select a subject and time slot (available 24/7, with live tutors during peak hours).
  3. Choose a tutor (professionally vetted educators with subject expertise).
  4. Join the session via video chat, text chat, or screen-sharing (depending on preference).

5. Cost & Eligibility

  • Completely free for Wake County residents.
  • No library card required (though it’s recommended for full access to other resources).
  • No income restrictions—open to everyone.

8 Actionable Strategies to Maximize Wake County’s Free Tutoring Services

While the service is easy to access, strategic use ensures the best results. Here are eight proven strategies to make the most of Wake County’s tutoring:

1. Schedule Regular, Short Sessions (Not Cramming)

Problem: Many students wait until the last minute, leading to overwhelming, ineffective sessions. Solution: Break study time into 20-30 minute blocks, 3-4 times a week. This prevents burnout and reinforces learning incrementally.

Example:

  • A high school junior struggling with AP Chemistry books a 15-minute daily session to clarify concepts before exams.
  • Instead of a single 2-hour cram session, they review one topic per week, leading to better retention.

2. Use the "Pre-Session Prep" Method

Problem: Walking into a tutoring session without clear goals wastes time. Solution: Before booking, list specific questions (e.g., "Explain how to balance chemical equations" or "Help me practice SAT math word problems").

Example:

  • A middle school student asks, "I don’t understand how to solve for x in two-step equations." The tutor can immediately provide step-by-step guidance instead of guessing what the student needs.

3. Leverage Screen-Sharing for Visual Learners

Problem: Some students grasp concepts better when seeing the problem solved in real time. Solution: Request screen-sharing during the session. The tutor can:

  • Write equations on a virtual whiteboard.
  • Show step-by-step solutions to math problems.
  • Highlight grammar mistakes in essays.

Example:

  • A struggling algebra student sees the tutor solve a quadratic equation while explaining each step, making the concept click instantly.

4. Record Sessions for Later Review

Problem: Some students forget key explanations after the session ends. Solution: Ask the tutor if you can record the session (most platforms allow this). Reviewing the recording helps reinforce learning.

Example:

  • A student records a 30-minute session on SAT vocabulary and listens to it during commutes or while exercising, improving retention.

5. Combine Tutoring with Library Resources

Problem: Tutoring alone may not cover all learning needs. Solution: Pair sessions with free library resources, such as:

  • Online textbooks (via OverDrive or Libby)
  • Educational databases (like Gale in Context)
  • Homework help videos (available on the library’s YouTube channel)

Example:

  • A student struggling with U.S. history uses a tutoring session to clarify key events, then watches a documentary on the library’s streaming service for deeper context.

6. Set Up a "Tutor Check-In" System

Problem: Without follow-up, students may revert to old mistakes. Solution: Schedule follow-up sessions to:

  • Review progress.
  • Adjust study strategies.
  • Reinforce weak areas.

Example:

  • A student books a weekly 15-minute check-in to discuss test results and adjust study plans accordingly.

7. Use Tutoring for Test Prep Beyond Schoolwork

Problem: Many students only use tutoring for homework, missing opportunities for college/career prep. Solution: Book sessions for:

  • SAT/ACT test prep (practice questions, timing strategies).
  • College essay editing (librarians can help with resume reviews too).
  • Career skill development (interview prep, LinkedIn profile reviews).

Example:

  • A high school senior uses two tutoring sessions to refine their college application essays, leading to stronger admissions chances.

8. Encourage Parental Involvement (For Younger Students)

Problem: Parents may not know how to guide their child’s learning. Solution: Parents can:

  • Attend sessions alongside their child (some tutors allow this).
  • Ask for study tips to reinforce learning at home.
  • Use the library’s parent resources (like homework help guides).

Example:

  • A parent books a session to understand how to teach fractions, then practices with their child using library-provided worksheets.

Common Mistakes to Avoid (And How to Fix Them)

Even the best resources can fail if misused. Here are five common pitfalls and how to avoid them:

1. Waiting Until the Last Minute for Help

Mistake: Students often book tutoring only when they’re failing a test. Solution:

  • Start early—even one session per week prevents last-minute panic.
  • Use tutoring for proactive learning, not just crisis management.

2. Not Setting Clear Goals Before the Session

Mistake: Walking into a session without a plan leads to wasted time. Solution:

  • Write down exactly what you need help with (e.g., "Explain how to write a thesis statement").
  • Ask the tutor to focus on one key concept per session.

3. Skipping Homework or Practice Before Tutoring

Mistake: Some students expect the tutor to do their work for them. Solution:

  • Attempt the problem first—tutors can guide you through mistakes, not just give answers.
  • Use library resources to practice on your own before sessions.

4. Ignoring Follow-Up Opportunities

Mistake: After one session, students stop engaging, leaving gaps in learning. Solution:

  • Schedule follow-up sessions to track progress.
  • Review recorded sessions to reinforce concepts.

5. Not Exploring All Available Resources

Mistake: Some students only use tutoring and miss other free library tools. Solution:

  • Combine tutoring with:
    • Online courses (Coursera, Khan Academy).
    • E-books and audiobooks (Libby, OverDrive).
    • Homework help databases (Gale, Britannica School).
