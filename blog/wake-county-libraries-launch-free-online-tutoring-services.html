4. Access to Specialized Help

From AP exam prep to job interview coaching, WCPL’s tutors cover a wide range of subjects, ensuring students get targeted support without breaking the bank.

10 Actionable Strategies to Maximize Wake County’s Free Online Tutoring

Now that you understand the basics, let’s explore how to get the most out of these services.

1. Identify Your Weakest Subjects Early

Before diving into tutoring, assess your strengths and weaknesses using:

  • Past test scores (quizzes, exams, standardized tests)
  • Teacher feedback (report cards, progress notes)
  • Self-assessment quizzes (many subjects offer free diagnostic tests online)

Example: If you’re struggling with algebra, focus on equations, functions, and word problems—these are common stumbling blocks.

2. Schedule Consistent Tutoring Sessions

Consistency is key—just like gym memberships, sporadic sessions won’t yield results. Aim for:

  • Weekly 30–60 minute sessions for K-12 students
  • Bi-weekly sessions for college students
  • Monthly check-ins for adult learners

Real-World Example: A Wake County high school senior, Jamie, struggled with chemistry but improved her grade from a C- to an A- after two 45-minute sessions per week with a WCPL tutor. She focused on practice problems and lab reports, which were her weakest areas.

3. Use the "Two-Tutor Method" for Complex Topics

Some subjects (like calculus or advanced physics) require multiple perspectives. If one tutor explains a concept poorly, switch to another until you understand it.

How to Do It:

  1. Start with Tutor A for an initial explanation.
  2. If confused, switch to Tutor B for a different approach.
  3. Summarize the concept in your own words to reinforce learning.

4. Leverage Homework Help for Immediate Clarity

WCPL’s tutors can help with current assignments—but don’t rely on them to do your work. Instead:

  • Upload a specific problem (e.g., "Help me solve this quadratic equation").
  • Ask for step-by-step guidance rather than just the answer.
  • Practice similar problems on your own afterward.

Common Mistake: Some students ask tutors to "do their homework," leading to wasted time. Instead, treat tutoring as a collaborative learning tool.

5. Prepare Before Each Session

Tutors are more effective when you come with clear questions. Before each session:

  • List 2–3 specific problems you need help with.
  • Bring relevant materials (textbooks, notes, past assignments).
  • Write down what you’ve tried so the tutor can build on your efforts.

Example: Instead of saying, "I don’t get this math test," Say, "I tried solving this problem using substitution, but I got stuck at step 3. Can you explain why my method didn’t work?"

6. Use Test Prep Resources for Standardized Exams

WCPL offers free SAT, ACT, and AP prep—but many students don’t take full advantage. To maximize this:

  • Take full-length practice tests (available on the WCPL website).
  • Review mistakes with a tutor—focus on patterns (e.g., "I keep getting word problems wrong").
  • Use flashcards for vocabulary (if prepping for the SAT/ACT).

Real-World Example: *A college-bound student, Priya, raised her SAT Math score from 580 to 720 by:

  1. Taking a practice test on WCPL’s platform.
  2. Reviewing errors with a tutor (she realized she struggled with algebra word problems).
  3. Practicing 10 problems daily on her weak areas.*

7. Explore Career & Job Aid for Adult Learners

WCPL’s tutoring isn’t just for students—adults can get help with:

  • Resume writing (tutors can review and suggest improvements).
  • Interview preparation (mock interviews, common questions).
  • Job search strategies (how to use LinkedIn, Indeed, etc.).

How to Use It:

  • Upload your resume and ask for feedback on clarity and keywords.
  • Practice interview questions with a tutor (they can act as the interviewer).
  • Get help with cover letters (many adults struggle with this).

8. Combine Tutoring with Self-Study Tools

Tutoring works best when paired with independent learning. Use these complementary resources:

  • Khan Academy (free video lessons and practice problems).
  • Duolingo (for language learners).
  • Grammarly (for writing help).
  • Quizlet (for flashcards and spaced repetition).

Example Strategy: If you’re learning Spanish, use:

  • Duolingo for daily practice (10–15 minutes).
  • WCPL tutor for conversation practice (1 session per week).
  • Quizlet for vocabulary review (before each session).

9. Track Progress & Adjust Strategies

Keep a learning journal to track:

  • Which topics improve fastest?
  • Which tutors explain concepts best?
  • What time of day works best for studying?

Example Journal Entry:

Date Topic Tutor Used Progress Notes
5/10 Algebra Ms. Lee Improved Struggled with word problems

Adjust based on data—if one tutor isn’t helping, switch methods.

10. Encourage Parents & Teachers to Use the Service

Parents and educators can supplement learning by:

  • Scheduling group sessions (e.g., a study group for a math class).
  • Using tutors for lesson planning (e.g., "Help me find resources for teaching fractions").
  • Encouraging students to use the service (many students don’t know it exists!).

How to Promote It:

  • Share the link in school newsletters or parent groups.
  • Set up a demo session for teachers to see how it works.
  • Offer incentives (e.g., "Students who use tutoring 3x get a book prize").

Common Mistakes & How to Avoid Them

Even the best resources can fail if misused. Here are five mistakes students make and how to fix them.

1. Mistake: Treating Tutoring Like a Crutch

Problem: Some students only use tutors when they’re behind, leading to last-minute cramming. Solution:

  • Use tutoring proactively—not just for bad grades.
  • Schedule regular check-ins (even if you’re doing well).

2. Mistake: Not Preparing Questions Beforehand

Problem: Walking into a session with vague questions ("I don’t get this") wastes time. Solution:

  • Write down specific problems before the session.
  • Bring examples of where you’re stuck.

3. Mistake: Ignoring the Platform’s Full Features

Problem: Many students only use live chat but miss recorded lessons, flashcards, and test prep. Solution:

  • Explore all sections of the WCPL tutoring portal.
  • Try different formats (video vs. chat) to see what works best.

4. Mistake: Comparing Yourself to Others

Problem: Some students get frustrated when tutors move faster than them. Solution:

  • Focus on your progress, not others’.
  • Ask tutors to slow down if needed.

5. Mistake: Not Following Up on Advice

Problem: Getting help but not practicing what you learned leads to no improvement. Solution:

  • Apply what you learn in homework or practice problems.
  • Revisit concepts in later sessions.
