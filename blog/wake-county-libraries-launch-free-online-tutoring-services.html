How Wake County Libraries’ Free Online Tutoring Works: A Step-by-Step Guide

Wake County Public Library has partnered with leading tutoring platforms to provide real-time, expert-led assistance across math, science, English, and test prep. Here’s how it works:

1. Accessing the Service

  • Website & App Login: Students can access tutoring through the WCPL website (wakelibrary.org) or the mobile app (available on iOS and Android).
  • Library Card Required: A free Wake County library card is needed to log in. If you don’t have one, registration is instant and digital—no in-person visit required.
  • No Appointment Needed: Tutoring is drop-in, meaning you can connect with a tutor immediately when you need help.

2. Available Subjects & Time Slots

Wake County’s tutoring covers:

  • K-12 Core Subjects (Math, Science, English, History)
  • College Prep (SAT, ACT, AP Exams)
  • Career & Technical Education (Computer Science, Coding Basics)
  • ESL (English as a Second Language)

Availability:

  • Monday–Thursday: 10:00 AM – 9:00 PM
  • Friday: 10:00 AM – 6:00 PM
  • Saturday: 10:00 AM – 5:00 PM
  • Sunday: Closed (but recorded lessons are available 24/7)

3. Connecting with a Tutor

Once logged in, users can:

  • Chat in Real-Time (text-based Q&A)
  • Video Call (face-to-face tutoring)
  • Submit Assignments for feedback
  • Access Lesson Plans & Worksheets

4. Additional Resources

Beyond live tutoring, Wake County offers:

  • Online Databases (Gale, ProQuest, Britannica School)
  • Digital Books & Audiobooks (Libby, Hoopla)
  • Career & Job Training (LinkedIn Learning, Coursera)

8 Actionable Strategies to Make the Most of Wake County’s Free Tutoring

Not all students (or parents) know how to optimize their tutoring experience. Here are proven strategies to get the best results:

1. Schedule Sessions During Peak Hours

Tutors are most available between 3:00 PM and 7:00 PM (weekdays), when students are typically doing homework. If you’re a working parent, try early mornings (7:00–9:00 AM) when demand is lower.

Pro Tip: Use the "Tutor Availability" dashboard to see when tutors are free.

2. Prepare Questions Beforehand

Walking into a tutoring session without clear questions wastes time. Instead:

  • List 3–5 specific problems you’re struggling with.
  • Bring your textbook or worksheet (if applicable).
  • Write down key concepts you need clarification on.

Example: ❌ "I don’t get math." ✅ "Can you explain how to solve quadratic equations using the quadratic formula? I’m stuck on step 3."

3. Use the "Assignment Submission" Feature

If you’re too shy to ask questions live, submit specific assignments for detailed feedback. Tutors will:

  • Correct mistakes
  • Explain concepts in simpler terms
  • Suggest alternative problem-solving methods

Best For: Students who prefer written explanations over live chats.

4. Take Advantage of Recorded Lessons

Not every student can make it to a live session. Wake County’s on-demand library includes:

  • Video tutorials (e.g., "How to Write a Thesis Statement")
  • Interactive quizzes (for self-assessment)
  • Cheat sheets (formulas, grammar rules, etc.)

Where to Find Them: Log in → "Digital Learning" tab → "Recorded Lessons"

5. Join Study Groups for Collaborative Learning

Some tutors facilitate group study sessions where students can:

  • Work on group projects
  • Discuss complex topics together
  • Get peer feedback

How to Join:

  • Check the "Events Calendar" on the WCPL website.
  • Some groups meet weekly (e.g., SAT prep, coding clubs).

6. Use the "Homework Help" Chatbot for Quick Answers

For minor questions, the AI-powered chatbot can provide:

  • Instant definitions
  • Step-by-step math solutions
  • Grammar corrections

Limitations: It’s not a substitute for human tutors, but great for quick checks.

7. Track Progress with the "Learning Dashboard"

Wake County’s system tracks:

  • Time spent on tutoring
  • Top subjects struggled with
  • Improvement over time

Why It Matters: Helps identify weak areas and adjust study plans.

8. Combine Tutoring with Library Resources

Don’t rely only on tutors—use complementary tools:

  • Khan Academy (free video lessons)
  • Duolingo (for language learners)
  • Coursera (for college-level courses)

Example Workflow:

  1. Watch a Khan Academy video on algebra.
  2. Use the WCPL tutor chat to clarify doubts.
  3. Practice with worksheets from the library’s database.

Common Mistakes & How to Avoid Them

Even with a free, high-quality service, some users waste time or miss opportunities. Here’s what to avoid:

❌ Mistake 1: Waiting Until the Last Minute

Problem: Students often cram before deadlines, leading to frustration and poor results. Solution:

  • Break assignments into smaller chunks.
  • Schedule tutoring 1–2 weeks before exams.
  • Use the "Learning Dashboard" to track progress early.

❌ Mistake 2: Not Using All Available Resources

Problem: Some students only use live tutors and ignore recorded lessons, databases, and study groups. Solution:

  • Mix live help with self-study.
  • Check the "Events Calendar" for free workshops.
  • Bookmark key resources (e.g., Khan Academy, Duolingo).

❌ Mistake 3: Being Too Shy to Ask Questions

Problem: Many students hesitate to ask for help, fearing they’ll seem "dumb." Solution:

  • Remember: Tutors are there to help—no question is too small.
  • Start with simple questions to build confidence.
  • Use the chatbot for quick, low-pressure answers.

❌ Mistake 4: Not Following Up on Feedback

Problem: Some students get tutor feedback but don’t apply it. Solution:

  • Review corrections and retry problems.
  • Ask for alternative explanations if something still doesn’t click.
  • Keep a "mistake log" to track recurring issues.

❌ Mistake 5: Over-Reliance on Tutors

Problem: A few students depend too much on tutors instead of practicing independently. Solution:

  • Use tutors as a supplement, not a replacement for study time.
  • Set a goal (e.g., "I’ll practice 3 problems before each session").
  • Use the library’s databases for extra practice.
