8 Actionable Strategies to Make the Most of Wake County’s Free Tutoring

While the service is easy to access, not all students use it effectively. Here are 8 proven strategies to boost your learning outcomes with Wake County’s free tutoring:

1. Schedule Regular, Short Sessions (Not Cramming)

Problem: Many students wait until the last minute and try to cover entire chapters in one session, leading to confusion. Solution:

  • Break topics into smaller chunks (e.g., 15-30 minutes per session).
  • Example: If struggling with quadratic equations, spend one session on solving basics, another on graphing parabolas, and a third on word problems.
  • Why it works: Short, frequent sessions improve retention (studies show spaced repetition enhances memory by 20-30%—Source: Anki App Research, 2022).

2. Prepare Questions Before Each Session

Problem: Walking into a tutoring session without clear goals leads to wasted time. Solution:

  • List 3-5 specific questions before logging in (e.g., "How do I balance chemical equations?" or "What’s the best way to structure a 5-paragraph essay?").
  • Use the "Session Notes" feature to jot down key takeaways.
  • Example: A high school student preparing for the SAT might ask:
    • "How do I eliminate answer choices in reading comprehension?"
    • "What’s the fastest way to solve geometry problems?"

3. Record & Review Sessions

Problem: Forgetting what was discussed in a session reduces long-term learning. Solution:

  • Enable the recording feature (if available) to rewatch the session later.
  • Take handwritten notes (studies show writing by hand improves recall by 14%—Source: Psychological Science, 2014).
  • Example: After a calculus tutoring session, a student might:
    • Record the session to review derivatives later.
    • Summarize key formulas in a notebook.

4. Use the "Drop-In" Feature for Urgent Help

Problem: Some students don’t know when tutors are available, leading to missed opportunities. Solution:

  • Check the "Drop-In" schedule (usually 24/7 for basic subjects).
  • Best for: Quick homework help, last-minute test prep, or clarifying confusing concepts.
  • Example: A middle schooler stuck on fractions can instantly connect with a tutor instead of waiting for a scheduled session.

5. Leverage the "Skills Building" Tools

Problem: Some tutoring platforms offer interactive exercises, but students don’t explore them. Solution:

  • Use the "Skills Practice" section to reinforce learning.
  • Example: After a chemistry tutoring session, a student could:
    • Take a 10-question quiz on molarity calculations.
    • Review incorrect answers with the tutor in the next session.

6. Form a Study Group with Tutoring

Problem: Tutoring is most effective when applied in real-time, but students often don’t practice alone. Solution:

  • Invite friends or classmates to join a group tutoring session.
  • Example: A group of AP Biology students could:
    • Schedule a 60-minute session to discuss cell respiration.
    • Take a practice test together afterward.

7. Follow Up with the Tutor

Problem: Some students only use tutoring once and don’t build a relationship with the tutor. Solution:

  • Ask the same tutor for follow-up sessions (they’ll recognize your progress).
  • Example: A student struggling with trigonometry might:
    • Book a first session to learn sine and cosine.
    • Schedule a second session to practice angle of elevation problems.

8. Combine Tutoring with Other Library Resources

Problem: Tutoring is great for live help, but students don’t use supplementary materials. Solution:

  • Pair tutoring with:
    • Online courses (e.g., Khan Academy, Coursera—available via WCPL).
    • E-books & textbooks (e.g., Schaum’s Outlines for test prep).
    • Research databases (e.g., Gale in Context for science projects).
  • Example: A college student prepping for the ACT could:
    • Use tutoring for live practice questions.
    • Read "The Official ACT Guide" (available digitally).
    • Take timed quizzes in the library’s test prep database.

Common Mistakes Students Make (And How to Avoid Them)

Even with free, high-quality tutoring, some students sabotage their progress with bad habits. Here are 5 deadly mistakes and how to fix them:

Mistake #1: Treating Tutoring Like a Crutch (Not a Tool for Learning)

What Happens:

  • Students only ask for answers instead of understanding the process.
  • Example: A student asks, "What’s the answer to this chemistry problem?" instead of "How do I balance this equation?"

How to Fix It: ✅ Ask "why" and "how" instead of just "what." ✅ Example: Instead of "Can you do my homework?" say:

  • "Can you explain how to solve this type of problem?"
  • "What’s the best way to approach this question?"

Mistake #2: Skipping Review Sessions

What Happens:

  • Students only use tutoring when stressed (e.g., before a test) and never reinforce learning.
  • Example: A student studies for a week, then forgets everything after the test.

How to Fix It: ✅ Schedule "review sessions" every 2-3 weeks to reinforce concepts. ✅ Use the "Skills Practice" section to test yourself before a test. ✅ Example: After learning exponents, take a 10-question quiz the next day.

Mistake #3: Not Using All Available Resources

What Happens:

  • Students only use live tutoring but ignore e-books, courses, and practice tests.
  • Example: A student asks for SAT help but never takes a full-length practice test.

How to Fix It: ✅ Combine tutoring with:

  • Online courses (Khan Academy, Coursera).
  • E-books (e.g., "The Official SAT Study Guide").
  • Practice tests (available in the library’s database). ✅ Example: For AP Biology, use:
  • Tutoring for live Q&A.
  • Khan Academy videos for concepts.
  • Past AP exams for practice.

Mistake #4: Being Passive in Sessions

What Happens:

  • Students sit back and let the tutor do all the work instead of actively participating.
  • Example: A student nods along while the tutor explains but doesn’t take notes.
