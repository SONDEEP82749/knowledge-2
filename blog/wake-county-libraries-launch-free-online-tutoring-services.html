8 Actionable Strategies to Maximize Wake County’s Free Tutoring Services

Now that you know how the tutoring works, let’s dive into how to use it effectively. These strategies will help you get the most out of the program and ensure long-term academic success.

Strategy 1: Schedule Regular Tutoring Sessions (Not Just for Crunch Time)

Many students wait until the last minute before a test or deadline to seek help, which can lead to frustration and poor results. Instead, integrate tutoring into a consistent study routine.

How to do it:

  • For K-12 students: Schedule 1-2 weekly sessions (30-60 minutes each) to reinforce concepts.
  • For college students: Use tutoring biweekly for test prep or essay reviews.
  • For adults: Dedicate one session per month for career-related questions.

Why it works: Consistency helps build confidence and identify weak areas early, preventing last-minute panic.

Strategy 2: Use the "Pre-Session Prep" Method

Before jumping into a tutoring session, prepare a list of specific questions to ask. Vague requests like "Help me with math" lead to wasted time—tutors can’t read minds!

How to do it:

  • Review your notes and highlight 3-5 key questions.
  • Write them down in order of priority.
  • Prioritize the hardest topics first.

Example: Instead of: "I don’t get algebra." Try: "Can you explain how to solve quadratic equations using the quadratic formula? I’m stuck on step 3."

Strategy 3: Leverage Group Study Sessions for Peer Learning

While one-on-one tutoring is great, group sessions can be equally valuable—especially for collaborative subjects like history or group projects.

How to do it:

  • Join a weekly group study in your subject area.
  • Take notes together and discuss concepts.
  • Assign roles (e.g., one person explains a topic while others ask questions).

Why it works: Group sessions reinforce learning through discussion and reduce social isolation in online learning.

Strategy 4: Record and Review Tutoring Sessions

If you’re using live tutoring, consider recording the session (with permission) to review later.

How to do it:

  • Ask the tutor: "May I record this session for my notes?"
  • Listen back to catch explanations you missed.
  • Revisit key points before the next study session.

Tools to use:

  • Otter.ai (for transcription)
  • Zoom’s recording feature (if available)

Strategy 5: Combine Tutoring with Study Tools

Tutoring works best when paired with other learning resources. Wake County Libraries also offer:

  • Online databases (like Gale in Context for research)
  • Digital textbooks (via OverDrive)
  • Interactive math tools (like Khan Academy)

How to do it:

  • Use tutoring for concept clarification (e.g., "Explain how to balance chemical equations.")
  • Practice independently using Khan Academy or Mathway.
  • Review mistakes with the tutor in the next session.

Strategy 6: Set Clear Goals Before Each Session

Every tutoring session should have a specific purpose. Without goals, sessions can feel directionless and unproductive.

How to do it:

  • Define SMART goals (Specific, Measurable, Achievable, Relevant, Time-bound).

    • ❌ "I need help with math."
    • ✅ "I will master solving linear equations by next week."

  • Track progress in a study journal.

Strategy 7: Use On-Demand Help for Quick Doubts

Not every question requires a full session. Wake County’s on-demand homework help is perfect for:

  • Quick clarifications (e.g., "What’s the difference between mitosis and meiosis?")
  • Last-minute test prep (e.g., "Can you explain the Pythagorean theorem?")

How to do it:

  • Ask specific questions in the chat feature.
  • Use the "Ask a Librarian" tool for general study advice.

Strategy 8: Encourage Parents and Educators to Participate

Parents and teachers can actively support students by:

  • Scheduling joint sessions to understand the student’s progress.
  • Using tutoring for lesson planning (e.g., "How can I teach this concept better?").
  • Encouraging consistency in study habits.

How to do it:

  • Attend a parent workshop (if offered by Wake County Libraries).

Common Mistakes to Avoid When Using Wake County’s Tutoring Services

While the program is free and powerful, some users waste opportunities by making avoidable mistakes. Here’s how to steer clear of pitfalls:

Mistake 1: Treating Tutoring as a Last Resort

Problem: Waiting until the night before a test to seek help leads to overwhelm and poor results.

Solution:

  • Start early—even if you’re just clarifying a single concept.
  • Use tutoring for consistent reinforcement, not just emergencies.

Mistake 2: Being Too Vague in Questions

Problem: Asking "Help me with this math problem" without explaining what you’ve tried wastes time.

Solution:

  • Show your work (even if it’s incorrect).
  • Explain your thought process: "I tried this, but I got stuck here."

Mistake 3: Not Following Up on Advice

Problem: Getting great tips in a session but not applying them in practice.

Solution:

  • Take notes and review them later.
  • Practice the concepts independently before the next session.

Mistake 4: Ignoring On-Demand Resources

Problem: Only using live tutoring and missing out on free study tools like:

  • Interactive quizzes
  • Video lessons
  • Flashcard decks

Solution:

  • Explore the full library of resources on the Wake County website.
  • Combine tutoring with these tools for a well-rounded study plan.

Mistake 5: Not Tracking Progress

Problem: Not measuring improvement means you don’t know what’s working.

Solution:

  • Keep a study journal with:
    • Topics covered
    • Scores on practice tests
    • Key takeaways from each session

Mistake 6: Over-Reliance on Tutors for Everything

Problem: Expecting tutors to do all the work instead of active learning.

Solution:

  • Take ownership of your learning.
  • Ask questions, but also try solving problems first.
