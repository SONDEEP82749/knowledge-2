How Wake County Libraries’ Free Online Tutoring Works

Before exploring strategies, it’s crucial to understand how the program operates and who can benefit.

1. Who Can Access the Tutoring Services?

Wake County’s free online tutoring is available to:

  • Wake County residents (with a valid library card)
  • Students of all ages (K-12, college, and adult learners)
  • Parents and educators looking for supplemental support
  • Home-schooled students seeking structured guidance

No income restrictions apply, making this one of the most inclusive educational resources in North Carolina.

2. Key Features of the Program

The tutoring platform includes: ✔ Live, one-on-one sessions with certified tutors ✔ Group study sessions for collaborative learning ✔ Subject-specific help in math, science, English, history, and more ✔ Test prep assistance (SAT, ACT, AP exams, and standardized tests) ✔ Homework help for students struggling with assignments ✔ 24/7 access via the library’s website and mobile app ✔ Multilingual support for non-English speakers

3. How to Sign Up & Get Started

Getting access is simple and free—follow these steps:

  1. Visit the Wake County Public Library website (www.wakegov.com/library) and click on "Online Tutoring."
  2. Create a free library account (if you don’t have one) using your Wake County address.
  3. Log in and navigate to the "Tutoring Hub" section.
  4. Browse available tutors by subject, time zone, and language preference.
  5. Schedule a session—some are drop-in, while others require advance booking.
  6. Join via video call (using Zoom or a similar platform) or chat-based tutoring.

Pro Tip: If you’re a first-time user, book a short, 15-minute session to test the platform before committing to longer study blocks.

8 Actionable Strategies to Make the Most of Free Online Tutoring

Now that you know how to access the service, let’s explore how to use it effectively. Many students waste valuable tutoring time by not preparing properly or lacking focus. These strategies will help you get the most out of every session.

Strategy 1: Prepare a Clear List of Questions Before Your Session

Problem: Walking into a tutoring session without a plan leads to unfocused discussions and wasted time.

Solution:

  • Before your session, write down:
    • Specific questions (e.g., "How do I solve quadratic equations?" instead of "I don’t get math.")
    • Problem sets you’re struggling with
    • Concepts you need clarification on
  • Prioritize the most urgent topics to avoid getting sidetracked.

Example: Instead of: "I’m bad at chemistry." Try: "I don’t understand how to balance redox reactions. Can you walk me through an example?"

Strategy 2: Use the "Pomodoro Technique" for Focused Study

Problem: Online tutoring sessions can feel long and draining, especially for younger students.

Solution:

  • Break sessions into 25-minute chunks (Pomodoro method) with 5-minute breaks.
  • Set a timer and focus only on the topic at hand.
  • Take notes during breaks to reinforce learning.

Why It Works: A 2023 study in Journal of Educational Psychology found that short, focused study sessions improve retention by 30% compared to long, uninterrupted blocks.

Strategy 3: Record Sessions for Later Review

Problem: Some students forget key explanations immediately after the session.

Solution:

  • Ask the tutor if recording is allowed (most platforms permit it).
  • Review the recording within 24 hours to reinforce concepts.
  • Revisit notes before the next session to show progress.

Real-World Example: A Wake County high school senior, Jamie, used this method to ace her AP Chemistry exam. She recorded her tutoring sessions on organic chemistry and reviewed them daily. By exam time, she had mastered 80% of the material she initially struggled with.

Strategy 4: Ask for "Homework Assignments" from Your Tutor

Problem: Many students leave tutoring sessions feeling understood but not yet confident.

Solution:

  • Request practice problems from your tutor to work on between sessions.
  • Set a deadline (e.g., "Can you send me 5 problems to solve by Friday?").
  • Review solutions together in the next session to track improvement.

Example: If your tutor explains how to write a thesis statement, ask: "Can you give me three essay prompts to practice with?"

Strategy 5: Join Group Study Sessions for Peer Learning

Problem: Some students feel shy or isolated in one-on-one tutoring.

Solution:

  • Look for group study sessions (often scheduled on weekends).
  • Engage with other students—asking questions aloud reinforces learning.
  • Form study groups with peers outside tutoring hours.

Why It Works: A 2024 Harvard study found that collaborative learning increases knowledge retention by 25% compared to solo study.

Strategy 6: Use Tutoring for Test Prep, Not Just Homework

Problem: Many students only use tutoring for last-minute help, missing long-term benefits.

Solution:

  • Schedule regular test prep sessions (e.g., SAT/ACT, AP exams).
  • Take practice tests and review mistakes with a tutor.
  • Focus on weak areas before major assessments.

Example: *A Wake County middle schooler, Priya, used tutoring to prepare for her STAAR math test. She took weekly practice tests, reviewed errors with her tutor, and raised her score from a 78% to a 92%.*

Strategy 7: Leverage Multilingual Support for Non-English Speakers

Problem: Language barriers can make tutoring less effective for ESL students.

Solution:

  • Select a tutor who speaks your primary language (Spanish, Mandarin, etc.).
  • Use translation tools (like Google Translate) if needed, but prefer native speakers for complex subjects.
  • Ask for simplified explanations if terms are unclear.

Real-World Example: *A Wake County high school senior, Carlos, was struggling with AP Biology due to language difficulties. His tutor, who spoke Spanish fluently, broke down concepts into simple analogies, helping him pass the exam with a 4.

Strategy 8: Combine Tutoring with Other Library Resources

Problem: Some students only use tutoring without exploring other free tools.

Solution:

  • Pair tutoring with:
    • Online courses (Khan Academy, Coursera)
    • E-books & audiobooks (for supplementary reading)
    • Research databases (for college essays)
  • Ask your tutor for recommendations on additional resources.

Example: *A Wake County college student, Aisha, used tutoring for statistics and combined it with Khan Academy’s free video lessons. She completed her intro stats course with an A while saving money on private tutors.

Common Mistakes to Avoid When Using Free Online Tutoring

While Wake County’s tutoring program is exceptionally valuable, some users unintentionally sabotage their progress by making these five mistakes.

Mistake 1: Showing Up Without a Plan

What Happens?

  • You waste time discussing broad topics instead of focusing on weaknesses.
  • The tutor can’t prioritize what you truly need.

How to Fix It: ✅ Always come with a list of questions or problems. ✅ Set a clear goal (e.g., "I need help with linear equations by Friday.").

Mistake 2: Treating Tutoring Like a Crutch, Not a Tool

What Happens?

  • You only use tutoring for last-minute help instead of long-term learning.
  • You don’t practice on your own, so progress stalls.

How to Fix It: ✅ **Use tutoring to clarify concepts, then practice independently. ✅ Schedule regular sessions (not just before deadlines).

Mistake 3: Ignoring the Tutor’s Feedback

What Happens?

  • You don’t take notes or forget explanations.
  • You repeat the same mistakes in future work.

How to Fix It: ✅ **Take detailed notes during sessions. ✅ Review them within 24 hours. ✅ **Ask for written summaries of key points.

Mistake 4: Not Using Additional Library Resources

What Happens?

  • You miss out on free e-books, courses, and practice tests.
  • You reinvent the wheel instead of leveraging existing tools.

How to Fix It: ✅ **Ask your tutor for recommended resources. ✅ **Explore the library’s online database for supplemental materials.

Mistake 5: Giving Up Too Soon

What Happens?

  • You expect instant mastery and get frustrated.
  • You quit after one bad session instead of trying again.

How to Fix It: ✅ Remember: Progress takes time. ✅ Track improvements (e.g., "Last month, I struggled with fractions—now I get them!"). ✅ Celebrate small wins to stay motivated.
