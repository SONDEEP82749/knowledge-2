8 Actionable Strategies to Succeed with Wake County’s Free Online Tutoring

Not all students benefit equally from tutoring—consistency, preparation, and the right approach make the difference. Below are eight proven strategies to ensure you get the most out of Wake County’s free tutoring services.

1. Set Clear Goals Before Each Session

Before logging in, ask yourself:

  • What specific topic am I struggling with?
  • What grade or score improvement do I want?
  • How will I apply what I learn outside tutoring?

Example: If you’re prepping for the SAT Math section, your goal might be: ✔ "Understand how to solve quadratic equations in 10 minutes or less." ✔ "Practice 5 problems daily using Khan Academy’s SAT prep module."

Why it works: Tutors can tailor explanations to your exact needs rather than wasting time on topics you already master.

2. Use the "Two-Session Rule" for Deep Learning

Many students make the mistake of attending only one tutoring session and expecting instant results. Instead, follow the "Two-Session Rule":

  • Session 1: Learn the concept (ask questions, take notes).
  • Session 2 (within 24-48 hours): Apply the concept (solve problems independently).

Real-World Example: A Wake County high school senior struggling with AP Chemistry used this method:

  • First session: Tutor explained chemical equilibrium and showed examples.
  • Second session (2 days later): She solved 5 practice problems and got 90% correct—her tutor then corrected mistakes and reinforced weak areas.

3. Leverage the "Tutor as a Study Partner" Approach

Instead of seeing the tutor as an authority figure, treat them as a collaborator. Ask:

  • "Can we work through this problem together?"
  • "What’s a common mistake students make here?"
  • "Are there any online resources you recommend for this topic?"

Why it works: This active learning approach helps you internalize concepts rather than just memorizing.

4. Combine Tutoring with Free Online Resources

Wake County’s tutoring is even more powerful when paired with complementary free resources:

  • Khan Academy (for math, science, and test prep).
  • Duolingo (for language learning).
  • Grammarly (for writing and grammar).
  • Coursera/edX (for college-level courses).

Example: A Wake County college student taking statistics used:

  • Tutoring for live explanations (e.g., probability distributions).
  • Khan Academy’s statistics course for practice problems.
  • Grammarly to refine her research paper writing.

If you have a quick question (e.g., "How do I solve for x in this equation?"), use the on-demand chat feature instead of scheduling a full session. This saves time and keeps you consistently engaged.

Why it works: Collaborative learning improves retention and makes studying less isolating.

Common Mistakes to Avoid When Using Free Online Tutoring

While Wake County’s tutoring program is highly effective, some students sabotage their own progress by making these avoidable mistakes.

Mistake 1: Treating Tutoring as a "Last Resort"

What to avoid: Only using tutoring when you’re failing rather than proactively for improvement.

Solution:

  • Use tutoring for:
    • Preparing for upcoming tests (not just catching up).
    • Clarifying doubts before they become major gaps.
    • Exploring advanced topics (e.g., if you love math, ask about linear algebra).

Mistake 2: Not Preparing Questions in Advance

What to avoid: Walking into a session without clear questions, leading to wasted time.

Solution:

  • Before each session, write down:
    • "I don’t understand how to solve systems of equations."
    • "Can you explain the difference between mitosis and meiosis?"
    • "What’s the best way to structure a 5-paragraph essay?"

Mistake 3: Relying Only on Tutoring (No Independent Practice)

What to avoid: Thinking tutoring will do all the work for you.

Solution:

  • Follow the "70-30 Rule":
    • 70% of your time = Independent practice (homework, online exercises).
    • 30% of your time = Tutoring for guidance.

Mistake 4: Ignoring the On-Demand Chat Feature

What to avoid: Only using scheduled sessions and missing out on instant help.

Solution:

  • Use the chat feature for:
    • Quick doubts (e.g., "What’s the formula for area of a circle?").
    • Last-minute test prep (e.g., "Can you explain how to graph a parabola?").

Mistake 5: Not Following Up with Resources

What to avoid: Ending a session without reinforcement.

Solution:

  • After each tutoring session:
    • Review notes (write down key takeaways).
    • Practice 3-5 problems on your own.
    • Bookmark recommended resources (e.g., Khan Academy videos).

Mistake 6: Comparing Yourself to Others

What to avoid: Getting frustrated because another student seems to progress faster.

Solution:

  • Focus on your own progress, not others’.
  • Celebrate small wins (e.g., "I finally understood exponents!").

Mistake 7: Not Using the Whiteboard/Tools Provided

What to avoid: Only listening without actively participating.

Solution:

  • Use the whiteboard to:
    • Write down equations together.
    • Draw diagrams for science topics.
    • Highlight key points in writing.
