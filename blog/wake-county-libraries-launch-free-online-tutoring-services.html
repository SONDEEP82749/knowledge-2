10 Actionable Strategies to Make the Most of Wake County’s Free Tutoring Services

Now that you know the basics, let’s explore how to use these services effectively. Whether you're a student, parent, or educator, these strategies will help you optimize your tutoring experience and achieve better academic outcomes.

1. Identify Your Weakest Subject Early & Schedule Consistent Sessions

Problem: Many students wait until the last minute to seek help, leading to frustration and poor grades. Solution: Pinpoint your weakest subject or concept (e.g., algebra fractions, grammar rules) before exams or major assignments are due.

How to Do It:

  • Review your latest test scores or quiz results to identify patterns.
  • If you’re a parent, ask your child which topics they find hardest—sometimes they don’t realize they need help until it’s too late.
  • Book 15-30 minute sessions weekly (instead of cramming before a test) to build confidence.

Real-World Example: Maria, a 9th-grade student in Wake County, struggled with geometry proofs but avoided asking for help until her final exam. After starting weekly 30-minute sessions with a tutor, she improved her grade from a 68% to an 87% in just four weeks.

2. Use the "Pre-Tutoring Checklist" to Maximize Efficiency

Problem: Walking into a tutoring session without clear goals wastes time and money. Solution: Prepare a checklist before each session to ensure you get the most out of it.

Your Pre-Tutoring Checklist: ✔ Write down your specific question (e.g., "How do I solve systems of equations using substitution?"). ✔ Bring relevant materials (textbook pages, past assignments, notes). ✔ Set a time limit (e.g., "I’ll only ask two questions this session"). ✔ Note down key takeaways during the session to review later.

Real-World Example: James, a high school junior, would often sit in tutoring sessions without a clear plan, leading to unfocused discussions. After starting a checklist, he began tracking his progress and saw his SAT math score increase by 100 points in three months.

3. Leverage the "Two-Tutor Method" for Complex Topics

Problem: Some subjects (like calculus or advanced physics) require multiple perspectives to fully grasp. Solution: Use two tutors in one session—one for foundational concepts, another for application.

How to Do It:

  • Request two tutors (if available) for a collaborative session.
  • If only one tutor is available, split your session into two parts:
    1. First 15 minutes: Ask about core principles.
    2. Next 15 minutes: Apply those principles to practice problems.

Real-World Example: A college-bound senior in Wake County was stuck on AP Chemistry equilibrium problems. By alternating between a tutor who explained theory and one who worked through practice questions, she mastered the topic in half the time compared to solo studying.

4. Record & Review Tutoring Sessions for Long-Term Retention

Problem: Many students forget key concepts after a tutoring session ends. Solution: Record sessions (with permission) and review them later.

How to Do It:

  • Ask the tutor if recording is allowed (most platforms permit it).
  • Take notes during the session and rewatch the recording within 24 hours.
  • Create flashcards or summaries of what you learned.

Real-World Example: Liam, a 7th grader, would forget algebra steps after tutoring. By recording sessions and reviewing them with his parents, he reduced his mistakes by 70% on homework.

5. Use Tutoring for "Pre-Learning" Before New Lessons

Problem: Students often fall behind because they don’t understand prerequisite concepts. Solution: Use tutoring to "pre-learn" before a new unit starts.

How to Do It:

  • Check your syllabus and identify foundational topics you’re weak in.
  • Book a session 1-2 weeks before the lesson to fill knowledge gaps.
  • Example: If your next math unit is on exponents, ask a tutor to explain exponent rules before the lesson starts.

Real-World Example: Sophia, an 8th grader, struggled with fractions but didn’t realize how they applied to algebra. By getting a fraction refresher before her algebra unit, she scored 90%+ on all subsequent tests.

6. Turn Tutoring into a "Study Group" for Group Learners

Problem: Some students learn better in groups but don’t have peers with similar struggles. Solution: Invite friends or classmates to join your tutoring session.

How to Do It:

  • Check if the platform allows multiple users (some do for group study).
  • Coordinate with classmates to split up questions and learn together.
  • Take turns explaining concepts to reinforce understanding.

Real-World Example: A group of Wake County high school students formed a virtual study group using tutoring sessions. By rotating who asked questions, they all improved their grades in chemistry, even though they started at different levels.

7. Use Tutoring for "Reverse Engineering" Problem-Solving

Problem: Students often don’t know how to approach a problem type. Solution: Ask tutors to "reverse engineer" solutions with you.

How to Do It:

  • Instead of just getting the answer, ask:
    • "How would you solve this step-by-step?"
    • "What’s the first thing you’d check if stuck?"
    • "Can you show me a similar problem?"
  • Practice the method on your own afterward.

Real-World Example: Ethan, a 10th grader, would guess answers on math tests. By asking tutors to walk through their thought process, he developed a systematic approach that cut his test anxiety in half.

8. Combine Tutoring with Free Library Resources

Problem: Tutoring alone may not be enough if study materials are lacking. Solution: Pair tutoring with free library resources for a holistic learning experience.

Wake County Library Resources to Use:

  • Online databases (Gale, Britannica School) for research help.
  • Digital textbooks (many available for free).
  • Khan Academy & other free courses (complement tutoring).
  • Local workshops & webinars (check the library’s event calendar).

How to Do It:

  • After a tutoring session, search the library’s website for related study guides.
  • Ask your tutor for recommended resources (they often know the best tools).
  • Use the library’s "Ask a Librarian" service for additional help.

Real-World Example: A Wake County parent combined tutoring with the library’s digital math resources, leading to her daughter improving her math grade from a C to an A in just two months.

9. Track Progress with a "Tutoring Journal"

Problem: Without tracking, it’s hard to measure improvement. Solution: Maintain a tutoring journal to document progress over time.

What to Include in Your Journal: ✔ Date & topic of each session ✔ Key takeaways (write them down immediately after) ✔ Homework/practice problems attempted ✔ Grades & feedback from teachers ✔ Areas still needing work

Real-World Example: Daniel, a college student, kept a tutoring journal and noticed that his writing essays improved by 30% after focusing on structure and thesis statements—a concept he’d struggled with for years.

10. Advocate for Tutoring in Your School or Community

Problem: Many students don’t know tutoring exists or can’t access it. Solution: Spread the word so others can benefit too.

How to Do It:

  • Share the library’s tutoring link with classmates.
  • Ask teachers to promote it in announcements.
  • Write a review on the library’s website or social media.
  • Organize a "Tutoring Day" at your school to introduce students.

Real-World Example: A Wake County high school student created a flyer about the tutoring service and posted it in the library and classrooms. Within a month, dozens of new students started using the service.

Common Mistakes to Avoid When Using Wake County’s Free Tutoring

Even the best resources can backfire if misused. Here are five common mistakes and how to avoid them:

❌ Mistake 1: Treating Tutoring as a "Last Resort"

Why It’s Bad: Waiting until before a test means you’re playing catch-up instead of preventing struggles. Solution: Use tutoring proactively—not just when grades drop.

❌ Mistake 2: Not Preparing Before Sessions

Why It’s Bad: Tutors can’t read your mind—you must know exactly what you need help with. Solution: Write down specific questions before logging in.

❌ Mistake 3: Relying Only on Tutoring (Ignoring Other Resources)

Why It’s Bad: Tutoring is supplemental, not a replacement for practice. Solution: Combine tutoring with practice problems, textbooks, and library resources.

❌ Mistake 4: Not Following Up After Sessions

Why It’s Bad: If you don’t review what you learned, you’ll forget it quickly. Solution: Take notes, record sessions, and practice what you learned.

❌ Mistake 5: Comparing Yourself to Others

Why It’s Bad: Every student learns at different speeds—don’t get discouraged if progress seems slow. Solution: Focus on your own improvement, not others’ success.
