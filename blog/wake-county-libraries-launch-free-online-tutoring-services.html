How Wake County’s Free Online Tutoring Services Work: A Step-by-Step Guide

Wake County Libraries’ Tutoring@YourLibrary program is a no-cost, 24/7 resource that connects students with certified tutors via live chat, video calls, and even homework help. Here’s how it functions:

1. Accessing the Tutoring Portal

To begin, you must have:

  • A valid Wake County Public Library card (available for free with residency verification).
  • A computer, tablet, or smartphone with internet access.
  • A Google account (for some platforms).

Where to find it?

  • Visit the Wake County Libraries Website and navigate to the "Online Resources" section.
  • Look for "Tutoring@YourLibrary" under the "Learning & Education" tab.
  • Alternatively, search "Wake County Libraries Tutoring" on Google.

2. Choosing Your Subject & Grade Level

The platform offers over 30 subjects, including:

  • Math (Algebra, Calculus, Statistics)
  • Science (Biology, Chemistry, Physics)
  • English & Language Arts (Grammar, Essay Writing, Literature)
  • History & Social Studies
  • Test Prep (SAT, ACT, AP Exams)
  • Foreign Languages (Spanish, French, Mandarin)
  • College & Career Readiness

You can filter by grade level (K-12, college, adult learners) or specific needs (homework help, exam prep, essay editing).

3. Scheduling a Session (Live or On-Demand)

Wake County offers two main tutoring formats:

  • Live Tutoring (Real-Time)
    • Available Monday–Thursday, 1:00 PM–9:00 PM (EST).
    • Tutors are certified educators with expertise in their fields.
    • Sessions last 30–60 minutes, with no limit on how many you can book.
  • On-Demand Tutoring (Asynchronous)
    • Upload a photo or document of your question.
    • A tutor responds within 24 hours with a detailed explanation.
    • Best for quick homework checks or last-minute study help.

4. Connecting with Your Tutor

Once scheduled, you’ll receive a link via email or text to join the session. The platform supports:

  • Video calls (via Zoom or similar tools).
  • Live chat (text-based Q&A).
  • Screen-sharing (for math problems, diagrams, or coding).

Pro Tip: If you’re using a mobile device, ensure your camera and microphone are working before joining.

5. Maximizing the Session

To get the most out of your tutoring, follow these best practices: ✔ Prepare your questions in advance (list topics you’re struggling with). ✔ Have your textbook or notes ready for reference. ✔ Engage actively—ask follow-up questions if something isn’t clear. ✔ Take notes during the session to reinforce learning. ✔ Practice what you learned immediately after the session.

6. Tracking Progress & Resources

Wake County Libraries provides:

  • Session summaries (after each live tutoring).
  • Downloadable worksheets and practice problems.
  • Recommended study guides and online courses.
  • Access to Mango Languages and Gale Courses for additional learning.

Solution:

Common Mistakes to Avoid When Using Wake County’s Tutoring Services

While Wake County’s tutoring program is incredibly valuable, some users make avoidable mistakes that limit its effectiveness. Here’s how to steer clear of pitfalls:

Mistake 1: Waiting Until the Last Minute

Problem: Cramming for a test or finishing homework at the last second makes it harder for tutors to explain concepts fully.

Solution:

  • Schedule sessions at least 2–3 days before a test.
  • Example: If you have a Friday quiz, book a session on Tuesday or Wednesday.

Real Fix: Instead of waiting until Thursday night to ask about chemistry reactions, schedule a Tuesday session to understand the basics first.

Mistake 2: Not Preparing Questions in Advance

Problem: Tutors can’t read minds—if you don’t know what you don’t know, the session will be unproductive.

Solution:

  • List 3–5 specific questions before the session.
  • Example: Instead of saying, "I don’t get math," ask:
    • "How do I solve systems of equations using substitution?"
    • "Can you explain the difference between independent and dependent variables?"

Real Fix: Before your session, review your notes and highlight confusing topics to discuss.

Mistake 3: Skipping Practice After Tutoring

Problem: Tutoring alone doesn’t reinforce learning—you must apply what you’ve learned.

Solution:

  • Do practice problems immediately after the session.
  • Example: If your tutor explains exponential growth, work 5 problems on your own.
  • Use Wake County’s online resources (like Gale Courses) for extra practice.

Real Fix: After your session, set a timer for 20 minutes to practice independently before moving on.
