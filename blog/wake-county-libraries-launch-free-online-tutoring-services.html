How Wake County Libraries’ Free Online Tutoring Works

Wake County Public Library’s new virtual tutoring platform is designed to be user-friendly, flexible, and inclusive. Here’s a breakdown of how it functions:

1. Who Can Access the Service?

The program is open to all Wake County residents, including:

  • K-12 students (public, private, and homeschooled)
  • College students (undergraduate and graduate)
  • Adult learners (career development, GED prep, language learning)
  • Parents and teachers (for homework help and lesson planning)

2. Subjects Covered

The tutoring service spans multiple disciplines, including:

  • Math (algebra, calculus, statistics)
  • Science (biology, chemistry, physics)
  • English & Language Arts (reading comprehension, essay writing)
  • History & Social Studies (AP exams, research papers)
  • Foreign Languages (Spanish, French, Mandarin)
  • Test Prep (SAT, ACT, AP exams, NC Final Exams)

3. How to Sign Up

Registration is simple and free:

  1. Visit the WCPL website (www.wakegov.com/library) and navigate to the Tutoring Services page.
  2. Create an account using your Wake County library card (or get one in-person or online).
  3. Schedule sessions via the online calendar or walk-in availability (for immediate help).
  4. Connect via Zoom or a secure virtual platform—no downloads required.

4. Tutoring Formats Available

Wake County offers three main tutoring models:

  • One-on-One Tutoring – Personalized sessions with a dedicated tutor.
  • Group Study Sessions – Collaborative learning with peers (great for test prep).
  • Drop-In Help – Instant assistance for quick questions (no appointment needed).

5. Hours of Operation

To accommodate busy schedules, tutoring is available:

  • Weekdays: 10 AM – 8 PM
  • Weekends: 12 PM – 6 PM
  • Extended hours during finals week (additional support for stressed students)

6. Technology Requirements

All you need is: ✔ A smartphone, tablet, or computer with internet access ✔ A Wake County library card (or a free digital card if you don’t have one) ✔ Basic tech literacy (tutors assist with setup if needed)

8 Actionable Strategies to Maximize Wake County’s Free Online Tutoring

Now that you know how the program works, let’s dive into how to use it effectively. These strategies will help students, parents, and educators get the most out of the service.

1. Schedule Sessions During Peak Productivity Hours

Not all students learn at the same time. Morning sessions (9–11 AM) are ideal for visual learners, while afternoon (2–4 PM) works better for kinesthetic learners. Experiment to find your child’s best focus window.

Pro Tip: If your child struggles with attention span, try shorter, frequent sessions (20–30 minutes) rather than long marathons.

2. Use the "Pre-Session Prep" Method

Before each tutoring session, review the material briefly. Tutors can:

  • Explain concepts you don’t understand
  • Provide practice problems (but not the full solution)
  • Clarify textbook confusion

Example: If your child is struggling with algebra word problems, ask the tutor to break down one problem step-by-step before attempting more.

3. Leverage Group Study Sessions for Test Prep

Group tutoring is perfect for standardized tests like the SAT, ACT, or NC Final Exams. Why?

  • Peer accountability keeps everyone engaged.
  • Different perspectives help reinforce learning.
  • Collaborative problem-solving builds critical thinking.

Real-World Example: A Wake County high school senior, Jamie, joined a group SAT math session. Instead of memorizing formulas alone, she worked with three peers to solve 10 practice problems in 45 minutes. By the end, she felt 30% more confident and scored 120 points higher on her next practice test.

4. Record Sessions for Later Review

Many tutors allow session recordings (with permission). This is gold for students who:

  • Missed a concept but can’t attend another session.
  • Want to rewatch explanations at their own pace.
  • Need extra reinforcement for tricky topics.

How to Ask: "Can I record this session so I can review it later?" Most tutors will approve if it’s for personal study—just confirm privacy policies first.

5. Combine Tutoring with Library Resources

Wake County libraries offer more than just tutoring. Pair sessions with:

  • Online databases (like Gale, Britannica School, or Khan Academy)
  • E-books & audiobooks (for supplementary reading)
  • Career & college prep tools (for high school juniors/seniors)

Example: A 9th-grade student struggling with AP Biology used tutoring for cell biology concepts, then borrowed an e-book on microscopy techniques from the library to deepen understanding.

6. Set Clear Goals Before Each Session

Vague requests like "Help me with math" lead to wasted time. Instead, be specific: ❌ "I don’t get this chapter." ✅ "Can we focus on solving quadratic equations? I’m stuck on the discriminant method."

Why It Works:

  • Tutors can tailor explanations to your exact needs.
  • You stay on track instead of drifting between topics.

7. Use Drop-In Help for Quick Doubts

Not every question requires a full session. Drop-in help is perfect for:

  • Homework verification (e.g., "Is my answer correct?")
  • Last-minute test prep (e.g., "What’s the best way to study for a pop quiz?")
  • Tech support (e.g., "How do I use the graphing calculator?")

When to Avoid Drop-In:

  • If you need deep conceptual help (schedule a full session instead).

8. Follow Up with Practice & Reinforcement

Tutoring is not a one-time fix. After each session:

  1. Review notes taken during the session.
  2. Practice similar problems (use Khan Academy or library resources).
  3. Reach out again if confusion persists.

Example: A Wake County middle schooler used tutoring to understand fractions, then practiced 10 problems daily using a free math workbook from the library. Within two weeks, her grades improved from a C- to an A.

Common Mistakes to Avoid When Using Wake County’s Free Tutoring

While the program is highly beneficial, some users make avoidable mistakes that reduce its effectiveness. Here’s how to steer clear of pitfalls:

Mistake 1: Waiting Until the Last Minute

Problem: Cramming before a test leads to information overload and poor retention. Solution:

  • Start early—even 1–2 sessions before a major exam can help.
  • Break study into chunks (e.g., 30-minute sessions, 3x a week).

Mistake 2: Not Being Specific About Needs

Problem: Vague requests like "Help me with science" waste time. Solution:

  • Narrow it down: "I’m stuck on photosynthesis equations—can we focus on that?"
  • Bring specific questions (e.g., "Why does this reaction occur?").

Mistake 3: Skipping Homework Practice

Problem: Tutors explain concepts, but students don’t apply them. Solution:

  • Do practice problems after each session.
  • Revisit old homework with the tutor’s guidance.

Mistake 4: Ignoring Library Resources

Problem: Some students only use tutoring and miss free e-books, databases, and study guides. Solution:

  • Explore the library’s digital collection (e.g., Khan Academy, Britannica School).
  • Ask tutors for recommendations on supplementary materials.

Mistake 5: Not Following Up

Problem: After one session, students stop practicing, and progress stalls. Solution:

  • Schedule regular check-ins (e.g., every 2 weeks).
  • Track progress in a notebook or digital doc.

Mistake 6: Assuming Tutors Are Like Teachers

Problem: Some students expect lecture-style teaching, but tutors guide, not lecture. Solution:

  • Engage actively—ask questions, take notes, and participate in discussions.
  • Treat it like a conversation, not a one-way lesson.

Mistake 7: Overlooking Drop-In Help for Quick Fixes

Problem: Students schedule long sessions for simple questions. Solution:

  • Use drop-in help for quick verification (e.g., "Is my answer correct?").
  • Save deep dives for scheduled sessions.

Mistake 8: Not Using Recordings Wisely

Problem: Some students record sessions but never review them. Solution:

  • Listen back within 24 hours of the session.
  • Highlight key takeaways in a notebook.
