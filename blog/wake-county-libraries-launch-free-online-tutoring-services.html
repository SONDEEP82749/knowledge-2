8 Actionable Strategies to Make the Most of Wake County’s Free Tutoring

1. Identify Your Weakest Subject Early

Before jumping into tutoring, assess your strengths and weaknesses using:

  • Practice tests (available on the WCPL portal)
  • Flashcard apps (Quizlet, Anki)
  • Past homework mistakes (review errors in textbooks or online assignments)

Example: If you’re struggling with algebra, take a diagnostic quiz on the tutoring platform to pinpoint exact gaps (e.g., solving quadratic equations, graphing functions).

2. Schedule Consistent Tutoring Sessions

Irregular tutoring leads to inconsistent progress. Instead:

  • Block 1-2 sessions per week (e.g., Monday & Thursday evenings).
  • Set a fixed time (e.g., 6-7 PM) to build a routine.
  • Use the calendar feature on the WCPL portal to auto-schedule.

Real-World Example: A high school senior in Raleigh used 3 tutoring sessions per week for SAT prep and raised their score from 1100 to 1350 in 3 months.

3. Prepare Before Each Session

Tutors are more effective when students come prepared. Before each session: ✔ List 3-5 specific questions (e.g., "How do I solve systems of equations?"). ✔ Bring past assignments (if working on homework help). ✔ Review notes from class to avoid repeating concepts.

Avoid: Walking into a session with no agenda—this wastes both your and the tutor’s time.

4. Use the Tutoring Portal’s Additional Resources

The WCPL platform offers more than just live tutoring. Explore:

  • Interactive lessons (Khan Academy, BrainPOP integrations)
  • Study guides & cheat sheets (downloadable PDFs)
  • Recorded sessions (for review later)

Example: A middle school student struggling with fractions used the interactive Khan Academy lessons alongside tutoring and mastered the topic in 2 weeks.

5. Leverage Group Tutoring for Peer Learning

While one-on-one tutoring is great, group sessions can:

  • Reduce costs (if available)
  • Encourage collaboration (students teach each other)
  • Build confidence (less pressure than 1:1)

How to find group sessions:

  • Check the "Group Study" tab on the WCPL portal.
  • Ask tutors if they offer subject-specific study groups.

6. Track Progress & Adjust Strategies

Use a simple progress tracker (Google Sheet or notebook) to:

  • Note topics mastered vs. still struggling with.
  • Compare pre- and post-tutoring test scores.
  • Adjust study methods based on feedback.

Example: A college freshman tracked her writing essays progress and realized she needed more grammar tutoring, so she scheduled extra sessions.

7. Combine Tutoring with Active Learning Techniques

Tutoring works best when paired with self-directed learning. Try:

  • The Feynman Technique (explain concepts aloud as if teaching someone else).
  • Spaced Repetition (use Anki for flashcards).
  • Practice Problems (after each session, do 10-15 related problems).

Avoid: Relying solely on tutoring—active practice reinforces learning.

8. Ask for Test-Taking Strategies

Many tutors specialize in standardized test prep (SAT, ACT, AP). Ask them for:

  • Time management tips (e.g., how to pace a 60-minute math section).
  • Question elimination strategies (e.g., marking wrong answers to avoid).
  • Common traps (e.g., misreading questions).

Example: A high school junior used ACT test-taking strategies from her tutor and improved her score by 20 points in one month.

Common Mistakes & How to Avoid Them

1. Not Setting Clear Goals

Mistake: Walking into tutoring without knowing what you want to achieve. Solution:

  • Before the first session, ask yourself:
    • "Do I need help with homework, test prep, or concept understanding?"
    • "What grade do I want to improve?"

Example: A student who just wanted "help with math" ended up wasting time on basic arithmetic when they needed calculus help.

2. Skipping Preparation

Mistake: Showing up to a session without questions or materials. Solution:

  • Review notes before the session.
  • List 3-5 specific questions (e.g., "How do I solve word problems?").

Avoid: Saying, "Just teach me everything I don’t know"—this is too broad.

3. Over-Reliance on Tutors

Mistake: Expecting tutors to do all the work while the student does nothing. Solution:

  • Practice actively between sessions.
  • Apply concepts in homework or practice problems.

Example: A student who only listened to tutoring but never practiced saw no improvement in his grades.

4. Ignoring Additional Resources

Mistake: Only using tutoring and nothing else. Solution:

  • Combine tutoring with:
    • Online courses (Khan Academy, Coursera)
    • Flashcards (Quizlet, Anki)
    • Study groups (Discord, Reddit)

Example: A student who only used tutoring struggled, but one who used tutoring + Quizlet saw faster progress.

5. Not Following Up

Mistake: Getting tutoring help but not reviewing what was learned. Solution:

  • Take notes during sessions.
  • Revisit concepts the next day.
  • Ask for practice problems to reinforce learning.

Avoid: Forgetting what the tutor taught—this leads to wasted sessions.

FAQs About Wake County’s Free Online Tutoring

Below are five frequently asked questions with detailed answers to help you make the most of this service.

1. Do I need a Wake County library card to use the tutoring services?

Answer: ✅ No, you do not need a library card to access Wake County’s free online tutoring. ✅ All services are available online via the Tutoring@YourLibrary portal. ✅ First-time users only need to create a free account with an email address.

Why? The program is designed to remove barriers for students across Wake County, regardless of their library membership status.

2. What if I can’t find a tutor for my specific subject?

Answer: If your preferred subject or time slot is unavailable, try these alternatives: ✔ Check for group tutoring sessions (often available for popular subjects like math and English). ✔ Ask the tutor scheduler if they can recommend another expert (some tutors specialize in multiple areas). ✔ Use the "Waitlist" feature—if a tutor is busy, you can be notified when a slot opens. ✔ Explore self-paced resources (Khan Academy, BrainPOP) while waiting for a tutor.

Example: A student looking for AP Chemistry help found a group study session when no one-on-one slots were available.

3. Can I use tutoring for college-level courses?

Answer: ✅ Yes! Wake County’s tutoring covers:

  • Undergraduate courses (math, science, English composition)
  • Graduate prep (GRE, GMAT strategies)
  • Career skills (resume writing, Excel, coding basics)

How to access college-level help:

  • Filter tutors by "College/Adult" in the subject selection.
  • Request a specialized tutor (e.g., a chemistry professor for organic chemistry).

Avoid: Assuming tutoring is only for K-12—it’s for all learners.

4. How do I handle technical issues during a tutoring session?

Answer: If you experience Zoom/connectivity problems, try these steps:

  1. Restart your device (sometimes fixes glitches).
  2. Switch to a different browser (Chrome, Firefox, Edge).
  3. Use a wired connection (Wi-Fi can be unstable).
  4. Contact WCPL tech support via the portal’s "Help" button.
  5. Reschedule if the issue persists.

Pro Tip: Test your Zoom/Meet setup 10 minutes before the session to avoid delays.

5. Is there a limit to how many sessions I can book?

Answer: ✅ No strict limits, but popular tutors may have scheduling caps. ✔ First-time users can book up to 3 sessions per week (increases after verification). ✔ Returning users (with consistent attendance) may get priority access.

How to maximize sessions:

  • Be punctual (tutors may deny late bookings).
  • Cancel with 24-hour notice to free up slots for others.
  • Use group sessions if one-on-one is fully booked.
