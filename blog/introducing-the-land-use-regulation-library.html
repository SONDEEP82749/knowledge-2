8 Actionable Strategies for Smarter Zoning

To get the most out of the Land Use Regulation Library, here are 8 actionable strategies for smarter zoning:

  • Conduct a thorough zoning audit: Review your existing zoning regulations to identify areas for improvement and opportunities for reform.
  • Engage with stakeholders: Collaborate with community members, developers, and other stakeholders to ensure that your zoning regulations reflect the needs and priorities of all parties involved.
  • Use data-driven approaches: Leverage data and analytics to inform your zoning decisions and create more effective land use policies.
  • Foster mixed-use development: Encourage the creation of vibrant, mixed-use neighborhoods that combine residential, commercial, and recreational spaces.
  • Promote affordable housing: Implement inclusionary zoning policies and other strategies to increase the supply of affordable housing and reduce displacement.
  • Support transportation planning: Integrate transportation planning into your zoning regulations to reduce congestion, promote walkability, and enhance overall mobility.
  • Protect environmental resources: Implement zoning regulations that protect and preserve natural resources, such as wetlands, waterways, and wildlife habitats.
  • Monitor and evaluate performance: Regularly assess the effectiveness of your zoning regulations and make adjustments as needed to ensure that they are meeting their intended goals.

Common Mistakes to Avoid in Zoning

While zoning can be a powerful tool for shaping the built environment, there are several common mistakes to avoid. These include:

  • Failing to engage with stakeholders: Zoning regulations that are imposed without input from the community can lead to resentment, resistance, and ultimately, ineffective policy.
  • Ignoring the needs of vulnerable populations: Zoning regulations that fail to account for the needs of low-income households, people of color, and other marginalized groups can exacerbate existing social and economic inequalities.
  • Overemphasizing single-use development: Zoning regulations that prioritize single-use development can lead to sprawl, congestion, and a lack of vibrancy and diversity in urban neighborhoods.
  • Disregarding environmental concerns: Zoning regulations that neglect environmental concerns can result in the degradation of natural resources, increased pollution, and reduced quality of life.
