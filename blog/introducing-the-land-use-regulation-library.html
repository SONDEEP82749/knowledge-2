Actionable Strategies for Smarter Zoning and Planning

To get the most out of the Land Use Regulation Library, here are 10 actionable strategies or tips to consider:

  • Conduct a thorough analysis of your community's existing land use regulations, including zoning codes, ordinances, and comprehensive plans. This will help you identify areas for improvement and opportunities for reform.
  • Engage with stakeholders and the community to build support for land use regulation reform and ensure that new regulations reflect the needs and values of local residents and businesses.
  • Use data and analytics to inform land use decision-making, including data on population growth, economic development, and environmental impacts.
  • Consider adopting form-based codes that focus on the physical form and character of buildings and public spaces, rather than traditional use-based zoning.
  • Incorporate green infrastructure and sustainable design principles into land use regulations to promote environmental sustainability and resilience.
  • Provide incentives for affordable housing and mixed-use development, such as density bonuses, tax credits, or streamlined permitting processes.
  • Ensure that land use regulations are accessible and easy to understand, including online resources and interactive tools to help users navigate complex regulations.
  • Monitor and evaluate the effectiveness of land use regulations over time, using data and feedback from stakeholders to identify areas for improvement.
  • Foster collaboration and coordination between different departments and agencies involved in land use regulation, including planning, zoning, and public works.
  • Stay up-to-date with the latest trends and best practices in land use regulation, including new technologies, policies, and research findings.

Common Mistakes to Avoid in Land Use Regulation

Despite the many benefits of effective land use regulation, there are also common mistakes to avoid. These include:

  • Failing to engage with stakeholders and the community, resulting in regulations that do not reflect the needs and values of local residents and businesses.
  • Not providing adequate incentives for affordable housing and mixed-use development, resulting in a lack of diverse and inclusive communities.
  • Not incorporating green infrastructure and sustainable design principles into land use regulations, resulting in environmental degradation and reduced resilience.
  • Not ensuring that land use regulations are accessible and easy to understand, resulting in confusion and frustration among users.
  • Not monitoring and evaluating the effectiveness of land use regulations over time, resulting in a lack of accountability and improvement.
